# ATS vendor mechanics: how CVs are filtered and ranked (2025–2026)

Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[DOC]** vendor documentation, help center, or release notes
- **[SURVEY]** a survey or study, with its sample noted
- **[LEGAL]** court filings or rulings
- **[3P]** third-party commentary or vendor marketing; weaker
- **[FOLKLORE]** a claim that is widely repeated but has no verifiable basis

Research date: 2026-11-03. Older facts are flagged where they appear.

## Q1. Vendor by vendor: what automated filtering exists, and what is on by default?

### Takeaway
Every major ATS can auto-reject applicants, but only through rules the employer sets up. In almost every documented case the rule runs on the candidate's **answers to application or screening questions** (knockouts), not on the CV text. AI match scores and grades (Workday HiredScore, Greenhouse Talent Matching, Ashby, Oracle, iCIMS, Lever Talent Fit, SmartRecruiters) are documented as **ranking and sorting aids that do not reject on their own**. They are mostly opt-in or need an admin to enable them. In practice, though, a low grade decides where a CV sits in the recruiter's queue.

### Cited Findings

**Workday (Recruiting + HiredScore)**
- [DOC] HiredScore grades candidates A–D. A means the candidate "meets or exceeds" the basic requirements, B "meets" them, C meets "some but not all", and D "doesn't meet" them. The docs say C/D candidates "might still be a great fit" and tell recruiters to sort applicants by grade, A to D. — [Workday HiredScore: Candidate grades](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/recruiter-productivity-/reference--candidate-grades.html); [Concept: HiredScore grades](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/recruiter-productivity-/concept--hiredscore-grades.html)
- [DOC] HiredScore grades come from how well the CV matches the skills, education and experience in the requisition's job description. — [HiredScore help: How does HiredScore assign grades](https://intercom.help/hiredscore/en/articles/6842043-how-does-hiredscore-assign-grades-to-candidates)
- [DOC] The August 2026 release note says grading is now LLM-driven: "Each qualification is now assessed directly instead of translating the qualifications through a fixed, preset framework." "Basic qualifications determine whether a candidate is graded A/B or C/D." "Candidates who meet all scoped basic qualifications receive an A or B." Preferred-qualification match and basic-qualification scores then split A from B and C from D. A new "Fit & Gap" view lists which requirements are met or unmet, with explanations linked to the parsed profile (experience, education, skills, languages, certifications, summary). The feature is **opt-in through Workday Support**. Requisitions opened before the cutover keep the legacy algorithm, and customers can revert. — [HiredScore release notes, 5 Aug 2026](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/hiredscore-release-notes/2026/august/wednesday--5th-august.html?toc=0.1.4.0)
- [DOC] An older HiredScore definition, last updated around 2023, also required that an A-grade candidate meet most preferred qualifications and pass an average match threshold. Definitions therefore differ by version. — [HiredScore grades explained (Intercom)](https://intercom.help/hiredscore/en/articles/9178940-hiredscore-grades-explained)
- [DOC] Workday's native **Candidate Skills Match** gives each application a Skills Match Score "based on the similarity of skills between the job application and job requisition". It runs in the background on active applications and shows "Skills Matched" and "Skills Not Found" columns in the candidate grid. It is "automatically available" to UMSA customers, while MSA customers must enable it through Innovation Services. Workday "plans to retire the functionality in a future release." — [Workday admin guide: Candidate Skills Match](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/candidate-skills-match/ave1604091571278.html)
- [DOC, via search summary] The Skills Match categories are Strong, Good, Fair, Low, Pending and Unable to Score. Required skills on the requisition carry more weight. The calculation "doesn't consider how recently a candidate acquired relevant skills, how long they used them, or total years of work experience." — [Workday admin guide: Candidate Skills Match concept](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/candidate-skills-match/bmj1604095304483.html)
- [DOC] Workday prospect and candidate rules "are evaluated in the order of display. When a candidate meets a rule, the step or disposition chosen for that rule is processed". This rule-driven disposition mechanism can move candidates automatically. — [Workday admin guide: prospect and candidate management](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/prospect-and-candidate-management/uwd1485375819245.html)
- [LEGAL] *Mobley v. Workday* (N.D. Cal., No. 23-cv-00770-RFL). In July 2024 the court held that an AI screening vendor can be directly liable as the employer's "agent". On 16 May 2025 it conditionally certified an ADEA collective of applicants aged 40+ who were rejected through Workday's AI screening since about September 2020. The claim is disparate impact, and the court found plausible a "unified policy" in the form of Workday's AI screening. — [Holland & Knight, May 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/federal-court-allows-collective-action-lawsuit-over-alleged); [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/news-insights/discrimination-lawsuit-over-workdays-ai-hiring-tools-can-proceed-as-class-action-6-things)
- [3P, unverified] One blog says Workday's filings disclosed about 1.1 billion applications rejected through its tools during the covered period. Not checked against the filings. — [jobcannon.io explainer](https://jobcannon.io/blog/mobley-workday-class-action-explained)

**Greenhouse**
- [DOC] Talent Matching: "Talent Matching is assistive AI, not automated-decision-making." "It does not automatically advance or reject candidates." Bulk advance or reject is user-initiated. — [Greenhouse Support: Talent Matching](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/41396009937307-Talent-Matching)

- [DOC] A candidate goes to "Needs manual review" when the resume could not be processed by AI, when AI is off for the job, or when the candidate used the optional AI opt-out. — [Greenhouse Support: Talent Matching](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/41396009937307-Talent-Matching); [Talent Matching Data Processing FAQ](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/41131616864283-Talent-Matching-Data-Processing-FAQ)

**Ashby**
- [DOC] Auto-reject runs **only on application-form answers**: "The Rejection Condition is based on application questions." It offers multiple-choice conditions (Equals, Is empty) and text conditions (Contains, Starts with, Ends with, "Similar to" fuzzy match) with nested AND/OR/NOT. It runs "only… at the point of the candidate's application submission" and not on historical applications. Rules apply in listed order. Applications submitted through application-request links skip the rules. The rejection email can be delayed by a configurable number of business days. — [Ashby docs: Auto-reject applications](https://docs.ashbyhq.com/auto-reject-applications)
- [DOC] AI-Assisted Application Review lets recruiters define criteria, which can be auto-suggested from the job description. The AI evaluates resumes against each criterion, and Ashby's own write-up says "no decisions are made" by the AI. It is billed per evaluation (credits), and editing criteria allows "save and re-evaluate". — [Ashby: AI-assisted application review release](https://www.ashbyhq.com/blog/product/ai-assisted-application-review); [Ashby: AI-assisted review in practice](https://www.ashbyhq.com/blog/recruiting/ai-assisted-application-review-in-practice); [Ashby docs: AI features](https://docs.ashbyhq.com/ai-assisted-application-review)

**LinkedIn (Jobs / Recruiter)**

**Indeed**
- [DOC] Employers mark screener questions as "deal breakers" (must-have). Applicants who fail one are "automatically deprioritized and placed in the Rejected Tab". The employer can review them later and move them to Reviewed. Pre-made questions are suggested from the job title: years of experience, high school diploma, valid license, willingness to travel, interview availability. Formats are yes/no, short answer, multiple choice, date/number and file upload. — [Indeed Hiring Hub: screener questions](https://www.indeed.com/hire/resources/howtohub/how-to-use-screener-questions-on-indeed)
- [DOC, via search summary] Candidates who fail a *preferred* question stay in the pool and are labeled as not meeting it. The optional rejection email goes out three days after the status changes to Rejected. — [Indeed employer help](https://in.indeed.com/help/employers/articles/115005660163)

### Inferences
- Automatic rejection has the same shape across vendors: an employer-written rule fires on structured answers (yes/no, multiple choice, number) at submission. A candidate-side agent should treat **the screening questions**, not the CV, as the main hard-reject surface, and should warn that a truthful "No" on a must-have usually ends the application at once.
- AI grades are documented as "assistive", but they set queue order. Under volume, a C or D grade (HiredScore) or "Limited" (Greenhouse) probably means the application is never opened, which works much like a soft reject. *Mobley* shows courts are willing to treat vendor AI screening as a decision-making tool regardless of the "assistive" label.
- Most AI ranking features are opt-in or admin-enabled: Oracle is off by default, Lever Talent Fit is off by default, HiredScore's new logic is opt-in, Greenhouse Talent Matching is admin-enabled and limited to certain tiers, Ashby is credit-billed. So for any given posting, the agent cannot assume AI ranking is running.

### Gaps
- No Greenhouse primary doc found on rule-based auto-reject from application questions. Greenhouse is widely reported to support rejecting on application-question answers, but I did not verify it this session.
- No primary iCIMS knockout docs. No primary SAP SuccessFactors AI-ranking docs. No Workday primary doc on questionnaire knockout configuration (only the generic rule-disposition page).
- LinkedIn Recruiter's AI "Hiring Assistant" and LinkedIn's AI applicant ranking (2024–2026) were not researched in depth.
- Default states are rarely stated explicitly. Defaults are known only for Oracle (off), Lever Talent Fit (off), and Workday Skills Match (auto-available for UMSA).

## Q2. How common is automatic rejection from CV content vs from knockout answers? (the "75%" claim, HBS Hidden Workers, recruiter surveys)

### Takeaway
The claim that "75% of resumes are rejected by ATS" is **folklore**. It traces to marketing by Preptel, a defunct resume-optimization vendor (around 2012), and no methodology was ever published. The best evidence available is weak in different ways: a small recruiter survey (Enhancv, n=25, 2025) says content-based auto-rejection is rare and knockouts are near-universal. Employer-side research (HBS/Accenture 2021) says rigid filters do exclude qualified people, mostly through employer-configured criteria. The *Mobley* litigation alleges large-scale automated rejection through AI screening, but those claims are unproven.

### Cited Findings
- [FOLKLORE] The 75% figure came from Preptel, a resume service that shut down around 2013. It was a 2012 marketing claim with no study, peer review or sample published. Some retellings say Preptel "tested 10,000 resumes". — [The Interview Guys: the ATS rejection myth](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=1967); [HiringThing: ATS aren't excluding applicants, people are](https://blog.hiringthing.com/applicant-tracking-system-myths); [Future of Good](https://futureofgood.co/what-everyone-gets-wrong-about-the-modern-job-search-and-what-actually-works/)
- [SURVEY, n=25, vendor-run] Enhancv interviewed 25 US recruiters in September–October 2025. 92% said their ATS does not auto-reject based on formatting, design, missing keywords or low AI match scores. The two exceptions (8%) used Bullhorn and BambooHR for high-volume seasonal hiring, with triggers tied to licenses, work authorization, location or very low match scores. Knockout questions were reported in use by all of them. 44% of their platforms had AI fit scores, used as a "loose guide". 68% said the myth reaches candidates through LinkedIn/TikTok. Enhancv sells resume tools. — [IT Brief UK coverage](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/study-reveals-ats-rarely-auto-rejects-cvs-debunks-75-myth); [BetaNews, 2025-11-10](https://betanews.com/2025/11/10/study-debunks-viral-claim-that-ai-filters-out-most-job-applicants/); [WBOC press release](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/press_releases/the-75-ats-rejection-myth-shattered-92-of-recruiters-confirm-applicant-tracking-systems-do-not/article_f141f112-dc9e-5082-9be5-1ea7b6444456.html)
- [SURVEY, 2021 report, 2020-era data] Fuller & Raman (HBS) with Accenture, *Hidden Workers: Untapped Talent*. 88% of employers agreed that qualified high-skilled candidates are screened out for not matching the job description's exact criteria (94% for middle-skilled). More than 90% of employers use such systems to filter or rank. The report estimates about 27.4 million US "hidden workers" as of March 2020 and recommends moving ATS filters from "negative" (exclusionary) to "affirmative" criteria. — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/report-applicant-tracking-systems-may-exclude-whole-segments-of-candidates/606614/); [IFLScience](https://www.iflscience.com/automated-recruiting-systems-are-dismissing-millions-of-talented-applicants-says-report-60888); [Report PDF (SHRM-hosted copy)](https://nahr.shrm.org/sites/default/files/hiddenworkers09032021.pdf); [Brian Heger summary](https://www.brianheger.com/hidden-workers-untapped-talent-harvard-business-school-and-accenture/)
- [LEGAL] *Mobley v. Workday* alleges, and the court found plausible enough for conditional certification (May 2025), that Workday's AI screening rejected applicants at scale, many with no human review. This is an allegation, not a merits finding. — [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/federal-court-allows-collective-action-lawsuit-over-alleged)
- [LEGAL, 2023] The EEOC's settlement with iTutorGroup concerned software that "automatically reject[ed] female applicants age 55 or older and male applicants age 60 or older". This is a documented case of a hard-coded content-based auto-reject (on birthdate). — [Fisher Phillips summary](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/news-insights/discrimination-lawsuit-over-workdays-ai-hiring-tools-can-proceed-as-class-action-6-things); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/federal-court-allows-collective-action-lawsuit-over-alleged)
- [3P] Practitioner forums report knockout questions as the main cause of instant rejections, while AI scoring "sends low-fit candidates to a separate pile". — [Reddit snapshot, May 2026](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tdk5q3/snapshots/2026-05-16T15%3A11%3A00.978857Z)

### Inferences
- A candidate rejected minutes after applying was almost certainly knocked out on a screening answer (work authorization, location, years, license), or on an Ashby text rule such as "Contains". A CV-content rejection is less likely. A rejection that arrives days later, often on a configured delay (Ashby business-day delay, Indeed's three days), is still consistent with a knockout.
- The honest framing for a prediction agent: the risk is that the CV is **never read**, because it is ranked low among hundreds, more than that it is "rejected by a robot". The HBS finding is about employer-configured *criteria* being too rigid, which agrees with the knockout-centric picture.

### Gaps
- No large, independent, recent (2024–2026) quantitative study measures the share of applications auto-rejected by content vs by knockout. Enhancv (n=25) is small and commercially motivated.
- I did not directly verify specific HBS sub-findings that are often quoted, such as the share of employers filtering on resume gaps longer than six months, or the exact survey sample sizes. Check the PDF.
- I did not find or verify Jobscan's Fortune 500 ATS-usage statistic or a SHRM survey on auto-rejection this session.

## Q3. How do match scores work where documented? How are years of experience computed?

### Takeaway
Documented scoring has moved from keyword and taxonomy matching to **per-requirement LLM judgments**. HiredScore (2026) checks each basic and preferred qualification against the parsed resume. Oracle generative AI scores skills, experience, domain and seniority. Ashby checks recruiter-defined criteria. Greenhouse still exposes **exact plus "similar" keyword matches** against a weighted skill calibration. Workday's older Skills Match is skill-to-skill similarity that explicitly ignores recency, duration and total years. Years of experience is typically **computed by the parser from position dates**, with overlaps merged. A "15 years of experience" line in the summary is not used.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] HiredScore 2026: each screenable qualification is checked individually against the parsed resume by an LLM. Basic qualifications set the A/B vs C/D tier, and preferred qualifications refine the grade within a tier. — [HiredScore release notes Aug 2026](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/hiredscore-release-notes/2026/august/wednesday--5th-august.html?toc=0.1.4.0)
- [DOC] Greenhouse: a weighted calibration of 4–6 skills plus optional years of experience, industry and titles. Scoring uses exact and similar keyword matches, and the breakdown shows "calibrated skills matched" and "matched terms found on the resume". — [Greenhouse: Talent Matching](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/41396009937307-Talent-Matching)
- [DOC] Workday Candidate Skills Match: required skills carry more weight, and it does not consider recency, duration or total years. — [Workday: Candidate Skills Match concept](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/human-capital-management/recruiting/candidates/candidate-skills-match/bmj1604095304483.html)
- [DOC] Oracle generative AI scores skills, relevant experience, domain knowledge and role seniority, averages them, and adds a 50-word rationale. The 25B rating gives 0–5 scores on Education, Experience, Profile and Skill. — [Oracle REST: job match scores](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/human-resources/farws/op-recruitingcejobmatchscoregenerativeairesponses-post.html); [Oracle 25B](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/readiness/hcm/25b/recr-25b/25B-recruiting-wn-f37003.htm)
- [DOC] Oracle's legacy "Best Candidates" combines skills fields with ML-found keywords and shows 0–3 stars on four criteria. — [Oracle: Best Candidates (23D)](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/talent-management/23d/faush/overview-of-best-candidates.html)
- [DOC] Lever Talent Fit uses only the job description and the resume. — [Lever: Talent Fit AI Brief](https://help.lever.co/hc/en-us/articles/28081525270685)
- [DOC] Oracle and SAP knockout scoring is plain arithmetic: per-answer scores are summed, a negative score on a disqualifying answer disqualifies (Oracle), and a weighted total below "Required Score" auto-disqualifies (SAP). — [Oracle scoring guidelines](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/talent-management/faimh/guidelines-for-scoring-questions.html); [SAP Learning](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-successfactors-recruiting-recruiter-experience-academy/setting-up-pre-screening-question-functionality-1)
- [DOC] Years of experience (Textkernel, a parser behind many ATSs): total experience is computed from the work-history date ranges, "Overlapping date ranges are not double-counted", and the value "is NOT derived from text like 'I have 15 years of experience'". Accuracy depends on an accurate RevisionDate, which serves as "now" for "Present" end dates. — [Textkernel SDK: ExperienceSummary](https://textkernel.github.io/tx-dotnet/Textkernel.Tx.Models.Resume.Employment.ExperienceSummary.html); [Textkernel data model](https://developer.textkernel.com/Parser/master/data_model)
- [DOC] In Textkernel's Salesforce connector, an explicit Years of Experience field is used first. If none exists, the figure is calculated from ATS work history, then from the resume's extracted history. — [Textkernel: processing edu/work exp items](https://developer.textkernel.com/Salesforce/master/data-mapping/processing-edu-work-exp-items/)
- [DOC, via search summary] RChilli, another parser vendor, describes a months-based calculation that merges overlapping periods. I could not open the page directly (HTTP 403). — [RChilli help: total years of experience](https://help.rchilli.com/hc/en-us/articles/360014208333-How-do-you-calculate-the-total-number-of-years-in-experience)
- [DOC] SmartRecruiters consolidates sub-scores into a 0–100 score, now shown as 1–5 stars. Its inputs exclude demographic fields. — [SmartRecruiters helpdesk](https://ines-helpdesk.freshdesk.com/en/support/solutions/articles/44002539505-new-5-star-match-score)

### Inferences
- In LLM-era graders, wording no longer needs to match the job description exactly. What matters is whether each **stated requirement is evidenced** in the parsed CV, with dates, titles and certifications explicit. Keyword exact matches still help in Greenhouse-style calibrations, and synonyms count as "similar".
- For years-of-experience requirements, the agent should compute total experience the way parsers do: merge overlapping ranges, date "Present" to the parse date, and ignore self-declared totals. It should flag ambiguity from year-only dates, unparseable date formats, or relevant experience buried under a non-matching title. Note that per-skill years, as opposed to total years, depend on the parser attributing the skill to dated positions.
- A requirement satisfied only through a knockout answer, not the CV, may still be graded unmet by AI review, because the AI usually reads the resume, not the questionnaire.

### Gaps
- No vendor documents numeric weights, embedding models, title-normalization rules, recency weighting or location-radius logic for its match scores.
- How parsers handle month granularity, gaps, and year-only dates (for example "2019–2021" read as Jan–Jan or Dec–Jan) is not documented in Textkernel's public docs found here.
- I found no documentation of location-radius matching in any of the AI scores. Location is filtered through screening questions or search.

## Q4. Which hard requirements typically act as filters?

### Takeaway
Hard filters are whatever the employer encodes as must-have, disqualifier or deal-breaker questions. The documented examples cluster on **work authorization and sponsorship, location and willingness to relocate, minimum years of experience, degree or diploma, licenses and certifications**, and availability or travel. In AI grading, "basic" or "required" qualifications set the tier (HiredScore A/B vs C/D), while "preferred" qualifications only reorder within a tier.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Indeed suggests screener questions on years of experience, high school diploma, valid license, willingness to travel and interview availability. Failing a deal breaker sends the applicant to the Rejected tab. Failing a preferred question only labels them. — [Indeed Hiring Hub](https://www.indeed.com/hire/resources/howtohub/how-to-use-screener-questions-on-indeed); [Indeed employer help](https://in.indeed.com/help/employers/articles/115005660163)
- [DOC] LinkedIn's "Must-have qualification" screening questions can drive automatic rejection. — [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a519651)
- [SURVEY] In Enhancv's sample, the auto-reject triggers were licenses, work authorization, location and very low match scores. — [IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/study-reveals-ats-rarely-auto-rejects-cvs-debunks-75-myth)
- [3P] Practitioner guidance recommends limiting auto-reject to binary, non-negotiable items such as work authorization, and warns against criteria tied to protected characteristics. — [discovered.ai](https://discovered.ai/?p=55159)
- [DOC] HiredScore: meeting all scoped basic qualifications is what earns A/B, and preferred qualifications only move a candidate within a tier. — [HiredScore Aug 2026](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/hiredscore-release-notes/2026/august/wednesday--5th-august.html?toc=0.1.4.0)
- [DOC] SAP: only multiple-choice or structured screening questions can be disqualifiers. Free text cannot, since "there's no way for the system to identify a correct answer". — [SAP KBA 2204476](https://userapps.support.sap.com/sap/support/knowledge/en/2204476)
- [DOC] Ashby text-answer rules support "Contains" and fuzzy "Similar to", so a free-text answer can trigger an auto-reject in Ashby, unlike SAP. — [Ashby docs](https://docs.ashbyhq.com/auto-reject-applications)
- [SURVEY, 2021] HBS: employers say rigid "exact match" criteria screen out qualified candidates (88%/94%). — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/report-applicant-tracking-systems-may-exclude-whole-segments-of-candidates/606614/)

### Inferences
- For a "would this pass?" prediction, the agent should split the posting's "Requirements" section into: (1) items likely to appear as knockout questions, such as authorization, sponsorship, location or on-site, minimum years, degree, named licenses or certifications, clearance and language; (2) items an AI grader will treat as basic qualifications; (3) preferred items that affect rank only. Missing a (1) item is near-certain rejection if the employer has a knockout for it. Missing a (2) item drops the CV into the C/D or "Limited" tier.
- Security clearance and language requirements are common in postings but did not appear explicitly in the vendor docs collected here. They likely act through custom knockout questions. This is an inference, not a documented finding.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on how often employers turn each type of knockout into an auto-reject versus a flag.
- No vendor documentation found on degree "equivalent experience" handling in AI graders.

## Q5. How do recruiters actually use the ATS (search, sorting, time per CV)?

### Takeaway
Recruiters work through a **sorted queue**: by HiredScore grade, Greenhouse match category, Oracle score, or knockout score. Vendors explicitly tell them to sort top-down. They also filter by screener answers and search parsed profiles. The most cited time-per-CV figure is Ladders' 2018 eye-tracking claim of a **7.4-second** initial screen (about 6 seconds in 2012), focused on titles and section headers. Its methodology is thin, so treat it as indicative only.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] HiredScore tells recruiters to sort applicants by grade, A to D, when screening. — [Workday: Concept HiredScore grades](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/recruiter-productivity-/concept--hiredscore-grades.html)
- [DOC] Greenhouse groups candidates by match category and orders each group by application date. Bulk advance or reject is available to the recruiter. — [Greenhouse: Talent Matching](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/41396009937307-Talent-Matching)
- [DOC] Oracle prescreening scores "help rank job applications". Indeed screener answers can filter and sort the Candidates tab. — [Oracle scoring guidelines](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/talent-management/faimh/guidelines-for-scoring-questions.html); [Indeed Hiring Hub](https://www.indeed.com/hire/resources/howtohub/how-to-use-screener-questions-on-indeed)
- [DOC/3P] Ashby case study: a team reviewed 1,500 resumes in 6 hours with AI-assisted review, showing how AI review compresses per-CV human attention. — [Ashby: AI-assisted review in practice](https://www.ashbyhq.com/blog/recruiting/ai-assisted-application-review-in-practice)
- [SURVEY, weak methodology, 2018] Ladders reported an average initial screen of 7.4 seconds, up from about 6 seconds in 2012. Recruiters scanned job titles and subheads and were put off by missing section headers and poor flow. Critics note the report gives no sample size or recruiter profile. The "30 recruiters" figure circulating online is unconfirmed. — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/); [Ladders report PDF (CSU Global copy)](https://csuglobal.edu/sites/default/files/blog-files/theladders-eyetracking-studyc2.pdf); [ERE: Is the 6-second scan a myth?](https://www.ere.net/articles/is-the-6-second-resume-scan-a-myth)
- [SURVEY] Enhancv's recruiters described AI fit scores as a loose guide and named application volume and timing, not software, as the real bottleneck. — [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/11/10/study-debunks-viral-claim-that-ai-filters-out-most-job-applicants/)

### Inferences
- Order effects matter. Greenhouse orders by application date within a category, and Ashby rules and AI review run at submission, so applying early within a match tier plausibly improves the odds of being seen.
- The human skim works at the level of title and section headers. A CV whose recent titles do not visibly map to the target role risks failing both the AI title and seniority checks (Oracle "role seniority") and the 7-second skim.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, recent (2024–2026) study of time per CV or of how often recruiters look below the top AI tier.
- Recruiter boolean search behavior, such as typical query construction in LinkedIn Recruiter or ATS profile search, was not documented in vendor material retrieved this session.
- No data on what share of employers on each platform actually enable AI ranking.
