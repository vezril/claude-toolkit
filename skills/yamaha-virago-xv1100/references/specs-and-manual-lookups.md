## The bike

**Yamaha Virago XV1100** — the big-bore Virago cruiser, built ~**1986-1999** (successor to the 1984-85 XV1000; the line goes back to the 1981 XV750/920). A classic air-cooled V-twin, and mechanically an **analog, carbureted, pre-electronic** machine — which shapes everything about owning and troubleshooting it.

- **Engine:** **1,063 cc air-cooled 75° V-twin**, **SOHC, 2 valves per cylinder**. *(Confident platform facts.)*
- **Fuel:** **carbureted** — twin **CV carburetors** (Hitachi). **No fuel injection, no OBD, no fault codes.** Diagnosis is old-school: spark, fuel, compression (see troubleshooting).
- **Fuel valve:** **vacuum-operated petcock** (ON / RES / PRI) — a known age failure (see known-issues).
- **Ignition:** **TCI** (transistor-controlled, electronic) — **no points to adjust/replace.**
- **Final drive:** **shaft** (no chain or belt) — so no chain maintenance, **but there's a final-drive gear oil** to service, and a starter/charging system that's the real maintenance focus.
- **Transmission:** **5-speed**, **wet clutch**.
- **Valvetrain:** **adjustable valves** (screw/locknut-type tappet adjustment on the SOHC 2-valve head) → **there IS a periodic valve-clearance check/adjust** (unlike modern hydraulic-lifter engines). *(High-confidence that it's adjustable; the exact clearances and interval are must-verify.)*
- **Starter:** electric, via the **sprag starter clutch + idle gears** that are the model's famous weak point (known-issues reference).
- **Instruments:** minimal — speedo/tach and a few telltales (neutral, high-beam, turn, oil/charge depending on year). No diagnostic display.

## ⚠️ Must-verify numbers — do NOT guess; here's where each lives

Deliberately blank rather than wrong — and on a bike this old, also confirm nothing's been **modified** (rejetted carbs, aftermarket exhaust, non-stock final gearing).

| What | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| **Engine oil grade & capacity** | Service manual → *Specifications* (wet clutch → needs a **JASO MA / MA2** motorcycle oil, **not** friction-modified "energy-conserving" car oil, or the clutch slips). Grade/capacity vary — confirm for your year. |
| **Oil-change interval** | Manual maintenance schedule. On an old, possibly-sitting bike, err toward more frequent. |
| **Valve clearances (intake/exhaust) + interval** | Manual → *Specifications* / *Valve adjustment*. Exact clearances are engine-specific; don't guess. |
| **Final-drive (shaft) gear oil** — type + capacity | Manual → *Final drive*. Different from engine oil; easy to forget, don't. |
| **Carburetor spec** — jetting, float height, idle/mixture, sync | Manual → *Carburetor*. **Confirm it's stock** — many Viragos were rejetted for pipes/pods. Carb sync matters on a twin. |
| **Spark plugs** — type & gap | Manual → *Specifications*. |
| **Ignition timing** | Manual — TCI, but verify the check procedure/spec for your year. |
| **Tire sizes & pressures (cold)** | **Sidewall** for size, the bike's tire info / manual for **pressure**. On a classic, also check the tires' **age (DOT date)** — old rubber is a safety item even with tread. Set cold. |
| **Brake specs / fluid** | Manual. DOT fluid type + pad/shoe limits. Safety-critical. |
| **Fastener torques** (axle, caliper, drain plugs, engine covers) | Manual torque tables — including the starter-clutch/cover bolts if you tackle that job. |
| **Battery spec** | Manual → *Electrical*, or the battery label. A good battery + tender is worth it. |
| **Fuse ratings** | Manual / fuse box. |

## Getting the authoritative source

- **Service manual:** a **Clymer** or **Haynes** manual covers the XV1100 (often the whole XV535-1100 Virago range); or a Yamaha factory service manual for your year. This is the authority for every spec above and for the starter-clutch job.
- **Owner's manual:** find the year-specific Yamaha owner's manual (PDFs circulate) for basic specs, the petcock/choke operation, and capacities.
- **Community:** the Virago owner communities (e.g. ViragoHelp / forums) are unusually good for this bike's quirks — useful, but community advice, not manufacturer spec.
