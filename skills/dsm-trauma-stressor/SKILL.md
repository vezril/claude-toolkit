# Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders

**New chapter in DSM-5.** Unique in the manual: grouped by **etiology** — exposure to a
traumatic or stressful event is a required criterion — rather than by symptom phenomenology.
That is why the presentations inside vary so widely, from anhedonic and dysphoric to angry and
externalising to dissociative.

## The disorders

- **PTSD** — restructured in DSM-5 from three symptom clusters into **four**: intrusion,
  avoidance, **negative alterations in cognitions and mood**, and alterations in arousal and
  reactivity. The third cluster was broadened to capture persistent negative beliefs, distorted
  blame and emotional numbing. Duration >1 month.
  - **Criterion A** tightened: exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury or sexual
    violence, by direct experience, witnessing, learning of it happening to a close relative or
    friend (violent or accidental), **or repeated/extreme exposure to aversive details as part
    of work** (first responders, investigators). **DSM-5 removed the subjective "fear,
    helplessness or horror" response requirement**, and media exposure in the course of
    ordinary life does not qualify.
  - **Specify:** with dissociative symptoms (depersonalisation or derealisation) · with delayed
    expression (full criteria not met until ≥6 months after).
  - **A separate criteria set for children 6 years and younger**, developmentally calibrated —
    a genuine DSM-5 innovation.
- **Acute stress disorder** — 3 days to 1 month after exposure. Past a month, it is PTSD.
- **Adjustment disorders** — emotional or behavioural symptoms within 3 months of an
  identifiable stressor, out of proportion or impairing, resolving within 6 months of the
  stressor ending. **Specify** with depressed mood / anxiety / mixed / disturbance of conduct /
  mixed disturbance of emotions and conduct / unspecified. The manual's residual category for
  stress response, and the most-used diagnosis in the chapter.
- **Reactive attachment disorder** and **disinhibited social engagement disorder** — both follow
  social neglect or insufficient care. RAD is internalising (inhibited, emotionally withdrawn,
  failure to seek comfort); DSED is externalising (indiscriminate approach to unfamiliar
  adults). DSM-5 split what DSM-IV treated as two subtypes of one disorder, because course and
  treatment response differ — DSED persists even after adequate caregiving is established.
- **Prolonged grief disorder** — **new in DSM-5-TR**. Persistent grief ≥12 months after the
  death (≥6 months in children and adolescents), with intense yearning or preoccupation plus
  additional symptoms, exceeding cultural and contextual norms. Contested on the same grounds
  as the bereavement-exclusion removal in [[dsm-depressive]] — note it as contested.

## Differentials

- **Adjustment disorder vs PTSD** — the stressor need not be traumatic for adjustment disorder,
  and the symptom pattern doesn't have to be PTSD's.
- **Adjustment disorder vs normal distress** — proportionality and impairment, judged in
  cultural context. This is where over-diagnosis lives.
- **PTSD flashbacks vs psychotic hallucinations** — flashbacks are re-experiencing with
  trauma-linked content and often preserved reality testing
  ([[dsm-schizophrenia-spectrum]]).
- **PTSD dissociation vs a dissociative disorder** — the dissociative subtype keeps it here;
  identity disruption points to [[dsm-dissociative]].
- **Prolonged grief vs major depressive episode vs uncomplicated bereavement** — yearning and
  preoccupation with the deceased is the grief signature; pervasive worthlessness is
  depression's ([[dsm-depressive]], and uncomplicated bereavement in
  [[dsm-other-conditions]]).
- **TBI co-occurring with PTSD** — common after blast and assault; both can hold
  ([[dsm-neurocognitive]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
