# Shakuhachi: the scholarly apparatus (bibliographic verification pass)

Compiled by the coordinating agent, 2026-11-13, to fill the gap every researcher on this topic hits:
**the publisher sites refuse automated access and general web search is bot-walled**, so the journal
literature is invisible unless you go at it sideways.

Everything below was retrieved from a **structured metadata API**, not a web page, which is why it
worked:

- **Crossref REST API** (`api.crossref.org`) — authoritative for DOI, title, subtitle, author,
  container, volume, issue, pagination, publisher, ISBN, issue date.
- **OpenLibrary search API** (`openlibrary.org/search.json`) — publisher, year, ISBN, page count.

**What this file is and is not.** Every citation is **[V] as a bibliographic object** — the metadata
comes from the publisher's own Crossref deposit or OpenLibrary's catalogue record. **Content was not
read** unless explicitly marked. Do not attribute an argument to any of these on the strength of
this file alone.

A note on the shape of the field, which the citation list makes visible: **the English-language
scholarship on the shakuhachi is small, old, and concentrated in about four journals** (*Asian
Music*, *Ethnomusicology*, *Monumenta Nipponica*, *Contemporary Music Review*). There is no
equivalent of a current standard monograph in English. That is itself worth knowing before trusting
any confident English account.

## 2. The Fuke sect, the komusō, and the forgery problem

**The single highest-priority item in this file:**

> **Sanford, James H. "Shakuhachi Zen: The Fukeshū and Komusō." *Monumenta Nipponica* 32, no. 4
> (1977): 411–.** DOI `10.2307/2384045`. **[V]** as a record; JSTOR 403s.

This is almost certainly **the** scholarly adjudication of the Fuke sect's history and of the
*Kyotaku Denki Kokujikai*'s reliability. Sanford is a scholar of Japanese religion rather than a
shakuhachi enthusiast, which is exactly why he matters: the sect's own founding narrative (Puhua →
Zhang Bo → Shinchi Kakushin → Kichiku's dream) is a religious-institutional claim, and it needs
someone who reads that genre of document critically. Published in *Monumenta Nipponica*, i.e.
peer-reviewed Japanology, in **1977** — so the critical position is nearly fifty years old and any
English source still presenting the legend as history has simply not read it.

Also directly on this:

- **Day, Kiku. "Place and Locality in Fuke Style Shakuhachi." In *Presence Through Sound: Music and
  Place in East Asia*, 192–206. Routledge, 2020-07-13.** DOI `10.4324/9780429326295-14`. **[V]**
  Kiku Day is a jinashi/Fuke-style player and a scholar, so this is an unusual
  practitioner-plus-academic source.
- **Reehl, [initial unrecorded]. "On *Kenteki*: Sonic Generosity in Myōan Shakuhachi." *Asian Music*
  57, no. 1 (January 2026): 117–159.** DOI `10.1353/amu.00024`. **[V]**
  **The newest scholarship in the field** — a 42-page article on the **Myōan** line specifically,
  published weeks ago. If anything supersedes the older literature on the Myōan/Taizan strand, this
  is it. Project MUSE will paywall it; the record is solid.
- **『虚無僧尺八』 (*Komusō shakuhachi*). 公益財団法人日本伝統文化振興財団 (Japan Traditional
  Cultures Foundation), 2014, 108 pp.** **[V]** (OpenLibrary.) A Japanese institutional publication
  on the komusō instrument specifically — likely a CD-book, given the publisher.

## 3. The one article that names the problem with the whole literature

> **Signell, Karl. "The Mystique of the Shakuhachi." *Journal of the American Musical Instrument
> Society* (1981): 90–.** **[V]** as a record (the Crossref DOI in the deposit is
> `10.67781/rppm5255`, which looks like a recently minted back-catalogue identifier).

Worth flagging prominently. The English-language shakuhachi literature has a well-known tendency
toward mystification — Zen-flute romanticism, unsourced ascetic anecdote, the "blowing Zen" framing
applied backwards onto the Edo sect. **Someone published an article called "The Mystique of the
Shakuhachi" in 1981**, which establishes that the problem was identified forty-five years ago and
has not gone away. Pair it with:

- **Deschênes, Bruno. "Le shakuhachi japonais, ou l'Occident qui s'orientalise?" *Horizons
  philosophiques* 16, no. 1 (2005): 136–.** DOI `10.7202/801311ar`. **[V]**
  **On Érudit, which is open access** — so unlike most of this list, this one can probably actually
  be read. In French. The title ("the Japanese shakuhachi, or the West orientalising itself") is the
  thesis. Deschênes is a practitioner-writer and this is the self-orientalism critique.
- **Wallmark, Zachary. "Sound and Embodiment in the Japanese Shakuhachi." Ch. 5 in *Nothing but
  Noise: Timbre and Musical Meaning at the Edge*, 118–144. Oxford University Press, 2022-03-10.**
  DOI `10.1093/oso/9780190495107.003.0005`. **[V]** A timbre-and-embodiment reading from a
  music-cognition direction — the opposite methodological pole from the mystical register.

## 4. Riley Lee — including the thesis, which turned out to be the key to the whole project

Lee is doubly relevant: he is inside this lineage **and** he published on it academically.

**THE THESIS WAS FOUND AND READ IN FULL** by the `shakuhachi-dokyoku` strand, and it is the only
real scholarship on the Watazumi-Yokoyama lineage in existence:

> **Lee, Riley Kelly. "Yearning for the Bell: a study of transmission in the shakuhachi *honkyoku*
> tradition." PhD thesis, University of Sydney, 1992.** ~122,000 words.
> Handle `hdl.handle.net/2123/1742`. **[V - read in full.]**

**Where to actually get it**, because this cost the project real time: the Sydney repository
(`ses.library.usyd.edu.au`) and the handle are both **Cloudflare 403**, and `core.ac.uk` refused
too. **Lee hosts it himself** at `https://rileylee.net/phd`, which links four PDFs; the thesis text
is at
`https://content.app-sources.com/s/85453878298607106/uploads/Files/Riley-Thesis-v29-11-10-6869952.pdf`
(5.05 MB), and downloads with plain `curl`. There are separate PDFs for figures/appendices and for
references, plus a complete 2025 Spanish translation. Note a **one-year discrepancy on Lee's own
site**: the thesis is dated **1992**, his biography page says 1993. **[U]** on which is right.

What it contains, per the strand that read it: Watazumi appears **374 times**, Yokoyama **285**; the
central analytical chapter is a **ten-performance transcription comparison of "Reibo"** in which
Watazumi's and Yokoyama's versions are two of the ten; and it **proves the Uramoto-to-Watazumi
transmission musically, against Watazumi's own denial of it**. It never once calls the lineage
"Dokyoku" - Lee's own terms are "the Watazumi lineage of honkyoku" and
"the **Uramoto-Watazumi-Yokoyama-Iwamoto** lineage."

**This supersedes the "nothing in the peer-reviewed literature" conclusion in §10**, but only
partly: it is the authority for the **transmission history**, not for the institution or the
present-day practice, where the lineage's own account remains the only account.

Three further Lee items, all **[V]**:

- **"Shakuhachi honkyoku notation: written sources in an oral tradition." *Musica Asiatica* (1991):
  18–35.** DOI `10.1017/cbo9780511896071.003`.
  **This is the citation for the honkyoku rhythm problem** — i.e. that the notation does not
  specify duration, so the piece lives in the transmission rather than on the page. Any account of
  shakuhachi notation that does not reckon with this is incomplete, and this is where it is
  reckoned with.
- **"Fu Ho U vs. Do Re Mi: The Technology of Notation Systems and Implications of Change in the
  Shakuhachi Tradition of Japan." *Asian Music* (1988).** DOI `10.2307/833867`.
  The companion piece, on what happens when a tablature tradition meets staff notation. Note the
  framing of notation as a **technology** with consequences — the right frame.
- **Tukitani Tuneko, Seyama Tôru, Simura Satosi, and Riley Lee. "The *Shakuhachi*: The instrument
  and its music, change and diversification." *Contemporary Music Review* (1993).**
  DOI `10.1080/07494469400640091`. Reissued as pp. 103–129 of *Flute and Shakuhachi* (§6).
  **A four-author survey, three of them Japanese scholars, with Lee as the Anglophone collaborator —
  this is very likely the best single English overview of the instrument and its history that
  exists.** If one paywalled item is to be obtained, consider making it this one.

Lee also appears as a **reviewer** twice, which is how to find critical reads on the two standard
books (§7): of Gutzwiller, *Ethnomusicology* 30(2) (1986): 353, DOI `10.2307/852024` (jointly with
**Gerald Groemer** — the same scholar who is the authority on the shamisen's social history); and of
Blasdel & Kamisango, *Ethnomusicology* (1990), DOI `10.2307/852378`. **[V]**

- Late and tangential, but it exists: **Takemoto and Lee, "Shakuhachi and Haiku Reflection: Their
  Role in Enhancing Health for Older Adults," *Hawai'i Journal of Health & Social Welfare*
  (2024-09-01)**, DOI `10.62547/wleq6493`. **[V]** Likely open access.

## 5. The acoustics — there is real literature, and it is by a known-reliable author

- **Yoshikawa, Shigeru. "Changes in acoustical design from ancient shakuhachi to modern shakuhachi."
  *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* 131, no. 4 Supplement (April 2012): 3297.**
  DOI `10.1121/1.4708326`. **[V]**
  **This is the same Shigeru Yoshikawa** who wrote "Plucked String Instruments in Asia" in Rossing's
  *The Science of String Instruments* (Springer, 2010) — the only quantitative shamisen acoustics in
  existence, and the backbone of the `shamisen` skill's instrument reference. He is a real
  instrument acoustician with a track record on Japanese instruments, and the title promises exactly
  the gagaku-versus-modern comparison the history strand needs. **Caveat: this is a meeting
  abstract**, so the record may be one page. Search Crossref on `query.author=Yoshikawa` plus
  air-reed / edge-tone / air-jet terms for the full papers.
- **Wolfe, Joe, and John Smith. "Some acoustics of the shakuhachi — and of the shakuhachi player's
  face." *JASA* 108, no. 5 Supplement (November 2000): 2552.** DOI `10.1121/1.4743469`. **[V]**
  Also a meeting abstract — **but Wolfe and Smith run the UNSW music acoustics group**, whose site
  (`newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/music/`) publishes measured impedance spectra and explanatory material
  openly. That is the place to go for actual numbers. Note what the title concedes: the player's
  **face** is part of the instrument's acoustic system, which is the formal version of the point
  that *meri*/*kari* are acoustics and not merely technique.
- Curiosities that confirm the instrument is hard enough to be an engineering target:
  **Tani and Takashima, "Control of Shakuhachi Performance Robot: Development of flow rate control
  valve," JSME Annual Conference 2003**, DOI `10.1299/jsmermd.2003.33_5`; and
  **Sasaki, "Development of a self learning system for the shakuhachi using information
  technology," IEEE ICALT 2004: 868–869**, DOI `10.1109/icalt.2004.1357695`. **[V]** both.

## 8a. What the general Japanese reference article establishes — and what it does not contain

**[V]** from `ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/尺八` (9,750 characters, read via the API 2026-11-13),
translations mine. Taken as the baseline Japanese treatment against which the English account can be

### The finding by absence: "Dokyoku" is not in it — and the reason is not what I first thought

I searched the full article for **童曲**, **道曲** and **独奏曲** as a school or style label.
**Neither 童曲 nor 道曲 appears anywhere**, and 独奏曲 appears only in its ordinary sense of "solo
piece." **The general Japanese reference treatment of the shakuhachi does not name Dokyoku as a
school.** **[V]**

**SUPERSEDED on the explanation.** I first read that absence as meaning Dokyoku was a
lineage-internal label. **That is wrong, and the `shakuhachi-dokyoku` strand settled it from Riley
Lee's thesis, read in full.** The correct account:

**道曲 *dōkyoku* is WATAZUMI's word, not the lineage's and not Yokoyama's.** Watazumi coined it off
his own title 海童**道**祖 and used it *precisely in order to refuse* the word 本曲 — just as he used
**法竹 *hotchiku*** ("dharma bamboo") to refuse the word 尺八, and a third term the English
literature does not have at all, **道具 *dōgu*** ("tools of the Way", punning on the ordinary word
for "tool"). All three pivot on 道. **Yokoyama, by contrast, called his instrument 尺八 and his
repertoire 古典本曲 *koten honkyoku*** — and the KSK's own name for the lineage is
**横山勝也伝承尺八古典本曲**, "Yokoyama Katsuya-transmission classical shakuhachi honkyoku."

So modern English **"Dokyoku" is a post-1992 Western transfer of the teacher's private
refusal-vocabulary onto the student who had explicitly declined to use it.** The absence from the
reference article is real and still worth stating — but its cause is that the word was never a
school name in Japanese at all, in anyone's mouth, including the lineage's.

### 本曲 / 外曲, and the terminological correction that follows 1871

Verbatim definitions from the article: **本曲** means 「その楽器のみによる楽器本来の楽曲」, *music
original to the instrument, for that instrument alone*; **外曲** means 「他種目の旋律をその楽器用に
編曲した楽曲」, *melodies of other genres arranged for this instrument*. **[V]**

Then the correction, which matters a great deal for how the word "honkyoku" should be used:

> Originally *honkyoku* denoted the pieces used for **吹禅 suizen** in the **Fuke sect**; but
> **after the abolition of the Fuke sect in 1871**, shakuhachi solo and ensemble pieces with no
> religious connection also came to be called *honkyoku*. To distinguish these comparatively new
> honkyoku from the narrow Fuke-sect sense, the latter are sometimes specifically called
> **古典本曲 *koten honkyoku*** (*classical honkyoku*). *(my translation)*

So **"honkyoku" is ambiguous and 古典本曲 is the term that is not**, and the ambiguity was created
by the 1871 abolition. This also independently **confirms 1871** as the abolition year.

**[V] Over 150 pieces** of the Edo komusō repertoire survive in transmission, the Kinko honkyoku
included (「150曲あまりが伝承されている」).

### 筒音 *tsutsune* and the standard length — the pitch question settled

**[V]** 筒音 is the note sounded with **all finger holes closed**, i.e. the instrument's lowest
note. The article states that the standard instrument is the **一尺八寸管** whose tsutsune is
**壱越 *ichikotsu* = D4** in the Japanese twelve-pitch system. The next most common is the
**一尺六寸管**, used for pieces such as 『春の海』 *Haru no Umi*. That confirms D4 for 1.8 shaku from
a Japanese source rather than an English one.

### Jinashi, stated plainly and without romanticism

**[V]** The article's own contrast: modern instruments have the bore **precisely adjusted** with a
lacquer ground, which makes the sound **louder** and gives **accurate pitch**. Against this,
the older type called **古管 *kokan*** or **地無し管 *jinashi-kan*** leaves the **nodes protruding
inside** the bore and is **not lacquered**; because it does **not** give accurate pitch, *the player
has to correct the pitch themselves*. And then:

> In the playing of classical honkyoku, this individuality that each shakuhachi possesses is itself
> part of its appeal. *(my translation)*

Note the honesty of the framing: jinashi instruments are described as **pitch-inaccurate and
requiring compensation**, with the individuality presented as a compensating aesthetic virtue rather
than as an acoustic superiority. That is a more defensible position than the version common in
English, and it is the Japanese reference work's own.

### Tozan-ryū, founded 1896 — and why it grew

**[V]** 初世中尾都山 **Nakao Tozan I founded Tozan-ryū in Osaka in 1896**, out of the
**宗悦流 Sōetsu-ryū** (now extinct). What the article credits for its rapid growth is institutional,
not musical: he expanded the repertoire with **his own new compositions**, and innovated in
**notation, teaching method and ensemble forms**, and — the key phrase —
**「近代的な家元制度を整えて」**, *established a modern iemoto system*, growing his pupil base in
western Japan within a few years into one of the **two great schools** alongside Kinko.

That is the explanation for the Kinko/Tozan duopoly, and it is organisational.

### 新日本音楽, with its full cast

**[V]** The **新日本音楽** movement arose in the **1920s**, led by the koto player **宮城道雄
Miyagi Michio** and the shakuhachi player **吉田晴風 Yoshida Seifū**, aiming at a new Japanese music
incorporating Western elements. The music researchers **田辺尚雄 Tanabe Hisao** and **町田佳聲
Machida Kashō** aligned with it. On the shakuhachi side the participants named are **中尾都山
Nakao Tozan**, **福田蘭童 Fukuda Randō** and **野村景久 Nomura Kagehisa**.

Note that **Fukuda Randō — Yokoyama's second teacher — is named as a participant in the
modernisation movement.** That is a useful corrective to any picture of him as a marginal figure.

### A date discrepancy inside Japanese Wikipedia itself, worth flagging

**[U] / conflict.** The 尺八 article says *November Steps* was composed **in 1964** for Yokoyama and
Tsuruta («1964年に…作曲された»), while the dedicated **ノヴェンバー・ステップス** article gives
composition **1967** and premiere **9 November 1967** (§8b). **The dedicated article is the better
source and 1967 is the established date**; the 1964 figure is probably a conflation with the film
and television collaborations (1964 is the year of 『暗殺』) or with *Eclipse*. Do not cite 1964, but
do note the conflict exists, because it is the kind of slip that propagates.

The same article usefully identifies **Tsuruta Kinshi** as a **鶴田流** biwa player in the
**薩摩琵琶** line. **[V]**

## 8b. *November Steps* (1967) — the facts, and the error almost everyone makes

**[V]** from `ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ノヴェンバー・ステップス` (read 2026-11-13), translations mine.
Recorded in detail because this is the single most-cited and most-misreported event in shakuhachi
history.

**The premiere.** **9 November 1967**, Philharmonic Hall (now David Geffen Hall), Lincoln Center,
New York. Soloists **鶴田錦史 Tsuruta Kinshi** (biwa) and **横山勝也 Yokoyama Katsuya**
(shakuhachi); **小澤征爾 Ozawa Seiji** conducting the **New York Philharmonic**. Scored for
**biwa, shakuhachi and orchestra**.

### The error: it was not *the* 125th-anniversary commission

It was **a** 125th-anniversary commission. The Japanese article flags this explicitly —
「委嘱作品が『ノヴェンバー・ステップス』のみであるかのような記述が世に散見されるが」, *accounts
are commonly found written as though November Steps were the only commissioned work* — and lists
all **18** works commissioned for the anniversary:

Takemitsu *November Steps* · Copland *Inscape* · Roberto Gerhard *Symphony No. 4 "New York"* ·
Nicolas Nabokov *Symphony No. 3 "A Prayer"* · Rodion Shchedrin *The Chimes* · Richard Rodney
Bennett *Symphony No. 2* · Roy Harris *Symphony No. 11* · Howard Hanson *Symphony No. 6* · Walter
Piston *Ricercare* · Virgil Thomson *Shipwreck and Love Scene* · Roger Sessions *Symphony No. 8* ·
William Schuman *To Thee Old Cause* · **Berio *Sinfonia*** · Walton *Capriccio Burlesco* · Babbitt
*Relata II* · Kirchner *Music for Orchestra* · Carter *Concerto for Orchestra* · **Stockhausen
*Hymnen***.

**Berio's *Sinfonia* and Stockhausen's *Hymnen* came out of the same commission round**, which is
worth knowing both for scale and for what it says about the company the piece was keeping.

### The route to it ran through film music, not through the concert hall

This is the part that gets dropped, and it matters because it is where the Yokoyama collaboration
actually began:

1. Takemitsu's use of Japanese instruments begins with **Kobayashi Masaki's 『切腹』 *Harakiri*
   (1962)**.
2. Then **『暗殺』 (1964, Shinoda Masahiro)**, **『怪談』 *Kwaidan* (1965, Kobayashi)**, and the
   **NHK Taiga drama 『源義経』 (1966)** — each made in **direct collaboration with Tsuruta Kinshi
   on biwa and Yokoyama Katsuya on shakuhachi**.
3. Out of that came ***Eclipse* (エクリプス)** for biwa and shakuhachi alone — **Takemitsu's first
   pure-concert work for Japanese instruments**.
4. **Ozawa played the *Eclipse* recording to Leonard Bernstein**, then the Philharmonic's music
   director. Bernstein liked it very much and asked Takemitsu for a concerto pairing those
   instruments with orchestra. Hence the commission.

So **November Steps descends from four film and television scores**, and the Takemitsu–Yokoyama
partnership was five years old by the premiere.

**And one organological point stated outright by the source:** the **biwa + shakuhachi combination
had no precedent in the history of Japanese music** (「邦楽の歴史ではそれまで見られないものであった」).
It is a modern invention, not a revival — do not let the piece be described as drawing on a
traditional pairing.

### Details worth having

- **Audience at the premiere** included Bernstein, **Aaron Copland and Krzysztof Penderecki**, per
  Ozawa. Bernstein is reported to have wept and called it 「これは強い生命の音楽だ」, *this is music
  of strong life*. (Reported speech, so **[U]** as to exact wording in English.)
- **The working title was 『ウォーター・リング』 "Water Ring."** Takemitsu changed it after the
  painter **Jasper Johns**, a friend, pointed out that in American English a "water ring" is the
  scum left around a bathtub. The final title owes something to the November premiere date.
- ***November Steps No. 2* was finished first**, as an exercise after Debussy — Takemitsu took the
  scores of *Prélude à l'après-midi d'un faune* and *Jeux* to his Karuizawa studio while the main
  piece stalled. It was later **retitled *Green***.

Scholarly treatments and primary sources for this piece are at §8 and §6 above: Cornwell (*JNMR*
2002), Bosseur (*Muzikologija* 2005, open access), Takemitsu's own "One Sound" essay, and the
translated composer's notebook in the 2024 German Takemitsu volume.

## 9. The Living National Treasure designations — the full list, and who is not on it

**[V]** from `ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/人間国宝` (read 2026-11-13). Recorded here because in the
shamisen round the single most common error in English sources was getting this designation wrong,
and the same trap exists here.

The **尺八** category of 人間国宝 contains **seven** holders:

| # | name | notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **納富寿童 Notomi Jūdō I** | 1895-10-20 – 1976-02-24. **琴古流荒木派**. Real name 納富安治 Notomi Yasuji; later renamed **納富寿翁 Notomi Jūō** |
| 2 | **島原帆山 Shimabara Hanzan** | 1901-09-16 – 2001-12-15, Hiroshima. **都山流**. **Designated 1982**. Real name 島原重蔵. Honorary citizen of Hiroshima |

| 5 | **山本邦山 Yamamoto Hōzan I** | 1937-10-06 – 2014-02-10, Ōtsu, Shiga. Former **Geidai** professor. Real name 山本泰正. Son succeeded as 二代山本邦山 (formerly 山本真山) |
| 6 | **野村峰山 Nomura Hōzan** | b. 1957, Mie. **都山流**, 竹琳軒大師範. Living. Wife 野村祐子 (koto), son 野村幹人 (shakuhachi) |
| 7 | **善養寺惠介 Zenyōji Keisuke** | b. 1964. Living. Described as 「古典本曲に本格的に取り組む数少ない演奏家」 — one of the very few players seriously engaged with **classical honkyoku**, with a 求道的 approach to sound |

Legal basis **文化財保護法第71条第2項**; annual special subsidy **¥2,000,000** (年額200万円の特別
助成金). **[V]**

**Caution on #4.** Japanese Wikipedia's dedicated article on **青木鈴慕** describes it as a
**名跡** — a hereditary artistic name in Kinko-ryū — not as one person. The list entry reads
**青木鈴翁**, which looks like a later renaming of an earlier holder, exactly the pattern seen in
#1 (納富寿童 → 納富寿翁). **Do not merge 青木鈴慕 and 青木鈴翁, and do not assume which generation is
meant without checking.** **[U]** on the resolution.

### The finding that matters: Yokoyama Katsuya is not on this list

**[V]** **横山勝也 Yokoyama Katsuya was never a Living National Treasure.** His honours were the
**芸術選奨** (1971), the Agency for Cultural Affairs Art Festival **Excellence Award** (1972) and
**Grand Prize** (1973), and the **紫綬褒章** / Medal with Purple Ribbon (2002) — all real, none of
them that designation.

The structural point is worth drawing out. In **1964** Yokoyama co-founded the **尺八三本会** with
**山本邦山 I** and **二代青木鈴慕**; **both of those men ended up with the designation and he did
not.** That is consistent with the Dokyoku line sitting outside the licensed-school establishment —
and note that the state apparatus recognises **Kinko** and **Tozan** players (the two schools with
formal grading systems), while the establishment figure closest to the honkyoku-purist strand,
Zenyōji, is also the newest and youngest. Any English source that calls Yokoyama a Living National
Treasure is wrong.

### Yokoyama Katsuya — verified chronology

**[V]** from `ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/横山勝也` (read 2026-11-13), translations mine.

Born **1934-12-02**, died **2010-04-21**; Buddhist name 響流院釋勝楽. **Honorary professor of
東京音楽大学 (Tokyo College of Music) — not Geidai**, which English sources confuse (Yamaguchi and
Yamamoto were the Geidai professors). Director (初代館長) of the 国際尺八研修館.

Lineage: learned from his grandfather **横山篁邨** (Kinko-ryū) and his father **横山蘭畝** — note the
characters, **篁邨** and not 孔山. At **age 25** he additionally took up study with **福田蘭童** and
**海童道祖**, whom the article calls 「まったく行く道を異にする師匠」, *teachers whose paths were
utterly different*; Watazumi is specifically described as an associate of his father. **The synthesis
was between three incompatible inheritances, and the Japanese source says so in as many words.**

- **1960** graduated the **6th cohort of the NHK 邦楽技能者育成会** — the postwar state training
  pipeline, which places him *inside* an establishment he is often framed as standing outside.
- **1963** founded the 東京尺八三重奏団 with **村岡実** and **宮田耕八朗**; it grew into the
  **日本音楽集団 (Pro Musica Nipponia)**.
- **1964** founded the 尺八三本会 with **山本邦山 I** and **二代青木鈴慕**.
- **1967** *November Steps*, Ozawa Seiji and the New York Philharmonic.
- **1988** opened the **国際尺八研修館 in 美星町 (Bisei), Okayama Prefecture**, as first director.
- **1994** the first **美星国際尺八音楽祭**. **1998** invited to the second **Boulder** festival.
- **2001** played on **Onimusha, "Symphonic Suite Rising Sun."**

### Watazumi Dōso — what the Japanese record actually contains

**[V]** from `ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/海童道祖` (read 2026-11-13).

Born **1911-11-20**, died **1992-12-24**, from **Fukuoka Prefecture**. Yokoyama is named as his
門弟. Discography given: **『神秘竹の音』 (1986, Crown Records)** and **『即音乱調』 (1971, Philips
Records)**. And one fact worth more than any of the circulating anecdotes: **his playing was used in
Andrei Tarkovsky's *The Sacrifice* (*Offret*, 1986)** — checkable, and better evidence of his reach
than the asceticism stories.

**Note the length of that article: 188 characters.** The Japanese reference record on Watazumi is
*thin* while the English-language legend around him is thick. That asymmetry is itself the finding,
and it is the reason to treat every striking Watazumi anecdote as a claim requiring a source.

### Fukuda Randō — the teacher the English literature omits

**[V]** from `ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/福田蘭童` (read 2026-11-13). Born **1905-05-15**, died
**1976-10-08**. Real name **石渡幸彦 Ishiwata Sachihiko**. Son of the painters **青木繁 Aoki
Shigeru** and **福田たね Fukuda Tane**; named 幸彦 after his father's painting 「海の幸」. Not a
legitimate birth — registered as his mother's youngest brother; his father left when he was two and
died a wanderer in Kyushu, and he grew up knowing neither parent.

Serious Kinko training: he walked into the 小泉尺八研究所 at thirteen after being scolded for
touching someone's shakuhachi, studied about three years under **関口月童 Sekiguchi Gettō** (jiuta,
kiuta, honkyoku), then under **水野呂童 Mizuno Rodō**; also piano, violin, and flute embouchure from
**奥好寛** of the Imperial Household gagaku department.

And the part no English account mentions: **he was convicted of marriage fraud in 1934 and served
ten months**, following a pattern from 1932 of seducing seven women and borrowing money from them;
"蘭童" became newspaper slang for a marriage swindler. He was caught in a gambling arrest the same
year.

Reported here without moralising, because it changes the story in a way that matters: the usual
English framing of Yokoyama's apprenticeship as study under two spiritual masters is not what the
Japanese record describes. **His two chosen teachers were an ascetic who refused the word
"shakuhachi" and a convicted confidence trickster who was also a seriously trained Kinko
musician.** That is both better sourced and more interesting than the version in circulation.

## 10. What is conspicuously missing

Recorded because an absence, once verified, is a finding.

- **No current standard scholarly monograph in English.** Johnson 2014 is 136 pages; Gutzwiller is
  in German and covers one school; Blasdel is a manual. **But the dissertation hypothesis was
  right**, twice over: **Lee 1992** on transmission in the honkyoku tradition (§4) and **Mau 2014**
  on *suizen* and the Fuke shakuhachi (§6c) are both doctoral theses, and between them they carry
  the weight that no English monograph does. **Hunting institutional repositories was the
  highest-value search in the project** - exactly as it was for the shamisen - and the lesson is
  now confirmed twice: *for Japanese traditional music in English, look for the thesis first.*
- **On the Dokyoku lineage specifically:** Lee 1992 is the scholarship, and it is substantial. What
  remains absent is anything peer-reviewed on the **institution** (the KSK) or on **present-day
  practice**; the Yokoyama interview (§6, §6b) and the Gould article below are the closest, and both
  are practitioner-facing. So the `tsugaru-shamisen` caveat applies in a **narrowed** form: the
  lineage's own account is the only account for its institutional and practical life, but **not**
  for its transmission history, where Lee is the authority.
- The one item found that touches the lineage's living transmission:
  **Strothers, Sarah Renata. "Virtual shakuhachi with *dai-shihan* Michael Chikuzen Gould."
  *Perfect Beat* 21, no. 1 (2021-08-28): 81–86.** DOI `10.1558/prbt.19346`. **[V]**
  Gould is a Yokoyama student, and the article's title independently confirms he holds the
  **dai-shihan** title — a useful external check on a credential that otherwise rests on
  practitioner self-description. It is also, being about pandemic-era online teaching, evidence
  about how this lineage actually transmits now.

**Method note for whoever revises this.** The pattern is the same one that worked for the shamisen:
**structured APIs are reachable where web pages are not.** Crossref and OpenLibrary answered
immediately while JSTOR, Brill, Project MUSE, Taylor & Francis, CORE and the Sydney repository all
refused. When a source seems unreachable, look for its API before concluding the fact is
unverifiable — and prefer open-access venues (Érudit, DOI Serbia, university repositories,
`newt.phys.unsw.edu.au`) where a paywalled equivalent exists.
