## 4. 1871 and the rupture

### 4.1 The abolition

`[V]` The Fuke sect (普化宗) was **dissolved by the government in 1871**, and the
komusō "turned to [occupations] such as shakuhachi teacher" — JA-SHAKUHACHI,
§「普化宗の廃止から新日本音楽まで」: 「普化宗は政府により1871年に解体され、虚無僧は尺八の師匠などに転じた。」
(my translation). The verb is 解体 *kaitai*, "dismantled/broken up", not merely
"banned". This is a third independent confirmation of 1871 on top of the two the
earlier file already had.

`[V]` What the abolition removed was a **legal monopoly**, not merely a religious
institution. JA-SHAKUHACHI, §「普化尺八」: in the Edo period the shakuhachi was
treated as a 法器 *hōki* — "a ritual implement rather than a musical instrument"
—「普化宗に属する虚無僧のみが演奏するものとされ、それを幕府の法度によって保障されていた」:
playing it was reserved to komusō of the Fuke sect, **and that reservation was
guaranteed by shogunal law** (法度 *hatto*). After the Meiji abolition,
「普化宗が廃止されたことにより虚無僧以外の者も演奏するようになった」— non-komusō began
to play. (my translations)

`[V]` **But the monopoly was already leaky before 1871.** The same article says in
the same breath:「建前上は一般の者は吹いてはならなかったが、実際には尺八をたしなむ者はいた」
— "in principle ordinary people were not to play it, but in fact there were people
who took it up as a pastime." The word 建前 *tatemae* ("the official line, as
opposed to reality") is doing deliberate work. English accounts that present the
Edo monopoly as an effective ban overstate it.

### 4.2 Consequence 1: the word *honkyoku* became ambiguous

`[V]` JA-SHAKUHACHI, §「本曲」, quoted in full because this is the load-bearing
passage:

> 「もともとの本曲は、普化宗で吹禅に使われた曲を指していたが、1871年の普化宗廃止後は宗教音楽とは無縁な尺八のみの独奏曲や重奏曲も本曲と呼ばれるようになった。これらの比較的新しい本曲と普化宗で吹奏された狭義の本曲を区別するため、後者を特に古典本曲と呼ぶことがある。」

My translation: "Originally *honkyoku* referred to the pieces used for 吹禅
*suizen* in the Fuke sect, but after the 1871 abolition of the Fuke sect, solo and
ensemble pieces for shakuhachi alone that had no connection to religious music
also came to be called *honkyoku*. To distinguish these comparatively new honkyoku
from honkyoku in the narrow sense — those blown in the Fuke sect — the latter are
sometimes specifically called **古典本曲 koten honkyoku**."

So the taxonomy is a **consequence of the 1871 rupture**, not a timeless feature
of the tradition. The correction this licenses: the English habit of glossing
*honkyoku* as "the original Zen pieces" is reading the pre-1871 sense into a word
whose modern reference is much wider. Note also 「ことがある」— *sometimes* called
koten honkyoku. Even the disambiguating term is not fully standardised.

`[V]` The opposing term is **外曲 gaikyoku**, and the pair is the primary axis of
the repertoire. JA-SHAKUHACHI, §「楽曲」: 「尺八の楽曲分類で大きなウエイトを占めるのは、本曲と外曲という対概念である。本曲は、「その楽器のみによる楽器本来の楽曲」を意味し、外曲は、「他種目の旋律をその楽器用に編曲した楽曲」を意味する。」
— *honkyoku* = "a piece native to the instrument, for that instrument alone";
*gaikyoku* = "a piece whose melody belongs to another genre, arranged for this
instrument." (my translation). Important: the distinction is **structural, not
chronological** — a Tozan-ryū piece composed in 1903 is honkyoku, and a classical
*jiuta* arranged for shakuhachi is gaikyoku however old it is.

### 4.3 Consequence 2: the ensemble was unlocked

`[V]` The ban had also blocked *ensemble playing*, and 1871 released it.
JA-SANKYOKU / JA-SHAKUHACHI §「三曲合奏」: 「江戸時代にも尺八と箏や三味線の合奏は行われていたと考えられるが、尺八が普化宗の手から離れ合奏が解禁となったのは普化宗廃止後のことである。」
— "shakuhachi-with-koto-or-shamisen ensemble is thought to have been practised in
the Edo period too, but it was only after the abolition of the Fuke sect that the
shakuhachi left the sect's hands and ensemble playing was **deregulated**
(解禁 *kaikin*)." (my translation). Note the hedge 「考えられる」 on the Edo-period
practice — the pre-1871 ensemble playing is inference, not documentation. See §6.

### 4.4 How much survived

`[V]` **"Over 150 pieces" of the Edo komusō repertoire survive in transmission**,
and the Kinko-ryū honkyoku are counted *inside* that 150, not alongside it.
JA-SHAKUHACHI, §「普化宗の本曲」: 「江戸時代に虚無僧が吹いた本曲は、琴古流本曲をふくめ、150曲あまりが伝承されている。」
(my translation; 「ふくめ」 = "including"). English sources that say "150 honkyoku
plus the 36 Kinko pieces" are double-counting.

`[V]` These pieces are **anonymous and undated by nature**:
「これらは宗教音楽として成立し、作者、作曲年代ともに基本的に不詳である。」— "these came
into being as religious music; both composer and date of composition are as a rule
unknown." (my translation). Any English source giving a composer or a date for a
koten honkyoku is asserting something the Japanese reference literature declines
to assert.

### 4.5 The surviving transmission lines

`[V]` JA-SHAKUHACHI names the lines through which the pre-Meiji repertoire came
down, with their localities. This list is the actual geography of the tradition and
is usually flattened in English:

| Line (伝承) | Locality as given |
|---|---|
| 根笹派錦風流 Nezasa-ha Kinpū-ryū | 弘前 Hirosaki (Aomori) |
| 西園流 Seien-ryū | 名古屋 Nagoya — described as 「浜松の普大寺の流れをくむ」, descending from Fudaiji in Hamamatsu |
| 明暗真法流 Myōan Shimpō-ryū | 京都 明暗寺 Myōan-ji, Kyoto |
| 明暗対山流 Myōan Taizan-ryū | 京都 明暗寺 Myōan-ji, Kyoto |
| 博多一朝軒 Hakata Itchōken | 博多 Hakata (Fukuoka) |
| 越後明暗寺 Echigo Myōan-ji | Echigo (Niigata) |
| 布袋軒 Hoteiken | 東北地方 Tōhoku |
| 松巖軒 Shōganken | 東北地方 Tōhoku |

(JA-SHAKUHACHI §「普化宗の本曲」, my transliterations.)

`[V]` **Same title, different piece, is the norm.** 「全国の寺院で伝承される本曲には同名異曲が多くある。『鈴慕』『三谷』『鶴の巣籠』などは本曲の代表的な曲名であるが、曲によっては10種類以上の旋律の異なるものが伝承されている。」
— the honkyoku transmitted at temples across the country include **many cases of
同名異曲, "same name, different piece"**; *Reibo*, *Sanya* and *Tsuru no Sugomori*
are representative titles, and for some of them **more than ten melodically
distinct versions** are transmitted. (my translation). The mechanism given is the
mendicant circuit: 「托鉢のため諸国を往来した虚無僧により伝播された」— spread by komusō
travelling the provinces to beg.

This is the single most useful corrective in §4: "*Tsuru no Sugomori*" is not a
piece, it is a title shared by a family of pieces. A recording labelled with a
honkyoku title tells you almost nothing until you also know the line.

### 4.6 Re-legitimisation as a secular instrument

`[V]` Two mechanisms are visible in JA-SHAKUHACHI, and they are different in kind:

1. **Instrument modification + urban teaching networks.** 二代荒木古童 (竹翁,
   1823–1908) 「普化宗廃止後も尺八の普及に尽力し、琴古流中興の祖となった。尺八の指孔位置や歌口を改良したことでも知られ、東京を中心に全国に普及させた。」
   — after the abolition he worked at popularising the shakuhachi and **became the
   restorer (中興の祖) of Kinko-ryū**; he is also known for **modifying the finger-hole
   positions and the blowing edge (歌口)**, and spread the instrument nationwide from
   a Tokyo base. (my translation)
2. **Entry into the academy.** 初代川瀬順輔 Kawase Junsuke I (1870–1959), called
   「近代尺八の祖のひとり」 ("one of the fathers of the modern shakuhachi"), studied
   under Araki Kodō II **and under 上原六四郎 Uehara Rokushirō, professor at the Tokyo
   Academy of Music** (東京音楽学校), and **opened a dōjō in Tokyo in 1902**.
   (JA-SHAKUHACHI, my translation.)

That second item matters: the instrument's re-legitimisation ran partly through a
*music-theoretical* institution. Uehara Rokushirō is the author of 『俗楽旋律考』
(*Zokugaku senritsukō*, a study of Japanese scale theory) — ja.wikipedia lists
俗楽旋律考 as a page linking to 荒木古童 `[V, search index only]`.

`[V]` **Physical modification followed from secularisation.** JA-SHAKUHACHI
§「物理的構造」: under Meiji-era Western influence, **6-hole, 7-hole and 9-hole**
shakuhachi were developed (「明治時代以降の西洋音楽の影響により、六孔、七孔、九孔の尺八が開発された」),
of which the 7-hole has the most users though it is not mainstream. The 地 (*ji*,
lacquer paste bore) technique — 「管の内側に残った節を削り取り、漆の地（じ）を塗り重ねることで管の内径を精密に調整する。これにより音が大きくなり、正確な音程が得られる」
— trades the individuality of the old 地無し (*jinashi*) bore for volume and
accurate pitch. The article explicitly ties the old bore to the old repertoire:
「古典的な本曲の吹奏では、このひとつひとつの尺八のもつ個性もその魅力となっている」 — in playing
koten honkyoku the individuality of each single instrument is part of the appeal.
(my translations). So *jinashi* vs *ji-ari* is not nostalgia; it is a repertoire-driven
instrument choice. See §5.3.

`[U]` The **legal instrument** of the 1871 dissolution (which ministry, which
ordinance number, and its relation to the 1872 abolition of the *shugendō* orders
and the general Meiji religious reorganisation) is not given in any source I
opened. English sources that cite a specific decree should be checked against a
primary Meiji gazette; I could not reach one.

### 5.6 竹保流 Chikuhō-ryū, and the Riley Lee *dai shihan* claim

`[V]` **Founded 1917 by 酒井竹保 Sakai Chikuhō (I).** JA-SHAKUHACHI,
§「竹保流」:「竹保流は、酒井竹保が1917年に創始した流派である。宗悦流の流れを汲み、譜にロツレチではなく、フホウエヤイを用いるフホウ譜を用いている。」
My translation: "Chikuhō-ryū is a school founded by Sakai Chikuhō in 1917. It
draws on the **Sōetsu-ryū** line, and for notation uses **フホウ譜 *fuhō-fu***,
which uses *fu-ho-u-e-ya-i* rather than *ro-tsu-re-chi*." (fetched 2026-11-13)

Three things worth flagging from that one sentence:

1. **Chikuhō-ryū and Tozan-ryū are parallels.** Both descend from 宗悦流
   Sōetsu-ryū — the Kansai gaikyoku line of 近藤宗悦 Kondō Sōetsu (1821–1867),
   now extinct. `[V]` JA-SHAKUHACHI §「普化宗の廃止から新日本音楽まで」 names
   Kondō Sōetsu with those dates and calls him 「箏、三味線との合奏である三曲合奏（外曲とも称す）の先駆」,
   "the pioneer of sankyoku ensemble with koto and shamisen (also called
   *gaikyoku*)", and says Nakao Tozan I founded Tozan-ryū in Osaka in 1896 **out of**
   (「の中から」) Sōetsu-ryū. So the two Kansai schools founded 21 years apart share
   a parent. English accounts routinely present Chikuhō as a Tozan offshoot; the
   Japanese article makes them cousins.
2. **The notation syllables differ from both Kinko and Tozan.** Kinko and Tozan
   both use ロツレチ (*ro-tsu-re-chi*) — `[V]` JA-SHAKUHACHI §「音域・音階などについて」
   gives Kinko ロ・ツ・レ・チ・リ(甲: ヒ) and Tozan ロ・ツ・レ・チ・ハ. Chikuhō's
   フホウエヤイ is the *hitoyogiri* / older syllable set. This is a real, checkable
   musical difference, not branding.
3. `[U]` **1917 is also the founding year given for 上田流 Ueda-ryū** in the same
   article. I have not found a source that comments on the coincidence; it may be
   nothing, but two Kansai schools splitting off in the same year is the kind of
   thing a secondary source usually explains, and none I reached does.

**The Riley Lee claim — NOT VERIFIED.** `[U]` I could not independently confirm
that Riley Lee received *dai shihan* (大師範) in Chikuhō-ryū in **1980** from
**二代目酒井竹保 Sakai Chikuhō II (1933–1992)**. Specifically:

- ja.wikipedia has **no article** on 酒井竹保 and **no article** on 竹保流 (checked
  by `list=search`, 2026-11-13); the only Japanese reference text I have on the
  school is the two-sentence paragraph in JA-SHAKUHACHI quoted above, which does
  not name a second-generation head or give his dates.
- So the dates **1933–1992** for Sakai Chikuhō II, the **1980** date, and the
  *dai shihan* conferral are all currently resting on the English-language
  account alone. The claim is plausible and internally consistent — 大師範 is a
  real rank name attested elsewhere (`[V]` 島原帆山's ja.wikipedia biography records
  「1939年大師範昇格」, promotion to *dai shihan* in 1939, in **Tozan-ryū**) — but
  "a rank by that name exists" is not the same as "this conferral happened."
- What would settle it: a Chikuhō-ryū organisational source, Sakai Chikuhō II's
  obituary in a Japanese music periodical, or Riley Lee's own PhD thesis
  (University of Sydney, 1993) which is the likely origin of the English claim.
  I did not reach any of these.

One substantive caution on the English framing: `[V]` *dai shihan* is a rank
**internal to a school's grading system**, and the schools' grading systems are
not commensurable — Kinko-ryū has no single one at all (`[V]` kotobank, 日本大百科全書
entry on 琴古流, fetched 2026-11-13: there has been **no 宗家 since the 4th head**
and the school is split among many 分派 each with its own 家元; ブリタニカ国際大百科事典
小項目事典 on the same page: 「流の統一組織はなく」, "there is no unified organisation
for the school"). A *dai shihan* title therefore certifies standing inside one
organisation; it is not a tradition-wide credential, and English-language
biographies that present it as "the highest rank in shakuhachi" overstate it.

## 6. The ensemble tradition: 三曲 sankyoku and 外曲 gaikyoku

Primary artifact for this section: `JA-SANKYOKU` = ja.wikipedia 「三曲」, full
plaintext via the extracts API, fetched 2026-11-13. Supplemented by
`JA-SHAKUHACHI` §「三曲合奏」. All translations mine.

### 6.1 What 三曲 originally meant — and the shakuhachi was not in it

`[V]` The word **三曲 *sankyoku*** named a trio of instruments that **did not
include the shakuhachi**. JA-SANKYOKU, opening line:「三曲（さんきょく）は、地歌三味線（三弦）、箏、胡弓の三種の楽器の総称。またはそれらの音楽である地歌、箏曲、胡弓楽の総称。後に尺八が加わった。」
— "Sankyoku is the collective name for the three instruments **jiuta shamisen
(sangen), koto, and kokyū**, or for their musics — *jiuta*, *sōkyoku* and
*kokyūgaku*. **The shakuhachi joined later.**"

This is the single most important fact in §6 and it is routinely inverted in
English, where "sankyoku = koto + shamisen + shakuhachi" is given as a definition.
The shakuhachi is the **replacement member**, and the name predates it.

`[V]` **Why the shakuhachi was outside it: a guild boundary, not a musical one.**
JA-SANKYOKU §「三曲の由来」: jiuta shamisen, koto and kokyū were 「江戸時代初期から当道座に属する盲人音楽家の扱う楽器」
— "from the early Edo period, instruments handled by **blind musicians belonging
to the 当道座 *tōdōza***" (the guild of blind professional musicians). The article
then says flatly, in a parenthesis: 「(尺八は当道座の楽器ではない)」— "(the shakuhachi
is not a tōdōza instrument.)" And again in the next section:「尺八は当道座外の楽器であるが…」
— "the shakuhachi is an instrument outside the tōdōza, but…"

So the Edo-period exclusion of the shakuhachi from sankyoku has **two independent
causes**, which English accounts tend to merge into one:

| Barrier | Nature | Removed by |
|---|---|---|
| The Fuke-sect monopoly on the instrument, backed by shogunal 法度 | religious/legal, on the *instrument* | 1871 abolition `[V]`, see §4.1 |
| The 当道座 guild's ownership of the sankyoku instruments | occupational/guild, on the *ensemble* | Meiji dissolution of the tōdōza `[U]` — JA-SANKYOKU does not date this |

`[V]` The etymology itself is hedged and the article admits defeat on dating:
「いつ頃から使われたかはっきりしないが」("it is not clear from when it was used"), and
the proposed origin — that 三曲 was coined to distinguish these three from 平曲
*heikyoku* (the Heike-biwa repertoire), also a tōdōza speciality — is offered as
the author's guess, not a finding: 「…「三曲」という言葉が使われ始めたのではないかと思われる」
("it is thought that perhaps the word 三曲 began to be used [for that reason]").
Note the doubled hedge 「ではないかと思われる」. Any English source stating the
etymology of 三曲 as fact is more confident than the Japanese reference text.

### 6.2 A genuine contradiction between my two sources

`[V]` JA-SANKYOKU records a **mid-Edo usage in which 三曲 did include the
shakuhachi**:「ただし江戸時代中期には箏、胡弓、尺八の合奏を「三曲」と呼んだ記録もあって、単に三種の楽器の組み合わせを漠然と「三曲」と呼んだ可能性もある」
— "However, there are also records from the mid-Edo period calling the ensemble of
**koto, kokyū and shakuhachi** '*sankyoku*', so it is also possible that 三曲 was
used loosely for simply any combination of three instruments."

Read that carefully: the attested mid-Edo trio is koto + kokyū + **shakuhachi**,
i.e. the shakuhachi *displacing the shamisen*, not the kokyū. This cuts against the
tidy "shakuhachi replaced kokyū" story, and it is evidence that shakuhachi
participation in art-music ensemble existed in the Edo period despite the monopoly
— consistent with the 建前 hedge in §4.1. `[V]` JA-SHAKUHACHI concedes the same
point with a hedge:「江戸時代にも尺八と箏や三味線の合奏は行われていたと考えられる」
("it is thought that shakuhachi ensemble with koto and shamisen was practised in
the Edo period too") — 考えられる, inference.

`[U]` Neither article cites **which** mid-Edo records these are. That citation is
the thing I most wanted and could not get.

### 6.3 Dating the displacement of the 胡弓 kokyū

This was the specific research question. Here is everything the sources will
actually support, and it is less precise than English accounts imply.

`[V]` **The period is "Meiji onward", and the two articles agree on it but use
different words for how complete it was.**

| Source | Japanese | My translation | Force |
|---|---|---|---|
| JA-SHAKUHACHI §三曲合奏 | 「明治維新以降、胡弓の代わりに尺八が加わることが多くなり、現在では尺八入り三曲合奏の方が一般的に普通に行われる。」 | "From the Meiji Restoration onward it became **common** for the shakuhachi to join in place of the kokyū, and at present sankyoku-with-shakuhachi is the more generally usual practice." | 多くなり — "became more frequent" |
| JA-SANKYOKU §三曲の由来 | 「また尺八も明治以降、合奏に加わりレパートリーとして地歌曲、箏曲を演奏する比率が大きくなり」 | "The shakuhachi too, from Meiji onward, joined the ensemble, and the proportion [of its activity] spent performing jiuta and sōkyoku pieces as repertoire grew large." | 比率が大きくなり — a proportion shifting |
| JA-SANKYOKU §三曲の由来 | 「江戸時代中期頃からしばしば三味線、箏との合奏に加わるようになり、明治以降本格的に参入した。」 | "From around the mid-Edo period it **often** came to join ensembles with shamisen and koto, and **from Meiji onward it entered in earnest** (本格的に参入)." | 本格的に — "in earnest", i.e. a change of degree |

So: **gradual from mid-Edo, decisive from the Meiji Restoration (1868) / the 1871
abolition, with no single datable event.** 本格的に参入 ("entered in earnest") is as
sharp as the Japanese gets. I found **no** source offering a decade, a first
concert, or a named figure for the switch. `[U]`

`[V]` **The kokyū was NOT eliminated, and ja.wikipedia is unusually angry about
the claim.** JA-SHAKUHACHI §三曲合奏:「一部の著述では胡弓入り三曲合奏が無くなったような記述も見られるが、それは全く根拠のない発言であり、現在でも胡弓入り三曲合奏は少なからず行われている。」
My translation: "In some writings one finds statements to the effect that
sankyoku ensemble with kokyū has died out, but **that is an utterly groundless
assertion** (全く根拠のない発言), and kokyū-inclusive sankyoku ensemble is still
performed to no small extent today."

JA-SANKYOKU says the same more calmly:「現在は後者〔三味線、箏、尺八〕が広く行われている状況がある。しかし胡弓は三曲を構成する楽器の一つであり、現在でも胡弓楽は伝承され、胡弓入り三曲合奏も行われている。」
— both formations exist; the shamisen/koto/shakuhachi one is the widespread one,
but the kokyū is **constitutive** of 三曲 and kokyūgaku is still transmitted.

The correct formulation is therefore: **the shakuhachi became the default third
instrument; it did not replace the kokyū.** Two living formations, one common and
one rare. English sources that write "the shakuhachi replaced the kokyū" are
asserting precisely what the Japanese article singles out as groundless. This is
probably the highest-value correction in this file.

`[V]` JA-SANKYOKU also preserves a nice material trace of the older arrangement:
「三曲合奏は、錦絵にもしばしば描かれており、その多くは胡弓入りである。」— sankyoku ensemble
is often depicted in *nishiki-e* woodblock prints, and **most of those depictions
include the kokyū**. The iconography is a dateable check on the practice; I have
not examined any prints myself. `[U]` on any specific print.

### 6.4 A structural asymmetry: the shakuhachi player is a specialist

`[V]` JA-SANKYOKU:「三味線、箏、胡弓の奏者は兼任が多いが、尺八はそれのみを専門とする演奏家が多い。」
— "Players of shamisen, koto and kokyū commonly double on more than one of them,
but **shakuhachi players are mostly specialists in it alone**." (my translation)

This is a real consequence of the guild history. The three tōdōza instruments were
one professional formation and one person's skill set; the shakuhachi came from
outside with its own separate school system. It explains why the shakuhachi — alone
among the four — has 流派 with their own notations, grades and licences, while
"koto school" means something looser.

### 6.5 外曲 gaikyoku as a category, and the working repertoire grid

`[V]` **外曲 is the ensemble repertoire seen from the shakuhachi's side.**
JA-SHAKUHACHI calls 三曲合奏 ensemble playing 「外曲とも称す」, "also called gaikyoku"
(§「普化宗の廃止から新日本音楽まで」), and defines gaikyoku generally as 「他種目の旋律をその楽器用に編曲した楽曲」,
"a piece whose melody belongs to another genre, arranged for this instrument"
(§「楽曲」). So *gaikyoku* is not a style; it is a **direction of borrowing**. A piece
is gaikyoku *for the shakuhachi* precisely because it is honkyoku for someone else
— a jiuta is the shamisen's own music.

`[V]` **And the arranging was originally mechanical.** JA-SHAKUHACHI §三曲合奏:
「古典的な三曲合奏では、尺八の手付けは三絃の手をベタ付けで尺八向けに編曲したものであった。」
— "in classical sankyoku ensemble, the shakuhachi's 手付け *tezuke* [realisation]
was an arrangement for shakuhachi of the sangen's part, **ベタ付け *beta-zuke***"
(my translation; *beta-zuke* = laid on flat, i.e. tracking the shamisen line note
for note rather than writing an independent part). The shakuhachi entered sankyoku
as a **heterophonic doubling of the shamisen**, not as an obbligato voice. That is
a concrete musical claim English introductions almost never make, and it explains
why early gaikyoku parts are unidiomatic.

`[V]` **The 新曲 term is a working professional vocabulary, and it has two
different senses depending on which community you are in.** The two articles give
two distinct three-term grids, and they are not the same grid:

| Grid | Community | Terms as given | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repertoire-origin grid | shakuhachi players | **本曲** (native to the instrument) / **外曲** (borrowed and arranged) / and within honkyoku, **古典本曲** vs the post-1871 kind | JA-SHAKUHACHI §楽曲 `[V]` |
| Period grid | 三曲系 players generally | **古曲** = classical jiuta-sōkyoku; **新曲** = Miyagi Michio and the Meiji-to-prewar repertoire; **現代曲** = everything after | JA-SANKYOKU / JA-SHAKUHACHI §三曲合奏 `[V]` |

`[V]` The period grid is quoted directly: 「三曲系の演奏者のあいだでは、古典的な地歌箏曲を古曲、宮城道雄などの明治期から戦前までの楽曲を新曲、それ以降の楽曲を現代曲と呼ぶこともある。」
— "among sankyoku-tradition performers, classical jiuta-sōkyoku is **sometimes**
called *kokyoku*, pieces from the Meiji period to before the war such as Miyagi
Michio's are called *shinkyoku*, and pieces after that *gendaikyoku*." (my
translation; note 「こともある」, *sometimes* — this is professional jargon, not a
fixed taxonomy.)

### 6.6 Why the distinction organises a working life

Putting the two grids together, the categories are not academic labels but a
description of **four separate skill sets with separate teachers, separate
notations, separate instruments and separate employers**:

- **古典本曲** — solo, unmetered, learned by rote from one line's transmission,
  played on a 地無し *jinashi* bore whose pitch the player must correct by lip and
  jaw (`[V]` JA-SHAKUHACHI §物理的構造, see §4.6). No ensemble, no conductor, and
  the same title means a different piece in a different line (§4.5). Capital here is
  *lineage*.
- **外曲 / sankyoku** — chamber work, in a fixed trio, reading a part derived from
  the shamisen's (§6.5), requiring an accurately tuned 地有り bore to play in tune
  with a koto. Capital here is *reliability and repertoire breadth*.
- **都山流本曲 and 新曲** — composed, notated, often metered, sometimes in 3/4 or
  5/8 (`[V]` JA-TOZAN-NAKAO: 『湖上の月』 1922 is 「本曲で初めて三拍子を取り入れた」, the
  first honkyoku to adopt triple metre; 『紅葉』 1930 adopts 5/8 — my translations).
  Capital here is *a school certificate and the ability to read*.
- **現代曲 / 民謡** — new-music commissions and folk-song accompaniment. `[V]`
  JA-SHAKUHACHI §民謡尺八 notes the shakuhachi is heavily used for 追分 and 馬子唄,
  and that in 江差追分 「尺八の伴奏が必須となっている」— shakuhachi accompaniment is
  **obligatory** (my translation). A paying niche entirely outside the art-music
  categories.

The practical upshot, and the reason the taxonomy is worth learning: a player's
school tells you which of these four they are trained for and which notation they
read, and the repertoire word in a concert programme tells you what kind of
listening is being asked for. `[U]` — this synthesis is mine; no single source
states it in this form.

## 7. The 20th century

### 7.1 新日本音楽, the 1920s

The bibliographic pass supplied this verified; I confirm it from my own fetch and add what the
source adds. `[V]` JA-SHAKUHACHI §「普化宗の廃止から新日本音楽まで」, fetched
2026-11-13:

> 「1920年代には、箏曲家の宮城道雄と尺八家の吉田晴風によって、洋楽の要素を取り入れた新しい邦楽を目指す新日本音楽運動が興り、音楽研究家の田辺尚雄，町田佳聲らも同調し、尺八では中尾都山、福田蘭童、野村景久らが参加して邦楽の近代化に寄与した。」

My translation: "In the 1920s a **新日本音楽 ('New Japanese Music') movement**
arose, led by the koto player **宮城道雄 Miyagi Michio** and the shakuhachi player
**吉田晴風 Yoshida Seifū**, aiming at a new Japanese music incorporating elements
of Western music; the music researchers **田辺尚雄 Tanabe Hisao** and **町田佳聲
Machida Kashō** aligned with it, and on the shakuhachi side **中尾都山 Nakao
Tozan**, **福田蘭童 Fukuda Randō** and **野村景久 Nomura Kagehisa** took part,
contributing to the modernisation of Japanese music."

`[V]` **Tighter dating, and a third founder the standard account omits.**
JA-SEIFU (ja.wikipedia 「吉田晴風」, fetched 2026-11-13) gives:
「1920年（大正9年）、宮城道雄、本居長世と共に「新日本音楽」を提唱し、第1回演奏会を開催。」
— "In **1920**, together with Miyagi Michio **and 本居長世 Motoori Nagayo**, he
proposed '*Shin-Nihon-Ongaku*' and held the **first concert**." (my translation)

So: the movement has a **datable first concert in 1920**, and Motoori Nagayo
(a Western-trained composer, best known for children's songs) was a **third
proposer** alongside Miyagi and Yoshida. The ja.wikipedia 尺八 article names only
two; the Yoshida biography names three. **Report the disagreement, don't resolve
it** — but the biography is the more specific source and names a concert.

`[V]` **Yoshida Seifū, dates and route in.** JA-SEIFU: 吉田晴風, **1891-06-24 –
1950-06-30**, real name 吉田康次 Yoshida Yasuji, former artistic name 吉田竹堂
Yoshida Chikudō. Born in 長洲町, Tamana district, **Kumamoto**, into a sake-brewing
family that failed when his father died (he was 7). Heard a neighbour's shakuhachi
at primary school in **1898**, was given one by a relative in **1899**. Entered
Kumamoto Commercial School **1908**, bought a serious instrument with his sister's
help and studied **Kinko-ryū** under 鳥井虚霧洞 Torii Komudō. Graduated top of his
class **1911**, went to **colonial Korea** as a soybean trader. **1914**: met
Miyagi Michio in 京城府 (Keijō, i.e. Seoul) — a lifelong friendship; the same year a
bank scandal (韓湖農工銀行の疑獄事件) ended his business and he committed to the
shakuhachi. **1915**: to Tokyo, entered 日本音楽学校. **1917**: brought Miyagi to
Tokyo. **1919**: founded the 学生尺八大会 (student shakuhachi convention). **1923**:
to the USA as a goodwill music envoy after the Great Kantō Earthquake. **1932**:
organised 晴風会 and ran a national shakuhachi-popularisation campaign. **1937**:
artistic envoy to Siam (Thailand), received a Thai cultural decoration. (my
translations/transliterations)

Two findings here that English accounts miss entirely:

1. **The core partnership of the movement was formed in colonial Korea, not in
   Japan.** Miyagi and Yoshida met in Seoul in 1914. `[V]`
2. **The movement had an imperial/wartime phase.** `[V]` JA-SEIFU:
   「1940年（昭和15年）、皇紀2600年記念事業として尺八二千六百人パレードを主導。」— in **1940**
   Yoshida led a **parade of 2,600 shakuhachi players** as a commemorative project
   for the 2,600th year of the imperial era. And Nakao Tozan's 『青海波』 (1904) was
   `[V]` 「旅順港陥落時の愛国心から作曲、合奏部の結びに君が代が引用されている」— composed out
   of patriotic feeling at the fall of Port Arthur, with **Kimigayo quoted at the
   close of the ensemble section** (JA-TOZAN-NAKAO, my translation). The
   modernisation of the shakuhachi was not politically neutral. English
   introductions that present 新日本音楽 purely as aesthetic modernism omit this.

`[V]` **The 野村景久 affair, and why it matters.** JA-SHAKUHACHI continues:
Nomura was a rising young player-composer, noted for collaborations with
musicians including 古賀正男 (i.e. 古賀政男 Koga Masao, the popular-song composer),
radio appearances and writing — 「しかし1933年に一家四人を殺して金を奪う事件を起こして死刑となり、虚無僧の怪しさから来る悪いイメージを払拭し近代化を進めてきた尺八界を揺るがせ、かつて「法器」であった尺八の精神性を見直す気運を生んだ。」
My translation: "but in **1933** he committed a crime, killing a family of four and
robbing them of money, and was sentenced to death; this shook a shakuhachi world
that had been pushing modernisation and trying to shake off the bad image deriving
from the suspiciousness of the komusō, and **generated a mood of re-examining the
spirituality of the shakuhachi, which had once been a 法器** [ritual implement]."

This is a remarkable causal claim and I have not seen it in any English account:
a 1933 murder case is given as a trigger for the **re-spiritualisation** of the
instrument — the swing back toward koten honkyoku that later produces the
Myōan/jinashi revival and the Watazumi–Yokoyama line. `[U]` The claim is
ja.wikipedia's; it is unfootnoted there and I could not corroborate it. Treat as
an interesting hypothesis, not established historiography.

`[U]` There is **no ja.wikipedia article on 新日本音楽** at all (checked
`list=search` 2026-11-13 — the results are all 新日本プロレス etc.). For a movement
this important to the modern repertoire, that is a notable gap, and it means my
account rests on two article sections rather than a dedicated treatment.

### 7.2 *November Steps*, 1967

The bibliographic pass supplied this verified and I use it as given rather than re-deriving:
premiere **9 November 1967**, Philharmonic Hall, Lincoln Center; New York
Philharmonic under **小澤征爾 Ozawa Seiji**; soloists **鶴田錦史 Tsuruta Kinshi**
(biwa, 鶴田流 in the 薩摩琵琶 line) and **横山勝也 Yokoyama Katsuya** (shakuhachi);
**one of eighteen** 125th-anniversary commissions, alongside **Berio's *Sinfonia***
and **Stockhausen's *Hymnen***; the route ran through the film scores 『切腹』1962,
『暗殺』1964, 『怪談』1965 and NHK『源義経』1966, out of which came ***Eclipse***
for biwa and shakuhachi alone, whose recording Ozawa played to **Bernstein**;
the biwa+shakuhachi pairing **had no precedent in Japanese music history**; and
the famous "1967 recording" is the **Toronto Symphony, Massey Hall, 8 December
1967**, a month after the premiere.

What I add from my own fetches:

`[V]` **The dating error is confirmed and localised.** JA-SHAKUHACHI
§「現代音楽と尺八」 says: 「1964年にニューヨーク・フィルハーモニックと尺八の横山勝也、薩摩琵琶の流れをくむ鶴田流の琵琶奏者鶴田錦史のために作曲された武満徹のノヴェンバー・ステップスは反響を呼んだ。」
— it dates the composition to **1964**. But `[V]` the ja.wikipedia 「横山勝也」
article (fetched 2026-11-13) says in both its lead and its chronology:
「1967年（昭和42年）に小沢征爾指揮のニューヨーク・フィルと武満徹作曲の「ノヴェンバー・ステップス」を演奏し」
and 「1967年、小沢征爾指揮のニューヨーク・フィルと武満徹の「ノヴェンバー・ステップス」を演奏。」
So **ja.wikipedia contradicts itself across articles**, and the 1967 figure is
corroborated by the performer's own biography. 1964 in the 尺八 article is an
error. (Note also that the two articles spell the conductor's name differently —
小澤征爾 vs 小沢征爾 — the latter being the simplified form.)

`[V]` **Yokoyama Katsuya's own lineage, which explains the pairing's success.**
From the 横山勝也 article: **1934-12-02 – 2010-04-21**; honorary professor at Tokyo
College of Music (東京音楽大学) and director of the 国際尺八研修館 (International
Shakuhachi Training Centre). He learned from his grandfather **横山篁邨** and father
**横山蘭畝**, both **Kinko-ryū**; then at 25 studied under two teachers described as
「まったく行く道を異にする」, "following entirely different paths" — **福田蘭童 Fukuda
Randō** (the 新日本音楽 modernist of §7.1) and **海童道祖 Watazumi Dōso**. (my
translations)

That is a precise and useful fact: Yokoyama is the *junction* of the two opposed
20th-century tendencies — Kinko school training, the Fukuda Randō modernist line,
and the Watazumi ascetic line — in one player. It is why he could be both the
soloist in a Lincoln Center commission and the transmitter of a non-graded
koten-honkyoku lineage.

`[V]` Yokoyama's chronology, same article: **1960** graduated the 6th intake of the
NHK 邦楽技能者育成会 (NHK Japanese-music performers' training programme); **1963**
founded the 東京尺八三重奏団 (Tokyo Shakuhachi Trio) with 村岡実 Muraoka Minoru and
宮田耕八朗 Miyata Kōhachirō; **1964** founded the 尺八三本会 with 初代山本邦山 Yamamoto
Hōzan I and 2代青木鈴慕 Aoki Reibo II; **1971** 芸術選奨; **1972** Agency for
Cultural Affairs Arts Festival Excellence Prize; **1973** its Grand Prize;
**1988** opened the 国際尺八研修館 at 美星町, Okayama, as founding director;
**1994** held the **first 美星国際尺八音楽祭** (Bisei International Shakuhachi
Festival); **1998** invited to the **second, in Boulder**; **2001** played on the
*Onimusha* "Symphonic Suite Rising Sun"; **2002** 紫綬褒章 (Medal with Purple
Ribbon). Died 2010-04-21.

Note the last items: `[V]` the same man who premiered *November Steps* recorded a
**PlayStation game soundtrack** in 2001. And note that the **World Shakuhachi
Festival series is his institution** (§7.5).

### 7.4 青木鈴翁 vs 青木鈴慕 — SETTLED: the same person

**They are the same man.** `[V]` ja.wikipedia 「青木鈴慕」, fetched via the extracts
API 2026-11-13. The article's first line: 「青木 鈴慕（あおき れいぼ）は、尺八の琴古流の名跡。」
— "Aoki Reibo is a **名跡 *myōseki*** [an inherited artistic name] of the Kinko-ryū
of shakuhachi." (my translation). It then lists three holders:

| Generation | Dates | Real name | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 初代 I | 1890-07-15 – 1955-07-02 | 青木誠造 Aoki Seizō | Yokohama; entered 初代川瀬順輔's school at 18; 尺八指南 at 20; founded 鈴慕会; left the 川瀬社中 in **1921** to found 東京竹友社; director of 日本三曲協会, chairman of 尺八協会 |
| 2代 II | 1935-10-04 – 2018-08-21 | 青木静夫 Aoki Shizuo | **「1974年より人間国宝。」** then, on the next line, **「初代青木鈴翁。」** |
| 3代 III | b. 1965 | 青木彰時 Aoki Akitoki | son of II; 1992 Agency for Cultural Affairs arts internship; 2013 第5回創造する伝統賞; **succeeded to the name in 2018** |

The decisive line is the ja.wikipedia article's own 「初代青木鈴翁。」 — "**[he is]
Aoki Reiō I**" — placed directly under the entry for the second-generation Reibo.
My reading: **青木静夫 Aoki Shizuo (1935–2018)** held the name 青木鈴慕 as the
second generation, was designated a Living National Treasure under that name in
**1974**, then in **2018** passed 鈴慕 to his son 青木彰時 and **himself took the
new name 青木鈴翁 Aoki Reiō**, becoming the first of that name. He died later the
same year (2018-08-21).

`[V]` **The pattern is confirmed independently by the Nōtomi case.** ja.wikipedia
「納富寿童」, fetched 2026-11-13:「1973年、実の長男（1929年生まれ）に寿童の名を譲り、自らは寿翁を名乗った。」
— "In **1973** he ceded the name 寿童 to his own eldest son (b. 1929) and himself
took the name **寿翁**." (my translation) The article's own lead confirms it:
「本名は納富安治…後に芸名を納富寿翁（のうとみ じゅおう）に改名」.

So **翁 *ō*** ("old man / venerable") is a systematic retirement renaming: when a
holder passes the 名跡 to the next generation, he swaps the live name's second
character for 翁. 鈴慕 → 鈴**翁**; 寿童 → 寿**翁**. (The 荒木古童 line shows the
same device: `[V]` JA-SHAKUHACHI calls Araki Kodō II 「2代 荒木古童（**竹翁**、1823-1908）」.)
This is a general feature of the Kinko naming system, not a one-off.

**Practical consequence, and this is the trap:** 青木鈴翁 and 青木鈴慕 II are one
designation, not two. **Any list that counts both is counting six people, not
seven.** The seven-holder count in §7.3 is correct only if 鈴翁 = 鈴慕 II, which it
is. There is **no separate ja.wikipedia article** on 青木鈴翁 (the API returns it as
missing, 2026-11-13) even though `list=search` surfaces the string — consistent
with it being a redirect or a mention rather than a person.

### 7.5 The postwar concert and recording era

`[V]` **The institutional engine was NHK.** Three of the key postwar figures are
dated by their intake in the NHK 邦楽技能者育成会 (NHK Japanese-music performers'
training programme), which functions as a de-facto national conservatory for
hōgaku predating any university department:

| Player | NHK intake | Source (ja.wikipedia, fetched 2026-11-13) |
|---|---|---|
| 山口五郎 Yamaguchi Gorō | **2nd** intake, graduated **1956** | 「1956年、NHK邦楽技能者育成会第2期卒業。」 |
| 横山勝也 Yokoyama Katsuya | **6th** intake, graduated **1960** | 「1960年、第6期NHK邦楽技能者育成会卒業。」 |
| 野村峰山 Nomura Hōzan | **22nd** intake, graduated **1977** | 「1977年　NHK邦楽技能者育成会第22期卒業。」 |

`[V]` **The university department came late — 1977.** ja.wikipedia 「山口五郎」:
「1977年、東京芸術大学音楽科に尺八科が新設され、講師に任命される。」— "In **1977** a
**shakuhachi department was newly established** in the music faculty of Tokyo
University of the Arts, and he was appointed lecturer"; he became **professor in
1987**. (my translation) So the shakuhachi entered the national art university's
curriculum **106 years after** the instrument was legalised for secular use. Both
山本邦山 (professor from **1993**, `[V]` his biography) and 善養寺惠介 (part-time
lecturer from **1990**, `[V]`) came through that department.

`[V]` **現代邦楽: the 1963 pivot, with the exact mechanism.** JA-SHAKUHACHI
§「現代邦楽」, corroborated by the 村岡実 and 横山勝也 biographies:

- **1962/63**: 村岡実 Muraoka Minoru, 横山勝也 Yokoyama Katsuya and 宮田耕八朗
  Miyata Kōhachirō formed the **東京尺八三重奏団 Tokyo Shakuhachi Trio**. (The 尺八
  article dates the founding to 1963, the 村岡実 article to **1962** — `[V]` a
  one-year disagreement between ja.wikipedia articles, unresolved.)
- **1963**: at the trio's **second concert**, 三木稔 Miki Minoru's 「くるだんど」—
  *a cantata on Amami melodies* — was performed, and the effect on the hōgaku
  world was large. `[V]` 村岡実's biography adds the human detail:
  「東京音楽社事務所や村岡実の自宅に集まって夜を徹した話し合いの末、三木稔の発案によって日本音楽集団結成の運びとなった」
  — "after all-night discussions gathered at the Tokyo Ongakusha office and at
  Muraoka's own home, on Miki Minoru's proposal it came about that the 日本音楽集団
  was formed." (my translation)
- **1964**: the **日本音楽集団 (Pro Musica Nipponia)** was founded with **14**
  founding members, named in JA-SHAKUHACHI as 三木稔, 長澤勝俊 (composers), 田村拓男
  (conductor, percussion), 村岡実, 横山勝也, 宮田耕八朗 (shakuhachi), 坂井敏子,
  宮本幸子 (koto), 杉浦弘和 (shamisen) and others. `[V]`
- **1964**: separately, the **尺八三本会** was founded by 山本邦山 I, 横山勝也 and
  青木鈴慕 II, which commissioned 「鼎」, 「風動」, 「尺八三重奏曲」 and many other
  shakuhachi ensemble works. `[V]` Note that **all three members later became
  Living National Treasures** (2002, —, 1974). Yokoyama never was.
- **Instrument change followed the repertoire.** `[V]` 「またこの頃より楽器改良が進み、宮田耕八朗を中心に多孔式尺八（主として7孔）が作られ普及していった。」
  — from this period instrument improvement advanced and **multi-hole shakuhachi
  (mainly 7-hole), centred on Miyata Kōhachirō, were made and spread** (my
  translation). And 野坂恵子 Nosaka Keiko joined and with Miki developed the
  **20-string koto (later 21-string)** from the 13-string. The 1960s new-music
  movement **physically rebuilt both instruments**.

`[V]` **The split in the movement, which is a real finding.** 村岡実's biography:
「しかし、村岡はポピュラー音楽の路線を想定していたため、日本音楽集団との方針の違いから1965年の第2回演奏会を前に日本音楽集団を退団し、東京尺八三重奏団も解散となる。」
— "However, because Muraoka envisaged a popular-music direction, over a difference
of policy with Pro Musica Nipponia he **left the ensemble before its second
concert in 1965**, and the Tokyo Shakuhachi Trio was dissolved as well." (my
translation). So the trio that triggered 現代邦楽 broke up within two years, over
art-music vs popular music.

`[V]` **The popular route was commercially the larger one.** 村岡実 **(1924 –
2014-01-02)**, from Miyazaki, is described as 「1960年代後半より登場した「モダン尺八」を最初に導入した中心人物」,
the central figure who **first introduced "modern shakuhachi"** from the late
1960s, and his work with 山本邦山 is credited as 「和楽器が世間に広く受け入れられる要因」,
a cause of Japanese instruments being widely accepted by the general public (my
translations). He played on 美空ひばり's 「柔」 (*Yawara*) and went on to accompany
北島三郎 and 村田英雄; in **1970** he released ***Bamboo*** (King Records) as a jazz
band with taiko, koto and biwa — the track list includes **"Take Five"**,
**"And I Love Her"**, **"House of the Rising Sun"**, **"Do You Know the Way to
San Jose"** and **"Scarborough Fair"**. `[V]` Reissued on LP by SUPERFLY RECORDS
in 2014.

`[V]` **山本邦山's jazz discography is extensive and early**, which contradicts the
English framing of him as primarily a traditionalist. From his biography: Helen
Merrill 『ヘレン・メリル、フォークを歌う』 (**1966**); 『ニューポートのシャープス・アンド・フラッツ』
(1967); 『スタンダード・ボッサ』 (1968); **『銀界』 (*Silver World*, 1970)** with
菊地雅章 Kikuchi Masabumi, **Gary Peacock** and 村上寛; 『竹の組曲』 (1975) with
前田憲男, 荒川康男, 猪俣猛; 『エターナル・エコー』 (1979) and 『スリー・マスターズ』
(1980, live in **Germany**) and 『無限の譜』 (1980) with 佐藤允彦 and 富樫雅彦;
『BREATH』 (1984) with **山下洋輔 Yamashita Yōsuke**; 『アフィニティ』 (Helen Merrill,
1982). He became a Geidai professor in **1993** and a Living National Treasure in
**2002** — i.e. the state designated a man with a thirty-year jazz recording
career. His real name was **山本泰正 Yamamoto Yasumasa**, b. 大津市, Shiga; his son
is 二代山本邦山 (previously 山本真山). He learned **Tozan-ryū** from his father
山本鵬山, then under 中西蝶山.

`[V]` **The single most-distributed shakuhachi recording.** JA-SHAKUHACHI and the
山口五郎 biography agree: 山口五郎's performance of the Kinko honkyoku **『巣鶴鈴慕』
*Sōkaku Reibo*** was included on the **Voyager Golden Record** (「1977年、ボイジャー2号搭載のレコードに、山口五郎の演奏による琴古流本曲「巣鶴鈴慕」が収録。」),
and it is 「日本の楽曲としては唯一」— **the only Japanese piece on it**. (my
translations) `[V]` He was also a visiting lecturer at **Wesleyan University for
one year from 1967** (「ウェズリアン大学（1967年より1年間客員講師を務める）」) — note that
this is the same year as *November Steps*, so **two different Kinko-lineage players
were in the United States in 1967** for different reasons.

### 7.7 The Tozan examination and rank system — rank names found

This belongs partly to §5 but the evidence surfaced here, so I record it.

`[V]` **The rank and examination vocabulary, from 野村峰山's ja.wikipedia
biography** (fetched 2026-11-13), which lists his own titles and offices:
「都山流尺八竹琳軒大師範、副道場長、検定委員」 and, in his chronology,
「都山流尺八准師範検定試験、師範検定試験ともに首席登第」.

My translation and gloss:

| Japanese | Reading | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **准師範検定試験** | *jun-shihan kentei shiken* | the **associate-master certifying examination** |
| **師範検定試験** | *shihan kentei shiken* | the **master certifying examination** |
| **大師範** | *dai-shihan* | **grand master** — the rank Nomura holds |
| **検定委員** | *kentei iin* | member of the **examining committee** |
| **道場長 / 副道場長** | *dōjō-chō / fuku-dōjō-chō* | head / deputy head of the training hall |
| **竹琳軒** | *Chikurinken* | a named 軒 unit within the school (Nomura's) |

Two solid conclusions: (a) Tozan-ryū ranks are **conferred by examination**
(検定試験), with a standing **examining committee**, and one can place **first
(首席登第)** in them — this is a bureaucratic credentialing system, not a
master-to-disciple gift; (b) the ladder visible here is at least
**准師範 → 師範 → 大師範**. `[V]` 島原帆山's biography independently attests
大師範 as a Tozan promotion grade (「1939年大師範昇格」, "promoted to dai-shihan in
1939") and also attests a **conferred honorific title** distinct from rank:
「1946年、流祖中尾都山の直門となり**琳帆山**の称号を受ける」— in 1946 he became a direct
disciple of the founder and received the title 琳帆山 (my translation).

`[U]` I do **not** have the complete rank ladder, the number of ranks, the
examination syllabus, or current membership figures from a Tozan-ryū source. The
`[V]` figure I have is kotobank's: 日本大百科全書 (via `kotobank.jp/word/都山流-105087`,
fetched 2026-11-13) estimates **more than 17,000 members**, making Tozan-ryū the
largest organisation in the shakuhachi world. `[U]` That figure is undated in the
summary I received and Nipponica entries can be decades old — **treat 17,000 as
"late 20th century", not current**.

`[V]` **Total player population.** JA-SHAKUHACHI §「尺八の流派と吹奏人口」 is refreshingly
honest: 「尺八の吹奏人口についての本格的な調査はされておらず、正確な人口は不明である。推定では3万人程度といわれている。」
— "No serious survey of the shakuhachi-playing population has been conducted and
the exact figure is unknown. **An estimate of around 30,000 is cited.**" (my
translation; note 「といわれている」 — *it is said*.) Any English source giving a
confident player count is going beyond its evidence. Note too that 17,000 Tozan
members against a ~30,000 total would make Tozan **over half** of all players,
which is either a strong claim or a sign the two numbers come from different eras.
`[U]` unresolved.

### 7.8 The schools' 20th-century fragmentation — both big schools split

`[V]` **Kinko-ryū never had a single organisation at all.** JA-SHAKUHACHI
§「琴古流」: 「琴古流は大小いくつもの組織の総体であり琴古流として統一した組織をもつものではない。現在代表的な会派としては、「鈴慕会」「童門会」「竹盟社」「竹友社」「国際尺八研修館」などがある。」
— "Kinko-ryū is an aggregate of organisations large and small and does not possess
a unified organisation as Kinko-ryū. Representative groups at present include
**鈴慕会 Reibokai, 童門会 Dōmonkai, 竹盟社 Chikumeisha, 竹友社 Chikuyūsha and the
国際尺八研修館 International Shakuhachi Training Centre**." (my translation)
Corroborated `[V]` by kotobank's Nipponica entry on 琴古流 (fetched 2026-11-13):
no 宗家 since the 4th head, the school divided among many 分派 each with its own
家元; and by ブリタニカ国際大百科事典 小項目事典 on the same page: 「流の統一組織はなく」.

Note the **cross-mapping**: the 竹盟社 is 山口五郎's house (he inherited the
宗家-ship of it on his father's death in **1963** — `[V]` 「1963年、四郎が死去し、竹盟社宗家を継承。」),
the 鈴慕会 was founded by 青木鈴慕 I, and the 国際尺八研修館 is Yokoyama's Bisei
centre. So four of the five named Kinko groups are the houses of the figures in
§7.3 and §7.5. The "school" is in practice a federation of named families.

`[V]` **Tozan-ryū, by contrast, was unified and then split — and the split is
documented in unusual detail.** JA-SHAKUHACHI §「都山流」:
「昭和50年前後に分裂しており、現在は「都山流尺八楽会」「日本尺八連盟」「新都山流」の3派が存在する。」
— it split **around 1975** (昭和50年前後) and three factions now exist:
**都山流尺八楽会**, **日本尺八連盟**, **新都山流**. (my translation) The same section
explains the pre-split strength: 「宮城道雄と提携し、宮城作曲の尺八譜の公刊を独占したこと、評議員制の導入など中央集権的な組織作りを行ったことなど都山流は尺八界最大の組織となった」
— it allied with **Miyagi Michio and monopolised the publication of the shakuhachi
parts to Miyagi's compositions**, and built a **centralised organisation** including
a 評議員制 (councillor system), becoming the largest organisation in the shakuhachi
world (my translation). **That is the real mechanism of Tozan's growth and it is
not musical: it is a publishing monopoly on the most popular new repertoire of the
1920s–30s** (Miyagi's 『春の海』 being the obvious case). English accounts credit
"modern organisation" vaguely; the specific lever was copyright in print.

`[V]` **The succession crisis, from the two biographies.** From 「中尾都山」
(fetched 2026-11-13): 初代 **1876-10-05 – 1956**, real name 中尾琳三, trained as a
komusō, founded the school **1896-02-15** (the article gives a day-level date, and
notes he 「大阪市天満にて都山流尺八指南の看板を掲げる」, hung out a shingle as a Tozan-ryū
shakuhachi instructor in Tenma, Osaka). 二代目: the founder's son, b. **1944-01-09**,
real name 稀一, succeeded at **age 12 in 1956**, graduated Musashino Academia
Musicae, **died at 30 on 1974-10-12**. 三代目: **the second head's own mother**,
中尾れん (**1905-04-22 – 1990-01-06**), who took the name on his sudden death.
四代目: the founder's grandson, b. 1948, real name 正幸, succeeded **1990**.

The article then records the contested claim in a parenthesis `[V]`:
「（一方で、二代目都山の長女中尾美都子（1969年3月7日生れ）が、流の幹部島原帆山らの後援により家元権を主張。最終的に新都山流初代宗家を名乗ることとなり、またその島原帆山も後に新都山流を離れ日本尺八連盟を設立するなど、都山流は分裂した。）」
— "(Meanwhile the second head's eldest daughter **中尾美都子 Nakao Mitsuko**
(b. **1969-03-07**) claimed the 家元 right with the backing of the school's senior
figure **島原帆山** and others. She ended up styling herself the first 宗家 of
**新都山流**; and Shimabara Hanzan himself later left Shin-Tozan-ryū and
established the **日本尺八連盟**, so Tozan-ryū fragmented.)" (my translation)

`[V]` 島原帆山's own biography gives dates and a **lawsuit**, and they conflict
slightly with the above: 「1975年、宗家を二代中尾都山の長女・中尾美都子とし自身を会長とする都山流尺八協会を設立。三年がかりの裁判となり1980年、新都山流日本尺八連盟と名称変更した。しかし先の宗家・美都子とも拗れて袂を分かち翌1981年、日本尺八連盟を結成、初代会長に就任した。家元制度から脱皮し民主的な組織を目指し法人化、1986年社団法人日本尺八連盟を成立させた。」
My translation: "In **1975** he established the **都山流尺八協会** with Nakao
Mitsuko as 宗家 and himself as chairman. This became **a three-year lawsuit**, and
in **1980** the name was changed to 新都山流日本尺八連盟. But he then fell out with
Mitsuko and parted from her, and the following year, **1981**, formed the
**日本尺八連盟**, becoming its first chairman. **Casting off the iemoto system and
aiming at a democratic organisation**, he incorporated it, establishing the
**社団法人日本尺八連盟 in 1986**."

So the chronology is: founder dies 1956 → second head dies 1974 → rival claim and
breakaway 1975 → **litigation 1975–78ish, resolved 1980** → further split 1981 →
incorporation 1986. `[V]` A real finding: **the 20th-century history of Tozan-ryū
includes a three-year court case over the 家元 right**, and the outcome was a
faction that explicitly **repudiated the iemoto system** (「家元制度から脱皮し」) in
favour of a democratic corporate body. The irony is exact: Nakao Tozan I's great
innovation was 「近代的な家元制度を整えて」, *establishing a modern iemoto system*
(`[V]` JA-SHAKUHACHI), and within 25 years of his death that system had been
litigated apart and one of its largest fragments had abolished it.

`[V]` 島原帆山's biography also supplies a vivid detail about the instrument's
regional competition and about the war: he went to **colonial Korea in 1927** to
spread Tozan-ryū, performed on the **opening-commemoration programme of the Keijō
(Seoul) Broadcasting Station in 1928**, lived there ten years, and returned home
in **1937** because of 「中国地方での都山流の下火、上田流の攻勢」— the decline of
Tozan-ryū in the Chūgoku region and **the offensive of Ueda-ryū** (my translation).
And: 「1945年の原爆投下の翌日、八本松（現：東広島市）の弾薬庫から広島市内に家族の安否を尋ねる。」
— the day **after the atomic bombing in 1945** he came into Hiroshima from an
ammunition depot at Hachihonmatsu to ask after his family's safety. He was
later made an **honorary citizen of Hiroshima**. (my translation)

provision is at **第3の2(4)**:

> 「また、和楽器については、3学年間を通じて1種類以上の楽器を用いること。」

My translation: "Further, as regards **Japanese instruments** (*wagakki*), **at
least one kind of instrument shall be used across the three school years**."

`[V]` **Effective date: April 2002.** MEXT's own notice page for that revision
(`https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/cs/1320061.htm`, fetched 2026-11-13) states
that the December 1998 revision **takes effect on 1 April 2002**. (That page also
gives the music timetable: **45 hours in year 1, 35 hours each in years 2 and 3**,
periods of 50 minutes — i.e. roughly one period a week, which is the real
constraint on what can be taught.)

**So the commonly repeated claim is half right.** The revision is **1998**, not
"a 2000s revision"; what happened in the 2000s is that it **took effect** (2002).
A writer who says "a 2002 curriculum change" is describing the implementation and
a writer who says "1998" is describing the promulgation; both are defensible, and
"a 2000s revision" conflates them. Date the promulgation and the implementation
separately.

`[V]` **It was retained and strengthened in the 2008 revision.** MEXT,
中学校学習指導要領 音楽 (平成20年改訂 set), at
`https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/new-cs/youryou/chu/on.htm`, fetched
2026-11-13, 第3の2(2):

> 「器楽の指導については，指導上の必要に応じて和楽器，弦楽器，管楽器，打楽器，鍵盤楽器，電子楽器及び世界の諸民族の楽器を適宜用いること。なお，和楽器の指導については，3学年間を通じて1種類以上の楽器の表現活動を通して，生徒が我が国や郷土の伝統音楽のよさを味わうことができるよう工夫すること。」

My translation: "As regards instrumental instruction, Japanese instruments, string
instruments, wind instruments, percussion, keyboard instruments, electronic
instruments and the instruments of the world's various peoples shall be used as
appropriate according to instructional need. Further, as regards instruction in
Japanese instruments, **through performance activity on at least one kind of
instrument across the three school years**, teachers shall contrive matters so
that pupils can appreciate the merits of the traditional music of our country and
their locality."

The 2008 wording adds **「表現活動を通して」** — *through performance activity* —
which tightens "use" into "pupils actually play it". `[V]` The same revision's
第3の2(3) adds a body-technique clause: 「我が国の伝統的な歌唱や和楽器の指導については，言葉と音楽との関係，姿勢や身体の使い方についても配慮すること。」

chose the **koto** (and secondarily 和太鼓 and 三味線) because class sets can be

One structural reason to expect the shakuhachi to lose this competition, which is

requires an embouchure that **「音を出すには熟練が必要である」**, "requires skill
even to produce a sound" (JA-SHAKUHACHI §奏法, explicitly contrasting it with the
recorder, where the fipple lets a beginner sound a note immediately). For a
once-a-week class across three years, that is close to disqualifying.

## 8. The naming question

Source throughout: `JA-SHAKUHACHI` = ja.wikipedia 「尺八」, full plaintext via the
extracts API, fetched 2026-11-13. All translations mine.

### 8.1 尺八 and its reading

`[V]` **The name is a measurement, and the measurement is the pipe's length.**
「名称は、標準の管長が一尺八寸であったことに由来し」— "the name derives from the
standard pipe length having been **one *shaku* eight *sun***." 尺 = *shaku*,
八 = *hachi* (from 八寸, eight *sun*); the reading しゃくはち *shakuhachi* is the
only one given. Players call it simply 竹 *take*, "bamboo":
「演奏者のあいだでは単に竹とも呼ばれる。」 In English it is *shakuhachi* or
**"Bamboo Flute"** (「英語ではshakuhachiあるいは、Bamboo Fluteとも呼ばれる」).

`[V]` **The etymology has a specific, hedged Chinese source and a caveat.** The
leading theory (「有力な説」— "a strong theory", not a fact) rests on the 「呂才伝」
chapter of the 『旧唐書』 (*Jiu Tang shu*, Old Book of Tang): when **呂才 Lü Cai**
(600–665), a Tang court musician of the early 7th century, made a set of
end-blown flutes tuned to the twelve *lü*, the one sounding the Chinese standard
pitch 黄鐘 *huangzhong* (Japanese 壱越 *ichikotsu*, Western **D**) was one shaku
eight sun. Note the reporting verb: 「…ためと伝えられている」— "**it is transmitted
that** it was for that reason."

`[V]` **And the unit is not stable, which the article flags itself:**
「ただし時代と国によって「尺」の単位の実際の長さが違うので注意が必要であり、日本の江戸時代の普化尺八では約54.5 cmである。」
— "However, caution is needed because the actual length of the unit 尺 differs by
period and country; for the Edo-period Japanese Fuke shakuhachi it is about
**54.5 cm**." So "one shaku eight sun" does *not* pick out a fixed length across
history. A Tang 一尺八寸 and an Edo 一尺八寸 are different objects. English sources
that convert 一尺八寸 to 54.5 cm and leave it there are using the Edo value for a
Tang-derived name.

`[V]` **The length word is also not a reliable guide to the instrument today**, for
a second and separate reason — the 正寸 / 正律 distinction (§「正寸と正律」):
**正寸 *seisun*** means "accurate in length" (so a 正寸一尺八寸管 is genuinely 1.8
shaku long but its 筒音 is not guaranteed to be exactly D), while **正律 *seiritsu***
means "accurate in pitch" (a 正律一尺八寸管 sounds a true 壱越/D and **may not be
exactly 1.8 shaku long**). 「現在は正律で示すのが主流となっており」— **the 正律 convention
is now mainstream**, and to be clearer still, instruments are sometimes called
**"D管", "E管", "A管"** by their 筒音. (my translations) So the modern naming
convention has quietly abandoned the literal measurement that gave the instrument
its name, in favour of pitch — and the standard sizes are **一尺八寸管** (D4,
*ichikotsu*) and **一尺六寸管** (E4, 平調 — the size used for 『春の海』), with the
full range running 一尺一寸 to 二尺三寸 in semitone (sun) steps, everything outside
1.8 and 1.6 being 「はるかに少ない」, far rarer.

### 8.2 普化尺八 and the three instruments that have borne the name

`[V]` **This is the central point of §8 and the article makes it in its second
sentence:**「「尺八」の名で呼ばれてきた楽器は時代ごとに複数ある。」— "**There are several
instruments that have been called by the name 'shakuhachi', differing by period.**"
(my translation). So the answer to "has 'shakuhachi' ever denoted other
instruments?" is an unambiguous **yes — at least three, and they are not
continuous with one another.**

| Name | Period | Fate | Holes | Length | Bore/make |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **古代尺八 / 雅楽尺八** *kodai / gagaku shakuhachi* | origin in Tang China; **transmitted to Japan late 7th–early 8th c.** as a *gagaku* instrument | 「平安時代中頃の10世紀には絶えた」— **died out by the 10th c.**, mid-Heian, and the external-slope type was also discontinued in China | **6** (5 front, 1 back) | avg **40 cm** | — |
| **一節切** *hitoyogiri* | appears in the **Muromachi** period; 「17世紀後半に全盛」, peak late 17th c. | 「19世紀にはほぼ絶えた」— **all but extinct by the 19th c.** | **5** (4 front, 1 back) | avg **33.6 cm**, outer dia. 3 cm | one internode from the **middle** of the culm (hence the name) |
| **普化尺八** *Fuke shakuhachi* | 「16世紀末の日本で開発され」— developed in Japan at the end of the 16th c. | **the instrument called "shakuhachi" today** | **5** (4 front, 1 back) | avg **54.5 cm**, dia. 4 cm | the **root end** (根元) of 真竹 *madake*, typically including **7 nodes**; 5 holes, 3 visible nodes in the modern form |

(All figures `[V]` from JA-SHAKUHACHI §§歴史, 普化尺八, 物理的構造; my translations.)

`[V]` Three consequences worth stating explicitly:

1. **There is a gap of roughly five centuries between the first and second
   instruments.** The article calls it exactly that: 「歴史上の空白期間ののち」—
   "after a blank period in history" — before the *hitoyogiri* appears. The
   shakuhachi is **not** a continuously transmitted instrument; it is a name that
   has been reapplied.
2. **The ancient instrument's music is unrecoverable, and the article says so:**
   「古台尺八に関連する楽譜の記録がないため、演奏方法や音階などの詳細は不明である。」— "because
   there are no records of notation relating to the ancient shakuhachi, details
   such as playing method and scale are unknown." (「古台」 is a typo for 「古代」 in
   the source.) Eight specimens of the six-hole, three-node ancient type survive
   in the **Shōsōin at Tōdai-ji** (「東大寺の正倉院には六孔三節の尺八が八管収められている」)
   — objects without a repertoire.
3. **普化尺八 is the precise term, and it is needed.** Because three instruments
   share the name, "shakuhachi" without qualification is ambiguous in any
   historical statement. 普化尺八 names the Fuke-sect instrument specifically; it
   is the one the schools in §5 all play and the one the whole of §§4, 6 and 7
   concerns. `[V]` The article's own framing: 「現在「尺八」と呼ばれているものは「普化尺八」であり」.
   English writing that says "the shakuhachi arrived from China in the 8th
   century" is attaching the modern instrument's name to an extinct one — a
   different object, 14 cm shorter, with an extra hole, no surviving music, and
   no transmission to the present.

`[V]` **A fourth name worth knowing: 虚鐸 *kyotaku / kotaku*.** In the legendary
account, the instrument was invented as 虚鐸 by 張伯 Chōhaku, a disciple of the
Tang Zen monk 普化 Puhua, around the 9th century; 心地覚心 Shinchi Kakushin brought
it back to Japan in **1254**; and 虚無 (identified with 楠木正勝 Kusunoki Masakatsu)
spread it around **1400**. The article's verdict is flat: 「…というものがあるが、検証された史実ではない。」
— "there is [such a tradition], **but it is not verified historical fact**." (my
translation). So 虚鐸 belongs to the legend, and the legend is explicitly
disavowed in the Japanese reference text while being repeated as history in much
English writing. The word 普化 in 普化尺八 and 普化宗 is the name of that legendary
Chinese monk.

### 8.3 Related and adjacent names

`[V]` The article's 関連項目 (see-also) list names, without discussion:
**シャクハチ類** ("shakuhachi-type [instruments]", a category), **短簫** (*tanso*,
"a similar instrument in Korean traditional music"), and **オークラウロ**
(*Ōkuraulo*, described as 「尺八とフルートを合体させたような1930年代の新案楽器」— a
newly-invented 1930s instrument like a shakuhachi and a flute fused). `[U]` I did
not open any of these three articles; the glosses above are from the see-also
list's own annotations.

`[V]` 「尺八管」 was used in Chinese as an alternative name for 簫管 *xiaoguan*:
「宋の陳暘『楽書』では尺八管を簫管の別名としている」— in 陳暘 Chen Yang's 『楽書』
(*Yueshu*, Song dynasty) 尺八管 is given as another name for 簫管. So in Chinese
sources the term is a synonym inside the *xiao* family, not a distinct instrument
name — a further reason not to read the two characters as naming one thing.

`[V]` **Modern materials have not changed the name.** Wooden (木管尺八), cheap
synthetic-resin, **3D-printed**, and **aluminium-alloy "metal shakuhachi"**
(「アルミニウム合金製の「メタル尺八」」) instruments all exist and are all called
shakuhachi. The article also records a genuine dispute and its current verdict:
「尺八の音色と材質は科学的には無関係とされているが、関係があるとする論争もあった。」— timbre
and material **are held to be scientifically unrelated**, though there has been
controversy claiming they are related. (my translations) `[U]` No study is cited.

## 9. Closing

### 9.1 Corrections to the commonly repeated English account

Numbered so they can be cited individually. Each says what English usually
asserts, what the Japanese reference sources actually say, and where in this file
the evidence sits.

1. **"Sankyoku means koto + shamisen + shakuhachi."** No. 三曲 named **jiuta
   shamisen + koto + 胡弓 kokyū**, three instruments of the blind musicians' guild
   (当道座). JA-SANKYOKU's opening sentence ends 「**後に尺八が加わった**」, "the
   shakuhachi joined later." The shakuhachi is the **replacement member and the
   name predates it**. (§6.1) `[V]`

2. **"The shakuhachi replaced the kokyū in sankyoku."** This is the specific claim
   ja.wikipedia singles out as **「全く根拠のない発言」, "an utterly groundless
   assertion."** Both formations are alive; the shakuhachi became the *default*
   third instrument, and kokyū-inclusive sankyoku 「現在でも…少なからず行われている」,
   is still performed to no small extent. Say "became the usual third instrument",
   never "replaced". (§6.3) `[V]`

3. **"The shakuhachi joined sankyoku in the Meiji period / in 18xx."** No date
   exists. The sources give a gradient — occasional from **mid-Edo**, then
   **「明治以降本格的に参入した」**, "entered in earnest from Meiji onward" — with no
   event, decade, concert or person attached. 本格的に ("in earnest") is as sharp as
   the Japanese gets. (§6.3) `[V]`/`[U]`

4. **"Honkyoku are the ancient Zen pieces."** Only *koten honkyoku* are. The word
   **本曲 was broadened by the 1871 abolition itself**: afterwards, religiously
   unconnected solo and ensemble pieces were also called honkyoku, so the Fuke
   repertoire had to be renamed **古典本曲** to be distinguished — and even that
   term is used only 「ことがある」, *sometimes*. A Tozan-ryū piece composed in 1903
   is honkyoku. (§4.2) `[V]`

5. **"There are about 150 honkyoku, plus the 36 Kinko pieces."** Double-counting.
   The Japanese is 「琴古流本曲を**ふくめ**、150曲あまり」— "over 150 pieces,
   **including** the Kinko-ryū honkyoku." (§4.4) `[V]`

6. **"The Kinko honkyoku number 36."** Sources disagree and the English figure
   picks one side silently. On a single kotobank page (fetched 2026-11-13):
   精選版日本国語大辞典, ブリタニカ国際大百科事典 小項目事典 and 日本大百科全書 all give
   **36**; but 百科事典マイペディア gives **「30余曲」** and the old 世界大百科事典 gives
   **「30曲余り」** — "thirty-odd". Cite 36 as the usual figure and note the
   dissent. (§5 / kotobank 琴古流) `[V]`

7. **"Kurosawa Kinko founded Kinko-ryū."** He assembled the repertoire; the
   *school* is a later construct, and the Japanese sources hedge the attribution
   twice over. 百科事典マイペディア says the consciousness of it **as a school** was
   established around the **second** head — 「流としての意識が確立した**という**」, *it is
   said* — and 日本大百科全書 notes the name only 「一般に使われるようになったのは明治初めの普化宗廃止（1871）以後」,
   came into general use **only after the 1871 abolition**. The school is named
   retrospectively, and its self-consciousness is post-Meiji. (kotobank 琴古流,
   fetched 2026-11-13) `[V]`

8. **"Kinko-ryū is a school with a head."** It has had **no 宗家 since the fourth
   head** and is 「大小いくつもの組織の総体」, an aggregate of organisations large and
   small; ブリタニカ: **「流の統一組織はなく」**, "there is no unified organisation for
   the school." In practice it is a federation of named houses — 鈴慕会, 童門会,
   竹盟社, 竹友社, 国際尺八研修館 — four of which are the private houses of the
   figures in §7. (§7.8) `[V]`

9. **"Tozan-ryū grew because of its modern organisation and new music."** True but
   too vague to be useful; the documented lever was commercial.
   「宮城道雄と提携し、**宮城作曲の尺八譜の公刊を独占した**こと」— it allied with Miyagi
   Michio and **monopolised publication of the shakuhachi parts to Miyagi's
   compositions**, i.e. it held print rights in the most popular new repertoire of
   the 1920s–30s. Add the 評議員制 councillor system and that is the growth
   mechanism. (§7.8) `[V]`

10. **"Chikuhō-ryū is an offshoot of Tozan-ryū."** They are **parallels**: both
    descend from the now-extinct **宗悦流 Sōetsu-ryū** of 近藤宗悦 (1821–1867), the
    Kansai gaikyoku pioneer. Tozan-ryū came out of Sōetsu-ryū in 1896, Chikuhō-ryū
    in 1917. And Chikuhō uses a **different notation syllabary** —
    **フホウエヤイ**, not Kinko/Tozan's ロツレチ. (§5.6) `[V]`

11. **"Tozan-ryū's iemoto system endures."** Nakao Tozan I's signature achievement
    was 「近代的な家元制度を整えて」, building a *modern iemoto system* — and it was
    **litigated apart**. Founder dies 1956; second head dies at 30 in 1974; rival
    家元 claim 1975; **a three-year lawsuit**, resolved 1980; further split 1981;
    and the faction that emerged, 島原帆山's 日本尺八連盟, explicitly
    **「家元制度から脱皮し」, cast off the iemoto system** for a democratic
    incorporated body (社団法人, 1986). Three Tozan factions exist today:
    都山流尺八楽会, 日本尺八連盟, 新都山流. (§7.8) `[V]`

12. **"November Steps was composed in 1964."** The general ja.wikipedia 尺八
    article says 1964 and **is wrong**; the 横山勝也 article gives **1967** twice,
    and the premiere is 9 November 1967. Note that this means ja.wikipedia
    **contradicts itself across articles** — a caution about using it singly.
    (§7.2) `[V]`

13. **"The 1967 November Steps recording is the premiere."** It is not. The
    premiere was the New York Philharmonic, 9 November 1967; the famous recording
    is the **Toronto Symphony, Massey Hall, 8 December 1967** — a month later and
    a different orchestra. (§7.2, per the usual account) `[V]`

14. **"November Steps revived an ancient biwa-and-shakuhachi tradition."** There
    was none. **The pairing has no precedent in Japanese music history** — it was
    invented for Takemitsu's film scores (『切腹』1962, 『暗殺』1964, 『怪談』1965,
    NHK『源義経』1966), crystallised in *Eclipse*, and reached the concert hall via a
    recording Ozawa played to Bernstein. The "ancient duet" is a modern
    composition. (§7.2, per the usual account) `[V]`

15. **"Yokoyama Katsuya was a traditionalist" / "a modernist."** He was the
    **junction**. Kinko-ryū from his grandfather 横山篁邨 and father 横山蘭畝; then at
    25, under two teachers 「まったく行く道を異にする」, "following entirely different
    paths" — **福田蘭童** (the 新日本音楽 modernist) and **海童道祖 Watazumi Dōso** (the
    ascetic). That double training is why one man could premiere a Lincoln Center
    commission, found the World Shakuhachi Festival, and transmit an ungraded
    koten-honkyoku lineage. (§7.2) `[V]`

16. **"The Living National Treasure designations go to Kinko and Tozan, the
    schools with formal grading."** True 1967–2022; **broken in 2025**. 善養寺惠介's
    primary formation is **根笹派錦風流 Nezasa-ha Kinpū-ryū** komusō shakuhachi —
    via his father and 岡崎自修, both pupils of **神如道 (1891–1966)**, then under
    神如正 — and he reached Kinko only through 山口五郎 at Geidai. He is
    「古典本曲に本格的に取り組む数少ない演奏家」. State recognition has moved toward the
    ungraded purist strand. (§7.3) `[V]`

17. **"青木鈴翁 and 青木鈴慕 are two different Living National Treasures."** One
    man. 青木鈴慕 is a **名跡**, an inherited name; **青木静夫 (1935-10-04 –
    2018-08-21)** held it as the second generation, was designated in **1974**,
    passed 鈴慕 to his son 青木彰時 in **2018** and took the name **青木鈴翁** — the
    ja.wikipedia 青木鈴慕 article states 「初代青木鈴翁。」 directly under his entry. The
    **翁 renaming on retirement is systematic** in Kinko-ryū: 寿童→寿**翁** (Nōtomi,
    1973), 古童→竹**翁** (Araki II). Counting both names gives six holders, not
    seven. (§7.4) `[V]`

18. **"The seven LNT designation years are hard to find."** They are all in the
    individuals' own ja.wikipedia biographies: **1967, 1974, 1982, 1992, 2002,
    2022, 2025**. Note the **twenty-year gap, 2002–2022**, with no shakuhachi
    designation at all. (§7.3) `[V]`

19. **"The Edo-period ban meant only komusō played the shakuhachi."** The ban was
    real and legally backed (幕府の法度), but ja.wikipedia marks it as the
    **建前** — the official line — and adds 「実際には尺八をたしなむ者はいた」, "in fact
    there were people who took it up as a pastime." Mid-Edo records of a
    koto/kokyū/**shakuhachi** trio called 三曲 point the same way. The monopoly was
    de jure and leaky de facto. (§4.1, §6.2) `[V]`

20. **"1871 freed the instrument."** It did two separable things, and English
    merges them. It removed the **Fuke sect's legal monopoly on the instrument**
    — and the 当道座 guild's ownership of the **sankyoku ensemble** was a *separate*
    barrier, whose removal 「解禁」 the sources date only loosely to the same era.
    Two locks, two keys. (§4.1, §6.1) `[V]`/`[U]`

21. **"Tsuru no Sugomori is a piece."** It is a **title shared by a family of
    pieces**. 「同名異曲が多くある」— "there are many cases of same-name-different-piece";
    for 『鈴慕』, 『三谷』 and 『鶴の巣籠』, 「10種類以上の旋律の異なるものが伝承されている」,
    **more than ten melodically distinct versions** are transmitted. A honkyoku
    title identifies almost nothing until you also know the transmission line.
    (§4.5) `[V]`

22. **"Honkyoku are ancient pieces by unknown Zen masters."** Composer and date
    are 「基本的に不詳」, as a rule unknown — so any English source that names a
    composer or a century for a koten honkyoku is asserting more than the Japanese
    reference literature will. And survival was partly a **20th-century salvage
    operation**: 神如道 (1891–1966) is credited with actively collecting and
    transmitting 「絶滅の危機に瀕していた数々の虚無僧尺八曲」, numerous komusō pieces on the
    brink of extinction. (§4.4, §7.3) `[V]`

23. **"The shakuhachi came to Japan from China in the 8th century."** A
    *different instrument* of the same name did. **古代尺八 / 雅楽尺八**: six holes,
    ~40 cm, extinct by the 10th century, **no surviving notation**, eight specimens
    in the Shōsōin, and no transmission to anything later. 「「尺八」の名で呼ばれてきた楽器は時代ごとに複数ある」
    — several instruments have borne the name. The present instrument is
    **普化尺八**, developed **in Japan at the end of the 16th century**, with a
    「歴史上の空白期間」, a blank period in history, in between. (§8.2) `[V]`

24. **"Shakuhachi = 54.5 cm = one shaku eight sun."** The unit 尺 differs by period
    and country — the article itself flags 「注意が必要」 — so a Tang 一尺八寸 and an
    Edo 一尺八寸 are different lengths; 54.5 cm is the **Edo Fuke** value. And
    modern practice has **abandoned the measurement**: the 正律 (pitch-accurate)
    convention is now mainstream over 正寸 (length-accurate), so a "1.8 shaku"
    flute is often not 1.8 shaku long, and instruments are increasingly called
    **D管 / E管 / A管** by their 筒音. (§8.1) `[V]`

25. **"The shakuhachi was invented by Puhua's disciple / brought back by
    Kakushin in 1254."** ja.wikipedia records the 虚鐸 legend in full and then says
    **「検証された史実ではない」, "it is not verified historical fact."** Likewise the
    1400 attribution to 虚無/楠木正勝. These are transmitted stories (伝承) that
    English writing routinely promotes to chronology. (§8.2) `[V]`

26. **"Japanese schools must teach the shakuhachi" / "a 2000s revision required
    it."** The requirement is for **和楽器 in general — at least one kind across the
    three junior-high years** — and it **names no instrument**. The date is also
    usually wrong: **promulgated December 1998**, 中学校学習指導要領 第2章第5節 音楽
    第3の2(4), 「和楽器については、3学年間を通じて1種類以上の楽器を用いること」; **effective
    1 April 2002**; retained and tightened in the **2008** revision, which adds
    「表現活動を通して」 (through performance activity). Promulgation and
    implementation are different years — do not say "a 2000s revision". And
    **which** instruments schools adopted is `[U]` — I could not source a
    distribution. (§7.9) `[V]` on the text and dates, `[U]` on adoption.

27. **"The World Shakuhachi Festival is an old international institution."** It
    begins in **1994**, at **Bisei, Okayama**, as 横山勝也's own 美星国際尺八音楽祭,
    six years after he opened the 国際尺八研修館 there in 1988. The series is
    irregular (4, 4, 2, 4, 4, 6, 7-year gaps), its name shifted between
    「国際尺八音楽祭」 and 「世界尺八フェスティバル」, and the retrospective "WSF #N"
    numbering is partly a later imposition — the official history page lists
    **eight** events while describing them as seven. **Do not state a count.**
    (§7.6) `[V]`/`[U]`

28. **"About 30,000 people play the shakuhachi."** Cite this only with its hedge.
    「尺八の吹奏人口についての本格的な調査はされておらず、正確な人口は不明である。推定では3万人程度と**いわれている**。」
    — no serious survey has been done, the figure is unknown, and ~30,000 is what
    **is said**. Note also that kotobank's Nipponica figure of **17,000+ Tozan
    members** against a 30,000 total would make one school over half of all
    players — either a strong claim or two numbers from different decades.
    (§7.7) `[V]` on the quotes, `[U]` on reconciling them.

29. **"*Dai shihan* is the highest rank in shakuhachi."** It is a rank **internal
    to one organisation's grading system**, and the systems are not commensurable —
    Kinko-ryū has no unified one at all (correction 8). In Tozan-ryū it sits above
    准師範 and 師範, is conferred by **examination** (検定試験, with a standing 検定委員
    committee, and one can place 首席), and coexists with separately conferred
    honorific names (島原帆山 received the title 琳帆山 in 1946). A *dai shihan*
    certificate certifies standing in one body, not tradition-wide eminence.
    (§5.6, §7.7) `[V]`

30. **"The 'Dokyoku school'."** Not a 流派 in the Japanese reference literature:
    **neither 道曲 nor 童曲 appears anywhere in the ja.wikipedia 尺八 article's
    treatment of schools** (I read the full plaintext, 2026-11-13, and its school
    list is 明暗流 / 琴古流 / 都山流 / 上田流 / 村治流 / 竹保流 / 錦風流). The line calls
    itself **横山勝也伝承尺八古典本曲** and has **no grading, licence or shihan system
    at all** — which, given corrections 8, 11 and 29, is the informative fact about
    it. Call it a **lineage**, not a school. (per the usual account; not researched here)
    `[V]` on the absence from the article, `[V]`-by-brief on the rest.

31. **"新日本音楽 was founded by Miyagi Michio and Yoshida Seifū."** The dedicated
    biography adds a **third proposer, 本居長世 Motoori Nagayo**, and a date: 「1920年…宮城道雄、本居長世と共に「新日本音楽」を提唱し、第1回演奏会を開催」
    — proposed in **1920**, with a **first concert**. The 尺八 article names only
    two. Report the two-vs-three disagreement. Also: the Miyagi–Yoshida partnership
    was formed in **Seoul in 1914**, not in Japan. (§7.1) `[V]`

32. **"新日本音楽 was aesthetic modernism."** It also had an imperial phase.
    Yoshida Seifū **led a parade of 2,600 shakuhachi players in 1940** for the
    2,600th imperial year; Nakao Tozan's 『青海波』 (1904) was written out of
    patriotic feeling at the fall of Port Arthur and **quotes Kimigayo** at the
    close of its ensemble section. The modernisation of the instrument ran through
    the empire — Yoshida as trader then envoy, Nakao touring **Russia (1915)** and
    **Korea/Manchuria (1916)**, 島原帆山 sent to Korea in **1927** and broadcasting
    on the opening of **Keijō Broadcasting Station in 1928**. (§7.1, §7.8) `[V]`

33. **"現代邦楽 began with November Steps."** The domestic pivot is earlier and
    separate: the **東京尺八三重奏団** (Muraoka / Yokoyama / Miyata, 1962 or 1963 —
    ja.wikipedia disagrees with itself by a year), whose **second concert in 1963**
    premiered 三木稔's 「くるだんど」 and led, after all-night meetings at Muraoka's
    house, to the founding of the **日本音楽集団 (Pro Musica Nipponia) in 1964** with
    14 members. The trio itself **broke up by 1965** when Muraoka left for popular
    music. And the movement **rebuilt the instruments**: multi-hole (mainly 7-hole)
    shakuhachi via 宮田耕八朗, and the 20- then 21-string koto via 野坂恵子 and Miki.
    (§7.5) `[V]`

34. **"Yamamoto Hōzan was a traditional master."** The state designated, in 2002, a
    man with a **thirty-year jazz recording career** — Helen Merrill from **1966**,
    『銀界』 (1970) with 菊地雅章 and **Gary Peacock**, live in **Germany** in 1980,
    『BREATH』 (1984) with **山下洋輔**. And 村岡実 (1924–2014), who introduced
    "modern shakuhachi" from the late 1960s, played on 美空ひばり's 「柔」 and
    released ***Bamboo*** (1970) covering **"Take Five"**, **"Scarborough Fair"**
    and **"House of the Rising Sun"**. Popular and jazz work is not peripheral to
    the 20th-century shakuhachi; it is how 「和楽器が世間に広く受け入れられる」, how
    Japanese instruments came to be widely accepted. (§7.5) `[V]`

35. **"The shakuhachi has always been taught at Japanese conservatories."** The
    **shakuhachi department at Tokyo University of the Arts was newly established
    in 1977** (山口五郎 appointed lecturer; professor 1987) — **106 years after**
    1871. Before that the pipeline was **NHK's 邦楽技能者育成会**: 山口五郎 2nd intake
    (1956), 横山勝也 6th (1960), 野村峰山 22nd (1977). A broadcaster, not a
    university, was the national conservatory for hōgaku. (§7.5) `[V]`

36. **"Shakuhachi players double on koto and shamisen."** The reverse of the real
    asymmetry. 「三味線、箏、胡弓の奏者は兼任が多いが、尺八はそれのみを専門とする演奏家が多い」—
    players of the three guild instruments commonly double; **shakuhachi players
    are mostly specialists in it alone**. This is why the shakuhachi alone among
    the four has 流派 with their own notations, grades and licences. (§6.4) `[V]`

37. **"In sankyoku the shakuhachi plays an independent part."** Not classically.
    「古典的な三曲合奏では、尺八の手付けは三絃の手を**ベタ付け**で尺八向けに編曲したものであった」—
    the shakuhachi's realisation was the **sangen's part tracked note-for-note**
    (*beta-zuke*) and arranged for shakuhachi. It entered the ensemble as
    heterophonic doubling of the shamisen, which is why early gaikyoku parts are
    unidiomatic. (§6.5) `[V]`

38. **"Gaikyoku means light or lesser music."** It means a **direction of
    borrowing**: 「他種目の旋律をその楽器用に編曲した楽曲」, a piece whose melody belongs to
    another genre, arranged for this instrument. A piece is gaikyoku *for the
    shakuhachi* exactly because it is honkyoku for the shamisen. And note the
    separate professional **period** grid — 古曲 / 新曲 / 現代曲 — which is a
    *different* three-term vocabulary from 本曲/外曲, used 「こともある」, sometimes.
    (§6.5) `[V]`

39. **"Jinashi instruments are a nostalgic affectation."** They are a
    repertoire-driven choice. The 地 lacquer bore 「音が大きくなり、正確な音程が得られる」—
    gives volume and accurate pitch, which sankyoku with a koto requires; the old
    地無し/古管 bore cannot hold pitch and 「奏者が音程の補正をする必要がある」, requires the
    player to correct it — and for koten honkyoku 「このひとつひとつの尺八のもつ個性もその魅力となっている」,
    each individual instrument's character is part of the appeal. Different
    repertoires want different instruments. (§4.6) `[V]`

40. **"The shakuhachi was re-legitimised as art music."** Partly — but the
    documented routes are odder than that. One ran through **music theory and the
    academy**: 初代川瀬順輔 (1870–1959) studied under 上原六四郎, professor at the
    **東京音楽学校**, who devised the **点符式記譜法** dot notation that Kawase then
    popularised (kotobank, 日本大百科全書 on 琴古流). Another ran through **folk
    song**: in 江差追分 「尺八の伴奏が**必須**となっている」, shakuhachi accompaniment is
    *obligatory*. A third, if ja.wikipedia is right, ran through scandal — the
    **1933 野村景久 murder case** is credited with 「尺八の精神性を見直す気運を生んだ」,
    generating a mood of re-examining the instrument's spirituality. (§4.6, §6.6,
    §7.1) `[V]` on the quotes; `[U]` on the 1933 causal claim, which is unfootnoted.

### 9.2 Open questions I could not resolve

Ordered by how much a reader would want them.

1. **Which 和楽器 Japanese schools actually adopted after 2002.** The single
   biggest gap. Almost certainly answered by a table inside one of two PDFs I
   reached and could not read — `jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjomep/7/2/7_48` (JBIG2
   page images) and `hyogo-c.ed.jp/~kenshu-p-lib/research/113_02.pdf` (CCITT fax
   scans). **An OCR pass on those two files is the highest-value next action in
   this whole file.** Alternative: a MEXT 教育課程実施状況調査. (§7.9)

2. **The Riley Lee / Chikuhō-ryū claim.** 1980, *dai shihan*, from 二代目酒井竹保
   (1933–1992) — **wholly unverified**. ja.wikipedia has no article on 酒井竹保 or
   竹保流; my only Japanese text on the school is two sentences. Try Riley Lee's
   University of Sydney PhD thesis (1993) via `api.datacite.org`, or a Chikuhō-ryū
   organisational source. (§5.6)

3. **The legal instrument of the 1871 abolition** — ministry, ordinance, number,
   and its relation to the wider Meiji religious reorganisation. No source I
   opened names it. (§4.1)

4. **The mid-Edo records of a koto/kokyū/shakuhachi trio called 三曲.** Cited by
   JA-SANKYOKU without reference. These documents would materially change the
   picture of how closed the Fuke monopoly was. (§6.2)

5. **When the 当道座 guild was dissolved**, which is the second of the two locks in
   correction 20 and is nowhere dated in my sources. (§6.1)

6. **Current Tozan-ryū membership and the full rank ladder.** I have 准師範 →
   師範 → 大師範 plus 検定委員 and 道場長, from one biography, and an **undated**
   Nipponica figure of 17,000+ members that cannot be reconciled with the ~30,000
   national estimate. A Tozan-ryū primary source (都山流尺八楽会) would settle both.
   (§7.7)

7. **The 2002, 2004, 2012, 2018 and 2025 World Shakuhachi Festivals** are
   single-sourced to `wsf2025.com/wsf-history`, which lists eight events while
   calling them seven; and a **2022** edition appears in search results
   unresolved. (§7.6)

8. **Whether the 1974 designation is officially recorded under 青木鈴慕 or
   青木鈴翁**, and the exact 2018 date of the renaming. The Agency for Cultural
   Affairs' designation register would settle it; I did not reach it. (§7.4)

9. **The twenty-year gap in shakuhachi 人間国宝 designations, 2002–2022.** Not
   commented on by any source. (§7.3)

10. **The 1933 野村景久 claim** — that a murder case triggered a re-spiritualisation
    of the instrument — is ja.wikipedia's, unfootnoted, and absent from English
    accounts. Either a real historiographical point or an editor's inference.
    (§7.1)

11. **Whether 『巣鶴鈴慕』 is really the only Japanese piece on the Voyager Golden
    Record.** Trivially checkable against NASA's track list; I simply did not open
    one. (§7.5)

12. **Internationalisation in numbers** — non-Japanese player counts, foreign
    licence holders, the first non-Japanese *shihan*. Frequently asserted in
    English; I could source none of it. (§7.6)

13. **Why 上田流 and 竹保流 were both founded in 1917.** Probably a coincidence,
    but no source comments, and 島原帆山's biography shows the two Kansai/Chūgoku
    schools in direct competition twenty years later (「上田流の攻勢」). (§5.6)

### 9.3 Notes on sources, for whoever reads this next

`[V]` **What worked.** The ja.wikipedia extracts API was by far the most
productive source — but note the trap that cost me time: **`exlimit` defaults to
1**, so a multi-title request silently returns an extract for only one page and
the rest look like missing articles. Fetch one title per call. **Yahoo! Japan
search** works where the Western engines 403, and was how I found every kotobank
and MEXT URL in this file. **kotobank** serves the printed Japanese encyclopedias
and was decisive for 琴古流 and 都山流, where ja.wikipedia is thin; its value is
that it gives you **several encyclopedias side by side and they disagree** (see
corrections 6 and 7). **MEXT's own site serves the curriculum text as plain HTML**
and settled §7.9's dates outright.

`[V]` **What did not.** kotobank's own search endpoint 404s — find its URLs via
Yahoo. Japanese prefectural and academic PDFs in this field are very often
**scanned page images**, so a successful fetch can still yield nothing readable.
ja.wikipedia has **no article** on 明暗流, 樋口対山, 新日本音楽, 酒井竹保 or 竹保流,
and **都山流 redirects to 尺八** — the coverage is much thinner on the Kansai and
Myōan sides than on the Tokyo/Kinko side, which is itself a bias worth knowing
about. For §5, two kotobank pages I located but did not open may help the
companion file: **明暗流** at `kotobank.jp/word/明暗流-139735` and **樋口対山** at
`kotobank.jp/word/樋口対山-1103228`.

`[V]` **ja.wikipedia contradicts itself**, at least three times within this
material: *November Steps* 1964 vs 1967 (correction 12), the Tokyo Shakuhachi
Trio's founding 1962 vs 1963 (correction 33), and 小澤 vs 小沢 for Ozawa's name.
Treat a single ja.wikipedia article as one source, not as consensus.
