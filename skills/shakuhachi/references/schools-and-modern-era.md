# The Shakuhachi Schools and the Modern Era

Research notes for a reference skill. Not a skill file.

**Scope.** Origins and discontinuities; the Fuke sect and the forgery problem; *suizen*;
1871; the schools; the ensemble tradition; the 20th century; the naming question.
Deliberately **excluded** (other researchers hold these): the instrument as a physical
object, the Dokyoku school in depth, technique and notation as such. Notation appears
only where it distinguishes one school from another.

**Evidence tags.** `[V]` = verified, with the artifact named and the fetch date.
`[U]` = unverified, with what is missing. All translations from Japanese are mine
unless otherwise marked. All fetches 2026-11-13 unless dated otherwise.

**Method note.** Japanese reference works were read as raw HTML/wikitext via `curl`,
not through a summarising fetch tool, so the Japanese quoted below is the source's own
wording. Publisher platforms (JSTOR, Brill, T&F, Project MUSE) 403 on this machine;
where a work appears below with metadata only, that is said explicitly and the claim is
**not** attributed to it.


## 5. The schools (流 *ryū*) — the core reference deliverable

### 5.1 The structural fact that must come first

`[V]` **There were no shakuhachi *ryū* in the Edo period, in the modern sense.**
Nipponica (S1, Tsukitani):

> 虚無僧の修行として尺八が吹かれた江戸時代には、普化宗寺院の尺八指南役（吹合(ふきあわせ)）によって教授され、**流派のような組織は存在しなかった**。京や江戸を中心に、全国に多くの尺八名人や製管師が活躍したと思われるが、**記録に残されているのはわずかである**。…しかし**家元組織としての流が確立するのは普化宗廃止（1871）後**で、1896年（明治29）に都山流、1917年（大正6）に上田流と竹保流の近代流派が誕生。

My translation: "In the Edo period, when the shakuhachi was blown as komusō practice, it
was taught by the shakuhachi instructors (*fukiawase*) of the Fuke temples, and **no
organisation resembling a school existed**. Many shakuhachi masters and pipe-makers are
thought to have been active nationwide, centred on Kyoto and Edo, **but very little is
recorded**. … **However, the establishment of *ryū* as *iemoto* organisations comes after
the abolition of the Fuke sect (1871)**; the modern schools Tozan-ryū were born in 1896
and Ueda-ryū and Chikuho-ryū in 1917."

**Consequences, and they reorganise the whole subject:**
1. "Kinko-ryū, founded in the 18th century" is at best shorthand. What Kurosawa Kinko I
   created was a **repertoire edition and a teaching office**, not an *iemoto* school.
   Nipponica says the *name* 琴古流 was used from about the second generation
   (ja.wikipedia 「黒沢琴古」, S8: 「琴古流という名称を用いたのもこのころだといわれる」, "it is
   **said** that the name Kinko-ryū was used around this time" — of the 2nd, 1747–1811).
2. **The *ryū* system is a Meiji institutional import** — the *iemoto* model from the
   *geidō* world — not a continuation of Fuke organisation.
3. The Edo-period teaching office has a name worth keeping: **尺八指南役 / 吹合 *fukiawase***.

`[V]` **The school-as-organisation taxonomy to use.** Nipponica (S1) splits the field in
two: the **近代流派** (modern schools: Kinko, Tozan, Ueda, Chikuho) and the lines
「普化宗時代の伝統を継承する諸派」 — 根笹派錦風流, 西園流, 明暗対山流 — which
「**近代流派のような組織力はもたない**」, **do not have the organisational power of the
modern schools.** That is the axis to build the reference table on: not
"old school / new school" but **organised school vs. transmitted lineage.**

`[V]` **Player population.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7): 「尺八の吹奏人口についての**本格的な調査はされておらず、正確な人口は不明**である。推定では**3万人程度**といわれている」
— no proper survey has been done and the exact figure is unknown; the estimate is
**around 30,000**. **The cited source is a music-shop blog post (邦星堂和楽器店ブログ,
2015-10-14, by 大橋鯛山), retrieved via a web-archive mirror because the site tripped
Wikipedia's spam filter.** `[U]` — treat 30,000 as a shop-owner's estimate, not a figure.
**I found no membership figure for any individual school from any source.** Any English
text giving school sizes is using numbers I could not locate.

### 5.2 琴古流 Kinko-ryū

`[V]` **Founder and dates.** 初代黒沢琴古 **Kurosawa Kinko I**, 宝永7 (**1710**) –
明和8年4月23日 (**1771-06-05**). Real name **黒沢幸八 Kurosawa Kōhachi**.
(ja.wikipedia 「黒沢琴古」, S8; ja.wikipedia 「尺八」, S7; Nipponica S1 gives 「黒沢琴古（1710―1771）」.)

`[V]` **Social origin — the rōnin story, with its hedge.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7):
「初代は俗名を幸八といい**黒田藩の藩士であったが浪人となり**、江戸へ出て**一月寺、鈴法寺の吹合指南役**となった」
— a retainer of **Kuroda domain (Fukuoka)** who **became a rōnin**, went to Edo, and became
the **fukiawase shinan-yaku (instructor) of Ichigatsu-ji and Reihō-ji** — i.e. of *both*
Kantō head temples. ja.wikipedia 「黒沢琴古」 (S8) hedges the domain:
「**黒田美濃守の家臣であったとされる**」 — "**is said to have been** a retainer of Kuroda,
Mino-no-kami." Note the hedge, and note that the *rōnin* biography matches the
samurai-only entry rule of §2.5 exactly.

`[V]` **THE NUMBER QUESTION: 36 or "thirty-odd"? The sources disagree, and the
disagreement is systematic.**

| Source | Count | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7), citing 上参郷祐康, 『季刊邦楽』 10, p. 12 | **36** | 「尺八曲の整理を行い、**全36曲**の琴古流本曲を制定した」 |
| ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7), 琴古流本曲 section | **36** | 「日本各地の虚無僧寺に伝わる楽曲をまとめ、本曲として制定した**36曲**」 |
| **『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 (S2, Kamisangō)** | **"thirty-odd"** | 「各地の虚無僧寺所伝の尺八曲を収集整理し，編曲して**30曲余り**の本曲を制定した」 |
| **ja.wikipedia 「黒沢琴古」 (S8)** | **"thirty-odd"** | 「曲の収集、整理を行い、琴古流として**30余りの曲**を制定し普化宗尺八の基礎を築いた」 |

**So: 36 in the two places that cite Kamisangō's journal article, and 30余り ("thirty and
some") in Kamisangō's own encyclopedia entry and in ja.wikipedia's biographical article.**
The same scholar is cited on both sides. The likeliest explanation (`[U]`, my inference):
Kinko I established an open-ended set of "thirty-odd" pieces, and **36 is the count of the
canon as it was later fixed** — plausibly by the 2nd or 3rd generation, or in the Meiji
Araki/Kawase redaction. **A reference entry should give "36 in the received Kinko-ryū
canon; Kinko I himself is credited with 'thirty-odd' by the Heibonsha encyclopedia," and
not state 36 as his own number.** Note also Kamisangō's verb: **編曲** — he *arranged*
them, he did not merely collect them.

`[V]` **What Kinko I actually did, in the sources' own terms.** Kamisangō (S2):
「各地の虚無僧寺所伝の尺八曲を**収集整理し，編曲して**」 — collected, organised and arranged
the pieces transmitted at komusō temples around the country. ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7):
「これらの曲の**譜字や習曲順の整理**を行い、**宗教音楽をはなれた琴古流の基礎**を築いた」 —
he regularised the **notation characters (譜字) and the order of study (習曲順)**, laying
the basis of a Kinko-ryū **separated from religious music**.

**That last clause is important and is almost never said in English: the departure from
religious music begins with Kinko I in the 18th century, not with 1871.** A graded
curriculum with a fixed study order is a pedagogical object, not a liturgy.

`[V]` **The Kurosawa succession, and its collapse.** ja.wikipedia 「黒沢琴古」 (S8) — the
name 黒澤琴古 is a **名跡 *myōseki***, a hereditary artistic name:

| Gen. | Dates | Real name | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 1710 – 1771-06-05 | 黒沢幸八 | Kuroda retainer → rōnin; instructor at Ichigatsu-ji & Reihō-ji; set the honkyoku |
| II | 1747 – 1811-07-31 | 黒沢幸右衛門 (later 幸八) | son of I; also instructor at both temples; **opened practice rooms across Edo and spread it to commoners**; the name 「琴古流」 said to date from here |
| III | 1772 – 1816-07-16 | 黒沢雅十郎 (name variously given) | son of II; noted player, composer **and pipe-maker**; wrote 『**琴古手帖**』, "a valuable source for knowing the Kinko-ryū of the time" |
| IV | b. unknown – 1860 | 黒沢音次郎 | **younger brother of III**; 「**技量不足のため、弟子は離散した**」 — "owing to insufficient skill, the pupils dispersed" |
| V | — | — | **did not succeed; the line itself died out** |
| VIII | — | 貞一 | 「宗家として活動復帰している」 — has resumed activity as *sōke* |

ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) puts it bluntly: 「**黒沢琴古の名は3代で途絶えた**が、琴古流はその後、吉田一調、荒木古童らにより隆盛を築いていく」
— **the name died out at the 3rd generation**, but Kinko-ryū went on to flourish under
**吉田一調 Yoshida Itchō** and **荒木古童 Araki Kodō**. (Slight conflict with the 黒沢琴古
article, which has a 4th generation whose pupils dispersed and a 5th who declined. Either
way: **the founding family lost control of the school by the mid-19th century, and the
school that bears its name was carried forward by outsiders.**)

`[V]` **The Araki line, which is the one that matters.** ja.wikipedia 「荒木古童」 (S13) —
also a 名跡:

| Gen. | Name / dates | Note |
|---|---|---|
| I | **豊田古童 Toyoda Kodō** (d. 1850-06-07), real name 豊田勝五郎 | studied under 初代山田如童 and **久松風陽 Hisamatsu Fūyō**; styled 風憬, then 古童; known as 「風憬古童」 |
| II | **荒木古童 Araki Kodō II** = **竹翁 Chikuō**, **1823 – 1908-01-17**, real name 荒木半三郎 | Minakuchi-domain samurai's son; first 一閑流 under 横田五柳, **then became a komusō**, then Toyoda's pupil; inherited the name 1850; **the 中興の祖, reviver, of Kinko-ryū** (per kotobank, cited at S7); improved finger-hole placement and blowing edge; pupils incl. **初代川瀬順輔, 上原六四郎, 三浦琴童** |
| III | **1879-02-01 – 1935-05-02**, real name 荒木真之助 | II's eldest son; with Kawase Junsuke I **the central figure of the shakuhachi world** from late Meiji to early Shōwa; **devoted himself to 三曲合奏** and was acclaimed a master; led 童窓会; pupils incl. 木村士童, 百瀬芳童, **納富寿童**, 石見綱 |
| IV | 1902-08-15 – 1943-07-01, real name 荒木聚, 号 梅旭 | III's fourth son; 東洋音楽学校 graduate; studied gagaku, Western music, sangen, koto; **died young** |
| V | b. 1938-07-30, real name 荒木達也; 二世竹翁 from 2008 | III's grandson; **Keio 1961; lecturer in ethnomusicology at UCLA, University of Washington, Wesleyan 1963–1971**; MA Wesleyan 1971; introduced on NHK as 「尺八を世界に広めた荒木古童」 |
| VI | b. 1970, real name 荒木半三郎 | V's third son; learned the full Kinko old transmission from his father, then **self-taught Celtic folk music**; **Juno Award** 1977 (album *Paperboys*) and a 2005 Dublin award |

**Two findings.** (a) **Araki Kodō III's own emphasis was *sankyoku*, not honkyoku** —
exactly the inversion Kamisangō describes (§4.3). (b) **Kinko-ryū's
internationalisation runs through this family from 1963**, a decade before the usual
1970s date, and via American university ethnomusicology departments. Note the *name*
Araki Kodō has been continuously held since 1850 while the *Kurosawa* name lapsed —
so the living Kinko lineage is the Araki one.

`[V]` **吉田晴風 Yoshida Seifū (1891-06-24 – 1950-06-30)**, ja.wikipedia 「吉田晴風」 (S13),
sourced there to 『新撰 芸能人物事典 明治～平成』 (日外アソシエーツ, 2010) via kotobank:
real name **吉田康次**; former style **吉田竹堂**. Born into a sake-brewing family in
長洲町, Tamana-gun, **Kumamoto**; the business collapsed when he was 7. Captivated at
school in 1898 by a neighbour's shakuhachi; given one in 1899. 1908: entered Kumamoto
Commercial School, bought a real instrument with his sister's help, and **studied Kinko-ryū
under 鳥井虚霧洞**. 1911: graduated top of his class, went to **colonial Korea** as a
soybean trader, then an independent grain merchant. **1914: met 宮城道雄 Miyagi Michio in
京城府 (Keijō/Seoul)** and formed a lifelong friendship; the same year, caught up in the
韓湖農工銀行 scandal, he abandoned business and resolved on a shakuhachi career.
1915: moved to Tokyo, entered the **日本音楽学校**, living poor in a 棟割長屋 in Ushigome with
his mother and his friend 堀潮. **1917: brought Miyagi to Tokyo.** 1918: married the koto
player 熊谷京子 (later **吉田恭子**). **1919: founded the 学生尺八大会** (Student Shakuhachi
Meeting). See §7.1 for 新日本音楽.

**Note what this biography is: a Kumamoto merchant in colonial Korea who became the
shakuhachi's modernist. The decisive meeting happened in Seoul.** English accounts that
introduce Yoshida simply as "a Kinko-ryū player" lose the point.

`[V]` **Kinko-ryū is not one organisation.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7):
「琴古流は**大小いくつもの組織の総体であり琴古流として統一した組織をもつものではない**。現在代表的な会派としては、「**鈴慕会**」「**童門会**」「**竹盟社**」「**竹友社**」「**国際尺八研修館**」などがある。」
— Kinko-ryū is **an aggregate of many organisations large and small and has no unified
Kinko-ryū body**; representative associations today are Reibo-kai, Dōmon-kai, Chikumeisha,
Chikuyūsha and the Kokusai Shakuhachi Kenshūkan (International Shakuhachi Training
Centre). **This is the structural opposite of Tozan-ryū and it explains everything about
how the two schools behave.**

`[V]` **Notation.** Nipponica (S1, Tsukitani): Kinko-ryū uses **ロツレ *ro-tsu-re*** 譜字,
the system that 「18世紀以降琴古流尺八譜として定着し今日に至る」 — settled as the Kinko-ryū
shakuhachi notation from the 18th century onward and continues today; it had earlier been
used briefly for the hitoyogiri/*kotake*. Syllables (ja.wikipedia 「尺八」, S7, from the
fingering table): Kinko ロ・ツ・レ・チ・リ (リ becoming ヒ in the upper octave).
**The Kinko/Tozan difference in the fifth syllable — Kinko リ/ヒ vs Tozan ハ — is the
quickest school diagnostic in a score.** Detail belongs to the notation researcher.

`[V]` **Repertoire markers.** ja.wikipedia (S7): representative Kinko honkyoku are
「一二三鉢返調 *Hifumi hachigaeshi no shirabe*」, 「鹿の遠音 *Shika no tōne*」 and
「巣鶴鈴慕 *Sōkaku reibo*」 (also called 鶴の巣籠). 『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 (S2) notes that
《鹿の遠音》 and 《鶴の巣籠》 are 「例外的な芸術曲」 — **exceptional *art* pieces** within an
otherwise religious corpus.

`[V]` **The Voyager datum, and it is worth getting right.** ja.wikipedia (S7):
「山口五郎によって演奏された本曲「巣鶴鈴慕」は**日本の楽曲としては唯一ボイジャーのゴールデンレコードに収録され**、ボイジャー探査機に搭載されている」
— *Sōkaku reibo* played by **Yamaguchi Gorō** is the **only Japanese piece** on the
**Voyager Golden Record**. Cited there to 竹盟社 (Chikumeisha) and to NASA's
`voyager.jpl.nasa.gov/spacecraft/music.html`. **I did not open the NASA page** — `[V]`
that ja.wikipedia claims it with those citations, `[U]` on the NASA page itself.

### 5.3 都山流 Tozan-ryū

Here the school's own official site is the best source and I read it directly:
**(公財)都山流尺八楽会, `tozanryu.com/about-tozanryu/history` and `/about-tozanryu/shikaku`,
fetched 2026-11-13.** Translations mine. I treat it as authoritative on dates and
institutions and as interested on significance.

`[V]` **Founder.** **初代中尾都山 Nakao Tozan I**, real name **中尾琳三 Nakao Rinzō**, born
**1876-10-05** at 枚方町, Osaka prefecture (now Hirakata city); died **1956-10-10**
(ja.wikipedia 「中尾都山」, S8, infobox).

`[V]` **The family connection to Sōetsu-ryū, which is the part English accounts drop.**
Tozan-ryū's own history page: 「都山の母**み津**は寺内検校の娘で、地歌、箏曲の師匠として、当時大阪で活躍していた尺八家、**近藤宗悦と合奏もしていました**」
— Tozan's mother **Mitsu** was the daughter of 寺内検校 and, as a teacher of *jiuta* and
*sōkyoku*, **played in ensemble with Kondō Sōetsu**, the shakuhachi player then active in
Osaka. 「このような環境で育った都山は**虚無僧修行にも出て**」 — raised in this environment,
Tozan **also went out on komusō training.**

**So the founder of the school usually described as "non-religious and non-Fuke" did
komusō training, and the school's lineage runs through the sankyoku world via his
mother.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) on **近藤宗悦 Kondō Sōetsu (1821–1867)** and
**宗悦流 Sōetsu-ryū (no longer extant)**: Sōetsu is 「箏、三味線との合奏である三曲合奏（外曲とも称す）の**先駆と言われる**」,
**said to be the pioneer** of sankyoku ensemble (also called *gaikyoku*), and Tozan I came
**out of** his school. 『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 (S2) agrees: 宗悦流 was the late-Edo Osaka
school working mainly in *gaikyoku*, now defunct, and Tozan-ryū arose 「その中から」, out
of it.

`[V]` **Founding date — exact, from the school itself.** 「明治29年（**1896**）**2月15日**には大阪市**天満**で尺八の教授を始めました。**この日を都山流の創立記念日としています。**」
— on **15 February 1896** he began teaching shakuhachi at **Tenma, Osaka city**; **the
school takes this day as its founding anniversary.** The same page captions a photo:
「明治29年（1896）に掲げられた招牌「**尺八指南竹琳軒**」」 — the signboard he hung out in
1896, *Shakuhachi shinan Chikurinken*. **1896 is confirmed to the day**, which no secondary
source I saw gives. (Note 竹琳軒 survives as a *daishihan* title — Nomura Hōzan holds
「都山流尺八竹琳軒大師範」, per ja.wikipedia 「野村峰山」, S13.)

`[V]` **The repertoire innovation — and it is a specific, datable claim.**
Tozan-ryū's history page: 「尺八は江戸時代に虚無僧が独占し、演奏する曲も宗教的なものがほとんどでしたが、都山は**明治36年（1903）の「慷月調」をはじめとして従来の古典尺八曲にはない新しい「都山流本曲」を次々と作曲**しました。また**古典尺八本曲はほとんど独奏曲でしたが、都山は合奏曲の本曲という新しい分野を開拓**しました。」
— from 「**慷月調** *Kōgetsuchō*」 of **1903** onward he composed a stream of new
"Tozan-ryū honkyoku" unlike the old classical pieces; and whereas classical honkyoku were
almost all solos, **he opened the new field of *ensemble* honkyoku.**
(The page's photo caption independently dates it: a November 1902 photograph of Tozan **in
komusō dress**, 「「慷月調」作曲の前年」, the year before composing Kōgetsuchō.)
『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 (S2) names the famous Tozan honkyoku: 《**岩清水**》《**慷月調**》《**寒月**》.

`[V]` **His relationship to honkyoku, stated precisely.** 『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 (S2):
of the four modern schools, 「**琴古流は古曲本曲と外曲を曲目とし，他の3流は外曲と各流独自の新しい本曲（一部の古典本曲も含む）を曲目としている**」
— Kinko's repertoire is **classical honkyoku + gaikyoku**; the other three (Tozan, Ueda,
Chikuho) are **gaikyoku + each school's own new honkyoku (with some classical honkyoku
included)**. And: 「都山流などの近代の本曲は**流儀独自の新作で固定化した曲**である」 — the
modern schools' honkyoku are **the school's own new compositions, fixed in form.**

**So the answer to "Tozan's very different relationship to honkyoku" is precise: Tozan-ryū
did not inherit the classical corpus and set about de-emphasising it — it *redefined the
word* by composing a new, authored, notated, fixed-text body of solo and ensemble pieces
and calling them honkyoku.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) is blunt about the school's relation
to the sect: 「都山流は…普化宗とは**直接のつながりを持たない**」 — Tozan-ryū has **no direct
connection** to the Fuke sect.

`[V]` **Why it became the largest — four institutional innovations with dates.**
Tozan-ryū's history page: 「月刊誌「**都山流楽報**」の発行（**大正3年**より）、**試験制度による教授者の育成**（**明治44年**より）、**評議員制度による組織の運営**（**大正6年**より）などの新基軸を打ち出し、**短期間で都山流は尺八界最大と言われる流派になりました**」

| Innovation | From |
|---|---|
| Publishing his works in **his own notation** | **1908** (明治41) |
| **Examination system** for training teachers | **1911** (明治44) |
| Monthly journal 『都山流楽報』 | **1914** (大正3) |
| **Councillor (評議員) system** of governance | **1917** (大正6) |
| Moved base from Osaka (Hirakata) to **Tokyo** | **1922** (大正11) |

「それまで西日本に片寄っていた門人が**これ以降全国に広がり**、また**山田流箏曲家との親交**も深めていきました」 — after the 1922 move his pupils spread nationwide and he
deepened ties with **Yamada-ryū** koto players.
ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) adds the decisive commercial lever: 「**宮城道雄と提携し、宮城作曲の尺八譜の公刊を独占したこと**、評議員制の導入など**中央集権的な組織作り**を行ったことなど都山流は尺八界最大の組織となった」
— it **partnered with Miyagi Michio and monopolised the publication of the shakuhachi
scores to Miyagi's compositions**, and built a **centralised** organisation.
Tozan-ryū's own page dates the partnership: 「**大正13年（1924）**には当時新進の箏演奏家として知られていた**宮城道雄の尺八楽譜を最初に出版**し、翌**14年（1925）**には宮城道雄と共に**西日本縦断の演奏旅行**を行いました」 — first published Miyagi's shakuhachi
scores in **1924** and toured western Japan with him in **1925**.

**This is the mechanism of the duopoly, and it is commercial and administrative, not
musical: an exclusive publishing deal on the most popular new koto repertoire in Japan,
plus a graded examination system, plus a monthly journal, plus a centralised governing
council.** Any English account that explains Tozan-ryū's size by its "accessible,
Westernised style" is explaining the wrong thing.

`[V]` **The licensing and rank system — from the school's official 資格 page**
(`tozanryu.com/about-tozanryu/shikaku`, fetched 2026-11-13). 「都山流の免状・免許状は、**すべて宗家より直接付与されます**」 — all certificates are granted **directly by the *sōke***.

| Stage | Instrument | Granted / gained |
|---|---|---|
| **初伝** *shoden* | 免状 (certificate) | applied for by one's teacher to the *sōke* |
| **中伝** *chūden* | 免状 | **on completion the teacher grants a 竹号 *chikugō*** (bamboo name) |
| **奥伝** *okuden* | 免状 | |
| **皆伝** *kaiden* | 免許状 (licence) | opens eligibility for the *jun-shihan* examination |
| **准師範** *jun-shihan* | 免許状, by **検定試験** (examination) | passing = 「開軒」; **3 full years** required before the next exam |
| **師範** *shihan* | 免許状, by **検定試験** | **「山号」が付与されます** — a ***-san / -zan* name is conferred** |
| **大師範** *daishihan* | promotion | for *shihan* 「特に優れた活動をした方」 |

Also: 「師匠の推薦により指南、准師範、師範の資格を得る**推薦制度**もあります」 — there is also
a **recommendation route** to shinan, jun-shihan and shihan.

**The 山号 rule decodes the names.** *Shihan* receive a name ending in **山**, taken from
中尾都山's own. That is why the Tozan-lineage Living National Treasures are
**島原帆山 Han-zan**, **山本邦山 Hō-zan** and **野村峰山 Hō-zan** (§7.4), and why
**山本邦山** and **野村峰山** both romanise as "Hōzan" with different characters.
(ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) notes that *shihan* also take the 「山」 character — the list there
gives 邦山, 海山.)

`[V]` **The school's own motto, which is the Tozan answer to *suizen*.** Tozan-ryū's
history page: a monument erected **1962 (昭和37)** at 石清水八幡宮 for the founder's seventh
memorial year bears 「**萬象尽蔵一管中**（ばんしょうじんぞういっかんちゅう）」 — "all phenomena
entirely contained within a single pipe," glossed by the school as 「人生の喜怒哀楽や森羅万象全てを余すところなく、一管の尺八で表現する」
and called 「**都山流人が尺八吹奏の究極の目標とする言葉**」, the words Tozan-ryū people take
as the ultimate goal of shakuhachi playing. **Note it is expressive, not soteriological:
*express* everything, not *attain* Buddhahood.** The contrast with 一音成仏 (§3.2) is the
cleanest statement of the two schools' different ideologies I found.
Tozan was awarded the **日本芸術院賞 in 1953** (same page).

`[V]` **And it split.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7): 「**昭和50年前後に分裂**しており、現在は「**都山流尺八楽会**」「**日本尺八連盟**」「**新都山流**」の**3派**が存在する」
— it split **around 1975 (Shōwa 50)** and now exists as three factions: the Tozan-ryū
Shakuhachi Gakkai, the Nihon Shakuhachi Renmei, and the Shin-Tozan-ryū.
**The split is datable through a Living National Treasure.** ja.wikipedia 「島原帆山」 (S13):
Shimabara Hanzan 「先の宗家・美都子とも拗れて袂を分かち翌**1981年、日本尺八連盟を結成、初代会長**に就任した。**家元制度から脱皮し民主的な組織を目指し法人化**、**1986年 社団法人日本尺八連盟**を成立させた」
— having fallen out with the then *sōke*, 美都子, he **founded the Nihon Shakuhachi Renmei
in 1981 and became its first president**, aiming to **shed the *iemoto* system for a
democratic organisation**, and incorporated it as a 社団法人 in **1986**. He was designated
a Living National Treasure in **1982** — i.e. **the year after he seceded**, and the body
that still carries the Tozan name is the 公益財団法人 whose site I quoted above.

### 5.4 明暗 Myōan and 明暗対山派 Taizan-ha

The best source here is **Myōan-ji's own site, myouan-doushukai.org (虚霊山 明暗寺 /
明暗導主会), fetched 2026-11-13**, which carries a continuous *kanshu* succession list.
It is a partisan source on origins (§2.3) and a primary source on its own modern history.

`[V]` **The temple.** 明暗寺 Myōan-ji, 虚霊山, now at 本町十五丁目797, Higashiyama-ku, Kyoto,
housed **within 善慧院 Zenne-in, a subtemple (塔頭) of Tōfuku-ji**; denomination
**普化正宗 Fuke Shōshū**; 本尊 the **虚竹禅師像** (image of Kyochiku Zenji); founded
**建武2 (1335)** by **天外明普** with **虚竹了円** as 開山, at 三条白川 in Kyoto; styled the
**尺八根本道場**, the fundamental training hall of the shakuhachi.
(ja.wikipedia 「明暗寺」, S8.) Relocations, per the temple's own page: in the **元禄 era
(1688–1704)**, under the 14th *kanshu* **淵月了源**, the Kyoto *shoshidai* **板倉重宗**
arranged its move to the foot of **三条白川橋**, and thence to **本池田町** south of the
Daibutsu, on land of the 妙法院.

**A chronology problem to flag.** `[U]` The temple's own page has 板倉重宗 (Itakura
Shigemune, *shoshidai* 1620–1654) arranging a move in the **Genroku era (1688–1704)** —
**three decades after Shigemune's tenure ended.** Either the era or the official is wrong
on the temple's own page. I could not resolve it.

`[V]` **The revival, and the 1888 vs 1890 split.** The 明暗教会 *Myōan Kyōkai* (Myōan
Church/Association) was the vehicle. The sources give two years:

| Source | Year | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| **Myōan-ji's own site** | **1890 (明治23) 7月** | 「1890（明治23）年7月に、「明暗教会」が設立され」 |
| ja.wikipedia 「明暗寺」 (S8) | **1890 (明治23)** | 「明治23年（1890年）、「明暗教会」として復興」 |
| 『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 「普化宗」 (竹貫) | **1888** | 「88年京都東福寺塔頭善慧院に明暗教会が設立され，東京に支部が置かれた」 |
| ja.wikipedia 「虚無僧」 (S8) | **1888 (明治21)** | 「明治21年（1888年）に京都東福寺の塔頭の一つ善慧院を明暗寺として明暗教会が設立されて虚無僧行脚が復活した」 |

**Two to two, with the temple itself on 1890.** `[U]` unresolved. (A plausible
reconciliation, my inference and unverified: founded or applied for in 1888, formally
constituted July 1890.) Heibonsha adds that **a Tokyo branch was established**, and that
the same wave produced **普化教会** at **興国寺** (Yura, Wakayama) and **法灯教会** at
**妙光寺** (Narutaki, Kyoto) — i.e. **three successor "churches," 明暗 / 普化 / 法灯**, which
Nipponica also lists 「明暗、普化、法燈などの教会として存続している」.

`[V]` **What the 明暗教会 actually did — this is the concrete answer to "how the repertoire
was re-legitimised."** Myōan-ji's own page: 「**規約を制定し、虚無僧行化の印鑑（吹簫行化証）を発行して、一般僧侶の托鉢法規に準拠し、虚無僧風儀の再現**を見るに至りました」
— it **drew up regulations, issued a komusō itinerancy seal (吹簫行化証 *suishō gyōke shō*),
conformed to the mendicancy rules for ordinary clergy, and so achieved the restoration of
komusō practice.** **The revival was legal-bureaucratic: it got komusō begging back by
re-filing it under the general clergy regulations.** ja.wikipedia 「虚無僧」 (S8) agrees that
this is when 「虚無僧行脚が復活した」, komusō itinerancy revived.

`[V]` **樋口対山 Higuchi Taizan — dates disputed.**
- ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7): **1856 – 1915**.
- **Myōan-ji's own site: 「1914（大正3）年、対山没後に小林紫山が36世を継承し」** — Kobayashi
  Shizan succeeded as 36th in **1914**, after Taizan's death. **This implies Taizan died in
  1914, not 1915.** `[U]` unresolved — **report both.**

`[V]` **What Taizan did.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7): 「**西園流を学び**明治期に**明暗対山流を興し**、**明暗教会の再興に尽力した**樋口対山」
— he **studied Seien-ryū**, founded **明暗対山流 Myōan Taizan-ryū** in the Meiji period, and
worked for the restoration of the Myōan Kyōkai. Myōan-ji's own page is more specific:
「**樋口対山が教会の訳教師（尺八指南役）となり、散逸した明暗寺所伝曲の蒐集、整備、復興に専心努力して、中興の祖として中絶した法系を承けて35世となりました**」
— Taizan became the association's 訳教師 (**the shakuhachi instructor** — the same office,
尺八指南役, that Kurosawa Kinko I held at Ichigatsu-ji), and **devoted himself to
collecting, putting in order and reviving the dispersed pieces transmitted at Myōan-ji**,
becoming the **中興の祖 (reviver-founder)** and taking up the **interrupted dharma lineage as
the 35th.**

**Note the exact parallel with Kurosawa Kinko I: an instructor appointed by an institution
collects scattered local repertoire and edits it into a canon. The Kinko canon was made
this way in the 18th century; the Myōan canon was made this way in the 1890s.** That is
the single most useful thing in this section: **the "purest" and "oldest" honkyoku line is
in its present form the *latest* editorial product.**

`[V]` **And the canon was then given a fixed number and fixed form by his successor.**
Myōan-ji's own page: 「1914（大正3）年、対山没後に**小林紫山**が36世を継承し、終生明暗本曲の宣揚と指導に当り、**師伝の曲から32曲の譜を整備し、更に明暗古典本曲を定型化し、明暗所伝本曲の吹奏の基礎を確立**しました」
— **小林紫山 Kobayashi Shizan** succeeded as 36th in 1914, spent his life promoting and
teaching Myōan honkyoku, **prepared notations for 32 pieces from his teacher's
transmission, standardised (定型化) the Myōan classical honkyoku, and established the basis
of Myōan honkyoku performance.** He died **1938 (昭和13)**.

**So: the Myōan corpus as performed today is a set of 32 pieces notated and
"standardised" between 1914 and 1938.** `[U]` on the 32 count outside this page — but it
comes from the temple itself and it is the number to cite, with that provenance.

`[V]` **The lineage was interrupted again, for about a decade.** Myōan-ji's own page:
「**1938（昭和13）年に小林紫山が没し、その後の約10年間は法系継承が途絶**してしまいましたが、**1949（昭和24）年に明暗道人並びに各流宗家の推挙により谷北無竹が37世を継承**いたしました」
— after Shizan's death in 1938 the dharma succession **lapsed for about ten years**; in
**1949** **谷北無竹 Tanikita Muchiku** succeeded as 37th **by the recommendation of Myōan
practitioners and the *sōke* of the various schools.**

**Read that: the head of the Myōan line was installed in 1949 by a committee that included
the heads of the other, organised, schools.** The Myōan succession is thus **doubly
reconstructed — in 1890 and again in 1949** — and the second reconstruction was ratified by
the Kinko/Tozan establishment.

`[V]` **Legal re-founding.** ja.wikipedia 「普化宗」 (S8) and 「明暗寺」 (S8): in **1950** the
**宗教法人 普化正宗明暗寺** was re-established as a religious corporation, 「善慧院に間借りする形で」
— **as a tenant of Zenne-in.** (ja.wikipedia 「普化宗」 dates this 1950; so does 「明暗寺」,
giving 昭和25.)

`[V]` **Why this line matters for the jinashi/honkyoku-purist strand.** Three threads
converge:
1. **Repertoire.** Myōan-ji is the transmitting centre for **明暗真法流** and **明暗対山流**
   honkyoku (ja.wikipedia 「尺八」, S7), and the Myōan line's whole business is *koten
   honkyoku* — it has no *gaikyoku* practice and no newly composed honkyoku to speak of.
2. **Notation.** Nipponica (S1): **明暗真法流 used フホウ notation**, the archaic family
   (now surviving in practice only in Chikuho-ryū), while **明暗対山流（明暗対山派）** uses a
   ロツレ system with its own treatment of derived pitches and duration. The Myōan line
   therefore straddles both notational families.
3. **Instrument.** Nipponica (S1): 地無し尺八 (unlacquered bore) 「**古典本曲に適し**」 suits
   classical honkyoku, while 地塗り尺八 suits sankyoku and modern pieces.
   **The repertoire specialisation and the instrument preference are the same choice.**
   ja.wikipedia (S7) is notably unromantic about *jinashi*: 「**正確な音程が得られないため、奏者が音程の補正をする必要がある**。古典的な本曲の吹奏では、**このひとつひとつの尺八のもつ個性もその魅力となっている**」
   — because accurate pitch cannot be obtained the player must correct it; in classical
   honkyoku playing **the individuality of each instrument is itself part of the appeal.**
   **Pitch inaccuracy reframed as individuality — that is the purist aesthetic in the
   Japanese source's own words, and it is a concession, not a boast.**

2025, so beginning around **1995/1996** `[U]`; a 「**愛岐献奏会**」 — 26th in 2025; a
「**普化明暗尺八四国錬成**」 Shikoku training meeting — 19th by 2025; a
「**四国明暗尺八吹禅会**」 — 37th (undated on the page); 「東福寺涅槃会」 participation; a
「**虚竹禅師奉讃会**」; a 「**法燈国師奉賛普化尺八献奏会**」 — 36th; and in 2016 a
「明暗寺四十二世看首『伝法会』」 and a 「虚無僧行列と大仏宝殿献笛」 (komusō procession).
**Note 献奏 — "offering-performance" — is the Myōan line's performance category, not
"concert."**

### 5.5 上田流, 竹保流, 西園流, 村治流, 錦風流 and the rest

`[V]` **上田流 Ueda-ryū.** Founded **1917 (大正6)** by **上田芳憧 Ueda Hōdō** (初世上田芳憧),
in **Osaka**, 「**都山流を除名された**」 — **after being expelled from Tozan-ryū**
(ja.wikipedia 「尺八」, S7); 『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 (S2): 「この都山流から1917年に上田流（流祖は初世上田芳憧）が大阪で**分派独立**し」
— split off from Tozan-ryū. ja.wikipedia (S7): 「上田は、**五線譜。七孔尺八などを導入**し、尺八の近代化につとめた。また**長唄に多く手付け**を行った。**五線譜の採用は途中で断念**したものの、**七孔尺八に関しては上田創案のものが現在でも使用**されている」
— he introduced **Western staff notation** and the **seven-hole shakuhachi**, and added
many *tetsuke* parts to **nagauta**; **he eventually abandoned staff notation**, but
**Ueda's seven-hole design is still in use.** Now styled **上田流尺八道**.
**Ueda is the school that tried Western notation and failed.** That is its historical
significance and it is a good corrective to any story of smooth Westernisation.

`[V]` **竹保流 Chikuho-ryū.** Founded **1917 (大正6)** by **酒井竹保 Sakai Chikuho** (初代),
in **Osaka**, 「**宗悦流の別系統から**」 — from **another branch of Sōetsu-ryū**
(『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』, S2; ja.wikipedia 「尺八」, S7: 「宗悦流の流れを汲み」).
**Notation is its distinguishing feature:** 「譜に**ロツレチではなく、フホウエヤイ**を用いる**フホウ譜**を用いている」
(S7) — it uses **フホウ notation (fu-ho-u-e-ya-i)** rather than ro-tsu-re. Nipponica (S1)
makes this the key survival: フホウ譜, used for the 17th-century hitoyogiri and by the
尾崎真龍 (1820–1888) line, 明暗真法流 and 宗悦流 (the last two 「ともに廃流」, both defunct),
「**宗悦流の末流、竹保流にのみ現行される**」 — **survives in current practice only in
Chikuho-ryū.** **So Chikuho-ryū is the living remnant of the older notational family,
and via Sōetsu-ryū the parallel of Tozan-ryū founded in the same year.**

`[V]` **西園流 Seien-ryū.** Not an *iemoto* school but a transmitted lineage.
『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 (S2): 「**浜松普大寺系統の西園（せいえん）流**」 — of the **Fudai-ji
line at Hamamatsu**. ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) locates it at **Nagoya**:
「浜松の**普大寺の流れをくむ名古屋の西園流**」. Nipponica (S1) lists 西園流 among the
「普化宗時代の伝統を継承する諸派」 that 「近代流派のような組織力はもたない」, and among the
lines with their own ロツレ-based notation conventions.
**Its importance is as Higuchi Taizan's own training**: ja.wikipedia (S7) says Taizan
「**西園流を学び**」 before founding 明暗対山流 (§5.4). The 虚鈴 variant table (§3.4) groups
西園流 with 明暗対山流 — both play a piece titled 《虚鈴》 — which corroborates the
Seien → Taizan transmission. **Seien-ryū is therefore the ancestor of the modern Myōan
honkyoku canon**, which is a link English accounts usually omit.
`[U]` founder, dates and founding year of Seien-ryū: **I found none.**

`[V]` **根笹派錦風流 Nezasa-ha Kinpū-ryū.** 『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 (S2):
「**弘前の津軽藩士のたしなみとなった錦風（きんぷう）流（根笹（ねざさ）派とも）**」 — Kinpū-ryū,
also called Nezasa-ha, which **became an accomplishment of the Tsugaru-domain samurai at
Hirosaki**. ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) lists it first among the surviving Fuke honkyoku
transmissions (「弘前の根笹派錦風流」) and among the schools with distinct notation
conventions, and is the only school ja.wikipedia names under 「その他の古典系流派」
(other classical-line schools) — where the section consists of the single word 「錦風流」.
`[U]` founder, dates, and the *komi-buki* breathing technique that is its signature in
English sources — **I could not source the latter at all**; it belongs to the technique
researcher in any case.
**Note the social fact: a *domain-samurai* accomplishment, in the far north, outside the
Fuke temple system's Kantō/Kansai axis.**

`[V]` **村治流 Muraji-ryū** — a school ja.wikipedia treats seriously and English sources
ignore. ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7): founded **1928** by **村治虚憧 Muraji Kodō**, who had
moved **from Tozan-ryū to Ueda-ryū**. 「村治は尺八と様々なジャンルの音楽との融合を目指し、成果を上げた。さらに**1952年には邦楽旋律の分析を行い、独自の多孔尺八「純律六孔尺八」を開発**した。**筒音と三孔の関係を正確な五度音程とする**この楽器のコンセプトは当時の尺八界では**画期的**であり、**その後の尺八の調律に大きな影響を与えた**。」
— in **1952** he analysed hōgaku melody and developed his own multi-hole instrument, the
「**純律六孔尺八**」 (just-intonation six-hole shakuhachi), whose concept — making the
relation between the **fundamental and the third hole an exact fifth** — was epoch-making
and **greatly influenced subsequent shakuhachi tuning.** His grandson **村治学** took a
doctorate on his work at **聖徳大学 in 2021**.
**If the claim about influence on tuning is right, Muraji-ryū matters to the modern
instrument far more than its obscurity suggests.** `[U]` — ja.wikipedia only.

`[V]` **廃流 — defunct schools worth naming**, since they are ancestors:
**宗悦流 Sōetsu-ryū** (近藤宗悦 1821–1867, Osaka, *gaikyoku* pioneer — parent of both
Tozan-ryū and Chikuho-ryū; 「現存せず」 S7, 「現存しない」 S2);
**明暗真法流 Myōan Shinpō-ryū** (Kyoto Myōan-ji; Nipponica S1 calls it 廃流 as a notation
user, but ja.wikipedia S7 still lists it as a *transmitted* honkyoku line at Myōan-ji —
**a disagreement: Nipponica says the school is defunct, ja.wikipedia says its repertoire is
transmitted.** `[U]`);
**尾崎真龍 line** (Ozaki Shinryū, **1820–1888**, a フホウ-notation lineage, Nipponica S1);
**一閑流 Ikkan-ryū** (宮地一閑 Miyaji Ikkan, a pupil of Kurosawa Kinko I, per ja.wikipedia
「黒沢琴古」 S8; 横田五柳 of this school was Araki Kodō II's first teacher);
**一節切 schools** 宗左流, 宗勲流, 指田流 (§1.3) — a whole dead school system for a dead
instrument.
ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) has an empty 「廃絶した流派」 heading — **the article has a section
for defunct schools and nothing in it.**

`[V]` **Other named Fuke honkyoku transmission centres** (ja.wikipedia 「尺八」, S7):
**博多一朝軒 Itchōken (Hakata)**, **越後明暗寺 Echigo Myōan-ji**, **布袋軒 Hoteiken** and
**松巖軒 Shōganken** (Tōhoku), plus 浜松普大寺 Fudai-ji (Hamamatsu) as Seien-ryū's root.

`[V]` **民謡尺八 — a whole category the school map omits.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) has a
「民謡系尺八」 heading, **empty**, but the 楽曲 section says: 「多くの民謡の伴奏に尺八が使用される。特に**追分、馬子唄**の伴奏には尺八が多用される。**江差追分では、尺八の伴奏が必須**となっている」
— shakuhachi accompanies many folk songs, especially *oiwake* and *magouta*, and
**in 江差追分 *Esashi oiwake* the shakuhachi accompaniment is obligatory.**
Nipponica (S1) places 民謡尺八 in its taxonomy as 民俗音楽 alongside 芸術音楽 and the
popular-music uses. **There is a large population of shakuhachi players who are in no
*ryū* at all.** ja.wikipedia (S7) notes this directly: 「近年では、**特定の流派に所属しない奏者・指導者も数多く存在する**」
— many players and teachers today belong to no particular school.

`[V]` **The Watazumi/Yokoyama line is not in the Japanese school map.** A co-researcher
verified (and I confirm by searching my own copy of the wikitext) that **neither 童曲 nor
道曲 appears anywhere in ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7)**, and that the lineage styles itself
**横山勝也伝承尺八古典本曲** with **no grading or *shihan* system**. The Japanese general
reference treatment **does not name Dokyoku as a 流派.** List it as a **lineage**, not a
school, and refer to the dedicated Dokyoku research. (See §7.4 for the structural
consequence.)

## 6. The ensemble tradition

### 6.1 What 三曲 *sankyoku* actually denotes

`[V]` **Three senses, and the shakuhachi is in none of the original ones.**
ja.wikipedia 「三曲」 (S8, fetched 2026-11-13):
「**三曲**（さんきょく）は、**地歌三味線（三弦）、箏、胡弓**の三種の楽器の総称。またはそれらの音楽である**地歌、箏曲、胡弓楽**の総称。**後に尺八が加わった。**また三曲合奏のこと。」
— *sankyoku* is (a) the collective name for the three instruments **jiuta shamisen
(sangen), koto, and kokyū**; or (b) the collective name for their musics — **jiuta,
sōkyoku, kokyū-gaku**; or (c) 三曲合奏, the ensemble. **"Later the shakuhachi joined."**

`[V]` **The original three belong to a guild the shakuhachi was outside of.**
「もともと地歌三味線、箏、胡弓は江戸時代初期から**当道座**に属する**盲人音楽家**の扱う楽器であり、彼らによってそれぞれの楽器による音楽である、地歌、箏曲、胡弓楽が順次成立した」
— from the early Edo period these three were the instruments of the **blind musicians of
the 当道座 *tōdō-za*** guild, who successively established the three repertoires.
And explicitly: 「**尺八は当道座の楽器ではない**」 — **the shakuhachi is not a tōdō-za
instrument.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) agrees: 「尺八は**当道座外の楽器**である」.

**That is the structural reason the shakuhachi's entry needed permission and took
centuries: it was outside the guild that owned the repertoire, and inside a sect that
forbade ensemble playing. Two separate exclusions.**

`[V]` **Why the word exists — a conjecture, flagged as one.** 「これらの楽器や音楽を、同じく彼らの専門音楽であり、はるかに以前から行なわれて来た「**平曲**」(平家琵琶の音楽) に対して区別するために「三曲」という言葉が使われ始めた**のではないかと思われる**」
— the word may have begun as a way of distinguishing these from **平曲 *heikyoku*** (Heike
biwa), the guild's other and much older specialty. Note 「のではないかと思われる」, a double
hedge — this is the article's own speculation.

`[V]` **And there is a counter-datum that undercuts the tidy story.**
「ただし**江戸時代中期には箏、胡弓、尺八の合奏を「三曲」と呼んだ記録もあって**、単に三種の楽器の組み合わせを漠然と「三曲」と呼んだ可能性もある」
— "**however, there are also mid-Edo records calling the ensemble of koto, kokyū and
shakuhachi 'sankyoku'**," so the word may just have loosely meant any three-instrument
combination. 「「三曲」という言葉が**文献に見えるのはこの頃から**である。江戸時代初期には色々な楽器が合奏されていたようだが、**まだ「三曲」と呼ばれた記録は見つかっていない**」
— the word appears in documents only from about then (mid-Edo); various instruments were
evidently played together in the early Edo period but **no record calling it "sankyoku" has
been found.**

**This is important for the chronology: there is a mid-Edo record of koto + kokyū +
shakuhachi being called *sankyoku*, which means the shakuhachi was in the ensemble at
least a century before 1871, notwithstanding the Fuke prohibition.** It matches Kamisangō
(§3.3): nominally forbidden, widely done.

`[V]` **The modern, extended sense.** ja.wikipedia 「三曲」 (S8): because the four
repertoires converged, 「これら**地歌、箏曲、胡弓楽、尺八楽の四種の音楽を総称して「三曲」と呼び**、他の邦楽種目、例えば長唄や義太夫節、清元、琵琶楽、能楽などと**区別するために使われる**」
— today "sankyoku" names the **four** musics collectively, in contradistinction to nagauta,
gidayū, kiyomoto, biwa and nōgaku. Modern players organise in bodies called
**三曲会 / 三曲連盟 / 三曲協会**. (Araki Kodō III was a director of the **日本三曲協会**, per
ja.wikipedia 「納富寿童」, S13 — Notomi Jūdō became a director in 1931.)

### 6.2 The kokyū question — the shakuhachi did NOT displace it

This is the point in section 6 that most needs correcting, and the Japanese sources go out
of their way to say so.

`[V]` **The timeline of entry.** ja.wikipedia 「三曲」 (S8):
「尺八は当道座外の楽器であるが、**古くから胡弓と交流があり**、また**江戸時代中期頃からしばしば三味線、箏との合奏に加わる**ようになり、**明治以降本格的に参入した**」
— the shakuhachi, though outside the *tōdō-za*, **had long had interchange with the
kokyū**, began **from about mid-Edo** to join shamisen/koto ensembles, and **entered in
earnest from Meiji onward.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) gives the legal version:
「江戸時代にも尺八と箏や三味線の合奏は行われていたと考えられるが、**尺八が普化宗の手から離れ合奏が解禁となったのは普化宗廃止後のことである**」
— ensemble playing is thought to have happened in the Edo period too, but **it was only
after the sect's abolition that the shakuhachi left Fuke hands and ensemble playing was
deregulated.**

`[V]` **Both instrumentations exist, and the sources are emphatic.** ja.wikipedia 「三曲」
(S8): 「従って三曲合奏には、今日、**三味線、箏、胡弓**と**三味線、箏、尺八**という**二通りの編成**があるが、現在は後者が広く行われている状況がある。**しかし胡弓は三曲を構成する楽器の一つであり、現在でも胡弓楽は伝承され、胡弓入り三曲合奏も行われている。**」
— **two instrumentations**; the latter is now widespread, **but the kokyū is one of the
constituent instruments of sankyoku, kokyū music is still transmitted, and sankyoku with
kokyū is still performed.**

ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) is blunter, and the vehemence is itself the finding:
「明治維新以降、胡弓の代わりに尺八が加わることが多くなり、現在では尺八入り三曲合奏の方が一般的に普通に行われる。**一部の著述では胡弓入り三曲合奏が無くなったような記述も見られるが、それは全く根拠のない発言であり、現在でも胡弓入り三曲合奏は少なからず行われている。**」
— "**Some writings describe sankyoku with kokyū as having disappeared, but that statement
is entirely without foundation, and sankyoku with kokyū is still performed in no small
measure today.**"

**So: "the shakuhachi replaced the kokyū in sankyoku" is a claim Japanese Wikipedia
explicitly calls baseless.** The accurate statement is **"became the more common of two
surviving instrumentations, from the Meiji period."** Write it that way.

`[V]` **A nice piece of iconographic evidence against the replacement story.**
ja.wikipedia 「三曲」 (S8): 「三曲合奏は、**錦絵にもしばしば描かれており、その多くは胡弓入り**である」
— sankyoku is often depicted in *nishiki-e*, **and most of those depictions include the
kokyū.** I.e. the visual record of Edo sankyoku is a kokyū record.

`[V]` **A sociological asymmetry that explains a lot.** ja.wikipedia 「三曲」 (S8):
「三味線、箏、胡弓の奏者は**兼任が多い**が、**尺八はそれのみを専門とする演奏家が多い**」
— shamisen, koto and kokyū players **commonly double on all three**; **shakuhachi players
mostly specialise in the shakuhachi alone.** The shakuhachi player is therefore
structurally the *guest* in sankyoku: a specialist joining a circle of generalists.

`[V]` **What the shakuhachi part actually was.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7):
「古典的な三曲合奏では、尺八の手付けは**三絃の手をベタ付けで尺八向けに編曲したもの**であった」
— in classical sankyoku, the shakuhachi *tetsuke* was the **shamisen line transposed
wholesale (*betazuke*)** and arranged for the shakuhachi.
『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 (S2): 外曲 「おおむね**箏または三味線の原旋律と斉奏**であるが、**装飾旋律の付け方は流派により異なり，そこにも流派の特色が現れている**」
— mostly in unison with the koto or shamisen original, **but the way ornamental melody is
added differs by school, and the school's character shows there too.**

**That is the practical answer to "what distinguishes the schools musically" in *gaikyoku*:
not the notes, which are the koto/shamisen line, but the ornamentation convention.**
And Nakao Tozan's distinctive contribution was to write *new* ones: Tozan-ryū's history
page, 「都山は**地歌・箏曲に新たな尺八の手付けを加え、三曲合奏（箏・三絃・尺八）における尺八の地位を高めました**」
— added new shakuhachi *tetsuke* to jiuta and sōkyoku, and **raised the shakuhachi's
standing in sankyoku.**

### 6.3 本曲 / 外曲 / 新曲 as the organiser of a player's life

`[V]` **The sankyoku world's own period vocabulary.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7):
「三曲系の演奏者のあいだでは、**古典的な地歌箏曲を古曲**、**宮城道雄などの明治期から戦前までの楽曲を新曲**、**それ以降の楽曲を現代曲**と呼ぶこともある」
— among sankyoku players: **古曲 *kokyoku*** = the classical jiuta/sōkyoku; **新曲
*shinkyoku*** = Meiji-to-prewar pieces including Miyagi Michio's; **現代曲 *gendaikyoku***
= everything after. (With a footnote warning that this 古曲 is **not** the *kokyoku* of
近世邦楽, which means 一中節・河東節・宮薗節・荻江節.)

`[V]` **Putting the two axes together** — this is the structure the user asked for, and it
is my synthesis of S1, S2, S7 and 「三曲」 (S8), `[V]` on the components:

| Axis | Categories | Decides |
|---|---|---|
| **Honkyoku vs gaikyoku** | 古典本曲 / 近代本曲 / 外曲 | whether you play **alone** or **inside someone else's genre** |
| **Period (sankyoku side)** | 古曲 / 新曲 / 現代曲 | which **repertoire community** and which notation publisher |
| **School type** | organised 流派 vs transmitted 法系 | whether your progress is **examined and licensed** (§5.3) or **conferred** (§5.4) |

**Why it organises a life, concretely.** A Tozan-ryū player's path is: licences from
*shoden* to *kaiden*, then *jun-shihan* and *shihan* examinations, a *chikugō* at *chūden*
and a *-san* name at *shihan* (§5.3); the repertoire is the school's own authored honkyoku
plus *gaikyoku* in the school's notation, bought from the school's publisher — which is
also the exclusive publisher of Miyagi (§5.3). A Myōan Taizan player's path is: a *kanshu*
lineage conferring succession, a corpus of 32 standardised classical pieces, no *gaikyoku*,
no grades, and *kenso* (献奏) offerings rather than concerts (§5.4). **These are not two
styles of playing the same instrument; they are two different careers with different
instruments (地塗り vs 地無し), different notations, different economics and different
words for what a performance is.**

`[V]` **One more axis English sources miss entirely.** Nipponica (S1, Tsukitani) places
the whole school/honkyoku apparatus inside a four-way map: 「内容的には、**古典本曲の大半が普化宗の宗教音楽**に、**古典本曲の一部と外曲や近代本曲が芸術音楽**に入り、その他これらとはまったく別に、**民謡尺八とよばれる民俗音楽**、**ジャズやポピュラーなどの大衆音楽**も含まれる」
— religious music (most koten honkyoku) / art music (some koten honkyoku + gaikyoku +
modern honkyoku) / **folk music (min'yō shakuhachi)** / **popular music (jazz and pop)**.
「このように尺八は、**民俗から芸術まで、古典から現代まで、独奏からさまざまな組合せによる合奏まで**広く用いられている」.

## 7. The 20th century

### 7.1 新日本音楽 shin-nihon-ongaku, 1920s

`[V]` **The movement and its cast.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7), citing kotobank's
「新日本音楽」 entry and 齋藤桂 (2017): 「**1920年代**には、箏曲家の**宮城道雄**と尺八家の**吉田晴風**によって、**洋楽の要素を取り入れた新しい邦楽を目指す新日本音楽運動**が興り、音楽研究家の**田辺尚雄**，**町田佳聲**らも同調し、尺八では**中尾都山、福田蘭童、野村景久**らが参加して**邦楽の近代化**に寄与した」
— in the **1920s** the koto player **宮城道雄 Miyagi Michio** and the shakuhachi player
**吉田晴風 Yoshida Seifū** launched the Shin-Nihon-Ongaku ("New Japanese Music") movement
aiming at a new hōgaku incorporating Western elements; the music researchers
**田辺尚雄 Tanabe Hisao** and **町田佳聲 Machida Kashō** aligned with it; on the shakuhachi
side **中尾都山 Nakao Tozan**, **福田蘭童 Fukuda Randō** and **野村景久 Nomura Kagehisa**
took part.

**Two things to note.** (a) **Nakao Tozan is in the movement**, and Tozan-ryū's own site
claims more than participation: 「正に都山は**その生みの親の一人であり、育ての親**でもあります」
— "Tozan was truly **one of its parents and also the one who raised it**"
(tozanryu.com history page, fetched 2026-11-13). **Two parties claim paternity of
shin-nihon-ongaku: Miyagi+Yoshida per the general reference, and Tozan per Tozan-ryū.**
Report the competing claim. (b) **Fukuda Randō** is in it — and he is Yokoyama Katsuya's
teacher, so the modernisation movement and the later honkyoku-purist Dokyoku line run
through one person. (For the Nomura Kagehisa murder and its ideological consequence, §4.4.)

`[V]` **Yoshida Seifū's institutional footprint.** ja.wikipedia 「吉田晴風」 (S13): founded
the **学生尺八大会** (Student Shakuhachi Meeting) in **1919** — i.e. a year or two before
the movement proper. The article's header notes a recording of 『**春の海**』 *Haru no umi*
with Miyagi (koto), **Victor, 1930**, available in the NDL digital collections
(`dl.ndl.go.jp/info:ndljp/pid/1319027`). There is also a photograph captioned

Michio, 宮城喜代子 and 吉田恭子 — **a 1930 Japan–US exchange radio broadcast.**
**So the shakuhachi's internationalisation has a 1930 radio date, four decades before the
usual story.** `[U]` on the broadcast's details; `[V]` that ja.wikipedia carries the image
with that caption.

`[V]` **一尺六寸管 and *Haru no umi*.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) lists the 一尺六寸管
(fundamental 平調/E4) as the second most used size, 「**春の海などで使用される**」 — used in
*Haru no umi* and the like. A small but genuinely useful reference fact: **the single most
famous piece of modern Japanese music is not on the standard 1.8 pipe.**

**All four screen scores were made in collaboration with Tsuruta on biwa and Yokoyama on

— **Ozawa**, formerly an assistant conductor of the NY Phil and a friend of Takemitsu's,
**played the *Eclipse* recording to Leonard Bernstein**, the Philharmonic's music director;
Bernstein liked it greatly and **asked Takemitsu for a concerto for these Japanese
instruments and orchestra.**

**So the Takemitsu–Tsuruta–Yokoyama partnership was five years old by the 1967 premiere and
it began in cinema.** `[U]` **the year of *Eclipse* itself is NOT given in S9.** The user
gives 1966, which is consistent with the sequence; **I could not verify it** and should not
assert it.

`[V]` **The biwa + shakuhachi pairing had no precedent.** S9: 「また**琵琶と尺八という組み合わせは邦楽の歴史ではそれまで見られないものであった**」
— "the combination of biwa and shakuhachi was something not seen until then in the history
of Japanese music." **It is a modern invention, not a revival.** Do not let it be described
as drawing on a traditional pairing. (And note Tsuruta Kinshi played in the 鶴田流 of
**薩摩琵琶** — ja.wikipedia 「尺八」, S7.)

`[V]` **Form and notation.** S9: 「曲は区切りなく演奏される**11の並列された「段」（ステップ）**からなる」
— **eleven juxtaposed 段 ("steps"), played without break**; 段 is the structural unit of
hōgaku. The solo parts are on staves **with note-heads only and no durations**, leaving 間
*ma* relatively free but with **detailed playing instructions**; the long **cadenza
(10th step)** is in **graphic notation** (図形楽譜). S9 notes the influence of American
experimental music and 偶然性の音楽 (aleatory), combined with hōgaku's traditional sense of
time. Crucially: 「初演者によるカデンツァの演奏法は、**鶴田・横山が作曲者に相談して、図形楽譜の約束事どおりに「翻訳」をして、ある程度演奏を固定化することに決めた**ため、琵琶と尺八の演奏はほぼ固定されている」
— Tsuruta and Yokoyama consulted the composer and **"translated" the graphic notation
according to its own conventions, deciding to fix the performance to a degree**, so the
solo performance is essentially fixed; S9 says comparison of the 1967 RCA (Ozawa/Toronto
Symphony), 1989 Philips (Saito Kinen), 1984 Sony (Iwaki/NHK SO) and 1991 Denon
(Wakasugi/Tokyo Metropolitan SO) recordings confirms it.
**And the transmission was oral:** 「特に間の取り方や図形楽譜によるカデンツァの演奏方法は、**伝統邦楽と同様にほぼ口伝による指導**が行われた」 — taught **almost entirely by
oral transmission (口伝), as in traditional hōgaku.**

**That is a first-rate finding: the most "aleatory" page of the most famous shakuhachi
score is in practice a fixed oral tradition.** And S9 records the policing of it: at a
concert shortly after Takemitsu's death, **ジョン・海山・ネプチューン John Kaizan Neptune**
played a cadenza so far from the convention and so jazz-inflected that opinion split and
the music magazines of the day were largely unfavourable.

`[V]` **Takemitsu's own statement of intent** (S9, from his writings):
「オーケストラに対して、日本の伝統楽器を**いかにも自然にブレンドするというようなことが、作曲家のメチエであってはならない**。むしろ、**琵琶と尺八がさししめす異質の音の領土を、オーケストラに対置することで際立たせるべき**なのである」 and
「**洋楽の音は水平に歩行する。だが、尺八の音は垂直に樹のように起る**」
— "It must not be the composer's *métier* to blend Japanese traditional instruments into
the orchestra as though naturally. Rather, the alien sonic territory that the biwa and
shakuhachi indicate should be thrown into relief by *opposing* it to the orchestra." And:
"**Western sound walks horizontally. But the shakuhachi's sound rises vertically, like a
tree.**" **The composer explicitly rejected the "East–West fusion" reading the piece is
almost always given.** S9 notes the reception split exactly there: some call it 「融合」
(fusion), others 「拮抗」 (antagonism) between hōgaku and Western music, and even between
biwa and shakuhachi.

`[V]` **Other details.** The working title was 『**ウォーター・リング**』 *Water Ring*,
changed after the painter **ジャスパー・ジョーンズ Jasper Johns** told Takemitsu that in
America a "water ring" means the scum round a bathtub; the November premiere supplied the
replacement (S9, sourced there to 武満徹『音、沈黙と測りあえるほどに』, 新潮社, 1971).
『**ノヴェンバー・ステップス第2番**』 was **finished first**, as an exercise after Debussy's
*Prélude à l'après-midi d'un faune* and *Jeux*, was thought retrograde by contemporary
critics, and was **retitled 『グリーン』 *Green*** (S9, sourced to 船山隆『武満徹 響きの海へ』,
音楽之友社, 1998). The audience included **Bernstein, Copland and Penderecki** (per Ozawa);
Bernstein reportedly wept and called it 「**強い生命の音楽**」, "music of strong life."
Tsuruta said she **counted to about the 150th performance and then lost track**
(1991 Philips CD notes, cited in S9).
Related works: 『**秋**』 *Autumn* (biwa, shakuhachi, orchestra, commissioned by Tsuruta)
and 『**旅**』 *Voyage* (three biwa), both **1973**; the NY Phil also commissioned
『**系図**』 *My Way of Life* for its 150th anniversary. Other composers' biwa+shakuhachi
works: 石井眞木 『ポラリテーテン』 (1973), 野平一郎 『河間地』 (2003, Japan–Netherlands 400th
anniversary).

`[V]` **A dating error inside Japanese Wikipedia itself, worth recording because it is
exactly how errors propagate.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7) says:
「**1964年**にニューヨーク・フィルハーモニックと尺八の横山勝也、薩摩琵琶の流れをくむ鶴田流の琵琶奏者鶴田錦史のために作曲された武満徹のノヴェンバー・ステップスは反響を呼んだ」
— **1964**. The dedicated article (S9) says composed and premiered **1967**.
**Do not cite 1964.** The dedicated article is the better source; 1964 is probably a
conflation with 『暗殺』 (1964) or with *Eclipse*. But **record that ja.wikipedia
contradicts itself**, because this is a heavily translated article.

### 7.3 The postwar concert, recording and internationalisation era

`[V]` **The 1960s hōgaku-renewal institutions.** ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (S7):

### 7.4 The Living National Treasure designations — the legal category, precisely

`[V]` **The legal category, which is where English sources go wrong.**
ja.wikipedia 「人間国宝」 (S10, fetched 2026-11-13): 「**人間国宝**」 is a **通称 (colloquial
name)** with **no formal legal equivalent**: 「文化財保護法には「人間国宝」という文言はないが、**重要無形文化財の各個認定の保持者**を指して人間国宝と呼ぶ通称が広く用いられている」.
Legal basis: **文化財保護法第71条第2項** (the Minister of Education designates 重要無形文化財
and certifies its 保持者). The Agency for Cultural Affairs' own site states that, among
重要無形文化財保持者, it is the **各個認定** holders who are 「いわゆる『人間国宝』」.

**The three certification modes, and only the first makes a "Living National Treasure":**

| Mode | What is certified | "Living National Treasure"? |
|---|---|---|
| **各個認定** *kakko nintei* | a **named individual** who 「高度に体現できる」 the designated art | **YES** — this is the one |
| **総合認定** *sōgō nintei* | the **members collectively** of a body (a 〇〇保存会) | **No** — 「人間国宝とは呼ばないのが通例」 |
| **保持団体認定** *hojidantai nintei* | the **organisation itself** (from the 1975 law revision) | **No** |

(Criteria: 「重要無形文化財の指定並びに保持者及び保持団体の認定基準」, 昭和29年文化財保護委員会告示第55号.)
**The user's warning about 記録作成等の措置を講ずべき無形文化財 is exactly right as a category
error to avoid — and note a second, commoner one: 総合認定 members are *also* 重要無形文化財保持者
but are *not* Living National Treasures.** All seven shakuhachi holders below are 各個認定.

`[V]` **Procedure and dates.** S10: designation and certification are deliberated and
resolved by the **文化審議会文化財分科会**, reported (答申) to the Minister, and become
official **upon 官報告示** (gazette notification). **So each holder has two dates: the 答申
and the 告示.** The press releases I opened give the 答申 date.

`[V]` **A hard budget cap, which explains the pattern of designations.** S10: the special
subsidy is **¥2,000,000 per holder per year**; the total is fixed in the national budget
and **since 2002 has been ¥232,000,000**, so the maximum number of living holders is
**116**; 「仮に生存している人間国宝が116名いる場合は、**死亡による欠員が発生しない限り**、どれだけ優れた技量を持つ人物でも新たに人間国宝に認定されることはない」
— if 116 are living, **no new holder can be certified unless a vacancy arises through
death**, however skilled. (S10 also cites a 2025 Yomiuri article for a budgetary ceiling of
**126**.) **Designation is therefore a zero-sum queue, not a judgement of merit alone.**

2014-02-10. Row 6 **文化庁 PDF 93741301_01.pdf (S11)** — opened and read.
Row 7 **文化庁 PDF 94246802_02.pdf (S12)** — opened and read.

`[V]` **The 青木鈴慕 / 青木鈴翁 identity question, RESOLVED.** 青木鈴慕 is a **名跡**, a
hereditary Kinko-ryū artistic name, not a single person — so a co-researcher was right to
flag it. But ja.wikipedia 「青木鈴翁」 (S13) settles it: 青木鈴翁 (1935-10-04 – 2018-08-21,
real name 青木静夫) was the **second-generation 青木鈴慕**, who 「昭和50年（1975年）に**2代目青木鈴慕を襲名**」,
was certified **in 1999** (「平成11年（1999年）に重要無形文化財保持者（人間国宝）認定」), and
**in 2018 passed the name 鈴慕 to his eldest son 彰時 and took the name 鈴翁**.
**So: the Living National Treasure is Aoki Reibo II, certified 1999 under the name 鈴慕 and
known as 鈴翁 for the last months of his life.** The same renaming pattern as
納富寿童 → 寿翁 (#1) and 荒木古童 → 竹翁 (§5.2). His other honours: 文化庁芸術祭優秀賞 1976
and 1980, 芸術祭大賞 1978, モービル音楽賞 and 日本芸術院賞 1990, 松尾芸能賞優秀賞 1999,
紫綬褒章 2002, 旭日小綬章 2016.

`[V]` **The exact legal mechanism, read off the Agency's own documents — and this is a
genuinely subtle point.** The **尺八** designation is **not permanent**: S10 explains that
「保持者の死去により重要無形文化財の**指定ならびに保持者の認定が解除**された」 cases are included
in its list — i.e. **on a holder's death, both the certification *and the designation
itself* are released.** The two PDFs show this working:
- **S11 (2022-07-22)** lists 野村峰山 under heading 「(1) 重要無形文化財の**指定及び**保持者の認定（各個認定）」
  — i.e. the art 「**尺八**」 was **re-designated** at the same time he was certified.
  Yamamoto Hōzan I, the previous holder, had died in **2014**; **the 尺八 designation had
  lapsed in the interval 2014–2022 and there was no shakuhachi Living National Treasure in
  those eight years.**
- **S12 (2025-07-18)** lists 善養寺惠介 under heading 「2. 重要無形文化財の保持者の**追加認定**（各個認定）」
  — an **additional** certification against the designation Nomura already holds.
  **So there are two living shakuhachi Living National Treasures, one designated and one
  added.**

**This is the structure English sources mangle. The pattern is: designation of the art +
certification of a person; both lapse together on death; a second person joins by
*additional* certification. "Shakuhachi has been a Living National Treasure category since
1967" is wrong — it has been designated and un-designated repeatedly.**

`[V]` **Also useful from the Agency's PDFs**: the designated-art name is written plainly as
「**尺八**」 with the reading しゃくはち — **not** by school. **No school name appears in the
designation.** (S11, S12.) And the running totals: after the 2022 答申, **75 重要無形文化財
designations and 110 各個認定 holders** across all fields (S11); the 2021 release gave 78
and 114.

`[V]` **The structural finding, and it is the sharpest thing in section 7.**
Of the seven: **four Kinko (#1, #3, #4, #7 by training) and two Tozan (#2, #5, #6 — three
Tozan actually)**. Precisely: Kinko-lineage #1, #3, #4; Tozan #2, #5, #6; #7 is a
koten-honkyoku specialist trained in Kinko (under Yamaguchi Gorō) with Nezasa-ha Kinpū-ryū
roots. **Every designation has gone to a player from one of the two schools with formal
grading and licensing systems, or to someone trained inside one.**

And: **横山勝也 Yokoyama Katsuya is not on the list.** He co-founded the 尺八三本会 in 1964
with Yamamoto Hōzan I and Aoki Reibo II, **both of whom were later certified**; he premiered
*November Steps*; he founded the World Shakuhachi Festival. His honours were the
**芸術選奨 (1971)**, Art Festival Excellence Award (1972) and Grand Prize (1973), and the
**紫綬褒章 (2002)** — *not* a 各個認定. **If a source says Yokoyama was a Living National
Treasure, it is wrong.** (`[V]` for his absence from the S10 list; `[U]` for the honours
list, which came from a co-researcher's reading of ja.wikipedia 「横山勝也」 and which I did
not re-verify line by line.)

**Read together with §5.5: the lineage that internationalised the instrument and played
its most famous piece sits outside both the Japanese reference work's school map and the
state's recognition system. The asymmetry is not an oversight; the designation apparatus
rewards exactly the thing that lineage refused — a graded, licensed, institutional
transmission.**

### 7.5 The shakuhachi in school music education

`[V]` **The Meiji settlement went the other way.** §4.4: the 音楽取調掛 (1879–87) set out to
create 唱歌 by 「東西両洋の音楽を折衷して」 — blending East and West — and became the
東京音楽学校. The shakuhachi's presence in that apparatus was **a person (Uehara Rokushirō,
in the bureau from 1882, professor from 1891) and a book (『俗楽旋律考』, 1895)**, not a
curriculum.

`[U]` **I could not verify the present state of shakuhachi in Japanese school music
education.** None of the sources I read — Nipponica, Heibonsha, Britannica Japan,
山川日本史小辞典, ja.wikipedia 「尺八」, 「音楽取調掛」 — says anything about the shakuhachi in
the 学習指導要領 (national curriculum standards) or in compulsory schooling. **This is a
real gap.** The things I *can* report that bear on it, all `[V]`:
- **Tertiary, not secondary, is where the institutional foothold is**: 山口五郎 and
  山本邦山 were both professors at **東京芸術大学**; 野村峰山 teaches at **東京藝術大学**
  邦楽科 and **愛知県立芸術大学**; 善養寺惠介 took both his degrees at Geidai under
  Yamaguchi Gorō (S13). **There is a national-university shakuhachi pipeline, at
  conservatory level.**
- **NHK ran a training institution**: 青木鈴翁 is 「**NHK邦楽技能者育成会**卒業」 (S13) —
  a graduate of the NHK Hōgaku Performers' Training Association. **A broadcaster, not a
  school system, trained a generation.**
- Tozan-ryū's own public-facing site has a 「**尺八を始めたい方へ**」 section including
  「**簡易尺八を作ってみよう**」 — "let's try making a simple shakuhachi"
  (tozanryu.com, fetched 2026-11-13). **The school itself is doing the outreach that a
  curriculum would otherwise do**, which is weak but real evidence that the curriculum is
  not doing it.
- The player population is **estimated at ~30,000 with no proper survey** (§5.1) — in a
  country of 120 million. If school music education were feeding the instrument, that
  number would be hard to explain.

**For a reference skill: say the state apparatus of Meiji music education adopted a
blended-with-Western *vocal* model and that the shakuhachi's institutional home became the
art universities and the schools' own licence systems, and flag the compulsory-curriculum
question as unresearched here.**

## 8. The naming question

`[V]` **尺八 means "one shaku eight sun" and nothing else.** All sources agree the name is
the measurement (S1, S2, S3, S7). The complications are three.

**(a) Which shaku, and how long.** §1.2: Nipponica 「中国唐代の律尺による1尺8寸（約**43.7**センチメートル）」
vs 『精選版 日本国語大辞典』 「唐尺から換算すると約**五六**センチメートル」, while the Edo
*fuke* shakuhachi is **1尺8寸 by the 曲尺 *kanejaku* ≈ 54.5 cm** (S1, S7) and the surviving
Shōsōin pipes average **≈ 40 cm** (S3, S7). **Four different lengths for one name.**
ja.wikipedia (S7) states the caution well: 「**時代と国によって「尺」の単位の実際の長さが違うので注意が必要**」.

`[V]` **(b) Readings and variants.** しゃくはち is standard. 『精選版 日本国語大辞典』 (S3)
records **さくはち** as a separate headword (「「さく」は「しゃく」の直音表記」), first attested in
**『源氏物語』(c. 1001–14), 末摘花**: 「大ひちりき・**さくはち**の笛などの、大声を吹きあげつつ」 —
**the earliest occurrence of the word in Japanese literature is a *Genji* passage, and it
is spelled *sakuhachi*.** The same entry also records **しゃくはつ** as a variant reading.
Among players the instrument is called simply **竹 *take*** (S7); in the Fuke tradition
**法竹 *hotchiku*** (『世界大百科事典（旧版）』「宗教音楽」, §3.3); in English
**shakuhachi** or **Bamboo Flute** (S7).

`[V]` **(c) 普化尺八 vs 虚無僧尺八 — and Tsukitani's verdict.** §1.5: 『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』
「普化尺八」 (S5) says the term is purely disambiguating and derives from the instrument's
Edo status as the sect's 法器. Nipponica (S1): 「普化尺八の名が一般化しているが、**歴史的には虚無僧尺八とよぶほうが適切**である」
— *komusō shakuhachi* is historically the more appropriate name. **And 普化尺八 has a second
sense — the *repertoire*** (= 古典本曲, per 『精選版 日本国語大辞典』, S5), so the term is
ambiguous between instrument and music.

`[V]` **Has "shakuhachi" ever meant other instruments? Yes, at least three, and this is
the part that matters most.**
1. **In China it was a class-name for vertical pipes, not a specific instrument.**
   陳暘『楽書』 (S7, quoted via ja.wikipedia): 「或謂之尺八管、或謂之竪籧、或謂之中管。尺八、其長数也。」
   — the *xiaoguan* "is sometimes called the *chiba* pipe, sometimes the *shuqu*, sometimes
   the *chūkan*; *chiba* is its length-number." **A length-label applied to whatever pipe
   measured that much.**
2. **The hitoyogiri was called simply 尺八.** Britannica Japan (S4): 「**当初は単に尺八と称した例も多い**」
   — early on it was often called just "shakuhachi." Both the 1430 『申楽談儀』 and the
   1593–1611 Ryūtatsu-bushi song use 尺八 for what is in fact the hitoyogiri
   (『精選版 日本国語大辞典』, S3; S4). **So pre-17th-century occurrences of 尺八 in Japanese
   texts cannot be read as the modern instrument.** This is the single most practical
   philological point in these notes.
3. **The broad Japanese taxonomy keeps four or five instruments under the name**:
   古代尺八 / 天吹 / 一節切 / 普化尺八 / 多孔尺八 (S1, S2) — see §1.1.

`[V]` **And four non-instrument senses, which are real dictionary senses and matter for
searching.** 『デジタル大辞泉』 and 『精選版 日本国語大辞典』 (S3) both give:
① the instrument; ② **paper or silk for calligraphy/painting one *shaku* eight *sun* wide**;
③ **a bamboo flower vase** — a 一重切 with the node slightly below the centre of a 1尺8寸
tube, 「**利休作に始まる**」, originating with Sen no Rikyū; ④ **the penis; and fellatio**
(「①(ハ)に形状が似ているところから」, "because it resembles the shape of ①(ハ)"), first
attested 『柳多留』4 (**1769**): 「尺八も男もよいがきたない手」; and 『日本国語大辞典』 adds
⑤ **a kind of *hagoita* (battledore)** two to two and a half *shaku* long.
Sense ④ is the one that will contaminate any naive web or image search in Japanese, and it
is **lexicalised in the standard dictionaries with an 18th-century citation**, so it is not
a modern vulgarism. (Note the 1593–1611 *Ryūtatsu-bushi* song in §1.3 is already punning on
it: 「君と**ひとよ**は**寝**も足らぬ」.)

`[V]` **The name is also a planetary feature.** ja.wikipedia 「黒沢琴古」 (S8): a crater on
**Mercury** is named **「クロサワ」 Kurosawa** after Kurosawa Kinko, 「この由来はしばしば映画監督の黒澤明と誤解されるが、**命名当時黒澤明は存命であった**」
— often mistaken for the film director Kurosawa Akira, **but Akira was alive when it was
named** and the IAU names features after dead artists. A pleasing footnote and a checkable
one.

## 9. Corrections to the commonly repeated English account

Numbered, because this is the section that gets read.

1. **"The shakuhachi came from China in the Nara period and has been played ever since."**
   **Wrong twice.** The gagaku shakuhachi **died out by the 10th century** — and a Japanese
   source of **1170** (『今鏡』) already calls it 「吹き絶えたる笛」, a flute whose playing had
   ceased (§1.2). And the modern instrument's descent from it is **not established**:
   『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 says 「普化尺八の起源や伝来は**まったく不明**で、古代尺八との関係も**わからない**」
   (§1.5). There are **at least four instruments** under the name, **three of them extinct**
   (§1.1).

2. **"The hitoyogiri was an earlier stage of the shakuhachi."** **It was a parallel, not an
   ancestor.** Both descend from a **14th-century five-hole prototype** (§1.5). And it was a
   complete, independent musical culture — named schools (宗左流, 宗勲流, 指田流), a named
   master with dates (大森宗勲, 1570–1625), **three printed self-instruction manuals
   (1664, 1669, 1687)**, a solo repertoire (手) and an ensemble repertoire with koto and
   shamisen (乱曲) — that **died, with a dated failed revival** (神谷潤亭, 『糸竹古今集』 1818)
   (§1.3). Its death had a **musicological cause**: it could not play the *miyako-bushi*
   scale that came to dominate Japanese music after the 17th century.

3. **"The Shōsōin holds the ancient shakuhachi."** **Eight of them** — plus a ninth at
   Hōryū-ji, now in the Tokyo National Museum; **nine extant** (Nipponica). Some are
   **jade, stone and ivory**, carved to imitate bamboo. The word is first attested in the
   **東大寺献物帳 of 756** (§1.2). But **no notation survives**, so any description of how
   the gagaku shakuhachi was played or scaled is speculation.

4. **"The Fuke sect was founded in the 13th century by Kakushin."** The Chinese
   transmission is **unverifiable** — 「傍証がないので史実とはみなし難い」, no corroborating
   evidence (§1.5) — and **the sect is documented only from the 17th century**:
   「普化宗と普化尺八の存在が確認できるのは17世紀から」. 「普化宗を公称し、一宗として展開をみるのは**近世に入ってから**」
   (§2.4). Shakuhachi-playing mendicants are attested from the **14th** century
   (『徒然草』, 『三十二番職人歌合』) — **three centuries before the institution.**

5. **The *Kyotaku denki kokujikai*: it is not merely unreliable, its source text does not
   exist.** Britannica Japan: 「『虚鐸伝記』なる漢籍に国文の注解を施した体裁であるが、**原典は存在しない**…**大部分は伝説的で史実とは認めがたい**」
   (§2.2). By **Yamamoto Morihide**, begun **1779**, published **Kyoto 1795**, three
   volumes. And the forgery case is **old**: apparently raised by **栗原広太, 『尺八史考』,
   1918**, and settled by **中塚竹禅, 『琴古流尺八史観』, 1932/1979** — i.e. decades before
   Sanford 1977, the usual English reference point (`[U]` on the attribution).

6. **The 1614 *Keichō jōsho* is a *second*, separate forgery problem — and it is not
   settled.** ja.wikipedia: the original exists **neither in the bakufu archives nor at
   either head temple**, so it is suspected a forgery. But 『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』's Zen
   historian treats it as real and reads it as **bakufu rōnin-control policy**. **Report the
   disagreement; do not resolve it** (§2.2).

7. **"Komusō" does not mean "monk of emptiness" etymologically.** The older form is
   **薦僧 *komosō*, "mat monk," from the rush mat worn at the waist** (『国語大辞典』).
   虚無僧 is a re-etymologising respelling, and the Kusunoki Masakatsu legend is a further
   re-etymologising of that (§2.3). Variants: 菰僧, 梵論字, 暮露, 普化僧.

8. **The 明暗-inscribed alms box is a late-Meiji busker's prop.** ja.wikipedia:
   「**明治末頃から**見受けられるようになったもので、**虚無僧の姿を真似た門付芸人が用いた**」; the
   Edo box bore the **imperial reverse crest** (円に五三の桐) and was **not** inscribed 明暗;
   and 明暗 has no religious meaning — it means "I belong to Myōan-ji" (§2.5). The most
   recognisable "Edo komusō" sign is a 20th-century invention.

9. **"Suizen is the Edo sect's term for its practice."** `[U]`-grade, and probably not.
   吹禅 has **no headword in kotobank and no ja.wikipedia article**; the standard
   encyclopedia mentions it once, as a **back-formation against 座禅**, while the terms the
   encyclopedias actually use for the doctrine are **虚無吹断 / 虚無吹奏 / 「尺八の吹奏による禅」**
   (§3.1). Today 吹禅 is chiefly an institutional term of the **Myōan line**, which was
   reconstructed from 1890. **The concept is Edo (法器, §3.3); the word's history is
   undocumented.** Say that, not "an ancient Zen term."

10. **"The Edo shakuhachi was a Zen instrument that no layperson could play."** That was
    the **sect's claim** — 「許さないと**主張していた**」 — and 「**18世紀中ごろ**になると…**表向き禁止といいつつも一般人の尺八吹奏や箏・三味線との合奏もかなり広まった**」
    (§3.3). There is even a **mid-Edo record of koto + kokyū + shakuhachi being called
    *sankyoku*** (§6.1). The monopoly was de jure and leaky.

11. **"Honkyoku means the classical Zen solo repertoire."** Ambiguous, and 1871 made it so.
    After the abolition, non-religious solo and ensemble pieces were also called honkyoku;
    **古典本曲 *koten honkyoku* is the unambiguous term** (§3.4). And in the Fuke period the
    distinction was unnecessary because everything was honkyoku (Nipponica). **Tozan-ryū's
    "honkyoku" are authored modern compositions, including ensemble pieces** (§5.3).

12. **A honkyoku title is not a work identifier.** Over **150** Fuke pieces survive, 「作者、作曲年代ともに基本的に不詳」;
    「**同名異曲が多く**」 and for some titles 「**10種類以上の旋律の異なるもの**が伝承されている」
    (§3.4). *Reibo*, *Sanya*, *Tsuru no sugomori* name families. Always give the lineage.

13. **"Kinko-ryū was founded in 1768 (or whenever) as a school."** **There were no
    shakuhachi *ryū* in the Edo period**: 「流派のような組織は**存在しなかった**」, and
    「**家元組織としての流が確立するのは普化宗廃止（1871）後**」 (Nipponica, §5.1). Edo teaching
    was by the Fuke temples' **尺八指南役 / 吹合**. The name 琴古流 is 「**いわれる**」 to date
    from the **second** generation. And Kinko-ryū **has no unified organisation** even now —
    it is 「大小いくつもの組織の総体」 (§5.2).

14. **"Kurosawa Kinko I compiled the 36 honkyoku."** **The sources split.**
    **36** in ja.wikipedia (citing Kamisangō's journal article); **30余り, "thirty-odd"** in
    Kamisangō's own 『改訂新版 世界大百科事典』 entry *and* in ja.wikipedia's 黒沢琴古 article
    (§5.2). Give 36 as the received canon, "thirty-odd" as his own number, and note that
    Kamisangō's verb is **編曲** — he **arranged** them. Also: his work explicitly laid the
    basis of a Kinko-ryū 「**宗教音楽をはなれた**」, **separated from religious music** — the
    secularisation starts in the 18th century, not in 1871.

15. **The Kurosawa family lost the school.** The 4th generation's pupils dispersed
    「技量不足のため」, for want of skill; the 5th did not succeed; **the name died out**
    (§5.2). **The living Kinko lineage is the *Araki* one**, continuously held since 1850 —
    and Araki Kodō III's own emphasis was ***sankyoku*, not honkyoku.**

16. **Kinko-ryū deliberately de-emphasised honkyoku after 1871.** Kamisangō:
    「以来、**琴古流では本曲よりも外曲に力を注ぐようになり**」 (§4.3). The school English readers
    associate with the classical solo repertoire shifted its weight to *gaikyoku* as the
    price of survival.

17. **Tozan-ryū is not large because it is accessible or Westernised.** It is large because
    of **four datable institutional moves** — own-notation publishing from **1908**, an
    **examination system from 1911**, a monthly journal from **1914**, a **councillor system
    from 1917** — plus **an exclusive deal to publish Miyagi Michio's shakuhachi scores
    (from 1924)** and a move to Tokyo in **1922** (§5.3). It was founded **15 February
    1896** in Tenma, Osaka, out of the now-extinct **宗悦流**, whose founder **played in
    ensemble with the founder's own mother**. And Nakao Tozan **did komusō training.**

18. **"Tozan-ryū abandoned honkyoku."** It **redefined the word**: a new, authored, fixed,
    notated corpus from 『慷月調』 (**1903**), and **it invented ensemble honkyoku**, classical
    honkyoku having been almost entirely solo (§5.3). It has 「普化宗とは**直接のつながりを持たない**」.
    And Tozan-ryū **split around 1975 into three bodies**; one of them, the 日本尺八連盟, was
    founded **in 1981 by a Living National Treasure who had fallen out with the *sōke*** and
    explicitly set out to 「**家元制度から脱皮し**」, shed the *iemoto* system.

19. **The Myōan/Taizan line is the *most* recently edited, not the least.**
    **Higuchi Taizan** became the Myōan Kyōkai's 尺八指南役 in **1890** and **collected and
    put in order the dispersed Myōan repertoire**; his successor **小林紫山** then
    **notated 32 pieces and 「定型化」 (standardised) the Myōan classical honkyoku between 1914
    and 1938**. The lineage was **suspended twice** (1871–1890 and 1938–1949), and the 1949
    succession was installed **by a committee including the *sōke* of the other schools**
    (§5.4). The "purest" honkyoku line is in its present form a **Meiji-to-Shōwa editorial
    product.** (And the 1890 revival worked by **re-filing komusō begging under the general
    clergy mendicancy regulations** and issuing a licence seal, 吹簫行化証 — a bureaucratic,
    not a spiritual, act.)

20. **"The shakuhachi replaced the kokyū in sankyoku."** ja.wikipedia calls this
    「**全く根拠のない発言**」, **entirely without foundation**: both instrumentations survive,
    kokyū music is still transmitted, and sankyoku with kokyū is still played; **most Edo
    *nishiki-e* of sankyoku show the kokyū** (§6.2). Say "became the more common of two
    surviving instrumentations, from Meiji." Also: the shakuhachi was **outside the
    *tōdō-za***, the blind musicians' guild that owned the other three instruments — two
    separate exclusions, not one.

21. ***November Steps* was *a* 125th-anniversary commission, one of eighteen** — alongside
    Berio's *Sinfonia* and Stockhausen's *Hymnen*. Japanese Wikipedia flags the error
    itself (§7.2). Premiere **9 November 1967**, Philharmonic Hall, Lincoln Center, **Ozawa
    Seiji / New York Philharmonic**, **Tsuruta Kinshi** (biwa) and **Yokoyama Katsuya**
    (shakuhachi), on a **二尺三寸 A pipe**. **Note also that ja.wikipedia's own 尺八 article
    says 1964 — the error is live in a much-translated source.**

22. **The route to *November Steps* ran through film music, and the biwa + shakuhachi
    pairing was unprecedented.** Four screen scores with Tsuruta and Yokoyama (**1962
    『切腹』, 1964 『暗殺』, 1965 『怪談』, 1966 NHK 『源義経』**) → *Eclipse* → **Ozawa played the
    *Eclipse* recording to Bernstein**, who commissioned the concerto (§7.2). And:
    「琵琶と尺八という組み合わせは**邦楽の歴史ではそれまで見られないものであった**」 — **not a revival
    of a traditional duo.** Takemitsu himself rejected the fusion reading:
    「洋楽の音は水平に歩行する。だが、**尺八の音は垂直に樹のように起る**」.

23. **The famous "aleatory" cadenza is in practice a fixed oral tradition.** Tsuruta and
    Yokoyama "translated" the graphic notation with the composer and fixed it; it has been
    transmitted 「**ほぼ口伝**」, almost entirely by oral instruction, and deviation was
    publicly criticised (§7.2).

24. **Internationalisation starts in the 1960s, not the 1970s — and possibly 1930.**
    五世荒木古童 lectured in ethnomusicology at **UCLA, Washington and Wesleyan from 1963**;
    **Yamaguchi Gorō was at Wesleyan in 1967**; and there is a **1930 Japan–US exchange
    radio broadcast** with Yoshida Seifū and Miyagi Michio, plus a **1900 shakuhachi
    recording in Gallica (BnF)** (§7.1, §7.3). The **World Shakuhachi Festival** began
    **1994 in Bisei, Okayama**, initiated by **Yokoyama Katsuya**; then 1998 Boulder,
    2002 Tokyo, 2004 New York, 2008 Sydney (Riley Lee), 2012 Kyoto (Kurahashi Yōdō II),
    2018 London.

25. **The Living National Treasure designations, precisely.** The category is
    **重要無形文化財保持者, 各個認定** under **文化財保護法第71条第2項**; 人間国宝 is a 通称 with no
    legal equivalent; **総合認定 and 保持団体認定 holders are NOT Living National Treasures**.
    **Seven** shakuhachi holders: 納富寿童 (**1967**), 島原帆山 (**1982**), 山口五郎 (**1992**),
    青木鈴慕 II / 鈴翁 (**1999**), 山本邦山 (**2002**), 野村峰山 (**答申 2022-07-22**),
    善養寺惠介 (**答申 2025-07-18**). **And the designation of the art 尺八 lapses when its
    holder dies** — the 2022 document re-**designates** 尺八 while certifying Nomura, which
    means there was **no shakuhachi Living National Treasure between Yamamoto Hōzan's death
    in 2014 and July 2022** (§7.4). The subsidy is **¥2,000,000/yr** against a fixed total,
    capping living holders at **116** — so designation is a **queue**.

26. **Yokoyama Katsuya was never a Living National Treasure**, despite premiering
    *November Steps*, co-founding the 尺八三本会 with two men who were, and founding the
    World Shakuhachi Festival. **Every shakuhachi designation has gone to the Kinko or
    Tozan lines — the two with formal grading and licensing** (§7.4). And the
    Watazumi/Yokoyama line **does not appear in the Japanese reference school map at all**:
    neither 童曲 nor 道曲 occurs anywhere in ja.wikipedia 「尺八」 (§5.5). **"Dokyoku" is a
    lineage label, not a 流派.**

27. **"Shakuhachi" has meant other instruments, and this has philological teeth.**
    In China it was a **length-class name for vertical pipes** (陳暘『楽書』); **the
    hitoyogiri was called simply 尺八** (「当初は単に尺八と称した例も多い」), so the 1430
    『申楽談儀』 and the 1593–1611 Ryūtatsu-bushi occurrences are **hitoyogiri**, not the
    modern instrument; and the earliest Japanese occurrence of the word, in **『源氏物語』
    (c. 1001–14)**, is spelled **さくはち** (§8). Pre-17th-century 尺八 citations cannot be
    read as the modern instrument.

28. **Two lesser corrections.** (a) 普化尺八 is ambiguous between **instrument** and
    **repertoire** (= 古典本曲), and Tsukitani holds that **虚無僧尺八 is historically the more
    appropriate name** for the instrument (§1.5, §8). (b) *Jinashi* is not presented by
    Japanese sources as acoustically superior: 「**正確な音程が得られないため、奏者が音程の補正をする必要がある**」,
    with the individuality of each pipe as the compensating appeal (§5.4). It is a
    concession reframed as an aesthetic.

## 10. Open questions

Ordered by how much a reference entry would suffer from leaving them unresolved.

1. **天吹 tempuku is essentially a blank.** The standard Japanese works list it in the
   taxonomy and the 山川 historical dictionary hedges even its popularity
   (「16世紀後半に薩摩武士の間で流行した**という**」). ja.wikipedia has **no article** (天吹
   redirects to 笛). Nipponica *has* a 天吹 entry (it is a 参照項目 target) and **I could not
   reach it** — kotobank's search is JavaScript-only and my URL guesses failed. **Missing:
   length, hole count, whether today's Kagoshima tradition is continuous or a revival, any
   named transmitter, and the relationship to the 14th-century five-hole prototype.**
   *How to close it:* find the kotobank URL for 天吹 (Nipponica + Heibonsha) by another
   route than its search box.

2. **The date and first attestation of the word 吹禅.** Pivotal: it decides whether
   "blowing Zen" is Edo doctrine or Meiji-revival branding. **The single best lead is the
   「吹禅行化誓願文」 on Myōan-ji's site** — if that document dates from the 1890s revival, the
   term is Meiji; if postwar, it is modern. **A second lead:** the start year of the
   「四国明暗尺八吹禅会」 (37 editions by the page's reckoning).

3. **一音成仏: reading, attestation, and whether any Edo Fuke document contains it.**
   Not in kotobank, not in ja.wikipedia, read two ways (いっとん / いちおん), and glossed with
   a hedge (「思われ」) by a shakuhachi website. The lead is the archived essay
   「普化宗の『一音成仏』と『音声説経』について」 cited by ja.wikipedia 「普化宗」.

4. **Whether the Fuke sect's privileges were really revoked in the Tenpō era (1830–44).**
   『百科事典マイペディア』 says so and **no other source I read mentions it** (§2.5). If true
   it means the sect was politically dismantled a generation before 1871, and the 1871
   story needs rewriting. **The highest-value single check in these notes.**

5. **The 1871 proclamation itself.** I have the year from four sources, the form
   (太政官布告) from two, and the **month (October) from Myōan-ji's own site alone**.
   Missing: the proclamation's **number and text**. *How to close it:* 法令全書 / 太政類典 in
   the NDL Digital Collections or the 国立公文書館 digital archive — the latter already showed
   up in a search snippet holding a 虚鐸伝記 manuscript (請求番号 193–0367), so the archive is
   reachable.

6. **The 36 vs 30余り Kinko honkyoku count, and *when* 36 was fixed.** Both numbers trace
   to Kamisangō. My hypothesis — that 36 is the later-fixed canon — is unverified. *How to
   close it:* 上参郷祐康, 「琴古流の始祖 黒沢琴古」, 『季刊邦楽』 10, p. 12; and 3代黒沢琴古's
   own 『琴古手帖』.

7. **明暗教会: 1888 or 1890?** Two sources each, with the temple itself on **July 1890**
   and the Heibonsha encyclopedia on 1888 (§5.4). Likely a founded-vs-constituted
   distinction, unverified.

8. **樋口対山's death year: 1914 (Myōan-ji's own succession list, implicitly) or 1915
   (ja.wikipedia)?**

9. **The Tang shaku: 43.7 cm or 56 cm?** Nipponica vs 『精選版 日本国語大辞典』, with the
   surviving pipes averaging ~40 cm and agreeing with neither. **No source I read addresses
   the discrepancy**, which is odd given it concerns the instrument's own name (§1.2).
   Related: **旧唐書 or 新唐書 for the 呂才伝?** Three sources, three answers.

10. **The Fuke sect's branch count: 6, 16 (in two incompatible lists), or 18 — and 120 or
    140 temples?** (§2.4.) ja.wikipedia says outright 「**諸説がある**」.

11. **Any membership figure for any school.** I found **none**, for any school, from any
    source. The only population number anywhere is **~30,000 total, from a music-shop
    blog, with no survey behind it** (§5.1). **If a reference table is to have a size
    column, the data does not exist in what I could reach.** *How to close it:* the
    公益財団法人 都山流尺八楽会's own 事業報告 / 公益法人 filings would carry member counts.

12. **The 1795 text's own content.** I have Britannica's verdict on the *Kyotaku denki
    kokujikai* but have **not read it**. The 1981 日本音楽社 edition (山本守秀解註 /
    木幡吹月編 / 徳山隆解説, 216 pp.) exists, and NDL and 国書データベース hold the 1795 edition;
    the 国立公文書館 holds a manuscript. **宋力's article in 『印度學佛教學研究』 2 (168),
    pp. 635–638 is the one piece of modern scholarship directly on it that I located, and I
    could not read it or date it.**

13. **The attribution of the forgery argument** to 栗原広太 (1918) and 中塚竹禅 (1932/1979)
    rests on a blog snippet (§2.2). **Both books are verified to exist**; the attribution is
    not. *How to close it:* 栗原広太, 『尺八史考』 (筑摩書房/竹友社, 1918) and 中塚竹禅,
    『琴古流尺八史観』 — both in NDL, and the 1918 book may be out of copyright and in the NDL
    digital collections.

14. **Whether the 法器 → 楽器 reclassification of the 1870s left a documentary trace.**
    Kamisangō says the Kinko leaders' efforts led to the shakuhachi's survival as an
    instrument being 「**認められ**」, *recognised* — which implies **somebody recognised it,
    in writing**, presumably a Meiji ministry. **I found no document** (§4.3). If one
    exists it is the founding charter of the modern shakuhachi.

15. **Whether the shakuhachi appears in the Japanese compulsory music curriculum**
    (学習指導要領) at all, now or historically. **None of my sources addresses it** (§7.5).

16. **『エクリプス』 *Eclipse*: what year?** The dedicated November Steps article gives the
    sequence but **not the year**. The commonly cited 1966 is consistent and unverified
    here (§7.2).

17. **The 朗庵 coincidence.** The hermitage name Kyochiku takes in Uji in the Myōan legend
    (朗庵) is **the same name as the hitoyogiri's legendary transmitter** in the Heibonsha
    encyclopedia (「ロアン（朗庵などと書かれる）なる外国人僧侶」) (§1.3, §2.3). **I have seen
    nobody connect these.** Coincidence, shared toponym, or a shared stock of legendary
    names? If the second, it would be a real link between the two traditions' myth-making.

18. **Myōan-ji's own chronology problem**: 板倉重宗 (*shoshidai* 1620–54) is credited on the
    temple's page with arranging a relocation in the **元禄 era (1688–1704)** (§5.4).
    One of the two is wrong on the temple's own site.

19. **The Myōan 真法流's status: defunct school (Nipponica) or living transmission
    (ja.wikipedia)?** (§5.5.)

20. **Founders, dates and founding years for 西園流, 根笹派錦風流, 博多一朝軒, 越後明暗寺,
    布袋軒, 松巖軒.** All `[U]`. These are precisely the lines that carry the classical
    honkyoku, and the Japanese general reference works name them without documenting them.

21. **村治流's claim to have influenced modern shakuhachi tuning** via the 1952
    純律六孔尺八 (§5.5). ja.wikipedia only. 村治学's 2021 聖徳大学 doctoral thesis is the lead.

22. **Whether the shakuhachi was ever actually used as a weapon**, as opposed to the komusō
    carrying a sword (which is documented) and being characterised as brawlers in
    『徒然草』 (which is documented). **I found no Japanese source for the club claim** (§2.5).
