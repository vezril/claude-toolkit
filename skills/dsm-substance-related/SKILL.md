# Substance-Related and Addictive Disorders

The largest chapter in the manual. Two structural things to hold:

1. **DSM-5 merged abuse and dependence into a single "use disorder"** with a severity gradient.
   The old two-category split was poorly supported, and "dependence" was routinely confused with
   normal physiological tolerance in patients taking prescribed medication. Now: **mild** (2–3
   criteria), **moderate** (4–5), **severe** (6+), out of eleven.
2. **Each substance class gets a parallel set**: use disorder, intoxication, withdrawal, and
   **substance-induced mental disorders**. Learn the pattern once and it applies throughout.

**DSM-5 also dropped the "recurrent legal problems" criterion** (poor psychometrics,
confounded by policing and jurisdiction) and **added craving**.

## The eleven criteria, in four groups

- **Impaired control** — using more or longer than intended; unsuccessful efforts to cut down;
  craving; a great deal of time spent obtaining, using or recovering.
- **Social impairment** — failure to fulfil major role obligations; continued use despite
  interpersonal problems; giving up important activities.
- **Risky use** — use in physically hazardous situations; continued use despite knowing it
  causes a physical or psychological problem.
- **Pharmacological** — **tolerance** and **withdrawal**.

**The pharmacological criteria do not count when the substance is taken as prescribed.** A
patient on long-term opioid analgesia or a benzodiazepine who has tolerance and withdrawal does
not thereby have a use disorder. This is a frequent and consequential error.

## The ten classes

Alcohol · caffeine · cannabis · hallucinogens (phencyclidine and other) · inhalants · opioids ·
sedatives, hypnotics or anxiolytics · stimulants (amphetamine-type, cocaine, other) · tobacco ·
other or unknown.

Not every class has every diagnosis, and the gaps are informative:

- **Caffeine** has **intoxication and withdrawal but no use disorder** — a deliberate decision;
  caffeine-use disorder sits in Section III as a condition for further study.
- **Tobacco** has use disorder and withdrawal but no intoxication.
- **Inhalants** have no recognised withdrawal syndrome.
- **Hallucinogens** have no withdrawal, but do have **hallucinogen persisting perception
  disorder** — re-experiencing of perceptual symptoms after cessation.

## Specifiers

**In early remission** (3–12 months without criteria, craving excepted) · **in sustained
remission** (12+ months) · **on maintenance therapy** (methadone, buprenorphine, nicotine
replacement) · **in a controlled environment** (incarceration, locked unit). The last two exist
so that remission isn't miscredited or misread — being unable to access a substance is not the
same as remission.

## Substance-induced mental disorders

A parallel diagnosis for psychiatric syndromes caused by a substance: **psychotic, bipolar,
depressive, anxiety, obsessive-compulsive, sleep, sexual dysfunction, delirium, and major or
mild neurocognitive**. Specify onset during intoxication, during withdrawal, or after medication
use.

These are coded in the substance chapter but described in the relevant chapter too, which is
why the induced-disorder rule-out appears everywhere in the manual. **It should be the second
step in every differential** ([[dsm-5]]).

The judgment is whether symptoms **exceed what the substance's known effects would produce**,
or **persist beyond the expected duration** (generally about a month) after cessation. If they
persist, a primary diagnosis becomes the better explanation.

## Gambling disorder

Moved into this chapter in DSM-5 — **the first behavioural addiction in the manual**, on the
strength of shared clinical features, comorbidity, neurobiology and treatment response with
substance use disorders. Specify episodic or persistent, and remission status.

**Internet gaming disorder** was deliberately **not** included; it sits in Section III as a
condition for further study, as does caffeine use disorder. Worth knowing when someone claims
the DSM recognises either.

## Withdrawal states that are medical emergencies

Not a formulation question: **alcohol withdrawal** (seizures, delirium tremens — potentially
fatal), **sedative/hypnotic withdrawal** (the same, and benzodiazepine withdrawal should not be
managed abruptly). Opioid withdrawal is severely unpleasant but rarely life-threatening in
itself; **opioid overdose** is. Stimulant withdrawal carries suicide risk during the crash.

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
