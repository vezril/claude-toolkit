# Feeding and Eating Disorders

DSM-5 **merged** DSM-IV's "feeding and eating disorders of infancy or early childhood" with the
eating disorders into a single lifespan chapter. The diagnoses are now mutually exclusive for a
given episode — one at a time.

## The disorders

- **Pica** — persistent eating of non-nutritive, non-food substances, inappropriate to
  developmental level and not culturally sanctioned. Diagnosable at any age.
- **Rumination disorder** — repeated regurgitation, not due to a gastrointestinal condition.
- **Avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder (ARFID)** — **new in DSM-5**, replacing "feeding
  disorder of infancy or early childhood". Restriction with weight loss, nutritional
  deficiency, dependence on supplements or marked psychosocial interference — **without** body
  image disturbance or fear of weight gain. Driven by lack of interest, sensory
  characteristics, or fear of aversive consequences such as choking. Applies across the
  lifespan, and it gave a home to a large group previously stranded in EDNOS.
- **Anorexia nervosa** — restriction leading to significantly low weight, fear of weight gain or
  behaviour preventing it, and disturbance in self-perceived weight or shape.
  **Specify** restricting type or binge-eating/purging type; severity by **BMI** (mild ≥17,
  moderate 16–16.99, severe 15–15.99, extreme <15); partial or full remission.
  Two DSM-5 changes: **amenorrhea was removed** as a criterion (it excluded males,
  premenarchal and postmenopausal patients, and those on contraceptives), and the rigid weight
  cut-off was softened toward clinical judgment.
- **Bulimia nervosa** — recurrent binge eating plus inappropriate compensatory behaviour, both
  **≥1× per week for 3 months** — DSM-5 lowered this from twice weekly. Severity by number of
  compensatory episodes per week.
- **Binge-eating disorder** — **promoted from a DSM-IV appendix to a full diagnosis in DSM-5.**
  Binge eating ≥1×/week for 3 months **without** compensatory behaviour, with marked distress
  and ≥3 of five features (eating rapidly, until uncomfortably full, when not hungry, alone
  from embarrassment, guilt afterwards).
- **Other specified** — **atypical anorexia nervosa** (all criteria met but weight is in or
  above the normal range) is the one to know; also purging disorder, night eating syndrome,
  and subthreshold bulimia or BED.

## What a binge is

Both bulimia and BED turn on the definition: an amount of food **definitively larger** than
most would eat in a similar period and context, **with a sense of loss of control**. Both
halves are required — large intake without loss of control is overeating, and loss of control
over a small amount is not a binge (though it may be subjective bingeing, captured under other
specified).

## Differentials

- **Anorexia vs ARFID** — body image and fear of weight gain. Present → anorexia; absent →
  ARFID. This is the chapter's most consequential distinction.
- **Anorexia vs atypical anorexia** — weight only. The cognitive and behavioural picture, and
  the medical risk from rapid loss, can be identical, which is why the other-specified category
  gets named explicitly.
- **Medical causes of weight loss or vomiting** — coeliac disease, inflammatory bowel disease,
  hyperthyroidism, malignancy, achalasia, gastroparesis, superior mesenteric artery syndrome.
- **Depression with appetite loss** — restriction without weight/shape concern
  ([[dsm-depressive]]).
- **Body dysmorphic disorder** — appearance concerns other than weight and shape
  ([[dsm-ocd-related]]).
- **Avoidant eating in autism** — sensory-based food selectivity is common; ARFID is diagnosed
  when it reaches the threshold of impairment ([[dsm-neurodevelopmental]]).
- **Rumination vs GORD** vs cyclic vomiting.

**Medical urgency:** anorexia nervosa has among the highest mortality of any psychiatric
diagnosis, from both medical complications and suicide. Refeeding syndrome, bradycardia,
electrolyte derangement and purging-related hypokalaemia are emergencies, not formulation
questions.

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
