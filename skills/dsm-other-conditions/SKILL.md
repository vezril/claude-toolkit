# Other Conditions That May Be a Focus of Clinical Attention

**None of these are mental disorders.** They are conditions and problems that affect diagnosis,
course, prognosis or treatment, coded with **ICD-10-CM Z-codes** so that context appears in the
record rather than being lost.

The longest chapter in the manual by page count, and the most under-used. Formulation without
these codes routinely misses what is actually driving a presentation.

## Suicidal behaviour and nonsuicidal self-injury — new in DSM-5-TR

A significant addition: **codes for current and historical suicidal behaviour, and for current
and historical nonsuicidal self-injury**, recordable **independent of any disorder**.

Why it matters: previously these could only be documented as symptoms of something else, so a
person with suicidal behaviour and no qualifying disorder had nowhere to put it. Now the risk
itself is codable, which supports continuity, risk communication between clinicians, and
epidemiology.

**Nonsuicidal self-injury is distinguished from suicidal behaviour by intent**, not by method or
severity. Both are coded; they are not alternatives.

**If either is live in the conversation, the taxonomy stops mattering.** 988 (call or text),
local emergency services, and staying with the person — see the rails below and in [[dsm-5]].

## Abuse and neglect

Child **physical, sexual, psychological abuse and neglect**, and **spouse or partner violence**
(physical, sexual, psychological) and **neglect**, plus adult abuse by a non-partner. Each in
three forms:

- **Confirmed** · **Suspected** · **Other circumstances related to** (including encounters for
  services, and **personal history (past history) of** the abuse)

The **confirmed/suspected distinction is deliberate** — it lets a clinician record a concern
without asserting a finding, which is what safeguarding practice actually requires.

**Note the direction of coding**: for factitious disorder imposed on another, the *perpetrator*
gets the disorder from [[dsm-somatic-symptom]] and the *victim* gets an abuse code from here.

## Relational problems

Parent-child relational problem (with biological, adopted, foster, or other caregiver
specified), sibling relational problem, relationship distress with spouse or intimate partner,
upbringing away from parents, child affected by parental relationship distress, disruption of
family by separation or divorce, **high expressed emotion level within family** (which predicts
relapse in psychosis and is actionable).

## Social, economic and environmental

- **Educational** — illiteracy, schooling unavailable, failed examinations, underachievement,
  discord with teachers, inadequate teaching.
- **Occupational** — unemployment, threat of job loss, stressful work schedule, discord with
  boss or workmates, **sexual harassment on the job**, military deployment status.
- **Housing** — **sheltered and unsheltered homelessness** (distinguished), inadequate housing,
  discord with neighbours or landlord, residential institution.
- **Economic** — **food insecurity**, lack of safe drinking water, extreme poverty, low income,
  insufficient insurance or welfare support.
- **Social environment** — living alone, acculturation difficulty, social exclusion or
  rejection, **target of (perceived) adverse discrimination or persecution**.
- **Legal** — conviction without imprisonment, incarceration, other legal circumstances.
- **Other** — victim of crime, victim of terrorism or torture, exposure to disaster or war,
  unavailability or inaccessibility of health care facilities or helping agencies, personal
  history of psychological trauma or of military deployment.

**DSM-5-TR expanded and sharpened these**, particularly around discrimination and the social
determinants, consistent with its revisions on racism and inequity throughout
([[dsm-5]]).

## Personal history and other circumstances

Genetic, sex and dietary counselling · wandering associated with a mental disorder ·
**uncomplicated bereavement** · phase of life problem · religious or spiritual problem · adult
and child/adolescent antisocial behaviour · **nonadherence to medical treatment** ·
**malingering** · **age-related cognitive decline** · **borderline intellectual functioning**.

Four of these do a lot of work:

- **Malingering is here, not in the disorders** — intentional production for external
  incentive. Contrast factitious disorder in [[dsm-somatic-symptom]].
- **Uncomplicated bereavement** — grief that is not a disorder, still worth recording. Contrast
  prolonged grief disorder in [[dsm-trauma-stressor]].
- **Age-related cognitive decline** — normal ageing, contrasted with mild neurocognitive
  disorder in [[dsm-neurocognitive]].
- **Borderline intellectual functioning** — below average but not meeting criteria for
  intellectual developmental disorder ([[dsm-neurodevelopmental]]).
- **Adult and child antisocial behaviour** — isolated antisocial acts without a disorder
  ([[dsm-disruptive-impulse-conduct]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
