# Gender Dysphoria

A small chapter carrying an unusual amount of conceptual weight.

## What changed, and why it matters

**DSM-5 replaced "gender identity disorder" with "gender dysphoria".** The shift is substantive,
not cosmetic: the disorder is located in the **distress arising from incongruence**, not in the
identity itself. Having a gender identity different from one's assigned sex is not, in DSM-5
terms, a disorder. The diagnosis requires **clinically significant distress or impairment**.

**DSM-5-TR revised the terminology further** — "individuals of all genders" rather than binary
framing, "experienced gender" in place of "desired gender", "gender-affirming treatment" in
place of older surgical language, and "assigned gender at birth" made consistent.

The chapter also moved **out** of the sexual disorders grouping to stand alone, separating
gender identity from sexual dysfunction and from the paraphilias — both of which it had been
grouped with historically, to real harm.

## Why it is still in the manual

The honest answer, and the one to give: it is retained largely because a diagnosis is what
provides **access to and insurance coverage for gender-affirming care** in many health systems.
That is a stated rationale, and it is also the core of the objection — that pathologising
language is the price of access. Present this as a live tension, because it is one, and the
manual itself acknowledges the debate.

The **post-transition specifier** exists for the same reason: it lets a person who has
transitioned and is no longer distressed retain a diagnosis for continuity of treatment access.

## The criteria sets

**Two separate sets**, developmentally calibrated, both requiring **≥6 months** duration and
clinically significant distress or impairment:

- **In children** — requires a strong desire to be of another gender *plus* several additional
  indicators (preference for cross-gender roles, toys, playmates, activities; rejection of
  typically-assigned ones; dislike of one's sexual anatomy; desire for the primary or secondary
  sex characteristics of the experienced gender). The child set is deliberately more demanding,
  requiring the stated desire, because childhood gender-variant behaviour commonly does not
  persist.
- **In adolescents and adults** — marked incongruence between experienced gender and
  assigned/primary-secondary sex characteristics, with a desire to be rid of or to acquire such
  characteristics, or to be treated as and have the feelings of another gender.

**Specify if:** with a disorder of sex development (a difference of sex development) · and for
adolescents and adults, **post-transition**.

## Differentials

- **Gender nonconformity is not gender dysphoria.** Nonconforming dress, role, interests or
  expression without incongruence-driven distress is not a diagnosis. This is the chapter's
  main boundary and the one most often crossed.
- **Body dysmorphic disorder** concerns a perceived defect in appearance, not sex
  characteristics or gender ([[dsm-ocd-related]]).
- **Transvestic disorder** is sexual arousal from cross-dressing — a paraphilic disorder, a
  different phenomenon, and conflating the two is a long-standing error
  ([[dsm-paraphilic]]).
- **Psychotic presentations** involving delusions about one's body or sex
  ([[dsm-schizophrenia-spectrum]]).
- **Autism co-occurs at elevated rates** with gender dysphoria; both can hold, and neither
  explains away the other ([[dsm-neurodevelopmental]]).
- **Comorbid depression and anxiety are common and are frequently consequences** of minority
  stress, discrimination and lack of access to care rather than of the dysphoria itself. The
  relevant social codes are in [[dsm-other-conditions]]; DSM-5-TR strengthened its text on
  discrimination as a contributor.

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
