# 2022 Indian Super Chief — recalls & known issues

**The only fully-authoritative layer in this skill.** Recall text below is quoted from the
US **NHTSA** recalls API (`api.nhtsa.gov`, public-domain government data, retrieved
**2026-09**). Both campaigns explicitly list **Super Chief** in their affected-vehicle list,
so both apply to a 2022 Super Chief. **This is not a substitute for a VIN check** — whether
*your* specific bike was already remedied depends on its VIN and service history.

## ⚠️ First thing to do: check your VIN against open recalls

Recalls are per-VIN. A campaign can list your model yet already be closed on your unit (or vice-versa).

- **NHTSA VIN lookup:** <https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls> — enter your 17-digit VIN for the current open/closed status. Authoritative for US bikes.
- **In Canada:** Transport Canada's recall database (<https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca>) tracks the Canadian campaigns, which may carry different numbers.
- **Or call Indian:** customer service **1-877-204-3697** with your VIN.
- Recall remedies are **free** — Indian pays, regardless of ownership history or how old the bike is.

## Recall 1 — Fuel pump may fail while riding

| | |
|---|---|
| **NHTSA campaign** | **22V837000** |
| **Indian's number** | **I-22-06** |
| **Reported** | 2022-11-11 (owner letters mailed 2022-12-06) |
| **Component** | Fuel system, gasoline: delivery: fuel pump |

**Summary (verbatim):** *"Indian Motorcycle Company (Indian) is recalling certain 2021-2022 Scout \[…\], Chief, Chief Bobber, Chief Bobber Dark Horse, Chief Dark Horse, \[…\] Super Chief, and Super Chief Limited motorcycles. The fuel pump may fail while riding."*

**Consequence (verbatim):** *"Fuel pump failure can cause an engine stall, increasing the risk of a crash."*

**Remedy (verbatim):** *"Dealers will replace the fuel pump and the fuel pump seal, free of charge."*

> **This is the important one.** A fuel pump that quits mid-ride = a dead engine in traffic. If your VIN shows this recall **open**, treat it as a priority, not a someday. Symptoms that could point at a failing pump (sputtering under load, stalling, hard hot-starts, whining from the tank) → get it in, and mention I-22-06.

## Recall 2 — Kickstand may not retract

| | |
|---|---|
| **NHTSA campaign** | **22V957000** |
| **Indian's number** | **I-23-01** |
| **Reported** | 2022-12-29 (owner letters mailed 2023-02-17) |
| **Component** | Structure: motorcycle: kickstand/center stand |

**Summary (verbatim):** *"Indian Motorcycle Company (Indian) is recalling certain 2022 Chief, Chief Bobber, Chief Bobber Darkhorse, Chief Darkhorse, Super Chief, and Super Chief Limited motorcycles. The kickstand may not properly retract upon ground contact when left extended while riding. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 123, 'Motorcycle Controls and Displays.'"*

**Consequence (verbatim):** *"A kickstand that does not properly retract can increase the risk of a crash."*

**Remedy (verbatim):** *"Dealers will replace the kickstand, free of charge."*

> A down kickstand catching pavement in a left turn is a classic low-side cause. Free fix — worth confirming it's closed on your VIN even if you always check the stand.

## Owner-reported complaints (NHTSA) — lower signal, still worth knowing

Only **2** complaints are on file for the 2022 Super Chief (it's a low-production model, so absence of complaints ≠ absence of issues). Both, paraphrased from the NHTSA filings:

- **Service brakes** — an owner reported an *"abnormal shushing sound"* around 15 mph and a perceived **increased stopping distance** ("~5 feet delay") when applying the brake pedal. One report, uninvestigated. If *you* feel any change in brake bite or travel → treat it as a **stop-riding, get-inspected** item (see the troubleshooting reference); brakes are not a wait-and-see system.
- **Fuel filler neck** — an owner objected that a **metal bar inside the filler neck blocks full insertion of a fuel nozzle**. This is a *design characteristic* of the Chief tank (an anti-siphon / splash feature), **not a defect** — many owners find pump nozzles auto-click-off early and learn to fuel slowly at a shallow angle. Annoying, not dangerous.

## Community-reported themes (NOT authoritative — experience, not manufacturer data)

These come up repeatedly in owner communities for the 2022 Chief / Thunderstroke platform. Treat as *things to be aware of and check*, not confirmed defects, and verify anything specific against a dealer:

- **Heat off the rear cylinder** in stop-and-go — inherent to a big air/oil-cooled V-twin; the engine's rear-cylinder behavior at idle is designed to manage it. Not a fault unless accompanied by a temperature warning.
- **Keyless fob** dropouts / low fob battery causing no-start or intermittent recognition — usually a **CR2032-class fob battery** swap (verify the type), and there is a documented backup start procedure using the fob against the console.
- **Parasitic battery drain** if the bike sits for weeks — common on modern keyless/CAN-bus bikes; a **battery tender** is the usual answer.
- **Belt debris damage** — an exposed final-drive belt can be cut by a lodged stone; worth a periodic visual.

None of these override the manual or a dealer diagnosis. Where a symptom overlaps a **recall** (stalling → fuel pump), the recall takes priority.
