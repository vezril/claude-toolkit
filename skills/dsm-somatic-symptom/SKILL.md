# Somatic Symptom and Related Disorders

**Substantially rebuilt in DSM-5.** The old somatoform chapter defined its disorders by
**medically unexplained symptoms** — a definition that required proving a negative, correlated
poorly with distress, and implicitly told patients their suffering was not real. DSM-5 replaced
it with **positive criteria**: excessive thoughts, feelings and behaviours about somatic
symptoms.

Consequence worth stating plainly: **a diagnosis here does not require the symptoms to be
unexplained**, and it can coexist with a confirmed medical illness. Somatoform pain disorder,
hypochondriasis, somatisation disorder and undifferentiated somatoform disorder were all
retired.

## The disorders

- **Somatic symptom disorder** — ≥1 distressing somatic symptom, plus **disproportionate and
  persistent thoughts, high health anxiety, or excessive time and energy devoted** to symptoms.
  ≥6 months. **Specify if with predominant pain** (the old pain disorder) and **if persistent**;
  severity mild/moderate/severe by how many of the three psychological features are present.
- **Illness anxiety disorder** — preoccupation with having or acquiring a serious illness, with
  **somatic symptoms absent or mild**. ≥6 months. **Specify care-seeking or care-avoidant
  type.** DSM-5 split the old hypochondriasis: most such patients have significant somatic
  symptoms and land in somatic symptom disorder; this category is for the rest.
- **Conversion disorder (functional neurological symptom disorder)** — altered voluntary motor
  or sensory function. **Criterion B requires positive clinical findings of incompatibility**
  between the presentation and recognised neurological disease (Hoover's sign, tremor
  entrainment, tubular visual fields) — a major DSM-5 shift from diagnosis-by-exclusion to
  diagnosis-by-positive-sign. **DSM-5 also dropped the requirement** to identify a psychological
  stressor and to rule out feigning as preconditions. Specify symptom type and acute/persistent,
  with or without psychological stressor.
- **Psychological factors affecting other medical conditions** — a real medical condition whose
  course or treatment is adversely affected by psychological or behavioural factors. The
  counterpart to the above.
- **Factitious disorder** — falsification of signs or symptoms, or induction of injury or
  disease, **with deception present but no obvious external reward**. Imposed on self or
  **imposed on another** (formerly Munchausen by proxy — note the diagnosis goes to the
  perpetrator, not the victim, and the victim gets an abuse code from
  [[dsm-other-conditions]]).

## The malingering boundary

**Malingering is not a mental disorder** — it sits in [[dsm-other-conditions]] as a Z-code. The
distinction from factitious disorder is **external incentive**: malingering is for a tangible
gain (money, drugs, avoiding duty or prosecution), factitious disorder is not. Both involve
deception, which separates both from somatic symptom and conversion disorders, where the person
is **not feigning**.

Saying this clearly matters, because the chapter is where patients most often get accused of
faking. The manual's position is that conversion symptoms are genuine.

## Differentials

- **Rule medical in, not out.** Conversion and somatic symptom disorders are not what remains
  after a negative workup, and misdiagnosed neurological disease is the chapter's classic error.
  Positive signs for conversion; disproportionate cognitions for somatic symptom disorder.
- **Illness anxiety vs OCD** — if contamination or illness obsessions come with compulsions and
  extend beyond health, consider [[dsm-ocd-related]].
- **Illness anxiety vs panic disorder** — panic is acute attacks interpreted catastrophically in
  the moment; illness anxiety is sustained preoccupation ([[dsm-anxiety]]).
- **Somatic symptoms in depression and anxiety** — extremely common; diagnose separately only
  when the somatic preoccupation is itself the problem ([[dsm-depressive]]).
- **Body dysmorphic disorder** concerns appearance, not illness ([[dsm-ocd-related]]).
- **Culture** shapes somatic idioms of distress heavily — somatic presentation of depression is
  normative in many settings and is not a somatic symptom disorder ([[dsm-5]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
