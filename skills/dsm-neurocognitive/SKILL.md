# Neurocognitive Disorders

The chapter where psychiatry meets neurology most directly, and the one most dependent on
investigation rather than interview.

**DSM-5 replaced "dementia" and "amnestic disorder"** with a two-level framework:

- **Major neurocognitive disorder** — significant cognitive decline **interfering with
  independence** in everyday activities.
- **Mild neurocognitive disorder** — **new in DSM-5.** Modest decline that **does not** interfere
  with independence, though greater effort or compensatory strategies may be needed. This
  captures the prodromal stage (roughly, mild cognitive impairment) and was added because
  early identification is where intervention has the most leverage.

"Dementia" is retained as acceptable usage, particularly in settings where it is established,
but the manual's term is major neurocognitive disorder — partly to extend the construct to
younger people, where "dementia" misleads (TBI, HIV).

## The six cognitive domains

**Complex attention · executive function · learning and memory · language · perceptual-motor ·
social cognition.** Decline is specified by which domains are affected, which is what makes the
etiological subtyping possible — memory-first points one way, executive and social cognition
first points another.

Both severity levels require **objective evidence**, preferably standardised neuropsychological
testing, not just report.

## Delirium

The chapter's acute condition and a **medical emergency**:

- Disturbance of attention and awareness, developing over **hours to days**, **fluctuating**
  during the day, with additional cognitive disturbance, not better explained by another NCD and
  not occurring in a severely reduced level of arousal such as coma.
- Specify substance intoxication, substance withdrawal, medication-induced, due to another
  medical condition, or multiple etiologies; acute or persistent; **hyperactive, hypoactive or
  mixed** level of activity.
- **Hypoactive delirium is the one that gets missed** — quiet, withdrawn, mistaken for
  depression — and it carries the worse prognosis.
- The acute, fluctuating course is the discriminator from the neurocognitive disorders.
  **Delirium superimposed on dementia is extremely common** and both are coded.

## Etiological subtypes

Each takes **probable** or **possible** depending on the strength of evidence — genetic
confirmation or a clear clinical picture gives probable:

- **Alzheimer's disease** — insidious, gradual, memory and learning early.
- **Frontotemporal** — behavioural variant (disinhibition, apathy, loss of social cognition,
  often mistaken for a psychiatric or personality change) or language variant. Younger onset.
- **Lewy body disease** — fluctuating cognition, recurrent well-formed visual hallucinations,
  spontaneous parkinsonism, and **REM sleep behavior disorder** as a core feature. **Severe
  neuroleptic sensitivity** — giving an antipsychotic can be catastrophic, which makes this
  subtype's recognition urgent ([[dsm-sleep-wake]]).
- **Vascular** — stepwise decline or temporal relation to cerebrovascular events, executive
  dysfunction prominent.
- **Traumatic brain injury** — onset immediately after injury or after recovery of
  consciousness, persisting past the acute post-injury period.
- **Substance/medication-induced** — including alcohol (Wernicke-Korsakoff), inhalants, sedatives
  ([[dsm-substance-related]]).
- **HIV infection · prion disease · Parkinson's disease · Huntington's disease · another medical
  condition · multiple etiologies · unspecified.**

**Specify with or without behavioural disturbance** — psychotic symptoms, mood disturbance,
agitation, apathy. This drives a great deal of management and is routinely omitted.

## Differentials

- **Delirium first**, in anyone with acute or fluctuating cognitive change. It is reversible and
  it kills.
- **Depression vs neurocognitive disorder** — the old "pseudodementia". Depression gives poor
  effort on testing, "don't know" answers, subjective complaints exceeding objective findings,
  and a clearer onset; it also genuinely co-occurs with and may precede dementia
  ([[dsm-depressive]]).
- **Normal ageing** — mild NCD requires objective decline beyond age norms;
  **age-related cognitive decline** is a Z-code in [[dsm-other-conditions]].
- **Reversible causes, and look for them every time** — B12 and folate deficiency,
  hypothyroidism, normal pressure hydrocephalus, subdural haematoma, neurosyphilis, HIV, sleep
  apnea, polypharmacy and anticholinergic burden.
- **Frontotemporal behavioural variant vs a primary psychiatric disorder** — late-onset
  personality change, disinhibition or apathy in middle age deserves imaging, not a personality
  disorder diagnosis ([[dsm-personality]]).
- **Intellectual developmental disorder** is a failure to attain, not a decline from, a prior
  level ([[dsm-neurodevelopmental]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
