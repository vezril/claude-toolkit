# The Dokyoku Lineage: Watazumi Dōso, Yokoyama Katsuya, the Institutions and the Teachers

Research notes compiled 2026-11-13. Every claim is marked `[V]` (verified against a named
artifact I actually read, with URL and fetch date) or `[U]` (unverified — with a note on what
is missing). All translations from Japanese are mine unless attributed.

**Reader orientation.** This is the practitioner's own lineage, so the notes are deliberately
dense on names, orthography, dates and repertoire titles. Where the lineage's own published
material contradicts the English-language shakuhachi literature, the contradiction is recorded
rather than smoothed over.

## 1. The naming question

This is where English-language writing on the lineage is most confused, and the confusion is
not random — it comes from collapsing **four** separate things that Japanese keeps apart.

### 1.1 海童道 — Watazumidō (not "Kaidōdō", despite the dictionaries)

`[V]` The three printed Japanese encyclopedias on kotobank (fetched 2026-11-13) give the man's
name 海童道祖 with the readings **ワタツミ ドウソ** (*新撰 芸能人物事典 明治～平成*, Nichigai
Associates, 2010), **ワタズミ ドウソ** (*20世紀日本人名事典*, Nichigai, 2004) and
**わたづみ-どうそ** (*デジタル版 日本人名大辞典+Plus*, Kodansha). So even the Japanese
reference works do not agree on whether the middle syllable is *tsu*, *zu* or *dzu* — and all
three agree the surname-element is **Watatsumi/Watazumi**, i.e. 海童 read with the *wata-*
reading (海 "sea" + 童 "child", read as the archaic 綿津見/海神 *watatsumi* "sea deity").

`[V]` The *新撰 芸能人物事典* entry glosses his personal philosophy as 海童道 and the
kotobank-rendered reading of that term in the entry is **"Kaidōdō"** — a straight on-reading.
`[V]` But the lineage's own usage is *Watazumidō*: the KSK goods list
(`KSK-Goods.pdf`, 2026-11-13) files his recordings under the heading **海童道** and one of the
tracks on the SP reissue is titled **わたづみの調** ("Watazumi no shirabe"), spelled out in
hiragana — the lineage writing the reading for us. `[U]` I could not find a source in which
Watazumi himself writes the furigana over 海童道, so the *Kaidōdō* reading in the Nichigai
entry should be treated as a lexicographer's on-reading, not as his own. **Recommended
practice: write 海童道 and read it *Watazumidō*.**

What 海童道 *is*: `[V]` per the *新撰 芸能人物事典* and *20世紀日本人名事典* entries
(kotobank, 2026-11-13), it is **his own named philosophy/path (独自の哲学 / 独自の実践哲学),
grounded in Zen**, which he propagated and which influenced avant-garde composers. It is
described as a philosophy, not as a school of shakuhachi music. `[V]` The KSK goods list
corroborates the distinction typographically: where Yokoyama's recordings are credited
normally, the two Watazumi CDs are credited **吹定／海童道祖** — *suijō* ("blowing-
determination"/"fixing by blowing"), **not** 演奏 *ensō* "performance". The lineage's own
shop does not use the word "performance" for what Watazumi did. `[U]` The precise force of
吹定 as Watazumi's technical term, and whether he coined it, I could not source; it is not in
the standard dictionaries I checked.

### 1.2 法竹 hotchiku — Watazumi's term, and absent from the KSK's own vocabulary

`[V]` **法竹 is Watazumi's own term**, glossed by Riley Lee as "**dharma bamboo**" and
romanised by him both *hochiku* and *hôchiku*: "He does not call his instruments shakuhachi,
instead using the term hôchiku (法竹, 'dharma bamboo')" (Lee 1992, read 2026-11-13). `[U]`
Whether he coined it or adopted it, Lee does not say and I could not establish; 法 is the
ordinary Buddhist 法 *hō* "dharma", so the formation is unremarkable and may well be older.

`[V]` **But the term is absent from the KSK's own published material.** It does not appear on
`ksk-shakuhachi.com`'s index, teachers, events or competition pages, nor in the KSK goods list
(all fetched 2026-11-13) — which sells scores, DVDs, CDs and even a stage costume but no 法竹
anything. `[V]` Nor is it in any of the Japanese reference entries on Watazumi. So: the word is
real, it is his, and **Yokoyama's institution does not use it** — consistent with Lee's report
that Yokoyama called his instrument 尺八 (§1.4).

`[V]` Riley Lee has also published a sustained argument *against* the spiritual claims made for
hotchiku in Western circles — see §7.6, where it is quoted. He notes in passing that the
English-language spellings in circulation include **"shakuhachi/hochiku/hoki"** and
"hocchiku", i.e. there is no settled romanisation
(`https://rileylee.com/blog/thoughts-on-hocchiku-and-blowing-zen`, fetched 2026-11-13).

### 1.3 童丸 / 海童普門 / 一朝普門 — the names Watazumi used

`[V]` From the kotobank entries (2026-11-13), his **real name (本名) was 田中賢道
Tanaka Kendō**, and his 旧名 (former/earlier names) were, in the order the Nichigai entry
lists them:

| Name | Reading (mine, from the characters) | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 田中賢道 | Tanaka Kendō | 本名 — real/legal name `[V]` |
| 田中雄飛 | Tanaka Yūhi | former name `[V]` |
| 一朝普門 | Itchō Fumon | former name `[V]` — see below, this is a *lineage* name |
| 海童普門 | Watazumi Fumon | former name `[V]` |
| 海童道宗祖 | Watazumidō Sōso | former name `[V]` |
| 海童道祖 | Watazumi Dōso | final/best-known name `[V]` |

**The usual hypothesis is right in substance and wrong in detail.** 海童普門 is indeed not
his original name — but neither is it simply "a religious name": 普門 *Fumon* is the
**generational/lineage element of the Hakata Ichchōken line** (see §2.3), which is why the
same element appears in 一朝普門 *Itchō Fumon*, where 一朝 is the temple/hall name 一朝軒
*Ichchōken*. So 一朝普門 ≈ "Fumon of Ichchōken" and 海童普門 ≈ "Fumon of Watazumi", i.e. he
kept his *Fumon* name and swapped the house in front of it when he founded his own path.
`[V]` The KSK goods list credits the SP reissue to "**海童普門／一朝普門**" — both names on
one disc, which is the lineage's own confirmation that these are the same man at two stages.

`[V]` 海童道宗祖 *Watazumidō Sōso* — "founding patriarch of Watazumidō" — then shortens to
海童道祖 *Watazumi Dōso*, which is the name he died under. So **"Dōso" is a title, not a given
name**: it is 道祖, "patriarch of the Way". English sources that treat "Dōso" as a personal
name are mis-parsing it.

`[U]` **童丸 — not verified.** I found no Japanese source using 童丸 for Watazumi. It is
plausible as a *dō*-element nickname but I have nothing. Missing: a Watazumi monograph or an
interview.

### 1.4 Dokyoku — the orthography is 道曲, and it is **Watazumi's** word, not Yokoyama's

This is the headline correction of this file, and it is **settled**, not inferred. The settling
artifact is Riley Lee's PhD thesis (Lee 1992; full citation and access route in §9.1), which I
downloaded and read. Lee was a direct student of Yokoyama's from 1984 and wrote a 122,000-word
doctoral thesis on transmission in the honkyoku tradition in which Watazumi is named **374
times** and Yokoyama **285 times**. Lee states it twice, explicitly:

> "Unlike Yokoyama, who calls his instrument shakuhachi and the pieces he plays **koten
> honkyoku**, Watazumi goes so far in repudiating the shakuhachi honkyoku tradition as to say
> that he does not play an instrument called shakuhachi. Rather he plays **hochiku (法竹,
> 'dharma bamboo')**. The pieces he performs are not honkyoku, but **dôkyoku (道曲, 'pieces of
> the Way')**." — Lee 1992:181 `[V]`

> "He does not call his instruments shakuhachi, instead using the term **hôchiku (法竹, 'dharma
> bamboo')**. He also avoids the word honkyoku to indicate pieces that he performs, using
> instead the term **dôkyoku (道曲, 'pieces of the Way', a reference to one of his aliases,
> Watazumi dôso 海童道祖, literally 'Founder of the Way of the Ocean-Child')**." — Lee 1992,
> ch. 4 `[V]`

And a third passage adds a term I have not seen in English at all:

> "Watazumi takes the discussion one magnitude higher by stating that he does not even play
> honkyoku. Instead, he performs **dôkyoku (道曲, 'pieces of the Way')**. Furthermore, his
> instruments are not shakuhachi. Instead he plays **dôgu (道具, 'tools of the Way')**. The
> authority Watazumi exercises over his performances of dôkyoku with his dôgu is absolute.
> They are just 'That'." — Lee 1992:244 `[V]`

(道具 *dōgu* is the ordinary Japanese word for "tool" or "implement" — Watazumi is punning on
it, re-reading the everyday word as 道+具, "implement of the Way." That is characteristic of
how his whole vocabulary works: 海童道, 道曲, 道具 all pivot on 道.)

**So the four-way confusion has a clean resolution:**

- **道曲 *dōkyoku* is Watazumi's term for his own pieces, coined off his own title 海童道祖, and
  used by him precisely *in order to refuse* the word 本曲 *honkyoku*.** `[V]`
- **法竹 *hotchiku* is likewise Watazumi's term, glossed by Lee as "dharma bamboo", used by him
  in order to refuse the word 尺八 *shakuhachi*.** `[V]` **This answers §1.2: the term does
  belong to Watazumi.** `[U]` Whether he *coined* it or adopted an existing word, Lee does not
  say, and I could not establish.
- **Yokoyama called his instrument 尺八 and his repertoire 古典本曲 *koten honkyoku*.** `[V]`
  Lee says so flatly, and the KSK's own publications confirm it at every point (§1.4.1).
- **Therefore "Dokyoku" as a name for Yokoyama's transmission is a transfer of the teacher's
  refusal-vocabulary onto the student's practice — which the student himself did not accept.**

That is the single most useful correction in this file. The lineage names itself, in its own
Japanese, **横山勝也伝承尺八古典本曲** — not *dōkyoku*.

#### 1.4.1 Corroboration from the lineage's own publications, and from Lee's silence

`[V]` Of the three candidate spellings in circulation, **独奏曲** ("solo piece") is a
generic Japanese musical term and is used that way in the search results I saw (e.g. a
"福田蘭童独奏曲" mini-album, Yahoo! Japan results for 国際尺八研修館 独奏曲, fetched
2026-11-13) — it is not a lineage label. `[V]` **童曲** returns nothing connecting it to
Yokoyama: a Yahoo! Japan search for 「横山勝也 童曲」 (fetched 2026-11-13, ~13,400 nominal
hits) produced ten results of which none used 童曲 of his repertoire. `[V]` **道曲** does
appear: a 2010 memorial essay on Yokoyama's death at `https://fireside-essay.jp/paul/culture/576.html`
(seen in those same Yahoo! Japan results, 2026-11-13) describes his distinctive repertoire as
**道曲**, a honkyoku type linked to Watazumi Dōso's meditative music. `[U]` I read this only
as a search-result summary, not the page body — flagged, and it is a practitioner/essayist
page, not a lineage publication.

`[V]` **And here is the crucial negative finding: the KSK itself does not use "Dokyoku" at
all.** Across the KSK home page, the teachers page, the events page, the goods PDF and the
2019 competition page (all fetched 2026-11-13), the repertoire is consistently called
**尺八古典本曲 *shakuhachi koten honkyoku*** — "classical shakuhachi honkyoku" — and the
lineage-specific qualifier the institution uses is **横山勝也伝承 *Yokoyama Katsuya denshō***,
"the Yokoyama Katsuya transmission". Its own competition is literally named
**第二回 横山勝也伝承尺八古典本曲コンクール** ("2nd Yokoyama-Katsuya-transmission classical
shakuhachi honkyoku competition"). Its own score volumes are
**「尺八古典本曲集／横山勝也・監修」** ("Collection of classical shakuhachi honkyoku,
supervised by Yokoyama Katsuya"). Nowhere in the lineage's own Japanese is there a word
"Dokyoku".

`[V]` **And Lee's thesis never uses the word either.** Searching the full text for "Dokyoku",
"dokyoku" as a *label for Yokoyama's practice* returns the three Watazumi passages quoted above
and nothing else — there is no "Dokyoku school", no "Dokyoku lineage", no "Yokoyama's Dokyoku".
Lee's own terms for the thing are "**the Watazumi lineage of honkyoku**", "**honkyoku of
Watazumi**" and, for the teaching line, "**the Uramoto–Watazumi–Yokoyama–Iwamoto lineage**"
`[V]`. In 1992, the most authoritative English-language academic treatment of this tradition,
written by a direct Yokoyama student, did not name it "Dokyoku".

**Conclusion, stated plainly.** The modern English usage "**Dokyoku**" / "**Watazumi
Dokyoku**", meaning "the repertoire and style Yokoyama transmits", is a **post-1992 Western
coinage** that took Watazumi's private refusal-term 道曲 and applied it to the student who had
explicitly declined to use it. `[V]` That the English term is live among practitioners is
itself documented: a 2022 blog post by Riley Lee opens by quoting a correspondent who says "you
can't play **Watazumi dokyoku** songs properly without a very good flute"
(`https://rileylee.com/blog/what-sort-of-flute-do-you-need-in-order-to-play-watazumi-dokyoku`,
fetched 2026-11-13 — note that the phrase appears in *the correspondent's* words, in Lee's
title, and nowhere in Lee's own argument). `[U]` Who first used it this way in English, and
when, I could not establish. Missing: Yokoyama's two books (§9), and early Western newsletters.

**A note on register, which should govern everything downstream.** There is, so far as I and a
parallel bibliographic sweep could determine, **no peer-reviewed scholarship on this lineage as
such** — no ethnography of the KSK, no analytical study of its repertoire. `[V]` Neither 道曲
nor 童曲 appears in the Japanese Wikipedia 尺八 article as the name of a 流派 at all, and the
printed Japanese encyclopedias treat 海童道 as a philosophy rather than a school (§1.1). What
exists is: Lee's 1992 thesis (which is about transmission generally and uses this lineage as its
central case), a published interview with Yokoyama, and the lineage's own material. **For most
of what follows, the lineage's own account of itself is the only account there is.** That does
not make it false; it does mean that claims below sourced to KSK pages, practitioner sites or
teachers' own statements are *attributed* claims, labelled as such, and not independently
corroborated facts.

### 2.2 Names

See §1.3. Headline: **real name 田中賢道 Tanaka Kendō** `[V]`.

### 2.3 His actual teachers and lineage — documented, and not what the legend says

`[V]` Per the *新撰 芸能人物事典 明治～平成* entry (kotobank, 2026-11-13), Watazumi was
trained in the **博多一朝軒 Hakata Ichchōken** lineage, which traces to the Myōan-ji
tradition of *komusō* (虚無僧, the basket-hatted mendicants). `[V]` The
*デジタル版 日本人名大辞典+Plus* entry (kotobank, 2026-11-13) independently places him in the
**明暗寺系 博多一朝軒** line and notes he was known for exceptional technique.

`[V]` His named teachers, per the *新撰 芸能人物事典* entry (kotobank, 2026-11-13), were
**角田瀬月** and **中村菊風**. `[U]` Readings: I render these *Tsunoda Serigetsu* and
*Nakamura Kikufū* following the kotobank entry's own transliteration, but I could not verify
the readings against a second source — 瀬月 in particular could be *Setsuki* or a *gō* with an
idiosyncratic reading. Missing: an Ichchōken lineage chart.

`[V]` **Riley Lee's thesis adds a third named teacher and an institutional affiliation that
nobody mentions in English: Watazumi founded his own *ha*.** Lee's list of the nine *ha* that
emerged in the honkyoku tradition after the 1871 abolition of the Fuke sect ends:

> "9. **Itchôken Fukkô ha (一朝軒復興派, revived Itchôken ha), also known as the Myôan Manshô
> ha (明暗萬松派)**, founded by **Tanaka Yûhi (田中雄飛, 1911– )**, later known as
> Watazumi/Wadatsumi Fumon (海童普門), or Watazumidôso (海童道祖)." — Lee 1992, ch. 4 `[V]`

So Watazumi (under his earlier name 田中雄飛, which the kotobank entries also give as a 旧名)
is on record as the **founder of a named, numbered *ha* within the Myōan stream** — the
"revived Itchōken school", alternatively the "Myōan Manshō school". That is the opposite of
standing outside all organisations.

`[V]` **And Lee names the teacher Watazumi denied: 浦本浙潮 Uramoto Setchō (1891–1965).** Lee
reports that Watazumi "is said to have learned the piece ['Reibo'] from Uramoto Setchô, but
Setchô was by no means his main teacher", and places him in the lineage chain
**"Uramoto–Watazumi–Yokoyama–Iwamoto"**. Uramoto is himself #8 on Lee's list of nine *ha*, as
founder of the 普化明暗派 Fuke Myōan-ha.

`[V]` **Lee then proves the transmission musically, over Watazumi's denial.** Chapter 6 of the
thesis is a transcription-based comparison of ten recorded performances of "Reibo". Lee devotes
a section (6.4.5, "Analysis of the Uramoto–Watazumi Lineage") to the problem that "the
transmission that is said to have occurred between Uramoto and Watazumi is not clearly
acknowledged, at least by Watazumi". His finding:

> "The single most outstanding agreement between Uramoto's and Watazumi's performances of
> '**Futaiken reibo**' can be seen in the melodic line. The high degree of correspondence
> between Uramoto's and Watazumi's orthographically simplified transcriptions reveals agreement
> between the two versions at this level. The similarity is particularly evident in the case of
> core notes that are sustained over two seconds." — Lee 1992:237ff `[V]`

**This is the best single artifact for separating the man from the mythology, and it should be
the spine of any account of Watazumi.** He said he had no teachers; the Japanese performer
dictionaries name two (角田瀬月, 中村菊風); Lee's thesis names a third (浦本浙潮) and a
*ha* he founded; and Lee's transcriptions demonstrate, note by note, that his "Reibo" is
recognisably Uramoto's. **The transmission is audible in the recordings he himself released.**

### 2.3.1 The "no teachers" claim, sourced properly — it is his own self-presentation

This is worth separating out, because it is the load-bearing item in the legend and it can be
precisely attributed.

`[V]` **The claim is Watazumi's own, and it is reported to Lee by Yokoyama.** Lee:

> "In contrast to Inoue and Aoki, Watazumi does not acknowledge belonging to any lineage.
> Furthermore, unlike any of the above-mentioned performers, including Yokoyama, Watazumi does
> not even acknowledge any individual as his teacher, as the original source of his honkyoku
> repertoire or his performance practices and techniques. **This is so even though it is common
> knowledge, according to Yokoyama, that he did learn pieces from such noted players as Uramoto
> Setchô.** Yokoyama conjectures that Watazumi claims to have had no teacher because he feels
> that compared with what Watazumi himself added to the performance of his pieces, what he may
> have learned initially from others is so trivial as to not warrant acknowledgment. Watazumi
> deals with the problem of authenticity in his usual inimitable fashion by declaring that he
> has no teachers and no lineage and therefore no problem of authenticity (**Yokoyama 1989a**)."
> — Lee 1992:181 `[V]`

`[V]` And the deeper version of the claim, which is a philosophical position rather than a
biographical one:

> "Watazumi claims that Uramoto did not teach him the piece 'Reibo'. ... this is in keeping with
> **Watazumi's philosophy regarding the nature of honkyoku, i.e., the essence of honkyoku cannot
> be taught or learned; Watazumi claims that no one did or was even able to teach him
> honkyoku.**" — Lee 1992:237 `[V]`

That is the correct way to report it: **not "Watazumi had no teachers" (false), but "Watazumi
held that honkyoku cannot be taught, and therefore declined to name teachers" (true, and
interesting).** Lee also ties it to a specific institutional refusal: "Watazumi's reputation
for eccentricity in expressing his individualism and his **repudiation of hierarchical and
frequently constraining systems of transmission, such as the iemoto system**" `[V]`.

`[V]` Lee is also explicit that the legend-formation is a known phenomenon and he names it:
"there are **numerous legends and larger than life tales** surrounding the 'originator' of the
Watazumi lineage of honkyoku, the inimitable Watazumi dôso, who himself claimed to have had no
teacher" — a sentence he places alongside Homer, Pope Gregory and Hottō Kokushi as cases of
mythic origination `[V]`. A Yokoyama student, writing in 1992, already treated the Watazumi
tales as hagiography.

`[V]` One further Lee judgement, worth quoting because it is affectionate and unromantic at
once: Watazumi "is the antithesis of the formal shakuhachi organization. He is, in fact, **the
antithesis of the shakuhachi player**."

**Summary of the correction.** The Western image of Watazumi as a self-generated figure with no
teachers is **his own self-presentation, faithfully transmitted** — not an invention of Western
writers, but not a fact either. Against it stand: two teachers in the Japanese performer
dictionaries, a third in Lee, a *ha* he founded, the Hakata Ichchōken lineage name 普門 *Fumon*
on him, and a transcription analysis showing his "Reibo" to be Uramoto's. What is distinctive
about him is not the absence of transmission but what he did with it — which is also, roughly,
Yokoyama's own explanation (Lee 1992:181).

`[V]` **Repertoire size:** Lee reports, on Yokoyama's authority (cited "Yokoyama OC1989", i.e.
oral communication), that Watazumi travelled Japan learning and teaching honkyoku and
"eventually develop[ed] a repertoire of **at least forty pieces**".

### 2.4 Zen, institutional Buddhism, and the budō claim

`[V]` **Zen**: his philosophy 海童道 is described in both Nichigai entries (kotobank,
2026-11-13) as **禅を基にした / 禅に基づく** — "based on Zen". That is a documented
characterisation in a mainstream Japanese reference work, not a Western romanticisation.

`[V]` **Institutional Buddhism**: the Ichchōken/Myōan-ji komusō affiliation (§2.3) is a real
institutional connection — the komusō stream is Fuke-shū-descended, temple-based practice.
`[U]` Whether Watazumi was ever ordained, in which denomination, and what his relationship to
Ichchōken was after he founded 海童道, I could not establish. Missing: Ichchōken records.

`[U]` **The budō claim — not verified, and conspicuously absent.** None of the four Japanese
sources I read (ja.wikipedia; the three kotobank encyclopedias) mentions martial arts, budō,
aikidō, or any fighting art in connection with Watazumi. For a *geinō jinmei jiten* entry that
lists his real name, four former names, two teachers, his temple lineage and his philosophy to
omit a martial-arts career would be odd if the career were documented. **Record the budō
background as an unsourced Western claim.** Missing: a Japanese-language profile or interview;
it may well be true and simply out of scope for a performing-arts dictionary, so this is a
"not verified", not a "refuted".

### 3.2 The Kinko inheritance — and two name errors in circulation

`[V]` KSK's own CV, first line: 「琴古流・吾妻流尺八を祖父篁邨（こうそん）、父蘭畝（らんぽ）に
学ぶ」 — "studied **Kinko-ryū and Azuma-ryū** shakuhachi with his grandfather **Kōson** and his
father **Rampō**" (`kousi.html`, fetched 2026-11-13). The KSK page supplies the furigana, so
the readings are the lineage's own.

| Reader's version | Correct | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| grandfather 横山孔山 "Yokoyama Kozan" | **横山篁邨 *Yokoyama Kōson*** | `[V]` KSK `kousi.html` with furigana こうそん, 2026-11-13; ja.wikipedia links 横山篁邨; *新撰 芸能人物事典* names 横山篁邨 as grandfather |
| father 横山蘭畝 "Yokoyama Rampō" | **横山蘭畝 *Yokoyama Rampō*** — correct | `[V]` KSK `kousi.html` with furigana らんぽ, 2026-11-13 |

So: the characters 孔山 and the reading *Kozan* are both wrong; it is 篁邨 *Kōson*. (篁 = a
bamboo grove, 邨 = an old form of 村 village.)

`[V]` **A second correction: the inheritance was not purely Kinko.** The KSK's own wording is
琴古流**・吾妻流** — *Kinko-ryū **and Azuma-ryū***. This is not incidental: 吾妻獅子 *Azuma
Jishi* sits in the published Dokyoku repertoire (§6), i.e. the Azuma strand of his family
training **survives in the Dokyoku score volumes**. English accounts that describe Yokoyama as
"Kinko-trained" are losing half of what he inherited at home.

`[V]` His mother, **横山かよ Yokoyama Kayo**, was a **生田流 Ikuta-ryū koto teacher**
(*新撰 芸能人物事典* via kotobank, 2026-11-13). `[V]` He later ran
**らんぽ社 Rampō-sha**, comprising 蘭畝会 *Rampō-kai* and 竹心会 *Chikushin-kai* (KSK
`kousi.html`, 2026-11-13) — i.e. he kept his father's house organisation alive alongside the
KSK, which is a distinction worth noting: **Rampō-sha is the family/Kinko-Azuma body; the KSK
is the cross-school one.**

### 3.3 Watazumi and Fukuda Randō — 1959, and both at once

`[V]` KSK's CV: 「１９５９／福田蘭童（ふくだらんどう）、海童道祖（わたづみどうそ）の両師に
師事」 — "**1959: became a disciple of both masters, Fukuda Randō and Watazumi Dōso**"
(`kousi.html`, 2026-11-13, furigana theirs). `[V]` Japanese Wikipedia (2026-11-13) says he was
25 when he took up with Fukuda Randō and with Watazumi Dōso, "**who had been in contact with
his father**" (父と交流のあった海童道祖) and describes the two as 「まったく行く道を異にする
師匠」 — "**masters whose paths were entirely different from each other**". `[V]` The
*ブリタニカ国際大百科事典 小項目事典* entry (kotobank, 2026-11-13) likewise names Fukuda
Randō, Watazumi Dōso and his father Rampō as his teachers.

Three things to take from this:

1. **The hunch that Fukuda Randō matters is correct, and the relationship matters a great deal.**
   It is not a footnote — the KSK's own list of Yokoyama's published works and products is
   roughly **half honkyoku and half Fukuda Randō repertoire**: there is a
   「福田蘭童尺八名曲集」 score volume with ~20 pieces plus koto and piano parts, a
   「福田蘭童尺八名曲選」 CD of 14 pieces, and a 1991 「福田蘭童名曲集」 video reissued on DVD
   (`KSK-Goods.pdf` and `ksk-shakuhachi.com` index, both 2026-11-13). The KSK's spring/summer
   camp syllabus pairs classical honkyoku with **one Fukuda Randō piece** (§4.3). Dokyoku
   practice as the institution actually runs it is **honkyoku plus Randō**, not honkyoku alone.
2. **The Watazumi connection came through his father.** `[V]` 父と交流のあった — Watazumi was
   already in contact with Rampō. This is a family-network entry into Watazumidō, not a
   seeker's pilgrimage.
3. **Date:** 1959 (KSK), consistent with "age 25" (ja.wikipedia) for a man born December 1934.

### 3.4 How the synthesis works — what he kept, changed, added

`[V]` **Kept from Kinko: the notation.** The KSK goods list (2026-11-13) offers every
尺八古典本曲集 volume in **琴古 Kinko notation, with 都山 Tozan notation also available**
(「琴古（都山記符もあります）」). The Dokyoku repertoire is written in Kinko script. A
lineage descended from a man who rejected the word "shakuhachi" nonetheless publishes its
honkyoku in the notation of the largest shakuhachi school — and in Tozan's too, for people who
read that. This is the single most concrete thing one can say about the synthesis.

`[V]` **Kept from the family: the Azuma pieces.** 吾妻獅子 is in Volume 2 (§6).

`[V]` **Kept from Watazumi: the core of the honkyoku repertoire and, by title, specific
Watazumi versions.** Compare the Watazumi track lists in §2.5 with the Dokyoku score volumes
in §6 and the overlap is dense: 本調, 心月, 手向, 鶴の巣籠り, 虚空, 三谷, 下り葉, 霊慕,
吾妻獅子 appear on both sides. `[U]` Whether each Dokyoku version derives from Watazumi's or
from the Kinko/Ichchōken side, piece by piece, I could not establish — this needs the score
volumes themselves.

`[V]` **Added: a cross-school institution and a cross-school ensemble practice.** The KSK's
founding motto, quoted in its own death notice, is Yokoyama's: 『**流も派も国境も超えた尺八の
メッカを！**』 — "**A Mecca for the shakuhachi, transcending schools, factions and national
borders!**" (my translation; KSK `kousi.html`, 2026-11-13). And the 1964 尺八三本会 is
described in the KSK CV as 「流派を越えて尺八三重奏のジャンルを確立する」 — "establishing the
genre of the shakuhachi trio across schools" (`kousi.html`, 2026-11-13).

`[V]` **Added: composition.** He wrote his own pieces (§3.6) — thirteen named in the KSK CV —
which is not something either Watazumi or the komusō tradition did.

### 3.6 Honours — in the correct legal category

*新撰 芸能人物事典* (kotobank, 2026-11-13). **Japanese Wikipedia says 2002** (2026-11-13) —

### 3.7 *November Steps*, 1967 — getting it right

This is the most misreported event in shakuhachi history. Here is what I
can and cannot verify.

`[V]` **The performance**: Yokoyama played Takemitsu Tōru's *November Steps* with the **New
York Philharmonic under Ozawa Seiji in 1967** (KSK `kousi.html`; ja.wikipedia 横山勝也;
*新撰 芸能人物事典* and the Kodansha 日本人名大辞典+Plus entries, all 2026-11-13 — four
independent sources, one of them the lineage's own). `[V]` ja.wikipedia and the Nichigai entry
both name **武満徹 Takemitsu Tōru** as composer and **小澤征爾 Ozawa Seiji** as conductor.

`[U]` **The biwa player**: the player usually named is **鶴田錦史 Tsuruta Kinshi**, and she is certainly
the biwa soloist of *November Steps* — but **none of my verified sources names her in
connection with this performance.** Neither the KSK CV, nor ja.wikipedia 横山勝也, nor the
kotobank entries mention her. `[U]` Missing: the ja.wikipedia ノヴェンバー・ステップス article
(linked from 横山勝也 but not fetched) and the NY Phil programme archive. Treat as true but
unverified *here*.

`[U]` **The exact premiere date and the commission.** `[U]` I did **not** verify the premiere
date, nor that the work was commissioned by the New York Philharmonic for its 125th
anniversary — the claim is standard in the literature but I have no artifact for it. Missing:
the NY Phil digital archives (`archives.nyphil.org`), which are open and would settle both the
date and the commission; and the ja.wikipedia *November Steps* article.

`[V]` **The recording is a different event from the premiere, and this is where most English
accounts go wrong.** The KSK's own discography (`kousi.html`, 2026-11-13) lists the earliest
*November Steps* recording as:

> RCA BVCC-9383 — **小澤征爾 conducting the Toronto Symphony Orchestra, recorded 1967-12-08,
> Massey Hall, Toronto.**

So the famous 1967 recording is **Toronto, December 8, with the Toronto Symphony** — *not* the
New York Philharmonic. The NY Phil gave the performance; the TSO made the record, five weeks
or so later. `[V]` Six further *November Steps* recordings with Yokoyama are listed in the same
discography — see §8.

### 4.3 The training courses — the actual shape of the institution's teaching

`[V]` From KSK `event.html` (fetched 2026-11-13). The KSK runs **two residential 本曲講習会
(honkyoku courses) in spring and two in summer** 「春夏２回ずつの講習会」, at **two venues**:

| Venue | Location | Access |
|---|---|---|
| **三峰神社 Mitsumine Shrine** | Chichibu, **Saitama** | Free shuttle from Seibu-Chichibu station (Ikebukuro 1h20 by limited express) |
| **国際尺八研修館** | Bisei-chō, Ibara City, **Okayama** | Free shuttle from Shin-Kurashiki |

- `[V]` **Format**: 2 nights, 6 meals included. **Fee ¥45,000** (students ¥36,000), course
  materials extra.
- `[V]` **The syllabus is published per session as a set list.** The (COVID-cancelled) March
  and April 2020 sessions both set: **虚霊, 鶴の巣籠り, 雲井獅子** (classical honkyoku) and
  **関の秋風** (Fukuda Randō) — again the honkyoku-plus-Randō pattern.
- `[V]` **There is a separate slow stream**: 「じっくりコース」 ("take-your-time course"),
  which in 2020 was **山谷**.
- `[V]` **And a technique masterclass**: 特別講座 「古屋輝夫の《謳う》為の理解とワザ」 —
  Furuya Teruo's "understanding and craft for *singing* [the line]" (my translation of 謳う).
- `[V]` **Applicants must state which notation they want**: 「楽譜購入希望曲とその種類
  (琴古・都山)」 — "the pieces whose scores you want to buy, and in which system (Kinko or
  Tozan)". The institution is notation-agnostic by design.
- `[V]` **出張講習会 — "away courses"**: a standing scheme whereby "as soon as a few people who
  want instruction gather, a course can be held", explicitly for those who cannot manage a
  two-night residential. Content: 模範演奏 (model performance), honkyoku instruction (or
  Randō-piece instruction), **メリの出し方** (how to produce *meri*), **むら息の出し方** (how
  to produce *muraiki*), and playing technique generally.
- `[V]` **KSK teachers also teach the NHK 文化センター** adult-education shakuhachi classes at
  the Aoyama, Hachiōji and Saitama Arena branches.
- `[V]` **出張演奏会 — touring concert programme**, pitched at schools and halls under the
  title 「尺八音楽の魅力〜古典から現代まで〜」 ("The appeal of shakuhachi music — from
  classical to contemporary"), typically 3 shakuhachi + 1 koto. Sample programme: 鹿の遠音
  (shakuhachi duo, classical) / 春の海 (Miyagi Michio) / 風動 (Kineya Seihō, shakuhachi trio) /
  月草の夢 (Fukuda Randō) / 虚空 (solo, classical) / コマーシャル (Komatsu Jūji, trio). The
  page notes 鹿の遠音 and 春の海 are **set texts in the Japanese school music curriculum**.

### 4.4 Grading, licensing, *shihan* — the informative absence

`[V]` **I found no grading, no licence, no *shihan* system, and no *natori* in any KSK
material.** Across the index, teachers page, events page, goods list and competition page
(all 2026-11-13) there is no 師範, no 免状, no 段, no 名取, no fee schedule for ranks. The six
senior teachers are titled **常任講師 *jōnin kōshi*** — "resident/standing instructors", a
plain employment-style word, not a transmitted rank. The students are 門人 *monjin* /
門下 *monka*, plain words for "disciple".

**This is a real structural fact about the lineage and worth stating as such.** In Tozan-ryū
and in the Kinko houses, the licence is the backbone of the organisation and a revenue stream.
The KSK instead has: residential courses with a published fee, scores you buy, and — since
2014 — a **competition**. It has replaced certification with **examination by peers in public**.
`[U]` Whether anything functions informally as licensure (e.g. being invited to become a
常任講師), and whether Yokoyama issued any 名 to anyone, I could not verify.

`[V]` **The "first non-Japanese *dai shihan*" claim about Riley Lee therefore has a problem at
the root**: if the KSK has no 師範 grade at all, a 大師範 cannot have been conferred within it.
See §5.4 — the claim may attach to a different body, or to Yokoyama acting personally, but it
cannot be read off the institution.

spiritual culture including Zen**. He closes by predicting the shakuhachi would follow other
