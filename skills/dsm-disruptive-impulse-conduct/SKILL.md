# Disruptive, Impulse-Control, and Conduct Disorders

Grouped by **problems in the self-control of emotions and behaviours** — and distinctively, by
behaviour that **violates others' rights or brings the person into conflict with social norms
and authority**. That outward direction is what separates this chapter from the rest of the
manual, where the distress is usually the person's own.

Mostly onset in childhood or adolescence, more common in males, with a shared association to
externalising temperament.

## The disorders

- **Oppositional defiant disorder** — DSM-5 **regrouped the criteria into three types**: *angry/
  irritable mood*, *argumentative/defiant behaviour*, and *vindictiveness*. The angry/irritable
  grouping matters because it identifies the children at risk for later mood disorders rather
  than later conduct problems. Frequency thresholds are calibrated by age (under 5 vs 5+), and
  severity is set by **how many settings** the symptoms occur in — mild (one), moderate (two),
  severe (three or more). **DSM-5 removed the exclusion** barring ODD alongside conduct
  disorder.
- **Conduct disorder** — a repetitive pattern violating others' rights or major age-appropriate
  norms, across aggression to people and animals, destruction of property, deceitfulness or
  theft, and serious rule violations. **Specify** childhood-onset (before 10), adolescent-onset,
  or unspecified onset; severity mild/moderate/severe.
  - **Specify if "with limited prosocial emotions"** — **new in DSM-5**: lack of remorse or
    guilt, callous lack of empathy, unconcern about performance, shallow or deficient affect.
    This is the operationalisation of **callous-unemotional traits**, and it identifies a
    subgroup with worse prognosis and poorer treatment response. Requires ≥2 features
    persistently over ≥12 months, across relationships and settings — deliberately demanding,
    because the label is consequential.
- **Intermittent explosive disorder** — recurrent outbursts representing failure to control
  aggressive impulses, grossly out of proportion to the provocation, not premeditated and not
  for a tangible objective. DSM-5 allowed **verbal aggression and non-destructive physical
  aggression** to count (two episodes weekly for three months), where DSM-IV effectively
  required property destruction or assault. Minimum age 6.
- **Pyromania** and **kleptomania** — deliberate fire-setting and stealing driven by tension and
  gratification rather than gain, motive or ideology. Both are rare and both require ruling out
  the far commoner alternatives (conduct disorder, mania, psychosis, ordinary theft or arson).
- **Antisocial personality disorder** is **cross-listed here** and coded with the personality
  disorders — the adult continuation of this chapter's developmental line
  ([[dsm-personality]]).

## Differentials

- **ADHD vs ODD** — ADHD's disruptiveness is driven by inattention and impulsivity, not by
  defiance toward authority. They co-occur very frequently, and both should be named when both
  are present. ADHD lives in [[dsm-neurodevelopmental]], which is itself a statement about
  which is developmental and which is behavioural.
- **Disruptive mood dysregulation disorder vs ODD** — DMDD requires severe recurrent temper
  outbursts **plus** persistent irritable mood between them, and **cannot be diagnosed with
  ODD**; when both pictures fit, DMDD takes precedence ([[dsm-depressive]]).
- **Mania** — irritability and aggression in an episodic course with changed sleep and energy
  ([[dsm-bipolar]]).
- **Trauma** — reactive aggression, hypervigilance and irritability after trauma, and the
  disinhibited social engagement picture after neglect ([[dsm-trauma-stressor]]).
- **Substance use** — intoxication and withdrawal aggression ([[dsm-substance-related]]).
- **Intermittent explosive disorder vs conduct disorder** — IED outbursts are impulsive and
  non-instrumental; conduct disorder includes premeditated, goal-directed violation.
- **Normal adolescent limit-testing** and **context** — behaviour that is adaptive in a
  dangerous or coercive environment is not a disorder, and the manual says to weigh the social
  context before diagnosing ([[dsm-5]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
