# Dissociative Disorders

Placed **immediately after** [[dsm-trauma-stressor]] — not alphabetically, but because
dissociative symptoms are frequent in the aftermath of trauma and the two chapters are
clinically continuous. DSM-5 made the adjacency explicit.

Defined by **disruption of and discontinuity in the normal integration** of consciousness,
memory, identity, emotion, perception, body representation, motor control and behaviour.
Symptoms can be *intrusions* into awareness (unbidden, fragmented) or *losses* of access
(amnesia, numbing).

## The disorders

- **Dissociative identity disorder** — two or more distinct personality states or an experience
  of possession, with recurrent gaps in recall. DSM-5 changes, all significant:
  - **Identity disruption may be self-reported as well as observed** — it no longer requires a
    clinician to witness a switch, which had made the diagnosis nearly unmakeable.
  - **Possession-form presentations are explicitly included**, which is how the condition most
    often appears outside Western settings.
  - **Amnesia need not be for traumatic events** — everyday gaps count.
- **Dissociative amnesia** — inability to recall autobiographical information, usually of a
  traumatic or stressful nature, inconsistent with ordinary forgetting. **Dissociative fugue is
  now a specifier**, not a separate disorder — purposeful travel or bewildered wandering with
  amnesia.
- **Depersonalization/derealization disorder** — persistent or recurrent detachment from one's
  self (depersonalisation: unreality, feeling like an observer, numbing) or surroundings
  (derealisation: unreality, dreamlike, visually distorted). **Reality testing stays intact** —
  the person knows the experience is subjective. That is the line against psychosis, and it is
  the whole differential. DSM-5 combined the two into one diagnosis.
- **Other specified** — includes identity disturbance from prolonged coercive persuasion, acute
  dissociative reactions to stressful events, and **dissociative trance**.

## Differentials

- **Reality testing** is the hinge against [[dsm-schizophrenia-spectrum]]: intact → here.
  Voices heard as internal and identified with identity states are not automatically psychotic.
- **PTSD with dissociative subtype** vs a dissociative disorder — if the picture is trauma
  re-experiencing with depersonalisation, the specifier in [[dsm-trauma-stressor]] handles it.
  Identity discontinuity points here.
- **Neurological:** complex partial (temporal lobe) seizures, transient global amnesia,
  post-concussive states, migraine aura. **Seizure workup belongs in the differential**, not
  after it ([[dsm-neurocognitive]]).
- **Substance-induced** — ketamine, cannabis, hallucinogens, alcohol blackouts
  ([[dsm-substance-related]]). A blackout is not dissociative amnesia.
- **Malingering and factitious presentation** — the manual puts this early in the differential
  for DID specifically, because of forensic incentives. Approach without presuming either way
  ([[dsm-somatic-symptom]]).
- **Culturally normative trance and possession** — expected and sanctioned religious or
  spiritual practice is **not** a disorder. The CFI exists partly for this
  ([[dsm-5]]).
- **Borderline personality disorder** — transient stress-related dissociation is one of its
  criteria; heavy overlap with DID ([[dsm-personality]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
