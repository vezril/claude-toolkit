# Sexual Dysfunctions

DSM-5 rebuilt this chapter. Three changes shape everything in it:

1. **Duration and frequency thresholds were added** — generally **≥6 months** and symptoms on
   **approximately 75–100% of occasions**. DSM-IV had neither, and the rates of diagnosis were
   correspondingly inflated. This is the chapter's main guard against pathologising ordinary
   variation.
2. **Diagnoses became gender-specific** rather than one set applied to everyone.
3. **Mergers:** vaginismus and dyspareunia became **genito-pelvic pain/penetration disorder**
   (the distinction was unreliable and the conditions overlapped), and female hypoactive sexual
   desire and female arousal disorder became **female sexual interest/arousal disorder**
   (because desire and arousal are not sequentially separable in the evidence — a direct
   challenge to the linear Masters-and-Johnson model).

Sexual aversion disorder was removed for lack of use and evidence.

## The disorders

- **Delayed ejaculation** — marked delay in, or absence of, ejaculation.
- **Premature (early) ejaculation** — DSM-5 added an explicit threshold of **approximately 1
  minute** following vaginal penetration, replacing a purely clinical judgment.
- **Erectile disorder**.
- **Female orgasmic disorder** — marked delay, infrequency, absence, or reduced intensity of
  orgasm.
- **Female sexual interest/arousal disorder** — absent or reduced interest, thoughts,
  initiation, excitement or genital/non-genital sensation.
- **Male hypoactive sexual desire disorder**.
- **Genito-pelvic pain/penetration disorder** — difficulty with penetration, pain, fear of pain,
  or pelvic floor tensing.
- **Substance/medication-induced sexual dysfunction** — SSRIs above all, also antipsychotics,
  antihypertensives, alcohol, opioids. Specify onset during intoxication, withdrawal or after
  medication use.
- **Other specified / unspecified**.

## Specifiers, and they are not optional

Every disorder takes: **lifelong or acquired**, and **generalized or situational**. These drive
formulation more than the diagnosis does — acquired and situational dysfunction points at the
relationship, the context or a medication; lifelong and generalized points elsewhere.

Severity: mild / moderate / severe by distress.

## The five factors the manual requires considering

The chapter instructs clinicians to assess, and document, the relevance of: **partner factors**
(partner's health, sexual problems) · **relationship factors** (communication, discrepant
desire) · **individual vulnerability** (body image, abuse history, psychiatric comorbidity) ·
**cultural or religious factors** (prohibitions, attitudes toward sexuality) · **medical
factors** (illness, medications, surgery).

If one of these fully accounts for the difficulty, **a sexual dysfunction diagnosis may not be
appropriate at all.** That instruction is the chapter's most important content and the most
frequently skipped.

## Differentials

- **Medical, and it is a long list** — diabetes, cardiovascular and peripheral vascular disease,
  hypogonadism and other endocrinopathies, multiple sclerosis and spinal cord injury, prostate
  and pelvic surgery, endometriosis, pelvic inflammatory disease, vulvodynia, atrophic changes.
  A sexual dysfunction with no medical review is an incomplete assessment.
- **Medication**, especially SSRIs — common, dose-related, and the single most likely cause in a
  psychiatric population ([[dsm-substance-related]]).
- **Depression and anxiety** — reduced libido is a depressive symptom; performance anxiety
  maintains erectile and orgasmic difficulty ([[dsm-depressive]], [[dsm-anxiety]]).
- **Relationship distress** is coded in [[dsm-other-conditions]], not here.
- **Inadequate sexual stimulation or knowledge** — the manual names this; it is not a disorder.
- **Paraphilic disorders are a different chapter** and a different question — unusual interests,
  not impaired function ([[dsm-paraphilic]]).
- **Gender dysphoria** may present with sexual distress; the distress is about gender, not
  function ([[dsm-gender-dysphoria]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
