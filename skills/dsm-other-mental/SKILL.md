# Other Mental Disorders and Additional Codes

A short residual chapter — five entries — and the manual's answer to "what if it doesn't fit
anywhere".

## What's in it

- **Other specified mental disorder due to another medical condition** — a medical condition is
  causing clinically significant mental symptoms that **do not meet criteria for any specific
  mental disorder due to another medical condition** elsewhere in the manual. The clinician
  states the reason.
- **Unspecified mental disorder due to another medical condition** — same, reason not stated.
- **Other specified mental disorder** — significant symptoms not meeting criteria for any
  disorder in any chapter, reason stated.
- **Unspecified mental disorder** — same, reason unstated. The emergency-department category:
  presentation is clearly disordered, information is insufficient.
- **No diagnosis or condition** — **made explicit in DSM-5-TR.** A positive statement that
  assessment was done and nothing is diagnosable, rather than leaving the field blank.

## How this differs from the chapter-level residual categories

Most chapters have their own *other specified* and *unspecified* entries — "other specified
depressive disorder", "unspecified anxiety disorder". **Use those whenever the presentation is
recognisably in that chapter's domain.** This chapter is only for presentations that don't
belong to any domain, which is rarer than it sounds.

So the decision order is: specific diagnosis → the relevant chapter's other specified →
that chapter's unspecified → **this chapter**.

## The "due to another medical condition" routing

The manual scatters "X disorder due to another medical condition" through the symptom-matched
chapters: psychotic disorder due to another medical condition in
[[dsm-schizophrenia-spectrum]], depressive in [[dsm-depressive]], anxiety in
[[dsm-anxiety]], and so on. **Go there first.** This chapter catches the medical-condition
presentations whose symptom picture doesn't match any of those — a mixed or atypical
neuropsychiatric presentation of an endocrine, autoimmune or neurological disease.

In all cases, **code the medical condition separately as well**; the mental disorder code does
not stand alone.

## Why "no diagnosis" being codable matters

It is a small change with real consequences. Without it, a completed assessment finding no
disorder looks identical in a record to an assessment never done. Being able to state the
negative supports continuity of care and makes the absence of a diagnosis a finding rather than
a gap — which is also the honest position when someone has been assessed and is simply not ill.

## Cautions

- **This is not a dustbin.** Reaching for "unspecified mental disorder" when a chapter-level
  residual category fits is a loss of information, and the manual's intent is that the
  specificity be preserved wherever possible.
- **"Unspecified" because information is missing is legitimate; because thinking stopped is
  not.** Where the reason is known, *other specified* with the reason stated is the better
  record ([[dsm-5]]).
- **Distress or impairment is still required.** A residual category is not a way around the
  threshold — it is a way around the criteria sets.
- Problems that are psychosocial or environmental rather than disorders belong in
  [[dsm-other-conditions]], not here. That distinction is the one most often got wrong with
  this chapter.

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
