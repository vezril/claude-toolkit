## Aaronson's critique (2014)

### The documents

- **Post 1.** Scott Aaronson, "Why I Am Not An Integrated Information Theorist (or, The Unconscious Expander)," *Shtetl-Optimized*, **21 May 2014**. 261 comments. Carries two "Update" blocks (5/22 with clarifications (1)–(6); 5/24 pointing to Chalmers's comment).
- **Post 2.** "Giulio Tononi and Me: A Phi-nal Exchange," **30 May 2014**. 217 comments.
- **Tononi's reply document.** Giulio Tononi, "Why Scott should stare at a blank wall and reconsider (or, the conscious grid)" — a **~14-page document sent to Aaronson on 28 May 2014** and hosted by him with permission at `scottaaronson.com/tononi.docx` (live, 60,416 bytes). Acknowledges feedback from Albantakis, Boly, Cirelli, Ghilardi, Gomez, Hoel, Koch, Mayner, Mensen, Oizumi and **Max Tegmark**.

**Provenance.** Aaronson says he was prompted by **Tegmark**, who *"enthusiastically adopted IIT as a plank in his radical mathematizing platform (see his paper 'Consciousness as a State of Matter')"* and *"challenged me to back up my doubts with a quantitative calculation."* He also records an earlier in-person exchange with Tononi and Koch at an **FQXi conference in Puerto Rico** (winter 2013/14).

⚠️ **This is a blog, not peer-reviewed — and his Φ is the 1.0/2.0 effective-information definition, not 3.0 or 4.0.** Both facts matter when citing it.

### The framing: the "Pretty-Hard Problem"

He sets the Hard Problem aside (*"no matter what the third-person facts were, one could always imagine a universe consistent with those facts in which no one 'really' experienced anything… I've just conjured into my imagination beings whose Φ-values are a thousand, nay a trillion times larger than humans', yet who are also philosophical zombies"*), then insists you cannot have it both ways: *"you can't say both that the Hard Problem is meaningless, and that progress in neuroscience will soon solve the problem if it hasn't already."*

> *"Since I don't know a standard name for the problem, I hereby call it the **Pretty-Hard Problem of Consciousness**"* — *"how to construct a theory that tells us which physical systems are conscious and which aren't—giving answers that agree with 'common sense' whenever the latter renders a verdict."*

**The methodological premise that does all the work:** *"The reason it's so important that the theory uphold 'common sense' on these test cases is that, given the experimental inaccessibility of consciousness, **this is basically the only test available to us. If the theory gets the test cases 'wrong' … it's not clear that there's anything else for the theory to get 'right.'**"*

He credits IIT as serious: *"I regard IIT as a serious, honorable attempt to grapple with the Pretty-Hard Problem of Consciousness: something concrete enough to move the discussion forward. But I also regard IIT as a failed attempt."*

### The thesis

> *"IIT fails to solve the Pretty-Hard Problem because it unavoidably predicts vast amounts of consciousness in physical systems that no sane person would regard as particularly 'conscious' at all: indeed, systems that do nothing but apply a low-density parity-check code… Moreover, IIT predicts not merely that these systems are 'slightly' conscious (which would be fine), but that **they can be unboundedly more conscious than humans are**."*

The logical form: *"it's even possible, for all I know, that having a large Φ-value is one necessary condition among many … **However, having a large Φ-value is certainly not a sufficient condition for consciousness, or even for the appearance of consciousness.**"*

**What he is *not* claiming** (clarification (2)): *"I wasn't arguing in this post that dualism is true, or that consciousness is irreducibly mysterious, or that there could never be any convincing theory… All I was arguing was that, at any rate, IIT is not such a theory."*

### His formalization of Φ

He complains he *"had a hard time finding a clear mathematical definition of it—one that not only listed formulas but fully defined the structures that the formulas were talking about,"* and notes competing variants (Φ_DM, Φ_E, Φ_AR). His reconstruction:

- A discrete system in state `x ∈ Sⁿ`, evolving by `f: Sⁿ → Sⁿ`.
- **Effective information** `EI(A→B) := H(z_B | A random, y_B = x_B)` — the Shannon entropy of the outputs in B when the inputs in A are drawn **uniformly at random** while the inputs in B are **clamped** to their values in x. Symmetrically for `EI(B→A)`.
- `Φ(A,B) := EI(A→B) + EI(B→A)`.
- Naively Φ = min over all `2ⁿ − 2` bipartitions. But unbalanced partitions trivially minimize this, so (per Tononi) **normalize**: divide each `Φ(A,B)` by `min{|A|,|B|}`, and define **Φ := Φ(A,B) for whichever (A,B) minimizes the ratio.**
- ⚠️ **The ambiguity he will exploit:** *"Unless I missed it, Tononi never specifies what we should do if there are multiple (A,B)'s that all achieve the same minimum of Φ(A,B)/min{|A|,|B|}."*

### Construction A: the Vandermonde / Reed–Solomon system

> *"The demonstration of this claim is embarrassingly simple."*

Let `S = F_p` with p prime, `p ≫ n`. Let **V** be the `n×n` **Vandermonde matrix** over `F_p`, `(i,j)` entry `= i^(j−1) mod p`. Define `f(x) = Vx`. For p large enough **every submatrix of V is full-rank** (*"every submatrix preserves all the information that it's possible to preserve"*). Hence **for every** bipartition:

- `EI(A→B) = EI(B→A) = min{|A|,|B|} · log₂ p`
- `Φ(A,B) = 2 min{|A|,|B|} · log₂ p`
- Normalized: `Φ(A,B)/min{|A|,|B|} = 2 log₂ p` — **the maximum possible value, identically for every bipartition.**

**Every bipartition ties, so "at this point, Φ is simply undefined."**

**The "ironic little hack" that makes Φ well-defined and large.** Replace V by **W**: the first `n/2` rows of the Vandermonde matrix, **each repeated twice** (n a multiple of 4). With `A = {1..n/2}`, `B = {n/2+1..n}`: `EI(A→B) = EI(B→A) = (n/4) log₂ p`; `Φ(A,B) = (n/2) log₂ p`; normalized `= log₂ p`. He then proves this bipartition essentially uniquely minimizes the ratio: writing `|A| = cn`, `c ∈ (0, 1/2]`, `Φ(A,B)/|A| = 1/2 + min{1, 1/(2c) − 1/2}`, *"uniquely minimized when c = 1/2."* Therefore

> **Φ = (n/2) log₂ p** — *"quite a large value (only a factor of 2 less than the trivial upper bound of n log₂ p)."*

**The quantitative punchline:** *"this system's Φ equals half of its entire information content. So for example, **if n were 10¹⁴ or so—something that wouldn't be hard to arrange with existing computers—then this system's Φ would exceed any plausible upper bound on the integrated information content of the human brain.**"*

**Why he says this discredits Φ:** *"evaluating a polynomial on a set of points turns out to be an excellent way to achieve 'integrated information'… In fact, that's precisely why polynomials are used so heavily in error-correcting codes, such as the **Reed–Solomon code**, employed (among many other places) in CD's and DVD's. **But that doesn't imply that every time you start up your DVD player you're lighting the fire of consciousness. It doesn't even hint at such a thing.** All it tells us is that you can have integrated information without consciousness (or even intelligence)."*

**On the hack as evidence of fragility:** *"in order to ensure a large value of Φ, I **decreased**—by a factor of 2, in fact—the amount of 'information integration' that was intuitively happening in my system! … I hope I'm not alone in fearing that this illustrates a **disturbing non-robustness in the definition of Φ**."*

**Why Vandermonde specifically** (clarification (4)): *"the reason I chose the Vandermonde matrix is not merely that it's invertible, but that **all of its submatrices are full-rank**… by contrast, note that the identity matrix is invertible, but produces a system with Φ=0."* Clarification (5): for bit-valued variables, use LDPC codes or compose Reed–Solomon with a binary encoding of `F_p` elements.

### Construction B: expander graphs of XOR gates

Anticipating the objection that his system is an abstract input–output map rather than a bounded-fan-in circuit — *"a good objection, but I don't think it can rescue IIT"*:

- **LDPC codes:** *"we can achieve the same qualitative effect … the same 'global information integration'… even using much 'sparser' computations."* ⚠️ He concedes he hasn't done the work: *"one would need to muck around a bit to construct a physical system based on LDPC codes whose integrated information Φ was provably large… **But I feel safe in asserting that this could be done**."*
- **Bipartite expanders:** *"we can achieve pretty good information integration by hooking together logic gates according to any **bipartite expander graph**… And it's well-known how to create expander graphs whose **degree … is a constant, such as 3**."* In comments he pins down the gates: *"a bipartite expander graph **together with some fixed, simple choice of logic gates at the nodes: for example, XOR gates**. Indeed, that's exactly what's done with LDPC codes."*
- **The title's claim:** *"**I feel 100% confident in saying that the so-constructed expander graphs are not conscious! The brain might be an expander, but not every expander is a brain.**"*
- On physical embeddability: he grants the wiring problem but says it *"**applies to the brain just as much as to artificial systems!** And hence, if those difficulties impose some effective upper bound on Φ in the one case, then they do the same in the other."*
- **Dynamics objection** (clarification (6)): *"this can be fixed by the simple expedient of **iterating the process over and over**! I.e., first map x to Wx, then map Wx to W²x, and so on."*
- **He predicts patching is hopeless:** *"it would be **unbelievably difficult to 'patch' the definition of Φ in any way that got rid of all counterexamples like mine**… the basic problem that the counterexamples illustrate is not one of detail."* And on a proposed "integration must emerge unguided" patch: *"I could easily design a system that started out with local connections only, then slowly changed its connections in a random way so as to evolve into an expander graph… but it would have an 'organically' large Φ."*

### The Valiant / superconcentrator historical point — a load-bearing move

> *"the concept of integrated information (though it wasn't called that) played an interesting role in computational complexity in the 1970s. As I understand the history, **Leslie Valiant** conjectured that Boolean functions with a high degree of 'information integration' … might be good candidates for proving circuit lower bounds… More strongly, Valiant conjectured that the property of information integration, all by itself, implied that such functions had to be at least somewhat computationally complex… **Alas, that hope was refuted by Valiant's later discovery of linear-size superconcentrators.** Just as information integration doesn't suffice for intelligence or consciousness, so Valiant learned that information integration doesn't suffice for circuit lower bounds either."*
>
> *"As humans, we seem to have the intuition that global integration of information is such a powerful property that no 'simple' or 'mundane' computational process could possibly achieve it. **But our intuition is wrong.** If it were right, then we wouldn't have linear-size superconcentrators or LDPC codes."*

**The point: the mistake IIT makes is one complexity theory already made and already corrected.**

### His closing assessment (post 1)

> *"In my opinion, the fact that Integrated Information Theory is wrong—demonstrably wrong, for reasons that go to its core—puts it in something like the **top 2% of all mathematical theories of consciousness ever proposed**. Almost all competing theories of consciousness, it seems to me, have been so vague, fluffy, and malleable that they can only aspire to wrongness."*

He flags the prior art: *"[Endnote: See also this related post, by the philosopher Eric Schwitzgebel: *Why Tononi Should Think That the United States Is Conscious*. While the discussion is much more informal, and the proposed counterexample more debatable, the basic objection to IIT is the same.]"*

And reports Tononi's earlier in-person response: *"**it's wrong to approach IIT like a mathematician**; instead one needs to start 'from the inside,' with the phenomenology of consciousness… This response perplexed me: of course you can start from phenomenology… However, once your theory has been constructed, surely it's then fair game for others to try to refute it with counterexamples?"*

### Tononi's reply, in his own words

**(a) He concedes the mathematics outright — and raises the stakes.**

> *"**Scott's mathematical argument is right**: certain systems whose structure and function are easy to describe from the extrinsic perspective of an observer, such as expander graphs performing parity checks, or worse, grids doing absolutely nothing, **may in fact have a large value of PHI** if they can be built to be large enough… **However, Scott's 'commonsense' intuition that such simple systems cannot possibly be conscious is wrong and should be revised.**"*

He goes **further** than Aaronson in three steps:

> *"Resorting to expander graphs is actually **overkill**… systems that are even simpler to describe than expander graphs, for example a **2D lattice of identical logic gates (a 'grid')** could also achieve very large values of PHI. So things are 'even worse' for IIT."*
>
> *"In fact, the situation for IIT is actually 'worse', since it allows for a large 2D grid to be conscious **even if it were doing nothing, with all gates switched off at a fixed point**. Thus, if IIT can be invalidated by an expander graph doing not much at all, it can be invalidated all the more by a mere grid doing absolutely nothing."*

And he takes the hardest case deliberately: *"Let us stick to the worst-case scenario, and consider the less forgiving calculation of PHI in IIT 3.0, a large 2D grid of physically implemented logic gates, and **assume that all the logic gates are off**."*

**(b) The only technical quibbles he raises — and sets aside.**

> *"Scott does not consider the latest formulation of IIT and PHI (**3.0**), so his arguments about **normalization**, for instance, are not relevant (**normalization is not necessary in 3.0**). Similarly, he does not consider how IIT addresses the problem of identifying the appropriate **spatio-temporal grain**… **However, his main point that certain systems that are simple … could have large values of PHI, still stands. In fact, the numbers for IIT 3.0 would be even 'worse'.**"*
>
> *"Scott considers at some length the Vandermonde matrix. As he himself realizes, this is an **abstract mathematical entity, and IIT deals explicitly with actual physical systems, not mathematical idealizations**… However, as he says, this cannot rescue IIT, because there are systems that can be implemented physically, such as large expander graphs."*

**(c) "Intuitions are not evidence."**

> *"it can be **dangerous to rely too much on one's pre-theoretical intuitions, however strong they may seem**. Examples in science are numerous, starting with the strong intuitions people once had that the earth must be still… Concerning consciousness, the **reliability of pre-theoretical intuitions is even worse, because different people often hold radically different ones**."*

He enumerates the divergence — Terri Schiavo, animals, newborns, functionalists like Dennett, machines, photodiodes, panpsychists — ending with *"**Scott's typical computer scientist's intuition: things that are computationally simple … cannot possibly be conscious**."* And the asymmetry he relies on: *"each of us can be quite certain of the essential properties of his own experience – I know directly what it is like to be me. By contrast, **no one can really trust his intuition about what it might be like to be some other entity, like a bat, an octopus, or an expander graph**. Making meaningful inferences in such cases requires a theory – mere intuitions are not to be trusted."*

**(d) Method, and the prohibition on ad hoc patches.**

> *"one starts from the axioms of phenomenology, and derives postulates about possible physical mechanisms. **This is the exact opposite of what is usually done**… As David Chalmers has argued convincingly, the problem of going from the brain to experience is indeed so hard that it may actually be impossible to solve. On the other hand, argues IIT, it may be hard, but not impossible to go from experience to the brain (or to expander graphs)."*
>
> *"the quantity and quality of an experience are an intrinsic property of a complex of mechanisms in a state – the property of shaping the space of possibilities… **just as it is intrinsic to a mass to bend space-time around it**."*

**He forecloses the obvious repair, unprompted:**

> *"Note that such predictions must derive directly from the heart of IIT (its axioms and postulates) – they must be **consequences, not premises** of the theory. In other words, **one cannot simply augment the postulates with ad hoc prescriptions that are not grounded in phenomenology, but merely accommodate some cherished intuition** (ensuring, say, that expander graphs, or the United States of America are not conscious by design)."*

**(e) The counterintuitive predictions he accepts**, verbatim:

> - *"A photodiode (with memory) is minimally conscious."*
> - *"Systems that may seem extremely complicated by most standards, including biological ones, may be unconscious if they do not have the right architecture."*
> - *"Systems that have a purely feed-forward architecture are unconscious, even though they may be functionally equivalent to systems that perform the same functions consciously."*
> - *"A conventional computer running a program that behaves just like a human being (think of the movie 'Her') would be unconscious."*
> - *"**Even a conventional computer running a virtual simulation of Scott's own brain down to all its neurons and synapses would be unconscious** (and of course it would 'report' the same things Scott would)."*
> - *"a neuromorphic [system] designed in a certain way could be as conscious, or more conscious, than a human being."*
> - *"**A functional cerebral cortex that happens to be silent – no neuron is sending any signal (spike) to any other neuron – would nevertheless be conscious.**"*
> - *"**Even a simple but large 2D grid could be highly conscious** … Worse still, it could be conscious even if it were doing 'nothing' (all its gates off), and **even if it were disconnected from the rest of the world** (no external inputs and outputs)."*

**(f) The positive case: "why certain grids may be conscious."** Four prongs.

1. **Phenomenology of 2D space.** *"It is now time to do what the title exhorts Scott to do - **stare at the blank wall**, or better still, at a large featureless screen… the difference in the level of consciousness between seeing a blank screen and a painting must be small compared to the difference between experiencing the blank screen and falling unconscious to the ground."* And: *"the experience of empty 2D visual space is **not at all empty, but contains a remarkable amount of structure**… we are aware of all the possible locations in space ('points')… their relative positions… **given that our experience of the blank screen definitely exists, and it is precisely the way it is … there must be physical mechanisms that specify such phenomenological relationships through their causal power.**"*
2. **What the postulates imply for a grid.** Assume a 1000×1000 grid of integrate-and-fire units, near-neighbour excitatory connections, all elements off, no inputs/outputs. Each element specifies a first-order concept (10⁶ of them); pairs sharing inputs and outputs specify second-order concepts; triples third-order; and so on. *"**this conceptual structure is highly irreducible** (as indicated by high PHImax)… **no matter how one partitions the grid, many concepts would be destroyed** … just as implied by Scott's argument for expander graphs."* And the ontological punchline: *"one may see that such a 2D grid is necessary not so much to **represent** space from the extrinsic perspective of an observer, but to **create** it, from its own intrinsic perspective."*
3. **Explanatory power.** *"Decades of neuroanatomy and neurophysiology have revealed that **many parts of the cerebral cortex itself are organized as grids**… **for many of these cortical grids, decades of investigations have shown that the way features are mapped on each grid bears a striking resemblance with the way phenomenological distinctions are organized within experience.**"*
4. **Predictive power.** Lesion/stimulation of V1 (blindness; phosphenes tracking the stimulation site), V5/MT and motion, Bálint's syndrome.

His summary: *"if we consider phenomenology and empirical evidence, **considering simple grids (and expander graphs) actually supports IIT, rather than invalidating it**… our best inference should be that, **if a 2D grid is large and well built, it could be quite conscious, though perhaps a bit boring and not that intelligent**."*

**(g) A falsification test he offers.** *"one could in principle **add non-neural hardware with the proper interconnectivity and double the extent of a grid-like visual area** that is thought to contribute to the experience of 2D space. If such an addition were to increase the associated conceptual structure and PHI **without** a corresponding expansion of experienced 2D space and associated increase in consciousness, **then IIT would be in serious trouble**."*

**(h) He shifts the burden on "extra ingredients."** *"**The burden … is to show that one's favorite candidate can account for the phenomenology of consciousness** … and that it does so better than IIT. Otherwise, adding one's favorite feature is **ad hoc and unparsimonious**."*

**(i) On mathematics, and on Aaronson's recollection.** *"IIT is all for mathematics, not against it… **What I actually said then, and many times before, is that IIT does not start by being infatuated with a particular mathematical quantity** … **IIT does not start from mathematics hoping to explain phenomenology, but rather it starts from phenomenology to end with mathematics.** Hence mathematical arguments are not only welcome, but are essential to the development and revision of IIT."*

**(j) He concedes the theory is in flux.** *"IIT is an evolving framework: the precise way of measuring PHI, the correct metric for concept space, and **even the correct set of axioms and corresponding postulates are still being developed and refined**. For example, should there be an independent axiom explicitly about time?"*

### Aaronson's rejoinder (post 2)

**(a) He credits the concession.** *"Giulio, not one to battle strawmen, first restates my own argument against IIT with crystal clarity… There, he doesn't 'bite the bullet' so much as **devour a bullet hoagie with mustard**."* And: *"I give Giulio **enormous credit for having the intellectual courage to follow his theory wherever it leads**. When the critics point out, 'if your theory were true, then the Moon would be made of peanut butter,' he doesn't try to wiggle out of the prediction, but proudly replies, 'yes, chunky peanut butter—and you forgot to add that the Earth is made of Nutella!'"*

**(b) A quantitative gloss on the grid, with the sharp corollary Tononi did not mention.** *"a **√n×√n square grid has decent expansion**: good enough to produce a **Φ-value of about √n**… And apparently, by Giulio's lights, **Φ = √n is sufficient for consciousness**!"* And: *"logic gates arranged in a **1-dimensional line** would produce a tiny Φ-value (**Φ = O(1)**). So even by IIT standards, such a linear array would not be conscious. Yet **the jump from a line to a two-dimensional grid is enough to light the spark of Mind**."* ⚠️ **Koch concurs in a comment (#72): "we agree on a 1-D array having a small but non-zero Phi value while Phi for a 2-D array increases as √(# of nodes)."**

**(c) Argument I — paradigm cases are not negotiable.** Analogies: a rival definition of continuity under which `⌊x⌋` is continuous and `x²` is not; a "Scott-thermometer" on which boiling water is colder than ice.

> *"before any formal definitions, any ε's and δ's, we start with an intuition for what we're trying to capture… And if we press hard enough on what that intuition involves, we'll find that it largely consists of various '**paradigm-cases**.' … Crucially, we use the paradigm-cases to guide our choice of a formal definition—**not vice versa**! … If, for example, our definition told us that x² was discontinuous, that wouldn't be a 'surprise'; it would just be **evidence that we'd picked a bad definition**. The definition failed at the only task for which it could have succeeded: namely, that of capturing **what we meant**."*
>
> *"what we mean by heat is 'whatever it is that boiling water has more of than ice'… **When the clock strikes 13, it's time to fix the clock, and when the thermometer says boiling water's colder than ice, it's time to replace the thermometer.**"*
>
> On the molecular-motion definition of heat: *"that turned out to be a superb definition … but crucially, because **it matched common sense on the paradigm-cases**. If it hadn't given sensible results for boiling water and ice, then the only possible conclusion would be that, whatever new quantity physicists had defined, they shouldn't **call** it 'temperature'… **They should call their new thing something else.**"*

His paradigm cases: you are conscious (not when anesthetized); other people appear conscious; many animals to a lesser degree; *"A rock is not conscious. A wall is not conscious. A Reed-Solomon code is not conscious. Microsoft Word is not conscious (though a Word macro that passed the Turing test conceivably would be)."* And: *"Fetuses, coma patients, fish, and hypothetical AIs are the **x·sin(1/x)'s of consciousness**: they're the tougher cases, the ones where we might actually need a formal definition to adjudicate the truth."*

**The clearest statement of the "collapse of appeal" point is in the thread, replying to Chalmers (Comment #136, 24 May 2014):**

> *"We're not talking about a scientific hypothesis that contradicts common sense in some subtle but manifestly-testable way, in a realm far removed from everyday experience—like relativity telling us about clocks ticking slower for the twin on the spaceship. Rather, we're talking about a theory that predicts, let's say, that **a bag of potato chips is more conscious than you or me**…*
> *I don't know about you, but if the world's thousand wisest people assured me that such a theory had been shown to be correct, my reaction wouldn't be terror that I had gravely underestimated the consciousness of potato-chip bags… **My reaction, instead, would be that these wise people must be using the word 'consciousness' to mean something different than what I meant by that word**—and that the very fact that potato-chip bags were 'conscious' by their definition was **virtually a proof of that semantic disagreement**. So … I'd simply want to **ban the word 'consciousness' from the discussion**, and see whether the wise people could then convey to me the content of what had been discovered about potato-chip bags.*
> *By contrast, suppose there were an improved consciousness-measure Φ', and suppose Φ' assigned tiny values to livers, existing computers, and my Vandermonde system, large values to human brains, and somewhat smaller but still large values to chimpanzee and dolphin brains; and after years of study, no one could construct any system with a large Φ' that didn't seem to look and act like a brain. In that case, I wouldn't be able to rule out the hypothesis that what people were referring to by large Φ' was indeed what I meant by consciousness…*
> ***What I'm missing, right now, is what sort of state of affairs could possibly convince me that (a) potato-chip bags have property X, and (b) property X refers to the same thing that I had previously meant by 'consciousness.'***"

And the same move in Comment #45: *"if you want me to pick a different word for this other quality that interests me—say, '**blorfness**'—while you take 'consciousness' for the thing the Vandermonde matrix maximizes, that's fine with me. **But crucially, I suspect Tononi and the other IIT researchers would then agree that they, too, were interested all along in 'blorfness'**… For otherwise, why would they have spent so much time talking about the human brain, whose Φ-value is mediocre at best? Why haven't they concentrated their intellectual energies on systems with better Φ-values, like Vandermonde matrices?"*

**(d) Argument II — the Axiom Argument.** *"First, I don't really understand them. Second, insofar as I do understand them, I don't necessarily accept their truth. And third, insofar as I do accept their truth, I don't see how they lead to Φ."*

- Understanding: *"the definitions never seemed to 'bottom out' in mathematical notions that I understood, like functions mapping finite sets to other finite sets. What, for example, is a 'mechanism'? What's a 'system of mechanisms'? What's 'causal power'? What's a 'conceptual structure'…?"*
- Truth: *"I'm far less confident about any of these postulates … than I am about my own 'postulate,' which is that you and I are conscious while my toaster is not. Note that my postulate, though not phenomenological, does have the merit of **constraining candidate theories of consciousness in an unambiguous way**."*
- Derivation: *"None of the papers I read … contained anything that looked to me like a **derivation** of Φ. Instead, there was general discussion of the postulates, and then **Φ just sort of appeared at some point**. Furthermore, given the many idiosyncrasies of Φ—the minimization over all **bipartite (why just bipartite? why not tripartite?)** decompositions… it would be quite a surprise were it possible to derive its specific form from postulates of such generality."*
- **The turnabout on IIT 3.0:** *"Giulio chided me for not using the 'latest, 2014, version 3.0' edition of Φ… Well, **if the postulates uniquely determined the form of Φ, then what's with all these upgrades?** Or has Φ's definition been changing from year to year because the **postulates themselves** have been changing? If the latter, then maybe one should wait for the situation to stabilize before trying to form an opinion of the postulates' meaningfulness, truth, and completeness?"*

**(e) Argument III — the "Ironic Empirical Argument" (the cerebellum *tu quoque*).** This is his strongest structural objection, and it is an **inconsistency** charge:

> *"Giulio has stated, as 'largely uncontroversial facts,' that certain brain regions (the cerebellum) and certain states (dreamless sleep) are **not** associated with our consciousness. He then views it as a victory for IIT, if those regions and states turn out to have lower information integration…*
> ***But how does Giulio know that the cerebellum isn't conscious?** Even if it doesn't produce 'our' consciousness, maybe the cerebellum has its own consciousness, just as rich as the cortex's but separate from it… Likewise, maybe 'dreamless' sleep brings about its own form of consciousness, one that (unlike dreams) we never, ever remember in the morning.*
> *Giulio might take the implausibility of those ideas as obvious… **But here's the problem with that: he just told us that a 2D square grid is conscious!** He told us that we must not rely on 'commonsense intuition'… **So then why shouldn't he say the same for the cerebellum, or for the brain in dreamless sleep?** By Giulio's own rules (the ones he used for the mesh), we have no a-priori clue whether those systems are conscious or not—so even if IIT predicts that they're not conscious, **that can't be counted as any sort of success for IIT**.*
> *For me, the point is even stronger: I, personally, would be a **million times more inclined** to ascribe consciousness to the human cerebellum, or to dreamless sleep, than I would to the mesh of XOR gates…*
> ***you can't count it as a 'success' for IIT if it predicts that the cerebellum is unconscious, while at the same time denying that it's a 'failure' for IIT if it predicts that a square mesh of XOR gates is conscious.** If the unconsciousness of the cerebellum can be considered an 'empirical fact,' safe enough for theories of consciousness to be judged against it, then surely the unconsciousness of the mesh can also be considered such a fact."*

**(f) Argument IV — the "blank wall" argument, answered twice.** The quick reply: *"Just because it feels like something to see a wall, doesn't mean it feels like something to **be** a wall. **You can smell a rose, and the rose can smell good, but that doesn't mean the rose can smell you.**"*

The one he prefers — **the 1D reductio**: *"**why shouldn't the same be true of 1D space?** After all, my experience of staring at a rope is also rich and structured, no less than my experience of staring at a wall… In fact, the rope even has a structure—namely, **a natural total ordering on its points**—that the wall lacks. So why does IIT cruelly deny subjective experience to a row of logic gates strung along a rope, reserving it only for a mesh of logic gates pasted to a wall? And yes, I know the answer: because the logic gates on the rope aren't 'integrated' enough. **But who's to say that the gates in the 2D mesh are integrated enough?** As I mentioned before, their Φ-value grows only as the **square root** of the number of gates, so that **the ratio of integrated information to total information tends to 0**… **When you cut a rope, does it not split? When you prick it, does it not fray?**"*

**(g) His bottom line — a declared impasse, not a victory.** *"**I personally feel completely safe in saying that this is not the theory of consciousness for me.** But I've also learned that other people, even after understanding the above, still don't reject IIT. And you know what? **Bully for them.** On reflection, I firmly believe that a **two-state solution** is possible, in which we simply adopt different words for the different things that we mean by 'consciousness'… How about '**paradigm-case consciousness**' for the one and '**IIT consciousness**' for the other."*

**What Aaronson conceded.** He did not retract the mathematics or the conclusion. The concessions are: (i) his Φ computation used the pre-3.0 normalized definition (he does not dispute Tononi's correction, and Tononi grants the conclusion survives it); (ii) he grants Tononi *"enormous credit"* for consistency; (iii) **he grants that his rejection rests on an axiom he states openly** (clarification (3)): *"it's true that my demonstration of IIT's falsehood **assumes—as an axiom, if you like**—that while we might not know exactly what we mean by 'consciousness,' at any rate we're talking about something that humans have to a greater extent than DVD players. If you reject that axiom, then I'd simply want to define a new word."*

### Chalmers's intervention in the thread

Chalmers, Comment #125, 24 May 2014. Aaronson flagged it as *"a very interesting comment."* Chalmers splits the problem and sides against Aaronson's framing:

> *"I'd love to see an information-based account of consciousness perhaps along the lines of IIT work out, **but this gives me some pause**…*
> *there are a few different problems in the vicinity worth distinguishing. The first distinction is between **PHP1**: 'Construct a theory that matches our intuitions about which systems are conscious' and **PHP2**: 'Construct a **correct** theory that tells us which systems are conscious'. Here I'd say that **PHP2 is more important and fundamental than PHP1**… One might think that intuitions are our best guide to a correct theory here, but **that's far from obvious** … And certainly I'd say that **IIT is intended as an answer to PHP2 rather than PHP1**.*
> *The second ambiguity is between **PHP3**: 'Construct a theory that tells us which systems are conscious' and **PHP4**: 'Construct a theory that tells us which systems have which **states** of consciousness'. Here I'd say PHP4 is more important… (Its answer to PHP3 is pretty trivial: **almost all physical systems are conscious**.)*
> *… One can then see **IIT as a partial answer** to the PHP. It's partial in that it gives us only a degree of consciousness rather than a full specification of a state… Presumably two systems could have the same degree of consciousness even though e.g. one has only visual consciousness and the other only auditory consciousness, and insofar as phi just measures degree of consciousness, **it won't distinguish these systems**."*

**Aaronson's reply refuses the PHP1/PHP2 split on epistemic grounds:** he asks what the *means of verification* would be for a PHP2 answer that renders absurd-seeming judgments in cases where we thought we already knew the answers.

### Downstream engagement

- **Tononi et al. 2025 cite it as their canonical "misconception."** In "Consciousness or pseudo-consciousness?" (*Nature Neuroscience* 28:694–702), Aaronson's blog post is **reference 6**: *"the realization that grids are an excellent substrate for high integrated information led to a widely mentioned criticism of IIT, on the assumption that grids are obviously 'too simple' to support consciousness⁶. **This gut-theoretical dismissal of grids flies in the face of the evidence that much of the likely substrate of consciousness in our brain is organized in a grid-like manner.** Moreover, the cause–effect structure specified by a set of stacked grids is extraordinarily rich, and it must be to account for the experience of space."* **So the 2014 position is unchanged in 2025.**
- **The 2023 pseudoscience letter cites it as reference 17**, for *"an inactive grid of connected logic gates that are not performing any useful computation can be conscious—possibly even more so than humans."* The published 2025 version makes the same point citing Hanson & Walker 2023 instead, adding: *"the theory readily ascribes consciousness to static systems that do not perform any function. **Stating that such systems have 'consciousness', when their 'consciousness' has no observable consequences is, in this respect, no different than stating that they have 'souls'.**"*
- **Merker, Williford & Rudrauf** (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 45:e41, 2022), "The integrated information theory of consciousness: A case of mistaken identity," reach the same conclusion from a different direction and a different list: IIT's identification *"has the consequence of requiring the attribution of consciousness to a range of natural systems and artifacts that include, but are not limited to, **large-scale electrical power grids, gene-regulation networks, some electronic circuit boards, and social networks**. Instead of treating this consequence of the theory as a disconfirmation, **IIT embraces it.** By regarding these systems as bearers of consciousness ex hypothesi, IIT is led toward the orbit of panpsychist ideation. **This departure from science as we know it** can be avoided by recognizing the functional misattribution at the heart of IIT's identity claim."* (That last phrase is one of the sources the 2023 letter quotes.)

### Methods as executed

- **n = 256** total: **fMRI n = 120**, **MEG n = 102**, **iEEG n = 34** (29 patients used for decoding; 31 for activation/LMM; 28 for RSA). Two or three independent labs per modality.
- Task: detect two infrequent target stimuli in sequences of single high-contrast fixated stimuli. Four **categories** (faces, objects, letters, false fonts), 20 **identities** each, three **orientations** (front, left, right), three **durations** (0.5, 1.0, 1.5 s). Blockwise task-relevance manipulation; **orientation was always task-irrelevant.**
- Design logic: *"analysis optimization phase (one-third of the sample) followed by a final testing phase (two-thirds of the sample) on independent data"*; theory proponents separated from acquisition and analysis; preregistered pass/fail criteria and pre-agreed interpretations.
- iEEG electrode counts: **PFC ROIs n = 576; posterior ROIs n = 583** (decoding); 655/657 (activation). ⚠️ **Explicit caveat the paper records: only 12 iEEG electrodes localized to V1/V2, versus 472 in PFC.**
- ⚠️ **The paper's own framing of what is NOT being tested:** *"Although it is difficult to test the mathematical or computational core of either theory directly, their competing biological implementations are empirically testable with current methodologies."* And: *"One consequence of this biological focus is that **theorists could respond to challenging data by modifying the proposed biological implementation while retaining the mathematical or computational core of a theory**."*
- Philosophy of science adopted: *"The consortium adopted **Lakatos' sophisticated falsificationist approach**… emphasizing that challenged predictions provide more valuable insights than those confirmed by the data."*

### The three preregistered predictions

**Prediction 1 — decoding of conscious content (location).**

- **IIT:** *"conscious content is maximal in posterior brain areas"*; PFC is **not necessary**, so *"adding PFC activity as additional information should not improve decoding accuracy."* ⚠️ Status in the preregistration: *"**This prediction was considered non-critical for testing IIT**, as the theory focuses on the intrinsic, causal perspective of information within a neural substrate rather than the amount of information decodable from the perspective of an extrinsic observer."*
- **GNWT:** *"a **necessary role for PFC**"*; conscious content decodable from PFC. Workspace neurons *"broadcast but do not add information,"* so no decoding improvement from adding PFC is consistent with GNWT too.
- Pass criterion: *"the theories would pass the test if decoding is possible **for both category (in at least one category pairing) and orientation (in at least one category)**, but would fail otherwise."*

**Prediction 2 — maintenance over time (duration).**

- **IIT:** *"conscious content is **actively maintained by neural activity in the posterior 'hot zone' throughout the duration** of a conscious experience."* Challenged *"if sustained content-specific information **and** activation tracking stimulus duration were absent in the posterior cortex."* **Both activation and information content were critical for IIT.**
- **GNWT:** *"**brief content-specific ignition in PFC within 0.3–0.5 s after stimulus onset**… Activity then decays back to baseline, with information maintained in a **latent / activity-silent state**, until **another ignition marks the offset**."* For GNWT, **activation was the primary measure.**

**Prediction 3 — interareal connectivity. Critical for both theories.**

- **IIT:** *"**sustained short-range connectivity within the posterior cortex**, linking low-level sensory (V1/V2) with high-level category-selective areas"* — specifically *"**sustained gamma-band connectivity**… throughout any conscious visual experience."*
- **GNWT:** *"**long-range connectivity between high-level category-selective areas and PFC**"* — *"brief, **late-phase metastable connectivity (more than 0.25 s)**… manifested in long-range **gamma-band or beta-band** synchronization."*
- Preregistered metric: **pairwise phase consistency (PPC)**. Gamma was chosen *"because of its close link to neuronal spiking, which IIT considers a constituent property of the physical substrate of consciousness."*

### Findings, prediction by prediction

**Prediction 1 — mixed, favouring IIT on orientation.**

- Category (faces vs objects) cross-task decoding succeeded in **both** posterior and prefrontal ROIs across all three modalities. iEEG posterior: >95%, sustained for approximately the stimulus duration. iEEG PFC: ~70%, but temporal generalization *"restricted to approximately 0.2–0.4 s."* fMRI searchlight: ~75% posterior; PFC *"lower accuracy (approximately 60%) and was spatially restricted."*
- **Orientation decoding diverged.** *"decoding of face orientation (left, right or front views) was achieved in **posterior but not in prefrontal ROIs**, both with iEEG (approximately 95% with pseudotrial aggregation) and the fMRI searchlight approach (approximately 45%)."* MEG posterior ~75%; MEG PFC weak but above chance (35%), *"with a possibility of signal leakage from posterior regions."*
- Bayesian support for the null in PFC: **iEEG BF₀₁ = 5.11–8.65**; fMRI *"substantial-to-very-strong support for the null hypothesis in 34–55% of prefrontal voxels (BF₀₁: 3–71.5), with support for the alternative hypothesis in only 1–9% of voxels."*
- **IIT's "PFC adds no information" sub-prediction confirmed**: *"adding prefrontal ROIs did not improve—and in some cases reduced—category and orientation decoding."* Strong Bayesian evidence against improvement: face–object **iEEG BF₀₁ = 1.94 × 10⁴**; letter–false font iEEG BF₀₁ = **1.91 × 10⁵**; face orientation iEEG BF₀₁ = 1,205.

**Prediction 2 — IIT passes the stated criterion but with a sparse substrate; GNWT fails hard on offset.**

- Posterior: **25 of 657** electrodes showed sustained activity tracking stimulus duration — 12 duration-tracking independent of category (mostly occipital pole), 13 category-specific (mostly faces, lateral fusiform). ⚠️ *"**only a small proportion of electrodes exhibited both category selectivity and duration tracking—for example, just 15% (8 of 53) of face-selective electrodes showed sustained activity as predicted by IIT, suggesting a sparse neural substrate.**"*
- Cross-temporal RSA in posterior ROIs *"revealed **sustained** face–object categorical representation… The RSA matrix **matched the IIT model better than the GNWT model**."* Sustained **object identity** information also found, IIT model fitting better.
- ⚠️ **IIT's failure *within* prediction 2:** *"**Face orientation information appeared weakly in posterior ROIs at stimulus onset but was not sustained, decaying after 0.5 s, contrary to the predictions of IIT.**"* Also: *"the predictions of neither theory were supported for category, identity or orientation **by the MEG data**"*; MEG LMMs: *"**none of the theory-based models adequately fit the data (BF₀₁ > 3 for all parcels)**."*
- **GNWT's failure:** PFC showed 99 non-selective and 24 category-selective **onset** responses — *"However, **none of the 655 electrodes measured the temporal profile predicted by GNWT (that is, onset and offset)**. Bayesian analysis confirmed this result (**BF₀₁ > 3 for all electrodes in PFC ROIs**)."* **Not a sensitivity failure:** *"the LMM successfully detected the GNWT-predicted pattern in **10 electrodes in other ROIs**."* An exploratory unrestricted analysis found exactly **one** electrode (inferior frontal sulcus) with the pattern, *"although with transient responses occurring earlier than expected."*
- RSA in PFC: *"transient face–object categorical representation at stimulus onset, **but not at stimulus offset**."* Robust to three control analyses.
- **PFC content poverty:** *"In PFC ROIs, **identity information was absent for all categories**… **No face orientation information was detected in PFC ROIs**."*

**Prediction 3 — the preregistered metric supported NEITHER theory. This is IIT's headline failure.**

- iEEG PPC: content-selective synchrony between category-selective electrodes and V1/V2 was found but *"**These effects were early and brief (for example, less than 0.75 s), and restricted to low frequencies (2–25 Hz)**. This synchrony was mostly explained by the stimulus-evoked response… **These results fail to align with IIT's predictions: the activity was neither sustained nor observed in the gamma frequency band.** Bayesian analysis further supported the null hypothesis (**BF₀₁ = 1.15–4.9**)."*
- *"**No content-selective PPC was found between face-selective and object-selective electrodes and PFC electrodes in the relevant time window, contradicting the prediction of GNWT** (BF₀₁ = 2.62–5.32)."*
- MEG PPC: again early, <25 Hz, mostly evoked-response driven; *"Bayesian analysis of the gamma-band synchronization further supported the null hypothesis (all BF₀₁ > 3)."*
- **"The results of the preregistered PPC metric for prediction 3, critical for both IIT and the GNWT, supported neither theory."**
- ⚠️ **An exploratory, non-preregistered metric (dynamic functional connectivity, DFC, amplitude-based) was then added** — the supplementary "Deviations" item 7 states why: *"**The reason for adding this extra analysis was that we could not find any connectivity through phase-locked synchrony.**"* DFC results: iEEG showed object-selective↔V1/V2 connectivity across bands, gamma predominant, but *"**In contrast to the predictions of IIT, the observed connectivity was brief**"*; face-selective↔V1/V2 connectivity *"was scarce."* Meanwhile *"**Significant connectivity was observed between PFC and both the face-selective and the object-selective areas in the gamma frequency band within the GNWT-predicted time window.**"* Net: *"The exploratory DFC results in iEEG were consistent with the predictions of GNWT while challenging the predictions of IIT… **However, V1/V2 were sparsely sampled in our population, with only 12 electrodes localized to V1/V2 compared with 472 in PFC.**"*
- fMRI gPPI: FFA showed content-selective connectivity with V1/V2, inferior frontal gyrus and intraparietal sulcus — *"consistent with predictions from both theories."* No object-selective connectivity to PFC or V1/V2.

### The consortium's own verdict

**Abstract:** *"These results align with some predictions of IIT and GNWT, while **substantially challenging key tenets of both theories**. For IIT, **a lack of sustained synchronization within the posterior cortex contradicts the claim that network connectivity specifies consciousness**. GNWT is challenged by the **general lack of ignition at stimulus offset** and **limited representation of certain conscious dimensions in the prefrontal cortex**. These challenges extend to other theories of consciousness that share some of the predictions tested here."*

**On IIT:** *"**For IIT, the lack of sustained synchronization within posterior cortex represents the most direct challenge**, based on our preregistration. This is **incompatible with IIT's claim that the state of the neural network, including its activity and connectivity, specifies the degree and content of consciousness**. Although this null result could stem from methodological limitations (for example, limited iEEG sampling of V1/V2 areas), our multimodal and highly powered study provided the best conditions so far for evaluating the prediction. **We urge IIT proponents to direct future efforts to evaluate this prediction and determine the implications of this failure.**"*

And: *"although IIT **passed** the predefined criteria for the duration prediction (number 2), there was **no evidence for a sustained representation of orientation**… This is an informative challenge for IIT, as orientation decoding was robust across all three data modalities."*

**On GNWT:** *"the most substantial challenge based on our preregistered criteria pertains to… the **lack of ignition at stimulus offset**. This result is unlikely to stem from sensitivity limitations… The lack of ignition at stimulus offset is **especially surprising given the change in conscious experience at the onset of the blank fixation screen**… **Thus, that aspect of consciousness remains unexplained within the GNWT framework.**"* Second: *"no representation of **identity** was found, and representation of **orientation** was evident only in MEG… **the role of PFC in consciousness might need to be redefined**."*

**Extended Data Fig. 7 summary scoring:** *"**For IIT, the results mix a passed prediction** (content-specific complex of neural units in posterior cortex, throughout the persistence of a percept, independent of the task) **with a failure** (maximum integrated information). **For GNWT, the results consisted of a mixture of a partly challenged prediction** … **and a partly supported one**."*

**Spillover:** *"the prediction of GNWT about PFC is shared by those higher-order theories… **the failures to confirm this prediction challenge not only GNWT but also those higher-order theories**."* Conversely, *"the non-core prediction 1 about the posterior cortex by IIT is also shared by many theories (for example, recurrent processing theory)… Its prediction 3 about interareal connectivity is **more distinctive to IIT**… so the challenge here is more specific as well."*

**No verdict was issued:** *"readers might expect a definitive verdict on the two theories under evaluation. **Instead, we invite readers to weigh the evidence themselves**… Scientific progress is rarely a matter of simple verdicts."*

### Deviations from the preregistration (the paper's own list)

From Supplementary Note 14 — relevant because critics focus here:

1. Three iEEG patients included despite falling *"marginally short of the predefined behavioral criteria"*, because COVID limited recruitment to 34 instead of the targeted 50.
2. fMRI minimum-trials criterion not applied.
3. Two Yale fMRI datasets excluded for incomplete runs (110 → 108).
4. RDM computation method changed (no trial split).
5. fMRI gPPI task-relevant and task-irrelevant conditions **pooled** to increase power (preregistration specified independent analysis).
6. Multiple-comparison correction changed from FDR to cluster-based permutation *"as it can profit from the spatial structure of the data and therefore affords **higher statistical power**."*
7. **DFC added** on top of preregistered phase synchrony *"because we could not find any connectivity through phase-locked synchrony."*
8. ⚠️ *"**Due to the low number of electrodes in V1/V2 and FFA/LOC, the preregistered criterion of 'sustained content-specific activation' was relaxed.** Instead, all electrodes located in V1/V2 and those showing content specificity for faces or objects were included… regardless of whether they showed sustained activation."*

**Item 8 is the one the IIT adversaries then invoke against the prediction-3 result.**

### What each side said afterwards, in the paper

The Nature paper adopted a **three-voice discussion format**: neutral consortium, then each adversary.

**IIT — Boly, Koch, Tononi** (Supplementary §12), verbatim:

> *"**The results corroborate IIT's overall claim that posterior cortical areas are sufficient for consciousness, and neither the involvement of PFC nor global broadcasting are necessary.** They support preregistered prediction #1… and prediction #2… **They do not support prediction #3 concerning sustained synchrony, although this negative finding is quite possibly the result of sparse electrode coverage.**"*

They restate the theory's commitments (*"Every conscious content (face, object, letter, blank screen) is thus a (sub)structure of integrated information… it is **neither a message** that is encoded and broadcasted globally, **nor a distributed activity pattern, nor a neural process**"*) and make a **falsifiable prefrontal commitment**: *"any PFC region organized in a grid-like way with dense interconnections with posterior regions may well be part of the main complex. As previously emphasized, '…**we bear no preconceived enmity to the prefrontal cortex**…' … **However, IIT predicts that we would still experience faces (sans aspects contributed by PFC regions) if PFC were selectively inactivated.**"*

⚠️ **Their response to the prediction-3 failure is a four-part defence — the clearest instance of the auxiliary-assumption retreat critics warn about:**

1. **Wrong frequency band:** *"Given the lack of evidence for sustained synchrony in the gamma band, **the prediction may be wrong with respect to the frequency range**. Synchrony may occur instead in lower frequency ranges… Indeed, main results using iEEG show increased content-specific synchrony (PPC) between category-specific cortical regions and V1/V2 (but not with PFC) in the 2-25 Hz frequency range."*
2. **Electrode coverage:** *"there were only **12 iEEG electrodes in V1/V2 (against 472 in PFC)**."*
3. **The analysis deviated from preregistration in a way that hurt IIT:** *"the analysis of iEEG data **did not strictly follow pre-registered analyses methods for selecting ROIs**, which required restricting measures of synchrony with anatomical V1/V2 to 'category-selective' channels … **exclusively** showing sustained activation … in **all three** pre-specified time windows. Instead, the final analysis included 'category-selective' channels showing an increase in activity … **in any time window**. **Such a lenient selection of ROIs decreased the relevance of the synchrony analysis to test IIT**… It may also partially explain findings of transient rather than sustained synchrony patterns."* ⚠️ **Note: this is exactly the paper's own Deviation #8, and the deviation was adopted because V1/V2 coverage was too sparse to apply the stricter criterion.**
4. **MEG ROI selection also inapt:** *"measuring changes in synchrony averaged across such ROIs **did not formally test IIT's pre-registered prediction**, which only concerns activated areas."*

On the orientation/RSA decay they offer a **microsaccade** explanation: *"micro-saccades appear to be more numerous beyond 500 ms after stimulus onset… If so, **IIT predicts that a different set of units in V1/V2 … would contribute to experienced low-level features**… **animal experiments with denser iEEG sampling of V1/V2 may be necessary to resolve this issue**."* They claim an additional win against GNWT: *"both the sparsity of iEEG electrodes showing duration-tracking and the widespread deactivation patterns found in PFC **strongly suggest that changes in contents of consciousness may be supported by localized activity changes, rather than by a global 'broadcasting' and ignition**."* And they endorse the exercise: the group *"did so in a way that was explicit, open, and truly collaborative—in short, **in a way that is paradigmatically scientific**."*

**GNWT — Dehaene** (Supplementary §13). He claims the headline positive:

> *"**Most importantly, the results confirm that PFC exhibits a metastable bout of activity ('ignition') for about ~200 ms, in a content-specific manner, even for task-irrelevant stimuli, irrespective of stimulus duration**… **Those findings, unpredicted by IIT but predicted by GNWT**, support previous findings that PFC contains a detailed code for conscious visual contents. They also **counter previous conclusions that were, in our opinion, too hastily drawn on the basis of insufficient evidence**."*

**Then his defences — and the asymmetry-of-specificity line is the single most-quoted sentence in the whole controversy:**

> *"there is a considerable asymmetry in the specificity of the theories' predictions. **None of the massive mathematical backbone of IIT, such as the φ measure of awareness, was tested in the present experiment. Instead, what are presented as unique predictions of IIT (posterior visual activation throughout stimulus duration) are just what any physiologist familiar with the bottom-up response properties of those regions would predict**, since visual neurons still respond selectively during inattention or general anesthesia. Such posterior stimulus-specific, duration-dependent responses are **equally predicted by GNWT, but attributed to non-conscious processing**."*

- **No conscious/unconscious contrast:** *"it is impossible to decide which of the activations reflected conscious versus non-conscious processing, because **the experimental design did not contrast conscious versus non-conscious conditions**… The present experiment relied on the **seemingly innocuous hypothesis that stimuli were 'indubitably consciously experienced' for their entire duration**. However, it is well known that perfectly visible stimuli, depending on attention orientation, may fail to be seen… **it seems likely that participants briefly gained awareness of all the images… but then reoriented their conscious thoughts to other topics, without waiting for image offset – and this interpretation perfectly fits the ignition profile that was found in PFC.** It is also unclear whether participants were ever aware of stimulus orientation, which was always irrelevant."*
- Offset failure explained away on the same basis: *"the absence of decodable activation at stimulus offset, while challenging, **may simply indicate that participants never consciously attended to that event**."*
- Why PFC decoding is weak: *"the prefrontal code … is thought to involve a **vector code distributed over millions of neurons which, unlike in posterior regions, are not clustered but spatially intermingled**. Thus, we are **not surprised that PFC responses are hard to decode**… Therefore, **the present positive results … seem to us more important than the null ones.**"*
- Causality: *"the present methods are **purely correlational** and do not evaluate causality… we therefore eagerly await the results of other adversarial collaborations using causal manipulations in animal models."*

**GNWT's formal follow-up paper.** Naccache, Sergent, Dehaene, Wang, Farisco & Changeux, "GNW theoretical framework and the 'adversarial testing…'," ***Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2025, niaf037**, doi:10.1093/nc/niaf037 (with a **Correction**, niag020, 2026). Verbatim:

- Claimed confirmations: *"**this article confirmed that stimuli that are clearly consciously visible elicit a brain-wide activation and synchronization between sensory areas and prefrontal cortex, even in the absence of task-relevance, validating a non-trivial prediction of the GNWT.** Also given that this study only used clearly visible stimuli, **it could not test the most central predictions of the GNWT** which tackle the critical contrast between conscious and non-conscious processing. Hence, **several conclusions drawn by the authors about GNWT predictions need to be reconsidered.**"*
- ⚠️ **They retract the offset-ignition prediction as non-core:** *"While ignition after stimulus onset is indeed a core prediction of GNWT, **offset ignition is not**… **none of the simulations** of the [GNW model] **and none of the experimental recordings revealed a definite offset ignition**. Thus, **the presence of ignition at stimulus offset cannot be taken as a central and discriminative prediction of GNWT, and its absence in the data is not a major issue for the theory**."*
- ⚠️ **They retract the activity-silent state too:** *"Such states may happen, **but they are not necessary and do not constitute a mandatory prediction of GNWT**."*
- They turn the posterior-decoding result against IIT: decoding in visual areas *"**is even possible under non-conscious conditions**, raising the possibility that the decoding success there, which is discussed as positive evidence for IIT, **could have arisen from purely non-conscious processes**."*
- ⚠️ **Competing-interests disclosure:** *"L. Naccache and S. Dehaene are co-inventor on patent 2019 EP 2983586 ('Methods to monitor consciousness'); and are **shareholders of NeuroMeters**."*

**Independent reanalysis that partly rescues GNWT (2026).** Bandara, Rowe & Garrido, "Testing the Spatiotemporal Predictions of Global Neuronal Workspace Theory and Integrated Information Theory," bioRxiv, **22 July 2026**. Re-examined the Cogitate **MEG** dataset (N = 100) with **dynamic causal modelling** to get *directed/effective* rather than functional connectivity: *"**We found support for both GNWT predictions of prefrontal ignition at stimulus onset and offset; noting that the latter was not previously observed in the original Cogitate analyses.** For IIT, whilst **feedback connectivity between the fusiform gyrus and primary visual cortex was not sustained throughout the stimulus duration**, intrinsic fusiform gyrus connectivity was broadly consistent with IIT's prediction of sustained processing within a content-specific [region]."*

### Critical commentary on whether the collaboration worked

**Hakwan Lau's open review — the most detailed technical attack.** "Where is the 'posterior hot zone'? Open Review of Cogitate et al (2023)," PsyArXiv, **18 Aug 2023**, doi:10.31234/osf.io/93ufe; with an **Update dated 22 Sep 2024** responding to Cogitate's PubPeer reply. He opens by crediting the work (*"the project is strong in large part because of the scale, ample funding… the current studies were skillfully executed"*; iEEG PFC coverage *"exceptional"*; open-science practices *"should be applauded"*). Then:

- **The ROI problem (his central charge).** *"The most surprising and critical problem here concerns the **anatomical definitions of the regions of interest (ROIs), without which the hypotheses are unspecified and meaningless in practice**. The selection of ROIs for IIT, **which neglected the formalisms of the theory**, **excluded areas originally labeled as part of the so-called 'posterior hot zone' (e.g. precuneus, posterior cingulate)**, and instead **included important frontal areas as 'extended' ROIs**, will puzzle many, and probably be considered **false advertisement** by others. **Excluding parietal regions for GNWT likewise went against how the theory has been characterized in print.**"*
- **Timing of the ROI preregistration.** *"these critical and surprising ROI definitions were **only uploaded to OSF in Dec 2022 - by which point data collection was probably already completed or near completion**… **the precise specifications of these ROIs were finalized and formally registered only after data collection and some preliminary data analysis. Without these ROI definitions, the hypotheses were basically unconstrained and meaningless, and yet the study was advertised as pre-registered.**"*
- **The ROIs look reverse-engineered for decodability.** *"curiously they look like **a set of regions which anyone would have chosen in order to maximize decoding accuracy for classifying visual stimuli**. Because IIT is not supposed to be specific only to vision, these ROIs do not appear to represent the theory in general."*
- **The "extended ROI" loophole.** *"**large parts of the frontal cortex were actually included as extended ROI for IIT, meaning that if effects were found in these areas, they would by stipulation not count against IIT.**"* His figure caption: *"In yellow are areas that IIT theorists predict as likely to contain the neural correlates of consciousness. In blue are areas that they **also** consider to be likely… '**rendering them non-diagnostic for the evaluation of the theory**.' **Only areas in red are those in which they commit to predicting no effects.**"*
- **The backwards logic of narrowing GNWT.** *"**But logically this seems backwards: if the two theories are indeed empirically indistinguishable, perhaps we should just accept that fact. We can't instead contrive to modify a theory so substantially just for this ad hoc purpose, and still convincingly call it GNWT.**"*
- **No unconscious control condition.** *"**The lack of non-conscious conditions unfortunately limits what we can say about consciousness**… This led to an **uncontrolled stimulus confound**, which could very well explain most of the presented results, especially in early visual areas. I have no doubt that the subjects consciously perceived the stimuli, but this doesn't mean that the conscious perception itself rather than the sheer presence of the stimuli was the explanation… **Just in general, how would skipping a control condition make things more interpretable?**"*
- **The offset prediction isn't GNWT's.** *"**I also could not find in print any reference supporting this prediction, or linking it to GNWT**… **upon stimulus offset, the next frame is invariably the same: blank**… **while the theorist representing GNWT here (Dehaene) made the prediction that there will be a high level of activity at offset, I'm not sure GNWT or its broader community of supporters would have necessarily made that same prediction.**"* (**Dehaene's own 2025 follow-up subsequently conceded precisely this.**)
- **Novelty.** *"By now, there are already **dozens of no-report studies**… **several recent papers using intracranial EEG / ECoG directly addressed this exact question, using a similar approach, with arguably more sophisticated and thorough data analysis**."*
- **Misleading PFC claim.** *"**the areas most important for PFC decoding have been excluded from this analysis**, because they have been considered extended ROIs of IIT. I suggest that the authors clearly state in the main text that **the analysis was performed after removing frontal areas.**"*
- **Necessity vs sufficiency.** *"according to the common definition of the NCC, **'necessity' was never meant to be a requirement**; it is **'joint minimal sufficiency'** that is the relevant logical concept… **Causal manipulation (e.g. ablation / inactivation, or even TMS) may be more appropriate.**"*
- **"Theory-neutral" is unverified.** *"The authors wrote that '[a]ll research was conducted by theory-neutral teams to guard against confirmatory bias.' **But what is the evidence for this?**… can we really say that everyone is truly neutral just because they are not currently in the same labs as the theory PIs?"*
- **"Posterior hot zone" is not an anatomical term.** *"these unconventional labels really allow **too much flexibility for the hypotheses to be meaningfully testable**… I recommend just calling these early and late visual areas."*
- **His 2024 Update, after Cogitate's reply** (which cited marmoset/rodent neuroanatomy, notably Watakabe et al., *Neuron* 2023): *"**Having studied the relevant papers carefully, and also having consulted several experts on the topic, I have to admit that I still find the logical link rather tenuous.** Some of the neuroanatomical papers just added **were not cited in the previous IIT theoretical papers**… Regardless of the degree to which these details may reflect any kind of **post hoc rationalization**, it seems clear that **we simply do not yet understand these prefrontal circuits in enough detail to link them precisely to a theory as mathematically specific as IIT.**"* And: *"the key hypothesis basically comes down to the observation that: **areas found to be somewhat topographical with respect to visual stimuli … will be better for decoding the identity of visual stimuli using current human neuroimaging data.**… as Stanislas Dehaene pointed out, **this prediction is relatively banal**."*
- **The "half right" problem.** *"if e.g. a 'new theory' of weather makes two predictions, that the Sun will come up in the east tomorrow, and that it will rain - and if only one of the two predictions is falsified, **does it make the theory half right?**… **This could be potentially very misleading.**"*
- **On degrees of freedom:** *"'posterior hot zone' seems to be a relatively new and ever evolving concept. **As the authors pointed out, the need to keep changing auxiliary assumptions is itself a sign of failure for the general research program.**"*

**Lau's institutional critique of the whole ARC model.** "What is a Pseudoscience of Consciousness? Lessons from Recent Adversarial Collaborations," PsyArXiv, **17 Sep 2023**, doi:10.31234/osf.io/28z3y. He attended the original Seattle conception meeting and **declined to be a theory PI.**

## The unfolding argument

### The original paper

**Doerig A., Schurger A., Hess K., Herzog M.H.**, "The unfolding argument: Why IIT and other causal structure theories cannot explain consciousness," ***Consciousness and Cognition* 72:49–59** (July 2019), doi:10.1016/j.concog.2019.04.002. Green OA at EPFL Infoscience. ⚠️ **Note co-author Kathryn Hess is a mathematician (EPFL) — the paper leans on computation-theoretic theorems.**

Abstract, verbatim: *"Certain theories suggest that consciousness should be explained in terms of brain functions… Other theories, such as Information Integration Theory (IIT) and Recurrent Processing Theory (RPT), **identify causal structure with consciousness**. For example, according to these theories, **feedforward systems are never conscious, and feedback systems always are**. Here, **using theorems from the theory of computation, we show that causal structure theories are either false or outside the realm of science**."*

### "Unfolding," stated precisely

The formal basis is the **universal approximation property**, cited to Hornik, Stinchcombe & White (1989) for feedforward networks and Schäfer & Zimmermann (2006/2007) for recurrent ones:

> *"Both recurrent networks and multilayer feedforward networks are **universal function approximators**… for a given input-output function **we can find both feedforward and recurrent networks that realize the same function in different ways**. If there is a recurrent network that does object recognition, there is an equivalent feedforward network that does it equally well. **If there is a recurrent network that exhibits the characteristics of binocular rivalry, there is an equivalent feedforward network that does so too.** … Anything that can be done by recurrent networks can also be done in a feedforward manner. **We call this unfolding.**"*
>
> *"Any input-output behaviour can be implemented not only by one particular feedforward network, but also by **infinitely many** equivalent feedforward networks and by infinitely many equivalent recurrent networks."*

They flag the cost (*"although feedforward networks often need many more neurons"*) and the time-bound condition: the equivalence holds *"for computations bounded by a maximal time step"* — **the same caveat Tononi & Koch 2015 state when they endorse the same mathematical fact.**

**The appendix contains a second, sharper construction** (Fig. A1a): **you can raise Φ arbitrarily while holding behaviour fixed**, by *"inserting a ϕ-increasing device in the network, and subsequently **canceling its functional effect**"* — because *"there are feedforward networks implementing the inverse, g⁻¹, thereby cancelling the functional effect."* Verbatim: *"we now have fβ ∘ h⁻¹ ∘ h ∘ fα = F, with **the quale of smelling ham (or any other quale)**."*

**So the double dissociation runs in *both* directions:** *"the unfolding argument shows that we can **completely reverse the picture** and, for example, implement conscious states with ϕ = 0 neural networks and unconscious states with high ϕ neural networks. Hence, **ϕ is neither necessary (wakefulness can be implemented with ϕ = 0) nor** [sufficient]… causal structure theories are **doubly dissociated from empirical data**."*

### The dilemma, in their own formal statement

> **(P1):** *In science we rely on physical measurements (based on subjective reports about consciousness).*
> **(P2):** *For any recurrent system with a given input-output function, there exist feedforward systems with the same input-output function (and vice-versa).*
> **(P3):** *Two systems that have identical input-output functions cannot be distinguished by any experiment that relies on a physical measurement (other than a measurement of brain activity itself or of other internal workings of the system).*
> **(P4):** *We cannot use measures of brain activity as a-priori indicators of consciousness, because the brain basis of consciousness is what we are trying to understand in the first place.*
> **(C):** *Therefore, **EITHER causal structure theories are falsified** (if they accept that unfolded, feedforward networks can be conscious), **OR causal structure theories are outside the realm of scientific inquiry** (if they maintain that unfolded feedforward networks are not conscious despite being empirically indistinguishable).*

**P4 is the premise the whole exchange turns on, and it is the one Kleiner attacks.**

### The circularity branch

> *"**Proponents of IIT try to avoid this problem by claiming that systems with ϕ = 0 are unconscious despite being empirically indistinguishable from conscious systems (Oizumi et al., 2014).** We will show next that this claim makes IIT **circular, and therefore unfalsifiable**. In other words, **causal structure theories are falsified by the unfolding argument, unless they decide to become unfalsifiable.**"*
>
> *"such a proposition quickly ends up in circularity because **we have no criteria to settle the matter**… **The only reason to believe that only the ϕ > 0 robot is conscious is to already believe in IIT, but this is circular.** The situation is even worse: there are many causal structure theories… **Even within IIT, the axioms do not uniquely determine ϕ (Barrett & Mediano, 2019; Bayne, 2018), and different empirical measures of ϕ yield very different results (Mediano, Seth, & Barrett, 2018). Which version of IIT is the 'right' one?** … **We are left with the conclusion that there are different types of 'consciousness' (i.e., consciousness_IIT_version_1, …, consciousness_RPT, and so on), depending on which theory we favour.**"*

⚠️ **So the unfolding argument explicitly recruits Bayne 2018 and Barrett & Mediano 2019 as part of its own case** — i.e. the non-uniqueness problem is used as a force multiplier for the circularity charge.

### Illustrations they give

- **The feedforward implant.** *"Imagine that one could surgically replace the brain's native recurrent sound processing system with an equivalent feedforward implant… **all subjective reports by the person are identical before and after the surgery.** The person will claim all the same things about sound as before, such as '**I hear the drizzle of the rain, it is music to my ears**'."* They footnote the obvious objection — *"even if sound processing areas cannot be isolated, **unfolding is applicable to the whole brain**."*
- **Feedforward brains.** *"there could be «**feedforward brains**» that behave exactly like human brains… **no behavioural experiment can distinguish between human brains and feedforward brains**."* Including the clinical evidence IIT trades on: *"sleeping humans cannot be explained by or support IIT because there are feedforward networks with human wakefulness characteristics."*
- **The real-world existence proof.** *"[Mnih et al. 2013] used feedforward convolutional networks to achieve super-human performance in Atari video games. **Hausknecht and Stone (2015) replicated this superhuman performance using recurrent networks.** … **These systems are empirically identical (to a close approximation). One is conscious but the other is not, according to causal structure theories.**"*

### Which theories they say escape

> *"**the unfolding argument only targets causal structure theories of consciousness, and not physicalist or functionalist theories in general. In fact, the unfolding argument favours functionalist** [theories] of consciousness, such as **Global Workspace Theory**, **Higher-Order Thought Theory** or **Predictive Processing Theory**…"*
>
> *"**What matters is the function, e.g., broadcasting, but not the (neural) implementation.** … For example, **an unfolded global workspace network retains the crucial function of broadcasting.** Hence, **by their very nature, functionalist theories are not subject to the unfolding argument.**"*

Their Fig. 4 is *"A feedforward toy model of Global Workspace Theory"*: *"the 'global workspace', which is simply another feedforward network maintaining activity through time by copying each layer to the next… **Hence, this model fulfills the crucial functions** [of GWT]."*

**They also concede Φ may still be a good *marker*:** *"causal structures may be good [markers for consciousness]. For example, high ϕ has obvious functional benefits… We argue that awake brains have high ϕ for this **functional** reason."*

⚠️ **An asymmetry worth flagging: the unfolding argument's authors — Doerig, Schurger and Herzog — all three signed the 2023 pseudoscience letter and are all authors on the 2025 Nature Neuroscience version. This is the same group on both fronts; their critique is a single programme, not independent convergence.**

### The five published replies

**Reply 1 — Tsuchiya, Andrillon & Haun (2020).** *Consciousness and Cognition* 79:102877, doi:10.1016/j.concog.2020.102877. Abstract (from the PsyArXiv version), verbatim: *"**By their implicit treatment of phenomenal structure as impossible to study, the authors seem to be advocating for a new mode of extreme methodological behaviorism.** We take issue with their view… **critiquing three of their four premises**… We then explain **what makes causal structure theories of consciousness empirical and falsifiable**. Specifically, we propose that **consciousness science must work on ways to consider phenomenal structure - i.e. to derive the structure of experience from reports, and to search for isomorphism between physical and phenomenal structures.**"*

**The core move:** reject the premise that the only admissible evidence is input–output evidence; **replace the one-dimensional "report → present/absent" inference with a structure-to-structure isomorphism test.** This is what later became the "structural turn." ⚠️ **The published full text was not obtained (Elsevier 403; no green OA). Which three of the four premises they attack is therefore not verified from the source.**

**Reply 2 — Kleiner, "Brain states matter" (2020).** *Consciousness and Cognition* 85:102981. Abstract, verbatim: *"**We unravel this argument mathematically and prove that the premises of the argument imply a much stronger result according to which the observed problem holds for almost all theories of consciousness.** We find, however, that **one of the premises is unwarranted** and show that **if this premise is dropped, the argument ceases to work**. … **The premise in question is that measures of brain activity cannot be used in an empirical test of theories of consciousness.**"* (i.e. **P4**.)

**Reply 3 — Kleiner & Hoel (2021): what they actually proved.** "Falsification and consciousness," ***Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2021(1):niab001**, doi:10.1093/nc/niab001; PMC8052953; arXiv:2006.07390. ⚠️ **Erik Hoel is IIT-aligned** (co-author on Tononi et al.'s 2025 reply), so this is not a neutral paper — **but the formal results are general.**

Their setup: a theory applied to physical systems **P**; an experiment yields datasets **O**; each dataset `o` has **prediction content** `o_i` (internal observables, e.g. brain imaging) and **inference content** `o_r` (report/behaviour). The theory supplies `pred(o)`, the experimenter `inf(o)`. A **falsification** is `inf(o) ∉ pred(o)`. A **substitution** transforms the physical system so as to change the prediction content while holding the inference content fixed; a **universal substitution** exists for every possible inference content.

**Minimal informativeness assumption:** *"for every prediction pred(o), there is at least one other prediction pred(o′) which is different… **we do not consider theories of consciousness which have only a single prediction.**"*

**Result 1 (the substitution/falsification horn):**

- *Lemma 3.5.* *"If there is an o_r-substitution, there is an o_r-falsification."*
- *Proposition 3.7.* *"**If there is a universal substitution, either every single inference operation is wrong or the theory under consideration is already falsified.**"*
- *Proposition 3.9.* *"**If inference and prediction data are independent, universal substitutions exist.**"*
- ***Theorem 3.10 (main theorem).*** *"**If inference and prediction data are independent, either every single inference operation is wrong or the theory under consideration is already falsified.**"*

**Result 2 (the unfalsifiability horn):**

- *Definition 4.1.* A theory with no falsification over **all possible** datasets is **empirically unfalsifiable**. They distinguish this from the benign sense: *"not being falsifiable by the set of possible experiments per se is not a bad thing… **Specifically, a pathological dependence between inferences and predictions leads to theories which are unfalsifiable.**"*
- *Definition 4.2.* Inference and prediction data are **strictly dependent** if `∃f` such that `o_i = f(o_r)` for any `o`.
- ***Theorem 4.3.*** *"**If a theory of consciousness implies that inference and prediction data are strictly dependent, then it is either already falsified or empirically unfalsifiable.**"*
- **Which theories fall under it:** *"This is the case, e.g., for **behaviorist theories** of consciousness… The definition is also satisfied in the case where prediction data are always a **subset** of the inference data… **This arguably applies to global workspace theory (Baars 2005), the 'attention schema' theory of consciousness (Graziano and Webb 2015) or 'fame in the brain' (Dennett 1991) proposals.** … **(10) holds true whenever a theory of consciousness is under investigation where access consciousness determines phenomenal consciousness.**"*

**This is the decisive reversal of the unfolding argument's rhetorical structure: Doerig et al. said causal-structure theories are impaled and functionalist theories escape. Kleiner & Hoel prove the theories Doerig et al. recommended are the ones caught by the second horn.**

**Their explicit positioning:** *"**in contrast to other similar results (Doerig et al. 2019), we do not put the blame on individual theories of consciousness, but rather show that a key assumption that is usually being made is responsible for the problems**… **Whenever there is an experimental test of a theory of consciousness on some physical system which does not lead to a falsification, there necessarily exists another physical system which, if it had been tested, would have produced a falsification of that theory.** We emphasize that this problem **does not only affect one particular type of theory, e.g., those based on causal interactions like IIT**."* And: *"**theories of consciousness seem caught between Scylla and Charybdis**, requiring delicate navigation."*

**How they place the unfolding argument inside their framework:** *"**A special case thereof is the unfolding transformation considered in Doerig et al. (2019)**… The arguments in that article constitute a **proof of the fact that for ANNs, inference and prediction data are independent**. **Crucially, our main theorem shows that this has implications for all minimally informative theories of consciousness.**"* They give three further classes of universal substitution: ANNs; **universal computers** (*"if one believes it is possible for a sufficiently fast Turing machine to pass the Turing test, one needs to accept that substitutions exist"*); and **universal intelligences** (AIXI / Solomonoff induction).

⚠️ **They note IIT itself has already conceded the key fact:** *"within IIT it has already been proven that systems with different internal structures and hence different predicted experiences, **can have identical input/output** (Albantakis and Tononi 2019). … within IIT it has already been acknowledged that **a Turing machine may have a wildly different predicted contents of consciousness for the same behavior or reports (Koch 2019)**. Therefore, data independence during testing has already been shown to apply to IIT under its normal assumptions."*

**Their two proposed escapes:**

1. **"Lenient dependency."** *"there seems to be room for relationships that are neither independent … nor strictly dependent… **No current theory or testing paradigm that we know of satisfies this definition.**"* Achieving it would require either *"**novel forms of inference**"* — *"likely constitute a major change in the methodologies of experimental testing"* — or theories designed for it — *"likely constitute a major change in constructing theories of consciousness."*
2. **"Physics is not causally closed."** *"only consider theories of consciousness which do not describe the physical as causally closed (Kim 1998). That is, **the presence or absence of a particular experience itself would have to make a difference to the configuration, dynamics, or states of physical systems above and beyond what would be predicted with just information about the physical system itself.**"* ⚠️ **This is a substantial metaphysical price — it means abandoning causal closure — and should be presented as such.**

A supplementary section proves the results survive a **much weaker** independence condition ("Weak Independence"), using minimally-differing predictions in a distance structure on prediction space — and notes IIT's prediction space of Q-shapes carries such a distance function.

**Reply 4 — Usher (2021): denying P2.** *Consciousness and Cognition* 95:103212. Abstract, verbatim: *"**Here I refute UA by appealing to computational theory and cognitive-neuroscience. I argue that a robust functional equivalence between FFN and RN is not supported by the mathematical work on the Universal Approximator theorem, and is also unlikely to hold, as a conjecture, given data in cognitive neuroscience; I argue that an equivalence of RN and FFN can only apply to static functions between input/output layers and not to the temporal patterns or to the network's reactions to structural perturbations.**"*

**So Usher attacks P2 on three grounds:** the universal approximation theorem does not deliver *robust* functional equivalence; equivalence is restricted to **static** input–output maps, not temporal dynamics; and **it does not survive structural perturbation.** **This is the strongest technical objection to the unfolding step, and it dovetails with Tononi's "cause–effect power" framing: a feedforward unfold that matches behaviour will not match counterfactual/perturbational behaviour.**

**Reply 5 — Negro (2020): the phenomenology-first defence.** *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 19(5):979–996, doi:10.1007/s11097-020-09681-3. ⚠️ **Citation verified; abstract and content NOT verified** (closed access; no OA copy, no indexed abstract). From the title and the surrounding dispute, Negro defends IIT by arguing the unfolding argument **presupposes a third-person methodology that IIT explicitly rejects** — i.e. it begs the question against phenomenology-first theories. **Flag as inferred, not sourced.**

Related and verified by Negro: "Emergentist Integrated Information Theory," *Erkenntnis*, 14 Oct 2022 — *"**In the philosophical literature, IIT is often associated with a panpsychist worldview. In this paper, I show that IIT can be considered, instead, as a form of emer**[gentism]…"* — **an attempt to detach IIT from the panpsychism charge.**

### Doerig, Schurger & Herzog's responses

**(a) The "hard criteria" paper.** "Hard criteria for empirical theories of consciousness," ***Cognitive Neuroscience* 12(2):41–62** (online 14 July 2020), doi:10.1080/17588928.2020.1772214. Abstract, verbatim: *"**many Theories of Consciousness (ToCs) have been proposed. These theories are diverse in nature… This contrasts with other fields of natural science, which host a smaller number of competing theories. We suggest that one reason for this abundance of extremely different theories may be the lack of stringent criteria specifying how empirical data constrains ToCs.** First, we argue that **consciousness is a well-defined topic from an empirical point of view** and motivate **a purely empirical stance**… Second, we present **a checklist of criteria**… Third, we **review 13 of the most influential ToCs and subject them to the criteria.**"* Published with **open peer commentary** and a response (doi:10.1080/17588928.2020.1853086). ⚠️ **Full text not retrieved; the actual list of "hard criteria" is NOT verified.**

**(b) The direct rebuttal to Tsuchiya et al. — the final statement of their position, and the strongest version of the charge.** Herzog, Schurger & Doerig, "First-person experience cannot rescue causal structure theories from the unfolding argument," ***Consciousness and Cognition* 98:103261** (Feb 2022). Abstract, verbatim:

> *"**It seems that we and the critics agree that the main question in this debate is whether first-person experience, independent of third-person data, is a sufficient foundation for theories of consciousness.** Here, we argue that **pure first-person experience cannot be a scientific foundation for IIT because science relies on taking measurements, and pure first-person experience is not measurable except through reports, brain activity, and the relationship between them.** We also argue that **pure first-person experience cannot be taken as ground truth because science is about backing up theories with data, not about asserting that we have ground truth independent of data.** Lastly, we explain why **no experiment based on third-person data can test IIT as a theory of consciousness. IIT may be a good theory of something, but not of consciousness.** We conclude by exposing a deeper reason: **IIT's consciousness is by construction fully dissociated from any measurable thing and, for this reason, IIT implies that both the level and content of consciousness are epiphenomenal, with no causal power. IIT and other causal structure theories end up in a form of dissociative epiphenomenalism, in which we cannot even trust reports about first-person experiences. But reports about first-person experiences are taken as ground truth and the foundation for IIT's axioms. Therefore, accepting IIT leads to rejecting its own axioms.**"*

**The last three sentences are the sharpest form of the objection in the whole literature: a *self-undermining* charge, not merely an unfalsifiability charge.** The argument: IIT's axioms are grounded in introspective reports; IIT entails reports can come apart arbitrarily from consciousness (the unfolded zombie reports identically); so IIT undercuts the evidential standing of the very reports it is built on.

⚠️ **Note the irony this creates for the Tononi side:** in his 2014 reply to Aaronson, Tononi grounds everything in *"I know directly what it is like to be me,"* and in 2025 Tononi et al. write *"were it not for this irrefutable fact, there would be no evidence that experience exists."* **Herzog et al. take that first-person foundation to be exactly what IIT's own feedforward-zombie commitment destroys.**

**The current state, stated fairly:**

1. **Nobody in the exchange disputes the unfolding theorem's mathematics as applied to static input–output maps.** Tononi & Koch 2015 assert it themselves (*"any neural network with feedback circuits can be mapped onto a purely feed-forward network in such a manner that the latter approximates its input–output relationships (for computations bounded by a maximal time step)"*), and Findlay et al. 2024 prove a sharper version (**full** functional equivalence, not approximation, for a stored-program computer simulating a Boolean system). **The IIT side treats this as a feature, not a bug.**
2. **The dilemma's selectivity is the contested point, and Doerig et al. lost that point.** Kleiner 2020 and Kleiner & Hoel 2021 proved the problem generalizes to essentially all minimally informative theories if inference and prediction are independent, **and proved that the functionalist theories Doerig et al. recommended fall to the *other* horn.** The unfolding argument therefore does not single out IIT.
3. **Doerig et al.'s best remaining card is not the dilemma but the self-undermining charge** (Herzog et al. 2022).
4. **The strongest technical objection to the unfolding step itself is Usher's (2021):** universal approximation does not deliver equivalence under **perturbation** or in **temporal dynamics**. ⚠️ **This matters a great deal, because IIT's actual empirical programme (TMS–EEG, PCI) is perturbational rather than observational. If Usher is right, the "empirically indistinguishable" premise (P3) fails for the methods IIT actually uses.**
5. **Both sides have moved toward "structure."** Tsuchiya et al. 2020 → Kleiner 2024 → Tononi et al. 2025's endorsement of the structural turn. **A genuine convergence on methodology — but it does not resolve the dilemma:** Kleiner & Hoel's own verdict is that no current theory or testing paradigm achieves lenient dependency, and the alternative escape requires abandoning the causal closure of physics.
6. **Unresolved and widely acknowledged on both sides: the non-uniqueness problem.** The axioms do not uniquely fix Φ (Bayne 2018; Barrett & Mediano 2019); different Φ measures disagree empirically (Mediano, Seth & Barrett 2019); the formalism has had four major versions; **and Tononi himself conceded in 2014 that "even the correct set of axioms and corresponding postulates are still being developed and refined."**

## The exclusion postulate and the aggregation worries

### What the postulate says

Tononi & Koch 2015, verbatim:

> *"The cause–effect structure specified by the system must be **definite**: it is specified over **a single set of elements—neither less nor more—the one over which it is maximally irreducible (Φmax) from its intrinsic perspective, thus laying maximal claim to existence.** … Among these, the system that specifies the cause–effect structure that is maximally irreducible intrinsically is the set of elements ABC, rather than any of its subsets or supersets. **The exclusion postulate provides a sufficient reason why the contents of the experience should be what they are—neither less nor more.** … if a mechanism in a state … specifies a particular cause–effect repertoire within one system (ABC), it should not additionally specify an overlapping cause–effect repertoire as part of other, overlapping systems (say AB or ABCD), **otherwise one would be counting multiple times the difference that mechanism makes**."*

**The justification they offer — and this is precisely what the ad-hoc charge targets:**

> *"**The exclusion postulate can be said to enforce Occam's razor (entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity): it is more parsimonious to postulate the existence of a single cause–effect structure over a system of elements—the one that is maximally irreducible—than a multitude of overlapping cause–effect structures whose existence would make no further difference.**"*

It also applies to grain: *"**a mechanism cannot have effects at a fine temporal grain, and additional effects at a coarser grain, otherwise causal exclusion would be violated.**"*

Tononi et al. 2025's compressed restatement: *"**As required by the exclusion postulate, there can be no superposition of consciousnesses and no aggregate consciousness.** For example, if a set of neurons in posterior–central cortex constitutes the main complex, then **no subset, superset, or paraset of it, at any grain, can be conscious: not individual cortical areas, not the whole cortex, the whole brain, or the whole body; and given that each of us is conscious, there can be no collective or group consciousness.**"*

### The conscious-nation-state problem, and IIT's answer

Tononi & Koch 2015 confront it head-on — the best single source for this sub-debate:

> *"'**Take a sentence of a dozen words, and take twelve men and tell to each one word. Then stand the men in a row or jam them in a bunch, and let each think of his word as intently as he will; nowhere will there be a consciousness of the whole sentence**'. This is how **William James** illustrated the combination problem of panpsychism. Or take **John Searle**: '**Consciousness cannot spread over the universe like a thin veneer of jam; there has to be a point where my consciousness ends and yours begins**'. **Indeed, if consciousness is everywhere, why should it not animate the United States of America?** IIT deals squarely with this problem by stating that **only maxima of integrated information exist**. Consider two people talking… Now let the two speak together. They will now form a system that is also irreducible (Φ > zero) due to their interactions. **However, it is not maximally irreducible**… **According to IIT, there should indeed be two separate experiences, but no superordinate conscious entity that is the union of the two. In other words, there is nothing-it-is-like-to-be two people, let alone the 300 plus million citizens making up the USA.** … **by the exclusion postulate, only the five smaller complexes exist, since they are local maxima of integrated information (Φmax = 0.19), while the larger system is not a complex (Φ = 0.03). Worse, a dumb thing with hardly any intrinsically distinguishable states, say a grain of sand … has no experience whatsoever. And heaping a large number of such zero-Φ systems on top of each other would not increase their Φ to a non-zero value: to be a sand dune does not feel like anything either—aggregates have no consciousness.**"*

⚠️ **Note the dialectical structure: the exclusion postulate is doing exactly the job the critics say it was introduced to do.** IIT's reply is that it was not introduced *for* that job — it is derived from the "definiteness" axiom and motivated by Occam's razor — **and that Tononi explicitly forbids postulate-patching for intuition-saving.** Whether the axiom genuinely delivers the postulate, independently of the conclusion it rescues, is the contested question.

**Also in the same paper: "many conscious minds within a single brain."** *"IIT also allows for the possibility of **two or more complexes coexisting within a single system**… there may well exist … a major complex and one or more **minor complexes**… **In split-brain patients the speaking, major complex is unaware of the presence of another consciousness**… **This counterintuitive scenario of 'many conscious minds within a single brain' could be assessed, at least in principle, by measurements of integrated information at the neuronal level.**"*

### The "two brains joined by a cable" claim — the actual source

This is **footnote 13 of Tononi & Koch 2015**, attached to the USA passage, and it is the sharpest bullet IIT bites:

> *"**By the same token, the exclusion postulate predicts a scenario that is the mirror image of the prediction that consciousness will suddenly split in two when the corpus callosum is 'cooled' below a critical point: if two people speaking were to increase their effective causal interactions by some, yet to be invented, direct brain-to-brain connectivity booster, to the point where the Φmax of the two interacting brains would exceed Φmax of the individual brains, their individual conscious mind would disappear and its place would be taken by a new Über-mind that subsumes both.**"*

**So: yes, IIT accepts fusion, and accepts that the two original subjects cease to exist.** The paired split prediction appears in Tononi's 2014 reply as a listed empirical prediction: *"**it predicts that if the corpus callosum … were progressively inactivated, there would be a discontinuity at which a single consciousness would split into two independent ones.**"*

**And exclusion applied to time yields a discrete stream** — footnote 11: *"The exclusion postulate requires that the set of mechanisms that specify one particular experience do so over the time window at which Φ reaches a maximum. If the next experience involves an overlapping set of mechanisms, it would seem that, to avoid multiple causation, it should be specified over a non-overlapping time window. **Accordingly, the seemingly continuous 'stream' of consciousness would actually be constituted by a discrete succession of 'snapshots'**, in line with some psychophysical evidence."* **Bayne 2018 attacks precisely this corollary.**

### Schwitzgebel's conscious-USA argument — and why he withdrew it

- *"Of our four theorists [Dennett, Dretske, Humphrey, Tononi], neuroscientist **Giulio Tononi's view (2004, 2008; Balduzzi and Tononi 2009) enables the quickest argument to the consciousness of the United States.**"*
- *"He says that '**even a binary photodiode is not completely unconscious, but rather enjoys exactly 1 bit of consciousness**' (2008, p. 236)… Likewise, **Tononi attributes 'qualia' (that is, consciousness) to simple logical AND and OR gates**."*

### Bayne 2018: the axioms cannot do the work demanded of them

**Bayne T., "On the axiomatic foundations of the integrated information theory of consciousness," *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2018(1):niy007**, doi:10.1093/nc/niy007; PMC6030813. **The most careful philosophical attack on IIT's foundations**, cited by the unfolding argument, by the 2025 published letter, and by the Cogitate critics.

Abstract, verbatim: *"**I begin by asking what exactly the axiomatic method involves, arguing that it is open to multiple interpretations. I then examine the five axioms of IIT, asking: what each axiom means, whether it is indeed axiomatic and whether it could constrain a theory of consciousness. I argue that none of the five alleged axioms is able to play the role that is required of it, either because it fails to qualify as axiomatic or because it fails to impose a substantive constraint on a theory of consciousness. The article concludes by briefly sketching an alternative methodology for the science of consciousness: the natural kind approach.**"*

**The dilemma he runs on every axiom — the structural shape of the whole paper: either an axiom is genuinely self-evident, in which case it is too weak to constrain any theory; or it constrains substantively, in which case it is not self-evident.**

**His standard for "axiomatic," sourced to IIT itself:** *"'axioms are **self-evident truths about consciousness—the only truths that, with Descartes, cannot be doubted and do not need proof**' (Oizumi et al. 2014, 2). The idea seems to be that **if a thesis is a genuine axiom, then its truth must be evident to any conscious creature who is able to understand it and has subjected it to serious reflection. The fact that there are reasonable individuals who, having reflected on a thesis, fail to find it compelling, not only provides evidence that it's not axiomatic but arguably makes it the case that it isn't axiomatic.** … **What matters is whether the truth of the relevant thesis is regarded as self-evident within the community of consciousness researchers.**"* He also notes the stakes: *"**It seems evident that the axiomatic approach is meant to establish the truth of IIT**."*

**Axiom by axiom:**

- **Intrinsic existence.** *"any substantive theory of consciousness presupposes that consciousness is a genuine feature of the world… **The only accounts … that might fall foul of this constraint are certain versions of fictionalism or eliminativism, but the advocates of those views won't accept the axiom of intrinsic existence in the first place.**"* On the second reading: *"**A second claim … is that consciousness is an intrinsic property.** … Although many scientists appear to regard this claim as self-evidently true, **few philosophers do. According to externalist accounts of consciousness, an entity's conscious state is constitutively dependent on its history and/or relations to its environment** … These 'externalist' accounts … are **certainly not self-evidently wrong**."* Verdict: *"**The claim that consciousness exists might indeed be axiomatic but it fails to impose a substantive constraint on theories of consciousness, while the claim that consciousness is a purely intrinsic property imposes a substantive constraint on theories of consciousness but isn't axiomatic.**"*
- **Composition.** *"**it is implausible to suppose that the mere existence of phenomenal differentiation could provide a useful constraint… After all, every extant theory of consciousness recognizes that consciousness contains phenomenal differentiation of various kinds.** What they disagree about is the nature of that differentiation… **But the axiom of composition says only that consciousness is differentiated, and this claim has no bearing on those debates.**"*
- **Information.** *"The axiom of information is **one of the most puzzling**."* He distinguishes three readings: *"**It is no doubt true that each experience 'is the particular way that it is', but the same can be said of any phenomenon (every toaster is the particular way that it is; every hamster is the particular way that it is; and so on). Tautologies may be self-evident but they illuminate nothing.**"* On the conceptual-role-semantics reading: *"**even if information3 is true its truth is hardly self-evident.** … **One problem is that it entails that no two people can share any one content (or concept) unless they share all of their contents… Not only is that implication deeply implausible in its own right, it is also at odds with the practice of consciousness science.**"*
- **Integration.** He reconstructs it as **representational unity**: *"Two experiences (e1, e2) are representationally unified if (and only if) they occur as components of a complex experience whose content cannot be identified with the conjunction of the contents of e1 and e2. For example, the experience of a word as a token of 'SONO' cannot be identified with a conjunction of an experience of 'SO' and an experience of 'NO'."* ⚠️ His further argument against its axiomatic status was not extracted verbatim — **partially sourced.**
- **Exclusion — the one most relevant here, and he attacks it three ways:**
  - On content determinacy: *"**their only explicit commitment is to the idea that certain contents are determinately present in one's experience … and certain contents are determinately not present… But if the axiom of exclusion is understood in this manner, then it is unclear what constraints it places on a theory of consciousness, for every theory of consciousness holds that some contents are determinately present and some are determinately not present.**"*
  - On the no-vagueness reading: *"**Would that claim be axiomatic? No.** Although some theorists have argued on a priori grounds that consciousness is not vague …, **that view is controversial and is rejected by a number of other theorists**… **Consider an experience of a striped tiger. Does this experience represent the tiger as having a precise number of visible stripes—say, 78?** Possibly; but it is prima facie more plausible to suppose that one's visual experience contains some indeterminacy… **It is doubtful whether there is any plausible version of the axiom of exclusion that focuses on the contents of consciousness.**"*
  - **On the temporal-grain reading — a direct hit on footnote 11 of Tononi & Koch 2015:** *"**Suppose that an 'instant of consciousness' … has a particular duration ('D') irrespective of the kind of creature in question or its state of consciousness. If that were the case, then we might begin to suspect that D was an essential feature of consciousness** … **But we have no reason to think that there is a particular duration that characterizes consciousness in all kinds of creatures and all kinds of conscious states. Indeed, immediately after claiming that the duration of an instant of consciousness is 'definite', Tononi et al. go on to say that this duration ranges from 'a few tens of milliseconds to a few hundred milliseconds'. In other words, the architects of IIT themselves regard the duration of consciousness as variable. But if that is the case, then what kind of constraint could the axiom of exclusion place on a theory of consciousness?**"*

**His positive proposal** is "the **natural kind approach**" ⚠️ (sketched in the conclusion; **not extracted verbatim**). Note he explicitly motivates the scrutiny by the clinical stakes: *"It functions as a guiding framework for a great deal of research into the neural basis of consciousness **and for attempts to develop a consciousness meter. In light of these developments, it is important to examine whether its foundations are secure.**"*

⚠️ **Why Bayne matters for the exclusion/ad-hoc charge specifically:** if the exclusion axiom imposes no substantive phenomenological constraint, then the exclusion *postulate* cannot be said to be derived from phenomenology — **and then Tononi's own prohibition on "ad hoc prescriptions that are not grounded in phenomenology, but merely accommodate some cherished intuition" bites against IIT itself, since exclusion is exactly what blocks the conscious-USA conclusion.** **That is the strongest form of the ad-hoc-patch objection — but note it is an inference from combining Bayne 2018 with Tononi 2014, and was not found stated in those terms in any single source.**

### "Φ is not well-defined" — the Barrett/Mediano/Seth line

**(a) Barrett & Mediano, "The phi measure of integrated information is not well-defined for general physical systems," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 26(1–2):11–20 (2019).** ⚠️ **Citation verified from Doerig et al.'s reference list; content NOT obtained** (JCS not indexed in Crossref/OpenAlex for this item). What *is* verified is **how the critics use it**: Doerig et al. cite it (with Bayne 2018) for *"the axioms do not uniquely determine ϕ."* The title asserts the measure is defined only for discrete, finite, time-stepped Markovian models, **so the theory's universal scope is not underwritten by its formalism.**

**(b) Mediano, Seth & Barrett, "Measuring Integrated Information: Comparison of Candidate Measures in Theory and Simulation," *Entropy* 21(1):17** (2018/19). Abstract: *"**While several candidate measures of integrated information ('Φ') now exist, little is known about how they compare, especially in terms of their behaviour on non-trivial network models.**"* **The finding Doerig et al. cite it for: "different empirical measures of ϕ yield very different results."**

**(c) Mediano, Rosas, Bor, Seth & Barrett, "The strength of weak integrated information theory," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 26(8):646–655 (2022)** — **the most constructive critique in the literature, and the one that best isolates what is and is not defensible:**

> *"**The integrated information theory of consciousness (IIT) is divisive: while some believe it provides an unprecedentedly powerful approach to address the 'hard problem', others dismiss it on grounds that it is untestable. We argue that the appeal and applicability of IIT can be greatly widened if we distinguish two flavours of the theory: strong IIT, which identifies consciousness with specific properties associated with maxima of integrated information; and weak IIT, which tests pragmatic hypotheses relating aspects of consciousness to broader measures of information dynamics. We review challenges for strong IIT, explain how existing empirical findings are well explained by weak IIT without needing to commit to the entirety of strong IIT.**"*

⚠️ **Note that "strong IIT" is defined by the maxima — i.e. by the exclusion postulate.** The strong/weak distinction is what the 2023 letter then uses to scope its attack (*"Our target was not what is sometimes known as empirical IIT or **weak IIT**"* — Lau), and what the published 2025 letter formalizes in its Box 1: *"**If proponents of IIT were to give up the core claims (axioms), some weaker version of the theory could perhaps be cast in terms of cognitive neuroscience** … **However, to the extent that IIT's metaphysical commitments remain promoted as key features, this stronger version of IIT — which is our target here — is incompatible with standard approaches in cognitive neuroscience for testing ideas empirically.**"*

**(d) IIT's reply to the Φ-incomputability charge** (Tononi et al. 2025, Box 2) — the best statement of their side:

> *"'**It is impossible in principle to compute Φ.**' — Defining integrated information in a way that fully and uniquely captures IIT's postulates has been a long theoretical effort (starting in the early 1990s), **which is finally approaching its goal with IIT 4.0. For simple causal models, it is possible to calculate all relevant quantities exhaustively, not just in principle, but in practice. For larger causal models, the measures, although well-defined, can be estimated only under various assumptions and approximations** … **This is not dissimilar to the situation in quantum (bio)chemistry in which textbook examples typically deal with a very small number of degrees of freedom, and solving the complete Schrödinger equation for entire proteins remains computationally infeasible.**"*
> *"**if estimates of integrated information were higher for a model of molecular interactions within a neuron than for a model of synaptic interactions among neurons in a dense cortical lattice, IIT would be challenged**, regardless of what might be the case below the grain of molecules."*
> *"**computing integrated information for larger models (say, connectomes) is an interesting mathematical challenge, rather than an impossibility 'in principle'.**"*
> *"it should give some pause to see IIT proscribed because … **it also offers a mathematical framework and precisely defined measures. How would this be a minus and not a plus? After all, the computational-functionalist paradigm offers no principled approach to establishing the border and grain of the neural substrate of consciousness, no account for its quality, and no measure for its quantity.**"*
> On the letter's pendulum analogy: *"**This criticism is incomprehensible, because IIT does just this. It derives the quality and quantity of the experience supported by a substrate starting from its transition probability matrix (TPM) or from its causal model, which can be transformed into a TPM. There is nothing comparable in the literature on consciousness.**"*
> **And the counter-charge of a double standard on "complexity" measures:** *"A growing number of studies are indeed finding that various 'complexity' measures are higher in conscious than unconscious states. **However, this was many years after the publication of the neural complexity paper**… Moreover, **the measures are typically non-causal, and they lack a principled connection to the properties of consciousness. Instead, IIT's integrated information is a special, causal kind of complexity** (hence calling for causal probing of the brain with TMS), **dictated by the properties of experience and often counterintuitive.**"*
> **They also name a possible defeater:** *"**data from lesion, stimulation, and recording studies are open to refinements, revisions, and reinterpretations, and may eventually point to a location inconsistent with IIT's predictions. IIT could also be challenged by a more detailed understanding of cortical fine connectivity that may reverse current neuroanatomical assumptions.**"*
> And on the Overreach/Incompatibility/Misrepresentation box: *"**The signatories' resort to such circumstantial evidence to make a case for pseudoscience is like using sulfuric smells to make a case for witchcraft, and just as compelling. If anything, it signals awareness that their argument lacks fundamental merit.**"*

⚠️ **Note the upper-bound numbers IIT itself gives (Box 3), which bear directly on Aaronson's "unboundedly more conscious than humans" worry:** *"IIT is unique in providing a principled measure of the quantity of consciousness (Φ). This makes it possible to estimate its bounds, **which can grow double-exponentially with the number of intrinsic units of the complex** … **Thus, a complex of 2 units could have at most 14 ibits of Φ, one of 1,000 units 2^2^1,000 (~10^10^300) ibits, and the main complex in the human brain presumably much more than that.**"*

### Cerullo 2015: exclusion is unjustified, and IIT has a trivial rival

**Cerullo M.A., "The Problem with Phi: A Critique of Integrated Information Theory," *PLOS Computational Biology* 11(9):e1004286**, 17 September 2015. Open access; Perspective article.

Summary, verbatim: *"**The goal of this paper is to show that IIT fails in its stated goal of quantifying consciousness. … The main theoretical argument for the relevance of integrated information to consciousness is the principle of information exclusion. Yet, no justification is given to support this principle. Tononi claims there is significant empirical support for IIT, but this is called into question by the creation of a trivial theory of consciousness with equal explanatory power. … Since IIT is not a form of computational functionalism, it is vulnerable to fading/dancing qualia arguments.**"*

**Four distinct lines, each independently worth citing:**

1. **Exclusion is unmotivated.** *"**Yet, no justification is given to support this principle.**"* — **the cleanest published statement of the ad-hoc-patch charge, and it predates both the unfolding argument and the open letter.**
2. **The trivial-rival argument.** Construct a deliberately trivial theory with equal explanatory power over the same clinical data (cortex vs cerebellum, sleep, anaesthesia, seizures), **thereby showing the data do not discriminate.** This is the same move Fleming later makes in Bayesian form, and the same move the open letter makes about complexity measures.
3. **Fading/dancing qualia.** Because IIT rejects computational functionalism, it is exposed to Chalmers's fading-qualia and dancing-qualia arguments — gradual neuron-by-neuron replacement with functionally identical but Φ-destroying components would produce either an implausible gradual fading of experience or an implausible dissociation between experience and judgments about it. ⚠️ **This is a different and in some ways deeper objection than the unfolding argument, because it targets the *gradual* case and so cannot be answered by appeal to the implausibility of a fully unfolded brain.**
4. **Limits of the phenomenological method** — the axiomatic approach itself (the same target as Bayne 2018).

### Other published exclusion-postulate objections

**Blackmon J., "Integrated Information Theory, Intrinsicality, and Overlapping Conscious Systems," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 28(11):31–[?]** (2021), doi:10.53765/20512201.28.11.031. Abstract, verbatim (partial): *"**Integrated information theory (IIT) identifies consciousness with having a maximum amount of integrated information. But a thing's having the maximum amount of anything cannot be intrinsic to it, for that depends on how that thing compares to certain other things. IIT's consciousness, then, is not intrinsic. A mereological argument elaborates this consequence: IIT implies that one physical sys**[tem]…"*

⚠️ **This is a sharp and underused objection: the exclusion postulate makes Φmax a *comparative* property, and comparative properties are not intrinsic — so exclusion is in direct tension with the intrinsicality axiom it is supposed to sit alongside.** Whether a given set of elements is conscious depends on facts about *other*, overlapping sets. **Combined with Bayne's result that the intrinsicality axiom is either trivial or not self-evident, this is the strongest internal-consistency pressure on the postulate set.** ⚠️ Abstract truncated in the index; **full argument not verified.**

### The IIT side's own defence of exclusion, assembled

For fairness, the full positive case as it appears in primary sources:

1. **It is derived, not bolted on.** Presented as the fifth axiom (*"definite, 'this whole', containing all it contains, neither less nor more"*) translated into the fifth postulate, in the same way as the other four.
2. **Independent motivation: no double-counting of causal difference.** *"otherwise one would be counting multiple times the difference that mechanism makes."* **This is a causal-exclusion principle with a pedigree in metaphysics independent of consciousness.**
3. **Occam's razor** (quoted above).
4. **It generates testable predictions, not just blocks conclusions:** the corpus-callosum split prediction, the brain-bridge fusion prediction, the discrete-snapshot stream, "no collective consciousness," and the grain predictions.
5. **It is what rules IIT out of classical panpsychism** — the thing the critics say the theory is, exclusion is the reason it isn't: *"**Unlike panpsychism, however, IIT clearly implies that not everything is conscious**"* (2015); *"**there can be no superposition of consciousnesses and no aggregate consciousness**"* (2025).
6. **Tononi's own anti-ad-hoc rule cuts both ways and he states it unprompted.** **Critics must therefore argue that exclusion fails *his* test, not merely that it is convenient — which is exactly what Cerullo 2015 ("no justification is given"), Bayne 2018 (the axiom imposes no constraint) and Blackmon 2021 (it contradicts intrinsicality) try to do.**
7. **A defender's reframing worth noting:** Tononi & Koch present exclusion as IIT's *solution* to the combination problem that defeated panpsychism — *"IIT offers a solution to several of the conceptual obstacles that panpsychists never properly resolved, **like the problem of aggregates (or combination problem)**."* So on their account **exclusion is not a patch against a reductio but a positive contribution to a pre-existing open problem in philosophy of mind.** ⚠️ Mørch's *Erkenntnis* 2018 paper is, on its title, an assessment of exactly this claim; **her verdict was not obtained.**

### The 2023 letter's actual arguments

The preprint is short — one page of argument. **It opens with a disclaimer both sides later leaned on:** *"**This short statement was intended as a 'Letter to the Editor'-type of article, with the general public as the intended audience. Following feedback from the community we are currently in the process of revising and expanding it into a longer article that will flesh out the rationale behind the letter; this is not the final version.**"*

**(a) The trigger, stated in the letter itself:**

> *"**The media, including news articles in both Nature and Science, have recently celebrated the Integrated Information Theory (IIT) as a 'leading' and empirically tested theory of consciousness.** We are writing as researchers with some relevant expertise to express our concerns.*
> ***The media coverage sprang from a public event where the authors of a large-scale adversarial collaboration shared their findings, which were reported as empirically testing and partially supporting IIT. This message was communicated directly to journalists and the general public prior to the preprint being available, and hence, prior to peer-review.** The experiments seem very skillfully executed by a large group of trainees across different labs. **However, by design the studies only tested some idiosyncratic predictions made by certain theorists, which are not really logically related to the core ideas of IIT, as one of the authors himself also acknowledges.** The findings therefore do not support the claims that the theory itself was actually meaningfully tested, or that it holds a 'dominant', 'well-established', or 'leading' status. **This important nuance was unfortunately lost in the media coverage.** These claims of dominance have also been questioned in the scientific community, yet **they have been repeatedly broadcast to the public by proponents of IIT over the years.**"*

⚠️ **"as one of the authors himself also acknowledges" is footnote 8 = the Cogitate preprint — i.e. Dehaene's own discussion-section remark.**

**(b) The substantive charge — the four specific consequences they object to:**

> *"According to IIT, **an inactive grid of connected logic gates that are not performing any useful computation can be conscious—possibly even more so than humans** [ref 17 = Aaronson 2014]; **organoids created out of petri-dishes, as well as human fetuses at very early stages of development, are likely conscious** according to the theory; **on some interpretations, even plants may be conscious**. **These claims have been widely considered untestable, unscientific, 'magicalist', or a 'departure from science as we know it'. Given its panpsychist commitments, until the theory as a whole—not just some hand-picked auxiliary components trivially shared by many others or already known to be true—is empirically testable, we feel that the pseudoscience label should indeed apply.**"*

**The three downstream citations — the letter's evidence that these are not strawmen:**

### What exactly triggered it: the ASSC event and the Koch–Chalmers bet

- **The meeting.** ASSC annual meeting, **New York, June 2023.** Fleming (on the Scientific Committee) calls it *"the 25th anniversary meeting in New York,"* notes *"a record **700 attendees**,"* co-directed by **David Chalmers and Ned Block**; keynotes by **May-Britt Moser, Doris Tsao and Yoshua Bengio**. ⚠️ **Official dates / whether it was ASSC 26 not confirmed — use "ASSC annual meeting, New York, June 2023."**
- **The public event.** Fleming: *"the write-up in the media of **the public event on Thursday evening, when the Cogitate results were revealed**."* Lau (who was **not** present): *"Cogitate also elected to present the first release of some of their results in **a promoted public event in New York City, with journalists known to be present**."* The event was *"associated with, but not part of, the main ASSC conference,"* and per Lau *"my understanding is that the conference organizer Dave Chalmers did not have the details of the public event discussed and formally approved by the relevant scientific program committees. **So the event was pretty much organized by Dave, with the full intention (as acknowledged by himself, in communication on social media) to draw public and media attention to the Cogitate presentation, through live music performance, and an announcement of the outcome of his personal 'bet' on the state of consciousness science with Christof Koch.**"*

The bettors were neuroscientist **Christof Koch** and philosopher **David Chalmers**… **Chalmers won. Koch paid up.**"* Terms: *"Koch—a natural optimist—believed that **25 years was more than enough time for scientists to uncover the neural correlates of consciousness**… **In 1998, the pair staked a crate of fine wine on the outcome.**"* Settlement: *"**Koch graciously handed Chalmers a bottle of Madeira on the conference stage.**"* And crucially: *"**What helped resolve the wager was the outcome, or rather the lack of a decisive outcome, of an 'adversarial collaboration'** organized by a consortium called COGITATE."* Koch's response: he was *"keen to double down on his bet."*

However, there was considerable backlash as the original letter had much fervor and little fact.**"* And a pointed timing claim of their own: *"**The original 'pseudoscience' letter appeared at the end of a nine-day course on IIT**, as it takes some effort to understand what the theory actually says."*

- **The scoping move (new):** *"**We distinguish the core claims of IIT, which we argue are unscientific, from work merely inspired by IIT that uses measures of complexity to investigate brain activity associated with different states of wakefulness. This latter work is uncontroversially scientific, but neither supports nor tests the core claims of IIT.**"*
- The axioms: *"IIT postulates that 'consciousness is a fundamental feature of the universe'… However, some have criticized these axioms and have argued that they lead to circularity. **The axioms have also evolved, which casts doubt on their 'irrefutability'.**"*
- The derivation gap: *"**the axioms do not by themselves specify a particular mathematical model. To develop a precise formula for Φ, further assumptions must be made. This challenge becomes evident in the evolving interpretations and understandings of the theory and in the changes in formulas that define Φ over time.**"*
- **The in-principle untestability claim — the paper's core:** *"An obvious test of IIT would be to measure Φ in humans. To calculate Φ one needs to consider **how every component of the system at every spatial and temporal scale — say from the quantum scale to brain regions, and from nanoseconds to days — constrains the past and future states of the system. Given the complexity of these calculations, it is impossible in principle to compute Φ, and hence impossible to identify conscious states — a point that proponents of IIT readily admit.**"*
- **The "souls" line:** *"the theory readily ascribes consciousness to static systems that do not perform any function. **Stating that such systems have 'consciousness', when their 'consciousness' has no observable consequences is, in this respect, no different than stating that they have 'souls'.**"* And: *"**Some consider the theory dismissible on the basis of these implications alone. However, proponents of IIT embrace this counterintuitive consequence without calling their theoretical assumptions into question.**"*
- **The demarcation principle they actually commit to:** *"**Making theoretical predictions that may not be testable by current methodology does not necessarily render the relevant theories unscientific. However, if the core claims of a theory are not testable even in principle, the scientific status of the theory should be called into question.**"*
- **The pendulum demand** (which Tononi et al. call "incomprehensible"): *"**What would be needed to make it a scientific theory is a measure that directly derived from the mathematical apparatus of IIT, akin to how a particular measurement of the motion of a pendulum — such as instantaneous velocity — is directly derived from the harmonic oscillation equation. Yet, the measures used by IIT-inspired studies do not assay Φ specifically, but only some more common notion of complexity.**"*
- **The complexity-measures critique — their answer to PCI:** *"**It is not surprising that human states of conscious awareness show greater neural complexity than states of unconsciousness, and this is likely to be true using almost any sensible measure of complexity.** Indeed, many other theories of consciousness also predict lower brain complexity during certain nonawake states… **Testing the hypothesis that conscious versus nonconscious states can be distinguished … does not distinguish IIT from these other theories.**"* Plus the confound: *"**the comparison of different global states, such as wakefulness and coma, is confounded by many different cognitive and behavioral factors. In particular, much of brain activity during wakefulness is not conscious, so a neural correlate of a waking state is not necessarily a neural correlate of consciousness as a mental state.**"*
- **Box 1** sets the contrast class explicitly (GNWT, higher-order theories, local recurrence theory) and **concedes their predictions "are often not quantitatively precise, but they are nonetheless constrained by the empirical literature."**
- **Box 2, "Evaluating the scientific status of a theory by applying criteria to IIT" — four named criteria beyond untestability:**
  - **Overreach.** *"Recent developments have seen the theory expanding into areas that traditionally belong to metaphysics, by asserting bold claims about the nature of reality, causation and **the nonexistence of neurons**. … IIT also ambitiously addresses fundamental existential questions, such as **the essence of being and the presence of free will**, elevating the theory to an '**all-encompassing explanatory framework**'. **According to some, this kind of ambition is a common hallmark of pseudoscientific theories.**"*
  - **Incompatibility with physics.** *"**Doubts have been raised regarding IIT's compatibility with current physics… The theory's proponents acknowledge these compatibility issues, yet IIT continues to be promoted as a promising or well-established scientific theory.**"* ⚠️ **The citation given is IIT 4.0 itself — i.e. they claim IIT's own flagship paper concedes the tension.**
  - **Misrepresentation and promotion.** *"**Although misrepresentation itself does not make the theory unscientific, it suggests that the label 'pseudoscience' may be appropriate.**"*
  - **The pseudoscience criterion.** *"**Standard dictionary definitions and philosophical discussions describe pseudoscience as featuring unscientific claims (for example, untestable in principle) that are falsely presented as scientific. This criterion applies to IIT as it portrays untestable assertions as empirically grounded science.**"* **They anchor the demarcation criterion in Hansson's SEP entry "Science and Pseudo-Science."**
- **The ethics paragraph, now more specific:** *"By promoting to the general public the untestable idea that '**Φ = consciousness**', proponents of IIT may ultimately have an unjustified effect on law and policy, including on decisions that involve **measures of quality of life, clinical triage, abortion, the rights of non-responsive patients, and welfare considerations for insects, organoids and artificial intelligences.**"*
- **Conclusion:** *"**Our main concerns with IIT are twofold. First, it lacks well-defined empirically testable consequences. Second, it explicitly asserts or entails bold nonempirical claims, such as that 'the entire cosmos is suffused with sentience', that neurons do not truly exist or that consciousness can be attributed to a static array of logic gates. These bold claims threaten to delegitimize the scientific study of consciousness.**"*

⚠️ **The source for the "overreach" charge** is **Tononi, Albantakis, Boly, Cirelli & Koch, "Only what exists can cause: An intrinsic powers view of free will," arXiv:2206.02069, 4 June 2022**: *"If IIT is right, **we do have free will in a genuine sense: we have alternatives, reasons, and values, we make decisions, and we—not our neurons or atoms—are the cause of our willed actions** and bear responsibility for them. … captured by its **intrinsic powers ontology: what exists absolutely, in physical terms, are intrinsic entities, and only what exists can cause.**"* **Tononi et al. 2025 confirm the commitment: "if IIT is right, it follows that we can have true free will, as opposed to the illusion of free will."**

### Lau's own definition of "pseudoscience" — the key clarification document

Lau, "What is a Pseudoscience of Consciousness?", PsyArXiv, **17 Sept 2023** (one day after the letter), doi:10.31234/osf.io/28z3y.

- **His three-part definition:** *"There is a colloquial sense in which pseudoscience refers to **i) a set of important claims with far-reaching implications that are ii) neither currently supported by science nor are they likely to be so in the foreseeable future (perhaps even in principle), and yet they iii) masquerade as being already scientifically tested and established. In this sense, IIT, specifically the panpsychist version championed by its originator and the most dedicated proponents (who are part of Cogitate), satisfies all three criteria.**"*
- **The flat-earth disanalogy — his answer to "is IIT really as bad as homeopathy?":** *"'**Pseudoscience' is not just another word for 'bad science' or 'empirically false hypotheses'. 'Pseudo' means fake. For IIT, it is the drastic discrepancy between ii and iii that concerns many of us. Flat earth theory is clearly wrong but we don't see articles appearing in Science, Nature, The New York Times, The Economist, NewScientist, etc, repeatedly, over many years, sometimes by authoritative figures, proclaiming that it is a leading, empirically tested, and well established scientific theory. Therefore, flat earth theory is, in a sense, more 'harmless' and less threatening than the rise of IIT and panpsychism in the media.**"*
- ⚠️ **He concedes the Popperian criterion is dead** and shifts to a sociological/Lakatosian basis: *"the simplistic Popperian notion of '**unfalsifiability' has long fallen out of favor within modern philosophy of science**… the problem with psychoanalytic theory, for example, is not that all the relevant claims are unfalsifiable even in principle. **It has more to do with how the Freudians reacted to empirical challenges, rendering themselves unfalsifiable in practice.**"* **Note the tension with the 2025 paper's thesis, which is squarely in-principle untestability — Lau had by then withdrawn from editing it.**
- **The family-resemblance list, attributed to co-authors:** *"IIT shares many common features with other pseudoscientific ideas: **that it is unresponsive to empirical challenges; that it uses an unnecessarily complex and impractical language that diverges from mainstream science; that its popularity is mainly driven by the opinions by a few authoritative figures, and populist appeal, rather than consensus within the scientific community or empirical success.**"*
- **"Pseudoscience" as a speech-act, not a verdict** — his most-criticized move, in his own words: *"**the usage of the word could also be understood as a speech-act rather than a philosophical exercise. Like crying for 'help!', it is not necessary to first analytically define what 'help' means. Of course, we don't cry for help unless we need to. If we do it too lightly, nobody will take us seriously in the future.**"* Hence the collective letter: *"**although many researchers within the community have long considered the theory in question to be so problematic as to be deserving of the 'pseudoscience' label, most have been reluctant to unilaterally say this in print. The unfortunate consequence is that the general public, editors, funders, new trainees, and policy makers may not be aware of our common academic perspective… The joint statement was, in a sense, primarily written for them.**"* And: *"one could also consider our joint statement as **a signal of SOS (Save Our Science)**… **Unpleasant and difficult as it was, writing the statement was simply a last resort.**"*
- ⚠️ **He is explicit that the letter is not a scientific contribution:** *"It should be read like a '**Letter to Editor**'-type of document, not a research paper. **It was not meant to be a comprehensive review of the scientific literature on the various problems of IIT. Nor was it meant to provide a novel argument against IIT. In fact, the short statement did not even explain what IIT is.** … **Those who are looking for a detailed review of existing or novel scientific arguments against IIT will understandably be disappointed.**"*
- ⚠️ **Not everyone invited signed, and signatories disagreed about the word:** *"**Although many people co-signed and endorsed the statement, not everyone invited did. Indeed, some strongly opposed the use of the word 'pseudoscience', and questioned what we really mean by that. For those who signed, some did so despite having some reservations.**"*
- **On the deliberate inclusion of outsiders:** *"**We deliberately invited a small number of authors from outside of the field … because we wanted to make sure there is diversity in representation, and to show that this is not an insider dispute between theory camps. In a sense, these authors served as our outside witnesses.**"*
- **On the funding allegation** — his clarification: *"the best I could hope for is **to reduce some of its unfair advantage in private funding** … **So, reducing unfair funding advantage was of course not the motivation for calling IIT pseudoscience - that would indeed be ridiculous - it is instead what I myself would see as a fair outcome**… This is my own view on the matter of funding implications, **and not necessarily shared by others**."* He discloses: *"**I myself have declined this kind of private funding in the past (Cogitate). Like most of the co-authors, I do not currently compete for this kind of funding.**"*
- **Chalmers's and a policymaker's counter-argument, and Lau's reply:** *"**Jacob Jolij**, who identifies as a 'senior policy maker'… **suggested that calling out anything as pseudoscience may make the entire discipline look bad, and may hurt our overall funding prospects. This was swiftly picked up by Dave Chalmers, who suggested that all we should do is to carefully criticize the theory, without making too much noise.** But we already know from years of experience that this would be ineffective, as hyperbolic promotion by celebrities drowns out our critiques. **Also, as Partha Mitra pointed out, the argument is essentially that IIT is 'too big to fail'. Rooting out a problem may hurt in the short term, but sometimes it is necessary.** … **The question is no longer whether IIT is actually really leading or well established. Rather, we need to explain to the world how a theory so extraordinarily controversial could ever be promoted as such in the first place.**"*
- **His "celebritocracy" diagnosis — the deepest layer of his critique:** *"**since the 1990s, the science of consciousness is simply unique in that it is heavily driven by stardom, amid the paucity of conventional academic mechanisms for meritocracy.** In part because of the historically taboo nature of the topic within mainstream science, we do not attract a lot of public funding and tenure-track positions in the US… **Without the conventional mechanisms for openly rejecting bad science, stardom runs wild.** … **Public engagement with science is a laudable exercise, which should be recognized and rewarded in a healthy scientific subfield. But in consciousness science, this has just gone way too far.**"* And: *"**Outsized influence calls for extra scrutiny, not sycophancy.**"* Plus the structural pessimism: *"**people who are granted exclusive access to behind-closed-door meetings tend to be less likely to challenge the very system that has given them such privileges in the first place.**"*
- ⚠️ **His postscript on the aftermath:** *"activities intensified on social media… **Unfortunately, the discussion quickly devolved into mudslinging, from trolls, folks from outside of academia, as well as what appeared to be fake accounts.** … **While the personal attacks were unhelpful, if others were willing to go this far, it probably meant that there were genuinely hurt feelings involved - for which I am truly sorry.**"* He rebuts two rumours: that he *"tricked"* senior colleagues into signing (*"**I can only urge my critics to stop overestimating my level of personal charisma - and be respectful to my coauthors**"*) and that he *"'promised' co-signers a Nature / Science 'paper'"* (*"**I definitely never said this, nor would any reasonable person assume that I would ever have such power.**"*).

**The most useful passage from the Tononi reply for a "both sides" treatment — their framing of what the fight is actually about:**

> *"Attempts to label as 'pseudoscientific' a theory distinguished by decades of conceptual, mathematical, and empirical developments **expose a crisis in the dominant computational-functionalist paradigm, which is challenged by IIT's consciousness-first paradigm.**"*
> *"**Paradigms tend to die when a stubborn fact refuses to be accommodated; in this case, the stubborn fact is consciousness itself.** By taking the extrinsic perspective and defining consciousness as a function or set of functions performed by a system, **the dominant paradigm does not account for why an experience is present and feels the way it does.**"*
> *"if we aim to explain consciousness, we need to account objectively for its subjective presence and properties — **for what it is, rather than what it does. Otherwise, we end up accounting for various cognitive functions and their implementation, for pseudo-consciousness rather than for consciousness as such. Moreover, we risk attributing consciousness to entities that may display those functions without harboring any subjectivity.**"*
> They open with **Bacon (*Novum Organum*, 1620)**: *"'Once the human mind has settled on certain views … **it draws everything else to agree with and support them. Should they be outweighed by more powerful countervailing considerations, it either fails to notice them, or disparages them, or makes fine distinctions to dismiss and reject them, with much dangerous prejudice, to preserve the authority of its first conceptions.**'"*
> And close with the sharpest rhetorical passage in the exchange: *"**more than three decades ago, some of us set forth to tackle consciousness scientifically, at a time when merely mentioning the word implied a disqualification from science.** Since then, our efforts and those of many others have won consciousness some degree of scientific respectability. **It is an ironic twist, then, to see a flock of those who can now safely pursue the study of consciousness issue indictments of pseudoscience.** … **It will not benefit from a self-appointed tribunal — the academic equivalent of the congregation for the doctrine of the faith (the computational-functionalist faith, that is) — deciding which topics or approaches are permissible and which should be banned.**"*
> Their characterization of the second letter: *"**This second letter attempts damage control by adding some polish and a philosophy-of-science veneer, claiming that IIT is untestable, even in principle.**"*
