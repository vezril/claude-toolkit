# Elimination Disorders

The manual's smallest chapter — four entries. Inappropriate elimination of urine or faeces,
usually involuntary, occasionally intentional. Previously grouped under disorders of infancy,
childhood and adolescence; DSM-5 gave it independent standing.

## The disorders

- **Enuresis** — repeated voiding of urine into bed or clothes, **≥2× per week for ≥3 months**
  or else causing clinically significant distress or impairment. **Chronological age ≥5** or
  equivalent developmental level.
  **Specify whether:** nocturnal only (the common form, "bedwetting") · diurnal only ·
  **nocturnal and diurnal**.
- **Encopresis** — repeated passage of faeces in inappropriate places, **≥1× per month for ≥3
  months**, age ≥4.
  **Specify whether:** **with constipation and overflow incontinence** (the majority — faecal
  retention with leakage around the impaction, so the clinical problem is constipation) ·
  without constipation and overflow incontinence.
- **Other specified** and **unspecified** variants of each.

Both may be **primary** (continence never achieved) or **secondary** (relapse after a period of
continence). Not formal specifiers, but clinically meaningful — secondary presentations more
often follow a stressor.

## What must be excluded first

The diagnosis is **not** made when the behaviour is attributable solely to a substance (a
diuretic, for instance) or a medical condition. Realistic candidates:

- **Enuresis:** urinary tract infection, diabetes mellitus and diabetes insipidus, structural
  urological abnormality, spina bifida and other spinal cord lesions, obstructive sleep apnea
  (which causes nocturnal enuresis and is routinely missed), seizures, constipation pressing on
  the bladder.
- **Encopresis:** chronic constipation of any cause, Hirschsprung disease, hypothyroidism,
  anal fissure causing withholding, coeliac disease, spinal cord pathology.

Because **encopresis with constipation and overflow incontinence is the common subtype**, the
primary intervention is often medical — disimpaction and bowel management — not behavioural.
Treating it as a purely psychological problem is the standard error here.

## Differentials and context

- **Developmental level, not just age** — the thresholds say "or equivalent developmental
  level", which matters when intellectual developmental disorder is present
  ([[dsm-neurodevelopmental]]).
- **Intentional soiling or wetting** can occur in oppositional presentations
  ([[dsm-disruptive-impulse-conduct]]), but is also seen with withholding and fear of the
  toilet; assuming defiance is usually wrong.
- **New-onset secondary enuresis or encopresis** warrants asking about stressors including
  abuse and neglect — relevant codes in [[dsm-other-conditions]], and see
  [[dsm-trauma-stressor]].
- **Sleep-related** — nocturnal enuresis co-occurs with sleep-disordered breathing and with
  arousal parasomnias ([[dsm-sleep-wake]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
