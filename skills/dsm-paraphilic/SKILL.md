# Paraphilic Disorders

The chapter turns on one distinction, made explicit in DSM-5 and the main reason the chapter was
revised:

> **A paraphilia is not a disorder.** A **paraphilic disorder** is a paraphilia that is
> currently causing **distress or impairment to the individual**, **or** whose satisfaction
> **entails personal harm, or risk of harm, to others**.

An atypical sexual interest, by itself, is not a mental disorder. DSM-5 made this structural
rather than implied, so that consensual, non-distressing interests fall outside the manual. This
is the chapter's most important content and the thing most often misstated about it.

## The disorders

Grouped by target and by whether consent is implicated:

**Anomalous activity preferences**

- **Voyeuristic disorder** — observing unsuspecting persons who are naked or engaged in sexual
  activity. Minimum age 18 for the diagnosis.
- **Exhibitionistic disorder** — exposing genitals to an unsuspecting person. **Specify** whether
  toward prepubertal children, physically mature individuals, or both.
- **Frotteuristic disorder** — touching or rubbing against a non-consenting person.
- **Sexual masochism disorder** — **specify if with asphyxiophilia.**
- **Sexual sadism disorder**.

**Anomalous target preferences**

- **Pedophilic disorder** — sexual attraction to prepubertal children (generally ≤13), person
  ≥16 and ≥5 years older. **Specify** exclusive or nonexclusive type; sexually attracted to
  males, females, or both; **limited to incest**. Note the structure: for this disorder,
  **acting on the attraction is not required** — marked distress or interpersonal difficulty
  suffices, and so does acting. It has no "in full remission" specifier in the sense the others
  do, because the attraction is understood as stable.
- **Fetishistic disorder** — nonliving objects or a highly specific non-genital body part.
  **Specify** body part(s), nonliving object(s), or other.
- **Transvestic disorder** — sexual arousal from cross-dressing. **Specify if with fetishism or
  with autogynephilia.**

Plus **other specified** (telephone scatologia, necrophilia, zoophilia, coprophilia,
klismaphilia, urophilia are the examples the manual names) and **unspecified**.

## Specifiers

Most take **in a controlled environment** (institutionalised or otherwise restricted access) and
**in full remission** (no distress or impairment for ≥5 years in an uncontrolled environment).
Both exist so that absence of opportunity is not misread as recovery — the same logic as the
controlled-environment specifier in [[dsm-substance-related]].

## Boundaries that matter

- **Transvestic disorder is not gender dysphoria.** One is sexual arousal from cross-dressing;
  the other is distress from incongruence between experienced and assigned gender. They are
  different phenomena in different chapters, and conflating them has caused real harm
  ([[dsm-gender-dysphoria]]).
- **Consensual BDSM is not sexual masochism or sadism disorder** — absent distress, impairment,
  or harm to a non-consenting person, there is no disorder. The chapter is explicit.
- **Sexual orientation is not in this chapter and is not a disorder.** Homosexuality was removed
  from the DSM in 1973; no edition since treats orientation as pathology.
- **A paraphilic disorder is not a legal category.** Having one is not a criminal offence, and
  committing a sexual offence does not establish one — many offences occur without any
  paraphilia. The manual warns directly about forensic misuse of its categories, and this
  chapter is where that warning bites hardest. Civil commitment and sentencing contexts
  routinely overreach here.
- **Differentials:** intoxication and disinhibition ([[dsm-substance-related]]); mania with
  hypersexuality ([[dsm-bipolar]]); frontotemporal neurocognitive disorder with new sexual
  disinhibition in middle age ([[dsm-neurocognitive]]); intellectual developmental disorder
  affecting understanding of consent ([[dsm-neurodevelopmental]]); OCD with intrusive sexual
  obsessions, which are ego-dystonic and **not** paraphilic interests — a critical distinction
  and a common source of patient terror ([[dsm-ocd-related]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
