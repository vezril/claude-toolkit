# Evolutionary algorithms

**An EA is a template with six slots, filled stochastically, that needs only a *comparison* between candidates — never a derivative.** Everything else in the field is a choice about how to fill the slots.

```
initialise population P(0)        <- REPRESENTATION + initialisation
evaluate each individual          <- FITNESS FUNCTION
repeat until TERMINATION:
    select parents from P(t)      <- PARENT SELECTION
    recombine selected parents    <- RECOMBINATION / CROSSOVER
    mutate the offspring          <- MUTATION
    evaluate offspring            <- FITNESS FUNCTION
    select survivors -> P(t+1)    <- SURVIVOR SELECTION
```

Two facts organise the whole subject:

1. **Selection operates on phenotype fitness; variation operates on genotypes.** Everything that makes EAs hard to reason about lives in the gap between them — the **representation**.
2. **Every parameter is a position on the exploration/exploitation dial.** Exploitation rises with selection pressure, falls with variation strength, and **the recurring failure mode is too much exploitation.**

## The dialects, in one table

| Dialect | Origin | Representation | Variation | Selection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Genetic algorithm** | Holland 1975 | bitstrings (textbook) / anything (practice) | crossover primary, mutation secondary | proportionate (textbook), tournament (practice) |
| **Evolution strategy** | Rechenberg/Schwefel, TU Berlin mid-1960s | real vectors + **self-adapted step sizes** | mutation primary | `(μ, λ)` comma or `(μ + λ)` plus |
| **Evolutionary programming** | L.J. Fogel 1960s | finite state machines → real vectors | mutation only, **recombination rejected on principle** | stochastic q-tournament |
| **Genetic programming** | Koza 1992 | expression trees | subtree crossover 90% / reproduction 10% | proportionate (tableau), tournament (implementations) |
| **Differential evolution** | Storn & Price 1995/1997 | real vectors | **difference vectors drawn from the population itself** | greedy one-to-one |

**Say "the labels can mislead because the approaches have progressively integrated" — not "the dialects converged."** Bäck, Hammel & Schwefel 1997 is the source for that framing, and ⚠️ it **never uses the word "dialects," never claims mathematical equivalence, and never proposes a unified framework** (grep-verified).

**And Holland's project was *adaptation*, not optimisation.** De Jong's "Genetic Algorithms Are NOT Function Optimizers" (FOGA 2, 1993) is the citation, and his symptom is the one practitioners recognise: GAs *"frequently don't exhibit a 'killer instinct' … although they rapidly locate the region in which a global optimum exists, they don't locate the optimum with similar speed."* That is why the competitive combinatorial EAs are **memetic** — a population wrapped around a real local search.

## The three continuous workhorses

**CMA-ES** samples from `N(m, σ²C)` and adapts **both** C and σ. It works because of **invariances**: to any strictly increasing transformation of f (only rankings are used), to scale, and to rotation. The rank-μ update learns the *shape* of successful steps but throws away their **sign**; the **evolution path** `p_c` recovers exactly that direction information as a momentum term — the single most important ingredient on ill-conditioned ridges. Default `λ = 4 + floor(3 ln n)`, `μ = floor(λ/2)`, and **Hansen's own advice is not to change the strategy parameters** — the only knobs are increasing λ and, on noisy functions, decreasing `α_cov`. Cost is **O(n²)** per sample, so plain CMA-ES runs out somewhere in the hundreds-to-low-thousands of dimensions.

**Differential evolution's whole trick**: the difference vectors come **from the population**, so the mutation distribution's scale *and orientation* track the population's current spread and correlation structure automatically. As the population converges, the differences align with the landscape's principal directions. **It is close to parameter-free**, and the one knob that matters is **CR — high (≈0.9–1.0) for non-separable/rotated problems, low for separable ones.** (People state this backwards; the mechanism settles it: binomial crossover changes ≈`CR·D + 1` coordinates, so low CR gives axis-aligned steps.)

**Adaptive DE has dominated the CEC single-objective competitions since 2014** — the L-SHADE lineage plus DE/CMA-ES hybrids, with HS-ES in 2018 the one non-DE exception. **But "therefore adaptive DE is the best general-purpose real-parameter optimiser" is contested in the same literature**, on two grounds: *complexity inflation* (ensembles accumulating redundant components) and *benchmark overfitting* (a significant **negative** correlation between CEC-2020 performance and the real-world CEC-2011 set).

## Multi-objective optimisation — where EAs are genuinely state of the art

**The structural advantage: one run returns a whole front approximation**, with N candidate trade-offs that **co-evolve and mutually inform each other** rather than N independent scalarized solves. And the a-posteriori workflow is the real argument: choosing weights *before* you have seen the front means committing to a trade-off rate you cannot yet evaluate, through a mapping that is nonlinear, geometry-dependent, **and discontinuous across front concavities.**

**Why weighted-sum fails on non-convex fronts — the geometric reason, not just the fact.** A weighted-sum minimiser is exactly a point where the hyperplane with normal λ *supports* `Y + R^M_+`. Supporting hyperplanes touch only the boundary of the **convex hull**. So **any Pareto-optimal point in a concave region is strictly inside that hull and unreachable by any non-negative weights** — and the weight→solution map *jumps* across the concavity, which clumps your front points even when the front is convex but unevenly curved. Tchebycheff and ASF have L_∞ level sets (a cone corner, not a flat face) and therefore can reach concave regions.

**Pareto dominance dies in many objectives, and here are the numbers.** With N = 100 random points and i.i.d. uniform objectives, the mean non-dominated fraction is 0.052 (M=2), 0.277 (M=4), **0.938 (M=10)**, 1.000 (M=20). At M = 10 non-dominated sorting degenerates to "everything is rank 1", and **all selection pressure comes from the diversity criterion — which is also the criterion that breaks down in high M**, because NSGA-II's crowding distance is summed per-objective and the per-objective sorts stop agreeing at M ≥ 3. That is the entire motivation for reference-point niching (NSGA-III), decomposition (MOEA/D) and indicator-based selection (SMS-EMOA, IBEA, HypE).

**Hypervolume is the only popular unary indicator that is strictly Pareto-compliant** — which makes maximising it a *sound* target, and SMS-EMOA's Reduce operator **provably never decreases it.** The costs: it is **#P-hard in the number of objectives** (not in n — for fixed d, polynomial exact algorithms exist), **deciding the least contributor is #P-hard**, and it is **sensitive to the reference point**, biased toward knees and convex regions. **HV is incomparable across papers unless the reference point and the normalisation are both stated.**

**And the most-misquoted result in the field, stated correctly.** Zitzler et al. 2003, Theorem 1: *"there exists **no comparison method based on a finite combination of unary quality indicators** that is ⊲-compatible and ⊲-complete at the same time."* It does **not** say unary indicators are useless. It says **no finite set of unary numbers is an exact oracle for set-dominance** — which is the formal argument for binary indicators and empirical attainment functions when you want to claim "better than."

## Theory: what is proven, and what is folklore

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| **Schema theorem** | True and nearly useless as stated: a **one-generation lower bound on an expected count**, under **fitness-proportionate selection** (not tournament/rank/truncation — i.e. not what anyone uses), counting only destructive effects. **It cannot be iterated.** Extends to arbitrary subsets and operators, which destroys the idea that schemata are privileged objects |
| **Building-block hypothesis** | **Empirically refuted on the Royal Road by the BBH's own proponents** (Mitchell, Forrest & Holland) — a random-mutation hill climber beat the GA, diagnosed as **hitchhiking** |
| **Implicit parallelism / "n³"** | A counting argument with preconditions nobody checks; Altenberg's analysis via Price's theorem is the strongest critique |
| **No Free Lunch** | True and routinely misused. It needs **closure under permutation of the function class** — which real problem classes are not. **It does not license a new algorithm**; "NFL says all algorithms are equal, so mine is as good as yours" inverts the theorem |
| **`p_m = 1/n`** | **Proven optimal for the whole class of linear functions** (Witt 2013), with the (1+1) EA an optimal mutation-based algorithm. As good as parameter advice gets in this field. The modern refinement is a **heavy-tailed** rate for multimodal problems |
| **Inversion = 2-opt** | A **theorem** (Lin 1965, Theorem 3), for the **symmetric** TSP with a path representation. Fails for ATSP, where reversal flips every internal arc |
| **Gray coding** | ⚠️ **The proven direction is the opposite of folklore**: Whitley 1999 proves reflected Gray induces **more** optima on the maximally-multimodal class, and NFL accounting then *forces* it to be better over everything else |
| **Convergence guarantees** | An elitist EA with everywhere-positive mutation converges to the global optimum with probability 1 (Rudolph 1994) — **and so does blind random search. No rate, so nearly vacuous** |
| **Crossover helps** | Provable, and the constant is modest: Sudholt 2017 shows **at least 2× faster** on royal-road and OneMax, by *repairing mutation's disruption*. Jansen & Wegener's Real Royal Road proof needs `p_c = 1/(n log n)` — **crossover must be rare**, close to the opposite of GA folklore |

**Where the real results are: rigorous runtime analysis.** `Θ(n log n)` for the (1+1) EA on OneMax and all linear functions; `Θ(n²)` on LeadingOnes; `Θ(n^n)` on Trap; **unimodality buys nothing.** Drift analysis, fitness levels and family trees are the techniques.

## When NOT to use one — the blunt list

| Situation | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Gradients available, problem smooth | Them. Derivative-free methods pay a factor of **at most √d** in rate (Duchi et al.) — sharp, and *not* "a factor of n" |
| Convex, or LP/QP/MILP/SDP | A real solver. **You get a certificate; an EA never can.** On a convex multi-objective problem weighted-sum scalarization is **complete** (Geoffrion 1968), so the geometric objection evaporates |
| Known structure with an exact/approximation algorithm | That algorithm. ⚠️ **But note TSP: Nagata's GA-EAX holds the World TSP record (1,904,711 cities, tour 7,515,755,912, 17 October 2025) while Concorde holds the complementary lower bound — gap 0.0471%.** A generic GA is hopeless here; a GA with a domain-specific recombination operator is the record holder. **The operator, not the metaheuristic, is where the power lives** |
| Evaluations expensive and few (tens–hundreds) | Bayesian optimisation. Crossover is roughly 10²–10³ evaluations |
| You need guarantees — optimality, feasibility, bounds, certification | Anything else |
| Very high dimension, no exploitable structure | Gradient or surrogate methods |
| Many objectives, no preference information | **Elicit preferences and reduce the problem.** At M ≳ 10 dominance is vacuous, visualisation fails, indicators get expensive or meaningless |
| Separable functions, especially multimodal/high-dim (CMA-ES specifically) | DE — *its own literature* says CMA-ES *"may fail to find comparable solutions"* here |
| n < 5 (CMA-ES specifically) | Downhill simplex, or kriging with expected improvement |

**The hard gate on the other side: can you afford 10³–10⁷ evaluations?** Calibration: CEC budgets are 10,000 × D; the WFG authors ran NSGA-II for **2.5M evaluations** and still had poor coverage on WFG1.

**And the genuine structural advantage: fitness evaluation is embarrassingly parallel.** OpenAI ES's seed trick reduces inter-worker communication to **a single scalar**, giving MuJoCo Humanoid in **10 minutes on 1,440 cores vs 657 minutes on 18.** ⚠️ **But wall-clock speedup is not a better algorithm. Compare by evaluation count, and report both axes.**

## The failure modes, with instruments

**Premature convergence.** Don't eyeball the best-fitness curve. Log **population fitness variance** (near-zero while far from target = collapse, not convergence); **mean pairwise genotypic distance** (fitness variance alone can't see it — neutral networks give identical fitness with intact diversity); **allele-frequency entropy** per locus (collapse = that bit is fixed and crossover can't recover it); and for CMA-ES, **`tolstagnation` firing with σ *not* small** is the signature. Remedies in order: **lower selection pressure** (tournament 7 → 2), **restarts with increasing population** (IPOP/BIPOP — the most effective and most under-used remedy), larger population, islands, niching, and finally **quality-diversity, which puts diversity in the objective so premature convergence stops being a failure mode.**

**A fitness function that rewards the wrong thing.** This has a citable catalogue — Lehman, Clune, Misevic et al. (50 authors), "The Surprising Creativity of Digital Evolution," *Artificial Life* 26(2), 2020 — **and the author list is the point: it is the field's practitioners documenting their own failures.** The canonical case study is **GenProg**: fitness was effectively `exit code == 0`, and Qi et al. (ISSTA 2015) found **104 of 110 plausible patches were equivalent to deleting functionality**, with only **2 of 105** defects correctly repaired. **Evolution optimised the proxy faithfully.** The prescription: **run 30 generations, then look at the best individual's *behaviour*, not its score**, and expect every patch to a proxy to produce the next-cheapest exploit.

**Population too small** is a *computable* quantity, not a table lookup: it grows as `√m` in the number of building blocks, **exponentially in building-block order k**, and with the noise-to-signal ratio (Harik et al.'s gambler's-ruin model). Under-sizing produces **decision errors no amount of later search recovers.** Run a population-size sweep at a fixed total budget before tuning anything else.

## The rigour problem — know this before citing any metaheuristic paper

The field has a documented, named problem with novelty claims. **Sörensen's "Metaheuristics — the metaphor exposed"** is the readable indictment; **Weyland formally proved Harmony Search is a special case of evolution strategies**; the IRIDIA group has dissected six "bestial" metaphors and shown **Intelligent Water Drops = Ant Colony Optimization**; and the **Evolutionary Computation Bestiary** catalogues the rest. Two consequences for practice:

1. **The comparison baseline for a new EA on a classic problem is the best known specialist solver, not another metaheuristic.** Using only similar metaphor-based methods as baselines is one of the named methodological failures.
2. ⚠️ **A CEC-only evaluation is not sufficient evidence.** Kudela documented a **center-bias** problem — most CEC suites use the symmetric `[−100,100]^D` domain with the optimum relatively near 0, so an operator that merely pulls candidates toward the domain centre gets free progress; **47 of 90 surveyed methods have it.** Report BBOB/COCO, whose functions are randomly shifted per instance.

## Always-apply

1. **Name the six slots before writing code.** Representation first, then operators that respect the problem's *heritable units* — edges for adjacency problems, positions for order problems, subtrees for programs.
2. **Say which selection scheme and which parameters.** pymoo's NSGA-II ships `η_c = 15`; jMetal's ships `η_c = 20`. **If you compare them you are not comparing the same algorithm.**
3. **Count function evaluations, not generations, and not wall-clock.** A "generation" differs by an order of magnitude between CMA-ES (λ ≈ 4+3 ln n), NSGA-II (100) and scipy's DE (15D), and population-size adaptation makes generations meaningless.
4. **Report distributions, not best-of-N.** `E[max of N]` is biased, increases monotonically in N, and **the bias grows with the algorithm's variance — so best-of-N rewards erratic algorithms.** If restarts are part of your method, count them in the budget.
5. **The tuning budget counts against the algorithm.** L-SHADE's authors are the model: they state they used ParamILS capped at 1,000 configurations over ~4 hours, report *both* the defaults and the tuned values, and note the tuned version is only *"slightly better."*
6. **Always publish an effect size next to every p-value.** Â12 (Vargha–Delaney) is the right default for EC: it is a probability of superiority over runs, needs no normality, and is **invariant to monotone transforms of the fitness scale.** With 51 runs, p < 0.05 can mean Â12 = 0.57 — i.e. nothing.
7. **Record the library version.** pymoo renamed `n_constr` in 0.6.0 and threads an explicit `random_state` through operators in 0.6.2, **so old seeded scripts produce different sequences.** A result without a version is not reproducible no matter how good your seed hygiene.
8. **Prefer comparison-based constraint handling** (Deb's constraint-domination, stochastic ranking at `P_f ≈ 0.45`, ε-constraint annealing) over penalties. **Penalties need a scale-matched coefficient you cannot know in advance, and too-small gives you a better-than-optimal *infeasible* answer — the most embarrassing bug in the literature.**
9. **Some elitism is nearly always right** — it is the precondition of the only convergence guarantee available — **but 1–2 elites, not 20% of the population.** Under *noise*, prefer non-elitism: plus-selection latches onto lucky over-estimates (**"overvaluation"**), which is why CMA-ES is non-elitist.
10. **Flag folklore as folklore.** The 1/5 rule is derived from the sphere and corridor models only; `p_c ≈ 0.7–0.9` and "population 50–200" come from a handful of 1970s–80s studies on ≤5 functions (ECJ ships population **1024**); and Grefenstette's own meta-GA found **different optima for online and offline performance measures** — there is no single best parameter set, only an answer to "best at what?"
