# Glossary

Definitions are written fresh from the sources listed at the bottom, not copied from them.
Usage varies, so treat each entry as the common meaning, not the only one (SKILL.md rail 1).
Survey numbers come from the Gender Census, an annual online survey of people outside the
gender binary. It's self-selected, so the numbers show relative popularity among respondents,
not prevalence in the population.

## Umbrella terms

### Non-binary (NBi, enby)
- **Meaning:** a gender that isn't exclusively man or woman. That can mean partly one or both,
  between, beyond, neither, or shifting.
- **How it's used:** both an identity in its own right and the umbrella for most terms below.
  It sits under transgender, but not every non-binary person claims "trans", and a few call
  themselves cis.
- **Microlabels:** *non-binary woman/girl* and *non-binary man/boy* are for people who feel
  aligned with a binary gender without being fully it. Both flags were posted on 17 Jan 2017.
- **Days:** International Non-Binary People's Day is 14 July, inside Non-Binary Awareness Week.
- **Flag:** designed by Kye Rowan in 2014 to fly beside the genderqueer flag, not to replace it.
  Four stripes:
  - yellow: genders outside the binary
  - white: many or all genders
  - purple: a mix of, or between, female and male
  - black: no gender

### Genderqueer
- **Meaning:** a gender that is "queer", meaning outside cisgender norms in some way. It's often
  used much like non-binary, but it's broader and more political, because it carries the
  reclaimed *queer* and the idea of deliberately subverting gender.
- **Origin:** the earliest known use as a single word and identity is Riki Anne Wilchins, in
  the Spring 1995 newsletter of the Transexual Menace. It spread through US activism in the late
  1990s and early 2000s, alongside "gender outlaw" (Kate Bornstein's 1994 book).
- **Flag:** Marilyn Roxie, 2010–2011, released under CC BY 4.0. Stripes are lavender (queer
  associations; it also mixes the pink and blue used for femininity and masculinity), white
  (as on the trans flag) and green (lavender's inverse). The order was fixed as
  lavender/white/green in June 2011.

### Transfeminine (transfem, transfemme)
- **Meaning:** someone trans, non-binary or GNC whose gender is mostly feminine, in identity,
  expression or both. Some definitions require being AMAB, and that requirement is disputed.
- **Covers:** trans women, plus non-binary, multigender, genderfluid and demigirl people who lean
  feminine. Not all of them identify as women.
- **Origin:** often traced to Jane Nance's 1985 article in *The TV-TS Tapestry*. Nance wanted a
  word for a feminine self-identity that didn't fit "transvestite", and that didn't hinge on
  surgery the way "transsexual" did.
- **Flag:** online by July 2015, with blue outer stripes and pink inner ones. The creator and the
  meaning are unknown, and all-pink variants exist.

### Transmasculine (transmasc)
- **Meaning:** the mirror of transfeminine: predominantly masculine identity, expression or
  both. Some definitions require being AFAB.
- **Covers:** trans men, plus non-binary, multigender, genderfluid and demiboy people who lean
  masculine. Not all of them identify as men.
- **Origin:** known in use by 1999 (the DC Area Transmasculine Society), probably earlier.
- **Context:** the source flags a specific healthcare gap. Reproductive care such as pelvic exams
  and pap smears is often both needed and avoided.
- **Flag:** online by July 2015, with pink outer stripes and blue inner ones. The creator is
  unknown.

## Specific identities

### Agender (genderless)
- **Meaning:** having no gender, or a neutral one; finding gender personally irrelevant; or
  rejecting the concept of gender. These are four distinct definitions that people use.
- **What it doesn't require:** any transition, any particular expression, or any particular
  pronouns (including using no pronouns).
- **Origin:** the word is first attested on Usenet around 2000, describing a god. It was applied
  to people about five years later.
- **Milestones:** Patch became the first legally agender person in the US (Multnomah County,
  Oregon) in March 2017. Agender Pride Day is 19 May, observed since 2017.
- **Related terms:**
  - *Gendervoid*: gender felt as a literal void.
  - *Neutrois*: a stronger emphasis on neutrality. Some people are both neutrois and agender.
  - *Genderblank*: a gender so indescribable it registers as a blank.
  - *Agenderflux*: moving between agender and some gender.
  - *Polyagender*: several agender-type identities at once.
- **Flag:** Salem, 18 Feb 2014. Seven stripes, black/grey/white/green/white/grey/black:
  - black and white: absence of gender
  - grey: semi-genderlessness
  - green: kinship with the non-binary community. It's deliberately the inverse of the
    non-binary flag's purple, to say agender isn't *between* the binary at all.

### Demigender
- **Meaning:** a partial, not full, connection to a particular gender. It's an umbrella built by
  adding *demi-* ("half") to a gender word.
- **Members:**
  - *Demigirl* (demiwoman, demigal): partly a girl or woman. Day: 21 June.
  - *Demiboy* (demiman, demiguy): partly a boy or man. Day: 22 June.
  - *Deminonbinary*: partly non-binary.
  - *Demiagender*, *demineutrois*, *demiandrogyne*: partly agender, neutrois or androgyne.
  - *Demifluid*: several genders, some static, some fluid.
  - *Demiflux*: several genders, some static, some varying in intensity.
- **How it differs:** from genderfluid, a demigender person always keeps the partial tie, while a
  genderfluid person moves between genders. From agender, the demigender person still has a
  connection to some gender.
- **Flags:**
  - Salem posted demigirl (pink) and demiboy (blue) on 18 Feb 2014, with no stated meanings.
  - Salem's deminonbinary flag (grey, light grey, yellow, white) is now widely used as the
    general demigender flag.
  - Demifluid: designed by @pride-flags-for-us, 2015.

### Genderfluid
- **Meaning:** a gender that changes over time. It can change at random or with circumstance,
  across any genders, both, or none. Pronouns may change with it, and some genderfluid people
  transition.
- **Origin:** Kate Bornstein's *Gender Outlaw* (1994) used fluidity in a broader, more radical
  sense. The current sense came later.
- **Numbers:** 24.4% of 2025 Gender Census respondents checked it, one of the most common
  answers.
- **Microlabels:**
  - *Genderfae*: fluid, but never man-aligned or masculine.
  - *Genderfaun*: fluid, but never woman-aligned or feminine.
  - *Genderflor*: fluid, but neither.
  - *Fluidflux*: fluid in which gender, and fluctuating in intensity. Coined in 2014.
- **History:** the Chevalier d'Éon (18th century) is sometimes claimed as a genderfluid
  figure, and the claim is disputed.
- **Misconception:** "it's a phase". A change in identity is the definition here, not
  evidence against it (SKILL.md rail 2).
- **Flag:** pink (femininity), white (all genders), purple (both), black (no gender), blue
  (masculinity).
  - It's usually credited to JJ Poole in 2012.
  - The source wiki instead reports it being seen at Columbus, Ohio Pride in 2005.
  - Treat the attribution as unresolved.

### Xenogender
- **Meaning:** a gender that human gender categories can't contain, described instead through
  animals, plants, elements, concepts or sensations. Some xenogender people avoid gendered words
  altogether and go by a name.
- **Status:** it's under the non-binary umbrella and is part of the MOGAI vocabulary
  (Marginalized Orientations, Gender Alignments and Intersex, an alternative umbrella to
  LGBTQIA+).
- **Origin:** proposed on Tumblr on 24 June 2014 by the user Baaphomett. *Xeno-* means foreign
  or strange. Related words are *xenine* (adjective) and *xenity* (noun).
- **Numbers:** in the 2025 Gender Census, 488 of 43,096 respondents (1.1%) wrote in
  "xenogender".
- **Examples:**
  - *catgender*: a gender bound up with cats
  - *seagender*: vast and unfathomable, like the sea
  - *firegender*: always in flux
  - *digigender*: in digital terms
  - *ethergender*: ethereal and inexplicable
- **Not the same:** *xenic* (xeno-aligned) is a gender *alignment*, being neither male- nor
  female-aligned. Its coiner says it isn't equivalent to xenogender.
- **Scholarship:** Christine Feraday's master's thesis treats xenogenders as "neo-identities",
  new words pushing back on rigid norms. Some counselling literature notes a possible link to
  synaesthesia. For the inclusion debate, see SKILL.md.
- **Flags:** several have been proposed. Roswell (Pastelmemer) posted iris-flower shades on
  15 Jan 2017, with yellow for genders unrelated to the binary and the astronomical symbol of
  the asteroid 7 Iris.

## Expression terms

### Feminine / masculine
- **Meaning:** culturally variable sets of traits, presentations and roles associated with women
  and men. Anyone of any gender or orientation can carry them, and what counts as either
  changes with culture, class, religion and era.
- **Femme and masc:** these are the deliberate, queer-coded versions. Femme is an intentional
  relationship between femininity and queerness. Butch, boi and trans man are masc identities.
- **Femmephobia:** the devaluing of femininity. It shows up in queer spaces too, for example
  "no femmes" and "masc4masc" on dating apps, or femme lesbians being read as straight.
- **Toxic masculinity:** the narrow, cishet ideal of strength that treats anything queer or
  feminine as weakness.

### Androgynous
- **Meaning:** presentation that reads as neither clearly masculine nor feminine, or as a mix.
  It's mainly about expression. *Androgyne* as an identity exists, but androgynous on its own is
  not an identity.
- **Etymology:** from Greek *andros* (man) and *gynē* (woman).
- **History:**
  - the 1920s flappers, Chanel and Marlene Dietrich's trousers
  - glam rock: Bowie's Ziggy Stardust (1972), Marc Bolan, Jagger's 1969 "man's dress"
  - the 1980s: Annie Lennox, Boy George, Yamamoto and Kawakubo
- **Research:** Sandra Bem (1970s) defined psychological androgyny and linked it to good
  outcomes such as self-esteem and flexibility. Androgynous people still faced penalties in how
  employers and peers perceived them.

### Gender non-conforming (GNC)
- **Meaning:** not following the gender stereotypes one's society expects, in expression, roles
  or otherwise. It's most visible in people with binary identities, cis or trans.
- **How it's used:** not a synonym for non-binary or trans, though "TGNC" (trans and gender
  non-conforming) uses it as an umbrella. Nearby terms are *gender diverse*, *gender expansive*
  (used more by parents and allies; the community tends to prefer GNC) and *gender variant*.
- **Discrimination:** the 2008 US National Transgender Discrimination Survey suggested that trans
  adults who are read as non-conforming face more discrimination. The sample wasn't
  representative.
- **Flag (gender creative flag):** Leslie Krause, 19 July 2015, for the Freedressing Campaign.
  Lavender bars blend the trans flag's pink and blue; a white bar stands for free expression.
  Krause also proposed "gender creative" as an alternative name.

### Gender neutral
- **Meaning:** "not gendered". It's mostly used for language (they/them, Mx, firefighter),
  facilities (restrooms) and products (clothing lines; demand rose around 2019). Some people use
  it as an identity, close to agender. PFLAG notes it isn't usually a word for people.
- **Restrooms:** in 2014 Vancouver became the first municipality to require gender-neutral
  restrooms in its park board buildings, citing the BC Human Rights Code.
- **Pushback:** the University of Tennessee removed its 2015 pronoun guide after political
  backlash. The "singular they is ungrammatical" objection ignores centuries of usage (Chaucer,
  Shakespeare, Austen).
- **Flag:** created by the DeviantArt user enbygsrd on 16 Sep 2016, with unknown meanings.

## Clinical-adjacent

### Gender dysphoria
- **Community sense:** distress at the mismatch between one's gender and one's body or social
  treatment. It ranges from mild to unbearable and can vary day to day. It isn't required to be
  trans.
- **How non-binary people may experience it:** discomfort with some gender signifiers and not
  others, or the same signifier feeling fine on one day and wrong on another. Plus the strain of
  defending an identity people call a trend.
- **What it's not:**
  - not gender expression
  - not questioning one's orientation
  - not body dysmorphia, which is a perceived flaw that isn't really there. Dysphoria is a real
    mismatch with one's gender.
- **Clinical sense:** a DSM-5-TR diagnosis. For the diagnosis, its history and what it does and
  doesn't claim, use the `dsm-gender-dysphoria` skill. Don't reproduce criteria here.
- **Debates:** ROGD and transmedicalism are covered in SKILL.md.
