# Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders

**Organised as a gradient**, least to most psychopathology: schizotypal personality → delusional
disorder → brief psychotic → schizophreniform → schizophrenia → schizoaffective. Reading the
chapter in order *is* the differential.

Defined by abnormality in one or more of **five domains**: delusions, hallucinations,
disorganised thinking (speech), grossly disorganised or abnormal motor behaviour (including
catatonia), and **negative symptoms** (diminished emotional expression, avolition). The first
four are "positive"; negative symptoms account for much of the morbidity and respond least.

## Duration is the spine of the chapter

| Disorder | Duration |
|---|---|
| Brief psychotic disorder | ≥1 day, <1 month, **full return to premorbid function** |
| Schizophreniform disorder | ≥1 month, <6 months |
| Schizophrenia | **≥6 months** total, including prodromal/residual, with ≥1 month of active symptoms |

Two DSM-5 changes worth knowing:

- **The classic subtypes are gone** — paranoid, disorganised, catatonic, undifferentiated,
  residual were dropped for poor reliability and little predictive value, replaced by
  **dimensional severity ratings** across the five domains.
- **Bizarre delusions no longer count double.** Under DSM-IV a single bizarre delusion
  sufficed; DSM-5 requires two Criterion A symptoms, and at least one must be delusions,
  hallucinations or disorganised speech. The judgment "is this delusion bizarre" was unreliable.

## The ones that trip people

- **Schizoaffective disorder** is a **longitudinal** diagnosis, not a snapshot: a mood episode
  concurrent with active psychosis, *plus* delusions or hallucinations for ≥2 weeks **without**
  a prominent mood episode, *plus* mood symptoms present for the majority of the illness. Most
  misapplications skip the second and third parts. Compare mood-disorder-with-psychotic-features
  in [[dsm-bipolar]] and [[dsm-depressive]], where psychosis occurs **only** within mood episodes.
- **Delusional disorder** — ≥1 month of delusions with functioning otherwise relatively
  preserved and no other Criterion A symptom prominent. Subtypes by content: erotomanic,
  grandiose, jealous, persecutory, somatic, mixed, unspecified. DSM-5 dropped the
  non-bizarre requirement.
- **Schizotypal personality disorder** is listed here *and* in [[dsm-personality]] — coded with
  the personality disorders, conceptualised as part of this spectrum.
- **Catatonia** gets three routes: as a **specifier** for another mental disorder, as
  **catatonic disorder due to another medical condition**, and as **unspecified catatonia**.
  It is a syndrome, not a disorder of its own in most uses, and it is medically urgent.

## Differentials

- **Substance/medication-induced first, always.** Stimulants, cannabis, hallucinogens,
  withdrawal states, steroids, anticholinergics ([[dsm-substance-related]]).
- **Medical:** delirium, temporal lobe epilepsy, autoimmune encephalitis, thyroid disease,
  B12 deficiency. Acute onset with clouded consciousness points at [[dsm-neurocognitive]].
- **Obsessions with poor insight** vs delusions ([[dsm-ocd-related]]); **body dysmorphic**
  beliefs vs somatic delusions.
- **PTSD flashbacks and dissociation** vs hallucinations ([[dsm-trauma-stressor]],
  [[dsm-dissociative]]).
- **Culture matters acutely here** — religious and spiritual experience normative in a community
  is not a delusion. See the cultural formulation material in [[dsm-5]].

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
