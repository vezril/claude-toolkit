# Personality Disorders

An **enduring pattern of inner experience and behaviour** that deviates markedly from cultural
expectation, is **pervasive and inflexible**, begins by adolescence or early adulthood, is
stable over time, and leads to distress or impairment.

The general criteria matter more than any individual disorder. A personality disorder requires
the pattern to show in **≥2 of four areas**: cognition, affectivity, interpersonal functioning,
impulse control. **Pervasive and enduring** is the whole point — a pattern confined to one
relationship or one period is not a personality disorder.

## The three clusters

- **Cluster A (odd, eccentric)** — **paranoid** (pervasive distrust), **schizoid** (detachment,
  restricted affect), **schizotypal** (eccentricity, cognitive-perceptual distortions; also
  listed in [[dsm-schizophrenia-spectrum]] as part of that spectrum).
- **Cluster B (dramatic, emotional, erratic)** — **antisocial** (disregard for others' rights;
  requires conduct disorder before 15, and cross-listed in
  [[dsm-disruptive-impulse-conduct]]), **borderline** (instability of relationships,
  self-image and affect, with impulsivity; includes recurrent self-harm and suicidality),
  **histrionic** (excessive emotionality and attention-seeking), **narcissistic**
  (grandiosity, need for admiration, lack of empathy).
- **Cluster C (anxious, fearful)** — **avoidant** (social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy,
  hypersensitivity to negative evaluation), **dependent** (need to be taken care of, submissive
  clinging), **obsessive-compulsive** (preoccupation with order, perfectionism and control).

Plus **personality change due to another medical condition** and the other specified/unspecified
categories.

**The clusters have known problems** and the manual says so: limited validity, heavy
co-occurrence across clusters, and patients frequently meeting criteria for several disorders
at once. They persist because they are clinically familiar, not because they are well supported.

## The Alternative DSM-5 Model (Section III)

A **dimensional** model, developed because the categorical system's weaknesses are
well-documented. It requires both:

1. **Level of Personality Functioning Scale** — moderate or greater impairment in **self**
   (identity, self-direction) and **interpersonal** (empathy, intimacy) functioning.
2. **Pathological personality traits** across five domains, each with facets: **negative
   affectivity, detachment, antagonism, disinhibition, psychoticism**. These map onto the
   Five-Factor Model's maladaptive variants.

The model defines six specific disorders this way (antisocial, avoidant, borderline,
narcissistic, obsessive-compulsive, schizotypal) plus **personality disorder—trait specified**
for everything else.

**Both models are in the manual simultaneously**, which is unusual and is itself the finding:
Section II's categories remain official for clinical and billing use, Section III's model is
offered for further study and use. DSM-5-TR retained the arrangement.

## Differentials

- **State vs trait is the central problem.** Assessing personality during an acute mood episode,
  intoxication, or acute trauma response will produce false positives. The pattern must be
  **enduring and present outside** episodes — which usually means waiting, and collateral
  history.
- **Borderline vs bipolar II** — BPD's affective shifts last hours and are interpersonally
  reactive; hypomania is sustained for days with reduced sleep need
  ([[dsm-bipolar]]).
- **Borderline vs complex trauma presentations** — large overlap; dissociation, self-harm and
  relational instability appear in both ([[dsm-trauma-stressor]],
  [[dsm-dissociative]]).
- **Cluster A vs the psychosis spectrum** — attenuated and trait-level vs episodic and frank
  ([[dsm-schizophrenia-spectrum]]).
- **Avoidant personality disorder vs social anxiety disorder** — so overlapping that the
  distinction's validity is questioned; both are often diagnosed ([[dsm-anxiety]]).
- **Obsessive-compulsive personality disorder vs OCD** — OCPD has no true obsessions or
  compulsions, despite the name ([[dsm-ocd-related]]).
- **Late-onset "personality change" is not a personality disorder.** New disinhibition, apathy
  or coarsening in middle age points at frontotemporal neurocognitive disorder, a tumour, or a
  medical cause, and warrants investigation ([[dsm-neurocognitive]]).
- **Culture** — the criteria are explicitly relative to cultural expectation, and applying them
  across a cultural boundary without the formulation interview produces false positives
  ([[dsm-5]]).

**One caution on use:** these diagnoses carry more stigma than most in the manual, and
borderline and antisocial especially are used pejoratively in practice. Describe patterns and
functioning rather than reaching for a label.

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
