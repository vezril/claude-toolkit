# Sleep-Wake Disorders

The second-largest chapter by page count, and the one most entangled with physical medicine.
DSM-5 made two framing changes that matter more than any individual criteria set:

1. **"Primary" vs "secondary" insomnia was abandoned.** The old model said treat the underlying
   disorder and sleep will follow; the evidence says sleep disturbance is frequently an
   independent, bidirectional contributor. So insomnia disorder is diagnosed **alongside** any
   coexisting condition, with a specifier naming the comorbidity. This is a genuine change in
   clinical posture, not nomenclature.
2. **Sleep disorders are not a symptom list to be inferred.** Several diagnoses here require or
   strongly indicate **polysomnography** — apnea, narcolepsy, REM sleep behavior disorder.
   Diagnosing them from interview alone is not possible.

## The groups

- **Insomnia disorder** — dissatisfaction with sleep quantity or quality (initiation,
  maintenance, or early-morning waking), ≥3 nights/week for ≥3 months, despite adequate
  opportunity. Specify comorbidity and whether episodic, persistent or recurrent.
- **Hypersomnolence disorder** — excessive sleepiness despite ≥7 hours, ≥3×/week for ≥3 months.
- **Narcolepsy** — recurrent irrepressible sleep attacks, ≥3×/week for 3 months, **plus**
  cataplexy, low CSF hypocretin, or characteristic MSLT/polysomnographic findings. DSM-5
  reorganised the subtypes around **hypocretin deficiency**, aligning the diagnosis with its
  biology — one of the few places in the manual where a biomarker is criterial.
- **Breathing-related sleep disorders** — **obstructive sleep apnea hypopnea** (severity by
  apnea-hypopnea index), **central sleep apnea** (idiopathic, Cheyne-Stokes, or comorbid with
  opioid use), **sleep-related hypoventilation**. These are medical diagnoses requiring
  polysomnography; their psychiatric relevance is that untreated apnea convincingly mimics
  depression, ADHD and cognitive impairment.
- **Circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders** — **specify** delayed sleep phase (familial, overlap
  with non-24-hour), advanced sleep phase, irregular sleep-wake, non-24-hour, or shift work
  type. The misalignment is between the endogenous rhythm and the required schedule, so the
  problem is often environmental.
- **Parasomnias** —
  - **Non-REM sleep arousal disorders:** specify **sleepwalking type** (with sleep-related
    eating or sleep-related sexual behaviour/sexsomnia) or **sleep terror type**. Incomplete
    awakening from deep sleep, limited recall.
  - **Nightmare disorder** — dysphoric dreams with full recall on waking, from REM.
  - **REM sleep behavior disorder** — **new as an independent DSM-5 diagnosis.** Dream enactment
    with loss of REM atonia, confirmed on polysomnography. Clinically important far beyond
    sleep: it is a **prodromal marker for the alpha-synucleinopathies**, so most cases
    eventually develop Parkinson's disease, dementia with Lewy bodies or multiple system
    atrophy ([[dsm-neurocognitive]]).
- **Restless legs syndrome** — **new as an independent DSM-5 diagnosis**, with frequency and
  duration thresholds. Associated with iron deficiency, pregnancy, renal failure, and with
  antidepressant and antipsychotic use.
- **Substance/medication-induced sleep disorder** — specify insomnia, daytime sleepiness,
  parasomnia or mixed type, and whether onset is during intoxication, withdrawal, or after
  medication use.

## Differentials

- **Apnea before anything psychiatric** when the complaint is fatigue, poor concentration,
  irritability or low mood. Snoring, witnessed apnoeas, obesity, hypertension, morning
  headache. This is the single highest-yield check in the chapter
  ([[dsm-depressive]], [[dsm-neurodevelopmental]]).
- **Insomnia is a criterion in** depressive, manic, anxiety and PTSD presentations — diagnose
  insomnia disorder in addition when it is independently persistent and impairing.
- **Nightmare disorder vs PTSD nightmares** — trauma-replicative content and the full PTSD
  picture point to [[dsm-trauma-stressor]].
- **Sleep terrors vs nightmares vs nocturnal panic** — sleep stage, recall and autonomic
  pattern separate them ([[dsm-anxiety]]).
- **REM sleep behavior disorder vs non-REM arousal disorders** — dream enactment with recall vs
  confused partial arousal without it; polysomnography settles it.
- **Delayed sleep phase vs insomnia** — a person who sleeps well on their own schedule has a
  circadian problem, not an insomnia one. Asking about free days separates them immediately.
- **Caffeine, alcohol, stimulants, and withdrawal** ([[dsm-substance-related]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
