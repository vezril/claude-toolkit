# The Shamisen Genre Taxonomy

Scope: the **genre map** of shamisen music — which genres exist, which neck class each uses, and
which social institution each belonged to. Origins and chronology are in `history.md`; the
instrument is in `instrument.md`; the blind guild and itinerant musicians who carried much of this
repertoire are in `blind-and-itinerant-musicians.md`; tunings and scales in depth are in the
`shamisen-performance` skill; and tsugaru-jamisen, placed here only, is covered by
`tsugaru-shamisen`.

## How to read this file

The part numbering (Part 4, with §4.0–4.5) is retained from the research so that the
cross-references between these four files stay valid.

**Verification key**

- **[V]** = verified. The artifact actually read is named inline (title, URL, and fetch date).
- **[U]** = unverified, or verified only against a weak source. Flagged, with what is missing.

All fetches were made **2026-11-13** unless stated otherwise. Where a Japanese source is the real
authority I have used it and said so; **all translations from Japanese in this file are mine** and
are marked *(my translation)* at first use in each section.

**Method note / what blocked.** General web search was unavailable for this research: DuckDuckGo,
Brave, Ecosia, Mojeek, JSTOR's and Brill's own site search, and Google Books' API all refused or
rate-limited the fetcher. **Yahoo! Japan search worked**, which is why the Japanese-language
evidence trail in this file is considerably richer than the English one. Paywalled
English-language scholarship (Brill, Project MUSE, Taylor & Francis, Scholarly Publishing
Collective) could not be read directly; where I cite it I say whether I verified the
*bibliography* only or the *content*.

**One structural caution about the English-language literature.** The standard English account of
shamisen origins (reproduced in Malm, in the Kodansha encyclopedia, and from there into Wikipedia
and most popular writing) compresses a contested, legend-heavy, and poorly documented Japanese
scholarly debate into one clean sentence with a date in it. The Japanese reference literature is
visibly more cautious: it uses 「という」「とされる」「説」 ("it is said", "is held to be",
"one theory") at precisely the points where English sources use the indicative. That gap is the
most important finding in Part 1.

## 4. The genre taxonomy

Everything in this part was verified against named Japanese reference works — chiefly
**kotobank.jp**, which aggregates 日本大百科全書（ニッポニカ）, 改訂新版 世界大百科事典,
ブリタニカ国際大百科事典, デジタル大辞泉, 精選版 日本国語大辞典, 百科事典マイペディア and
山川 日本史小辞典, with signed entries — and the **Japan Arts Council's 文化デジタルライブラリー**
teaching module 「日本の伝統音楽」 at `www2.ntj.jac.go.jp/dglib/contents/learn/edc29/`, which has a
dedicated page per genre. All fetches 2026-11-13. Translations from Japanese are mine.

**Caveat on the verification standard used throughout this part:** the fetcher returns a small
model's reading of each page rather than raw bytes, and both kotobank and Japanese Wikipedia
refused verbatim transcription on copyright grounds, returning paraphrase plus short quotes. So
**[V]** here means *an identified artifact at a named URL was read and reported this* — strong, but
one remove from eyeballing the page. Worth re-reading in a browser before anything goes to print.

### 4.0 How to carve it up — there are two standard Japanese taxonomies, not one

**[V] Taxonomy A: 歌い物 utaimono vs 語り物 katarimono.** This is a real Japanese
reference-work distinction with named authors, not an English-language convenience.

- 歌い物 — "song pieces," where the weight falls on the melody rather than the content of the text:
  デジタル大辞泉's gloss is 「詞章の内容よりも曲節に重点を置くもの」. Its list: 神楽歌, 催馬楽,
  朗詠, 今様, **地歌, 箏曲, 長唄, 端唄, うた沢, 小唄**. 改訂新版 世界大百科事典's 歌い物 entry,
  by **西角井正大**, gives the same and adds **荻江節** and 民謡. (kotobank.jp/word/歌い物.)
- 語り物 — narrative delivered 「特別な口調，抑揚，曲節などをつけて」 ("with a special manner of
  speech, intonation and melodic style"). 世界大百科事典's entry, by **山本吉左右**, lists
  **義太夫節, 清元節, 常磐津節, 富本節, 河東節, 一中節, 古浄瑠璃** (grouped under 浄瑠璃), plus
  曲舞, 説経 and regional 琵琶 forms; デジタル大辞泉's list runs 平曲, 幸若舞曲, 説経節, 祭文,
  浄瑠璃, 薩摩琵琶, 筑前琵琶, **浪花節**. (kotobank.jp/word/語り物.)
- The Japan Arts Council has a dedicated page for the split (`edc29/shiru/koegakki/utakatari/`):
  歌いもの music conveys the *scenes and mood* of the lyrics; 語りもの music conveys *events,
  scenery and emotion described in the text*, with the weight on the story.

Three caveats worth carrying into the write-up:
1. **The split is not shamisen-specific.** Both lists are full-breadth Japanese *vocal* music
   taxonomies reaching back to 神楽歌 and 平曲 and forward to the biwa genres. Calling it "the
   two-way split of shamisen music" narrows it misleadingly.
2. **新内節 and 浪曲 are katarimono by inheritance**, not by direct citation — shinnai because it is
   a 浄瑠璃 school, rōkyoku via the 浪花節 heading. Neither is named on the JAC 語りもの page.
3 **[V]** 世界大百科事典 says the boundary is a *tendency*: some 声明 is classed as utaimono while
   another group of 声明 is read as a precursor of katarimono.

**[V] Taxonomy B: the Japan Arts Council's venue split.** JAC files shamisen music as
**劇場の音楽** (theatre music: gidayū, nagauta, tokiwazu, kiyomoto) vs **その他の三味線音楽**
(everything else: ichūbushi, katō, shinnai, miyazono, ogie, hauta/utazawa/kouta, rōkyoku), with
浄瑠璃 as a heading above both. This cross-cuts taxonomy A: it sorts by *where you hear it* —
theatre versus the 座敷 parlour — rather than by whether it narrates. Note also that **JAC files
地歌 under 三曲, not under 三味線音楽 at all**, classifying by ensemble tradition rather than by
instrument. Both taxonomies are standard; say which one you are using.

**[V] 浄瑠璃 jōruri as the umbrella term.** JAC's definition (`edc29/kiku/genre/shamisen/joururi/`):
「浄瑠璃とは、義太夫節、常磐津節、清元節など、三味線の伴奏による語りもの音楽の総称です。」 —
"Jōruri is the collective term for katarimono music accompanied by shamisen, such as gidayū-bushi,
tokiwazu-bushi and kiyomoto-bushi." Japanese Wikipedia 浄瑠璃 stresses that jōruri is 語る (narrated),
not 歌う (sung). The name comes from the princess 浄瑠璃姫 of the Muromachi tale
『浄瑠璃十二段草子』; the older attribution to 小野お通 is now disputed; and **a 1531 entry in
『宗長日記』 shows jōruri predates the shamisen** — it was accompanied by 琵琶 and 扇拍子 before the
shamisen was joined to it in the late 16th century (see §1.5 of `history.md`). The eight living/named schools are
**義太夫節, 河東節, 一中節, 常磐津節, 富本節, 清元節, 新内節, 宮薗節 (= 薗八節)**; JAC adds the
古浄瑠璃 schools 嘉太夫節, 播磨節, 薩摩節, 金平節, 半太夫節.

**[V] How the "-bushi" (節) naming works — and why the obvious gloss needs qualifying.** A 節 is a
named melodic style taking its name from a founding chanter. But: in **古浄瑠璃 a style was named
after its performer and normally died with him** (many chanters held the court title 掾, hence
豊後掾, 若狭掾, 丹後掾). **義太夫節 broke the pattern** by surviving as a named style after
Takemoto Gidayū's death, *without* a formal iemoto system — Japanese Wikipedia 浄瑠璃 reads this as
evidence of the style's completeness. And not all 節 names are personal: **河東節** is named for its
Edo origin, not a founder. **[U]** No source states a general rule for 節-naming; JAC explicitly
declines to give one. So write: "a 節 is a named narrative style, usually but not always taking its
name from the chanter who founded it; before Gidayū such names were performer-labels that rarely
outlived their holders."

**[V] And the single most load-bearing fact in the whole taxonomy: the 1739 Bungo-bushi ban.**
See §2.3 of `history.md` for the documentation. In short: 豊後節, founded by 宮古路豊後掾 (d. 1740) and a craze in
Edo from 1734, was banned in **1739 (元文4)** for inciting lovers' suicides, and its disciples
re-founded under new names. **常磐津節 (1747), 富本節 (1748) → 清元節 (1814), 富士松節 (1745) →
鶴賀節 (1751) → 新内節, and 宮薗節 (c. 1762)** are all its descendants; the first three are the
**豊後三流**. If the reference document explains only one thing about why Edo has so many 〜節, it
should be this.

### 4.1 Genre-by-genre

#### 長唄 nagauta — kabuki's lyrical music

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neck** | **[V] 細棹 hosozao.** JAC nagauta page; 日本大百科全書 三味線 (藤田隆則) lists 細棹 for 長唄, 荻江節, 小唄; 世界大百科事典 and マイペディア agree. |
| **Class** | **[V] 歌い物.** JAC: 「歌舞伎舞踊の伴奏音楽として生まれた歌いものの三味線音楽」. Japanese Wikipedia calls it 歌い物 explicitly; formal name **江戸長唄**. |
| **Social context** | Edo kabuki, then the Edo/Tokyo amateur parlour and the concert hall. |
| **Role** | **[V] Accompaniment** — specifically for **kabuki dance** — later also autonomous concert music. |
| **Tuning** | **[V] All three, with a documented historical arc.** |

**[V] Formation — sources disagree on the decade, which is worth saying out loud.**
世界大百科事典 (名称・沿革 by **平野健次**, 特色 by **植田隆之助**): origins in Genroku-era
Kamigata, developed in Edo across **享保–宝暦 (1716–64)**, 唄浄瑠璃 emerging in **明和 (1764–72)**,
peak in **文化・文政 (1804–30)**. ニッポニカ (植田隆之助): the *name* appears around **元禄
(1688–1704)** and nagauta is said to derive from the Kamigata jiuta category 長歌. ブリタニカ: arose
in the early 18th century as Edo kabuki dance accompaniment, with the name 長唄 settled from
**享保12 (1727)**. JAC: the name appears from **宝永 (1704–11)**, the foundations of today's nagauta
laid in **享保 (1716–36)**. Japanese Wikipedia adds an intermediate stage: short nagauta brought into
Edo kabuki under the name **メリヤス**, sung by one or two performers from the 下座, before 宝暦
onward put nagauta on stage.

**[V] 出囃子 vs 陰囃子 / 黒御簾 — and a terminology warning.** JAC nagauta page: **出囃子** is
performed **on stage** (singers + shamisen + 笛・小鼓・大鼓・太鼓) to accompany kabuki dance;
**陰囃子 / 黒御簾音楽** is performed in a curtained room **stage left (下手)** for mood and effects
(rain, snow), adding 大太鼓 and 鉦 — **and nagauta performers staff this music too**.
世界大百科事典 corroborates. **[V]** The labels 下座 / 黒御簾 / 陰囃子 **overlap but are not
identical**: Japanese Wikipedia uses 下座 for the early メリヤス position, JAC and 世界大百科事典
use 黒御簾/下座音楽 for the offstage music and 陰囃子 for the nagauta-staffed version. Don't treat
"geza" and "kagebayashi" as synonyms.

**[V] The Kineya / Yoshizumi claim — confirmed, and the role division confirmed with it.**
世界大百科事典's 長唄 entry states that **杵屋 Kineya is the 三味線方 (shamisen) line and 芳村
Yoshizumi the 唄方 (singer) line**, each with many 名跡 (inherited stage names), and records a
tradition that 初世杵屋勘五郎 was the Kineya progenitor **while saying there is no firm evidence**.
Japanese Wikipedia 杵屋: 「長唄三味線方の家名。多数の流派がある。」 ブリタニカ calls the
杵屋六左衛門 house 「長唄宗家」. ニッポニカ names the 唄方 名跡 芳村伊三郎/伊十郎 and the 三味線方
杵屋勘五郎/勝三郎/正次郎. **Important correction:** succession is by **襲名** — taking the stage
name — and the Japanese Wikipedia article on 芳村伊十郎 **does not establish blood descent**. Write
"name-succession houses," not "bloodlines."

**[V] Tunings, with the arc.** 世界大百科事典: early nagauta centred on **二上り**; from around
**享保**, **三下り** became the basis; **本調子** was adopted later; present practice combines
本調子・二上り・三下り・六下り(三メリ)・一下り, with the techniques 上調子, 替手 and 「たま」.
ブリタニカ dates the adoption of 本調子 to **明和–天明 (1764–89)**.

**[V] Forces.** JAC: 唄方 (singers — some solo passages, some tutti, a bright projecting voice),
三味線方 (from two players plus one singer up to large ensembles, with the support roles **上調子**
= a higher-tuned shamisen and **替手** = an ornamental part), 囃子方 (flute and percussion, sometimes
omitted).

**[V] The split from the dance function is real and datable.** 世界大百科事典: 「鑑賞用長唄」
(お座敷長唄, parlour nagauta) arose once composers could work free of stage constraints; the
**研精会 Kenseikai, founded 1902**, drove the spread among domestic amateurs; concert nagauta peaked
in the Taishō era. ニッポニカ: the 1902 Kenseikai made nagauta independent of kabuki as pure
listening music. ブリタニカ places お座敷長唄 in the 文政 era and also cites the 1902 Kenseikai.

**Canonical pieces (all [V] via kotobank and Japanese Wikipedia):**
- **勧進帳 Kanjinchō** — premiered **March 1840 (天保11)** at the 河原崎座, Edo, with 五代目海老蔵
  (later 七代目團十郎) as Benkei. Lyrics **三代目並木五瓶**, music **四代目杵屋六三郎**.
  *(Attribution conflict resolved: Japanese Wikipedia 長唄 credits 初代杵屋六翁 — the same man.
  四代目杵屋六三郎 (1780–1856) 「天保11年（1840年）杵屋六翁と改名する」 and is credited with 勧進帳,
  晒女, 老松, 吾妻八景, 松の緑. ブリタニカ independently writes the 吾妻八景 composer as
  「4世杵屋六三郎（1世六翁）」.)*
- **吾妻八景 Azuma Hakkei** — composed **1829 (文政12)**, April per 世界大百科事典, by 4世杵屋六三郎.
  **This is the single best-documented example of the tuning conventions in practice.** Structure per
  世界大百科事典 (author **浅川玉兎**): a **本調子** 前弾き, then 日本橋/御殿山/高輪 and the
  「佃の合方」; modulating to **二上り** for 駿河台/宮戸川/浅草/隅田川 and the 「砧の合方」; then
  **三下り** for 吉原 and 上野忍岡 and the 「楽の合方」, closing on the sanctity of Benzaiten. Three
  instrumental interludes (合方) and the shamisen on equal footing with the voice. ニッポニカ (author
  **茂手木潔子**) gives the sequence 本調子 → 二上り → 三下り. ニッポニカ calls it 「演奏会用長唄」,
  ブリタニカ 「純鑑賞用長唄」 — the type-specimen of concert nagauta.
- **京鹿子娘道成寺 Kyōganoko Musume Dōjōji** — premiered **March 1753 (宝暦3)** at the Nakamura-za,
  Edo, with 初世中村富十郎. Nagauta by **初世杵屋弥三郎**, with **杵屋作十郎** named as co-composer;
  lyrics **藤本斗文**. ニッポニカ (author **松井俊諭**) calls it a 「先行作の集大成版」; the older
  世界大百科事典 道成寺物 entry calls it 「道成寺物の決定版」.
- **越後獅子 Echigo-jishi** — the nagauta version composed **1811 (文化8)** by **9代目杵屋六左衛門**,
  as one of the accompaniments to the seven-transformation dance 『遅櫻手爾葉七文字』 staged by
  3代目中村歌右衛門 at the Nakamura-za. **Derived from an earlier 地歌 手事物 of the 天明 era
  (1781–89) by 峰崎勾当 of Osaka** — the concrete evidence for jiuta *tegotomono* influence on
  nagauta; Japanese Wikipedia 長唄 also lists 秋色種 and 吾妻八景 as so influenced. **[U]** premiere
  month/day.

#### 義太夫節 gidayū-bushi / 竹本 takemoto — bunraku's narrative music

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neck** | **[V] 太棹 futozao**, with **[V]** the heaviest, largest plectrum: JAC 「太棹三味線を用います」; Japanese Wikipedia 三味線 「大きく厚い撥を用いる」; ニッポニカ (藤田隆則) describes the gidayū bachi as heavy and large, thick at the tip with only a small outward flare. |
| **Class** | **[V] 語り物.** JAC: 「人形芝居のために作られた音楽です」 — music made for the puppet theatre. Designated an Important Intangible Cultural Property. |
| **Role** | **[V] Narrative.** |
| **Tuning** | **[V] Almost always 本調子** — ニッポニカ (藤田隆則). The clearest tuning statement for any genre. |

**[V] 竹本義太夫 Takemoto Gidayū, 1651–1714** — two independent sources agree (JAC gidayū page;
Japanese Wikipedia gives 慶安4 (1651) – 正徳4年9月10日 = **18 Oct 1714**, aged 64). Real name
五郎兵衛; early stage name 清水五郎兵衛; later held the court title **竹本筑後掾** (received 元禄14
/ 1701). Born in 摂津国天王寺村 to a farming family; trained under 清水理太夫 (a follower of
井上播磨掾), then under 宇治加賀掾 in Kyoto; built a 四条河原 theatre in 延宝5年12月; adopted the
name 竹本義太夫 around 延宝8 (1680).

**[V] 竹本座** founded **1684 (貞享元)** in Dōtonbori, Osaka, with Gidayū as 座本, opening with
『世継曽我』.

**[V] Chikamatsu.** 近松門左衛門 wrote 『世継曽我』 in 天和3 (1683) **for 加賀掾** — that was his
first connection with Gidayū. In **貞享2 (1685)** the Takemoto-za staged Chikamatsu's 『出世景清』,
narrated by Gidayū, and their joint work came to be called **新浄瑠璃** as against **古浄瑠璃**.
**[U]** JAC's 浄瑠璃 page dates 出世景清 to 1686 — a one-year discrepancy with Japanese Wikipedia's
貞享2 = 1685, which I could not resolve.

**[V] Style.** Japanese Wikipedia 義太夫節: 「17世紀末に成立し、播磨節、嘉太夫節、小唄などを融合した
豪快で華麗な曲節が特徴。」 — formed in the late 17th century by fusing Harima-bushi, Kadayū-bushi
and kouta; characterised by a bold, splendid melodic style.

**[V] The tayū–shamisen pair.** JAC: a performance normally uses **one 太夫 and one shamisen
player**; the tayū narrates dialogue, feelings and scenery alone, and the shamisen player shapes
mood and scene through tone, timing and melodic patterns. The Japan Arts Council's English UNESCO
bunraku site puts it: *"One narrator tells the story. This includes describing the scene, to
reciting the lines of each character"* and *"The shamisen player is the narrator's partner and adds
atmosphere and depth to the story."*
**[U] — correct this claim.** I could **not** verify that the tayū and shamisen player are formally
*billed or announced as a named duo*. No source says so; the term **相三味線** ("partner shamisen")
turns up only for **rōkyoku**, not gidayū. Soften to "an inseparable working pair," or drop.

**[V] Vocal style.** JAC: the tayū uses the mid-Edo Osaka accent, a wide range and varied vocal
techniques to portray characters and scenes. Japanese Wikipedia adds the **山城風** standard set by
豊竹山城少掾. Women performers (**女流義太夫**) have continued the tradition since the Meiji
娘義太夫 craze — see §2.3 of `history.md`.

**[V] 竹本 in kabuki — richer than the usual one-line gloss.** Via kotobank.jp/word/竹本
(デジタル大辞泉 and ニッポニカ, author **松井俊諭**; 世界大百科事典 entry by **山田庄一**):
竹本 are 「歌舞伎専門の義太夫節演奏者」 — gidayū-bushi performers specialising in kabuki. In
義太夫狂言, the actors deliver most spoken lines and the 竹本 take the narration and sung passages,
with the music frequently rearranged to fit the actors' movements — which is why it became a
specialism. Kabuki 竹本 ranked **below** bunraku tayū and shamisen and were disparaged with the
nickname **チョボ** (etymology uncertain; ニッポニカ says the "dotted score" account is unconfirmed).
A rule bars a performer who moves from bunraku to 竹本 from returning. Their standing rose after the
National Theatre began training successors in **1975**. They sit behind a 御簾 on the upper
stage-right side as seen from the audience, or on a purpose-built **床**. Japanese Wikipedia 義太夫節
adds that bunraku and kabuki 竹本 are 「人的に別の流れである」 — separate streams in personnel.

**Canonical works ([V] via kotobank):**
- **曽根崎心中 Sonezaki Shinjū** — premiered from **7 May 1703 (元禄16)** at the Takemoto-za, Osaka.
  近松門左衛門. ニッポニカ: 「近松最初の世話浄瑠璃」; デジタル大辞泉: 「近松世話物の第1作」 —
  **the first sewamono**. **[U]** who chanted the premiere (the natural assumption that it was
  Gidayū himself is not sourced on the page).
- **義経千本桜 Yoshitsune Senbon Zakura** — premiered **November 1747 (延享4)**, Takemoto-za, Osaka.
  Authors **二代目竹田出雲, 三好松洛, 並木千柳 (= 並木宗輔)**, collaboratively.
- **仮名手本忠臣蔵 Kanadehon Chūshingura** — premiered **August 1748 (寛延1)**, Takemoto-za, Osaka
  (世界大百科事典, author **今尾哲也**). Same three authors. **Discrepancy to watch:** the
  歌舞伎・浄瑠璃外題よみかた辞典 entry on the same kotobank page gives a December 1748 premiere at
  the Osaka 嵐座 — that is the **kabuki adaptation**, not the puppet premiere.
- JAC illustrates with 一谷嫩軍記 熊谷陣屋の段, 絵本太功記 尼ヶ崎の段, 鬼一法眼三略巻 一條大蔵譚,
  壺坂観音霊験記 沢市内の段.

#### 地歌 / 地唄 jiuta — the Kamigata chamber tradition

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neck** | **[V] 中棹 chūzao**, with some schools using a Kyoto instrument *thinner* than the 細棹 (JAC; Japanese Wikipedia 地歌; ニッポニカ 三味線). |
| **Class** | **[V] 歌い物.** |
| **Role** | **[V]** Song accompaniment, chamber ensemble, and accompaniment to 上方舞 dance. Not narrative; not primarily solo. |
| **Tuning** | **[V] All three, with mid-piece retuning as a norm.** |

**[V] The name means "songs of our own place."** ブリタニカ: the term 地歌 came into use *after*
Edo genres reached Kamigata around 1804–18 — i.e. it is a **Kamigata self-designation contrasting
local music with imported Edo music**, which is exactly how JAC's jiuta page frames it (against
長唄 and 常磐津節). In Edo the same repertoire was called **上方歌** (ニッポニカ, author
**井野辺潔**). JAC: 「三味線伴奏の歌曲で、現行の三味線音楽の中では最も古いといわれています」 —
*said to be the oldest of the currently practised shamisen genres*.

**[V] Social context — and blind musicians are central, not incidental.** Japanese Wikipedia 地歌:
「江戸時代以降、京都・大坂を中心とする上方で、検校・勾当などの盲人音楽家によって専門的に創作・
演奏・伝承されてきた三味線歌曲である。」 — *a shamisen song genre professionally composed, performed
and transmitted from the Edo period onward in Kamigata, centred on Kyoto and Osaka, by blind
musicians such as kengyō and kōtō.* It grew in parlours and homes, not theatres. ニッポニカ: already
established in the Kyoto–Osaka area by the **late 16th century**. The **当道座** guild, which had
transmitted *Heike* biwa recitation, took up the shamisen (JAC dates this to the late Muromachi
period); because 当道 required mastery of **both 三絃 and 箏**, jiuta and 箏曲 developed in tight
connection. **[V] The institutional end point is dated:** 「1871年（明治4年）の当道座廃止によって、
地歌・箏曲・胡弓楽を支えてきた近世以来の制度的基盤は失われた。」 — *the 1871 abolition of the
Tōdōza destroyed the institutional basis that had supported jiuta, sōkyoku and kokyū music since
early modern times.* (Japanese Wikipedia 地歌; see Part 3.)

**[V] Early forms and composers.** 三味線組歌 was the main early form (§1.5 of `history.md`), transmitted via the
**野川** and **柳川** lineages; 長歌 and 端歌 developed from the late 17th century — and **this early
jiuta 端歌 is a different thing from the Bakumatsu-era Edo 端唄**. Eighteenth- and nineteenth-century
composers: **峰崎勾当, 松浦検校, 菊岡検校, 石川勾当, 光崎検校**.

**[V] Plectrum.** The jiuta instrument uses an ivory **津山撥 Tsuyama bachi**, and Japanese Wikipedia
三味線 notes that **because high positions are used constantly, the neck-to-body joint differs from
other shamisen**. **[U] / contested:** Japanese Wikipedia 地歌 gives the 野川流 Tsuyama bachi as
c. 7寸5分 long with a tip c. 3寸4分 wide — *narrower*-tipped than standard jiuta bachi at 4寸+;
ニッポニカ describes the jiuta bachi as *wide-flared with a thin tip*. The two descriptions conflict.

**[V] 三曲 sankyoku — and an important terminological correction.** The word has three senses:
1. **A genre-group of four, not three.** JAC's 三曲 page: 「地歌、箏曲、尺八楽、胡弓楽を総称して
   三曲といいます。」 — jiuta, koto music, shakuhachi music and kokyū music collectively.
2. **The ensemble (三曲合奏)**: 三絃 (shamisen) + 箏 + either **尺八 or 胡弓**. **[V] The substitution
   is dated and has a cause.** JAC: 「江戸時代には、三絃と箏と胡弓による合奏が一般的でした」 →
   「胡弓の代わりに尺八を加えた合奏が盛んになりました。」 — the sangen/koto/**kokyū** combination was
   standard in the Edo period; ensembles adding **shakuhachi in place of kokyū** became prevalent
   afterwards. The cause: **the 1871 dissolution of both the 当道座 and the 普化宗** (the Zen sect
   that trained shakuhachi players) opened each instrument to the general public, and the newly
   freed shakuhachi players actively sought ensemble work. kotobank 三曲 (世界大百科事典 author
   **三谷陽子**, ニッポニカ author **千葉潤之介**) corroborates, noting Edo ensembles more often used
   胡弓 or 一節切 than 尺八 — **but ブリタニカ cites a 1785 (天明5) instance of koto + kokyū +
   shakuhachi**, so the change was a shift, not a switch. ニッポニカ warns that 三曲合奏 ≠ a trio,
   since several players may double an instrument.
3. Three especially formal pieces within a genre.
   **[V]** JAC also states that shakuhachi music has its own history and is **not** derived from
   jiuta or koto.

**[V] 手事 tegoto and 手事物 tegotomono — a precise definition.** Via kotobank.jp/word/手事.
日本大百科全書 (author **谷垣内和子**): 手事 = 「歌の途中に挟まれる器楽的なまとまりのある長い間奏
部分」 — *a long, self-contained instrumental interlude inserted in the middle of a song* — and in
current usage the term is **restricted to 地歌 and 箏曲**. 改訂新版 世界大百科事典 (author **平野
健次**) agrees. The simplest tegotomono form is **前歌 – 手事 – 後歌**, optionally with an
introduction (**マクラ**) and a closing section (**チラシ**) around the tegoto. **[V] Chronology:**
the *classification* 「手事物」 was made in **Osaka around 寛政 (1789–1801)** — examples 『残月』 and
『越後獅子』 — and it then flourished especially in **Kyoto from 化政 (1804–30)**, where it is called
**京風手事物 / 京流手事物** — examples 『八重衣』, 『四季の眺』.

**[V] 柳川三味線 Yanagawa shamisen — the "oldest surviving form" claim is partly contradicted.**
柳川検校: birth year unknown, became 検校 in **1639 (寛永16)**, died **11 July 1680 (延宝8)**; from
Osaka, active in Kyoto; founder of the 柳川流 of jiuta shamisen; マイペディア calls him
「〈三味線組歌〉の大成者」. 世界大百科事典 (平野健次): performing the 柳川流 kumiuta uses
「古態の柳川三味線と小さな京ばち」 — *the archaic-form Yanagawa shamisen and a small Kyoto bachi*.
Japanese Wikipedia 地歌: a **thinner neck and smaller body than a standard 細棹**, strung by the
**水張り** method. **But** 世界大百科事典 says the 柳川流 *altered* the kumiuta system, that the
oldest classical pieces were transmitted in a pre-Yanagawa form, and that **only about six kumiuta
survive in 柳川流 transmission**, against the Osaka **野川流** which transmits the whole set of 32.
So: "the Yanagawa shamisen preserves the oldest surviving **instrument form**" is [V]; "the Yanagawa
**line/repertoire** is the oldest surviving tradition" is **contradicted** — Nogawa has the fuller
repertoire and is the only kumiuta line still transmitted. Note also that
`kotobank.jp/word/柳川三味線` and `ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/柳川三味線` both **404** — there is no
standalone reference entry on the instrument.

**[V] Tunings with worked examples.** Japanese Wikipedia 地歌: common tunings 本調子, 二上り,
三下り, with retuning mid-piece. 『融』 低本調子 → 低二上り → 三下り → 本調子; 『玉川』 本調子 →
三下り → 本調子 → 二上り; 『八重衣』 the **shamisen stays in 本調子 throughout while the koto
retunes repeatedly**.

**Canonical pieces — two corrections to the usual list ([V] via kotobank):**
- **残月 Zangetsu** — composer is **峰崎勾当, NOT 松浦検校.** 地歌・箏曲; composed around 天明–寛政
  (1781–1801); 世界大百科事典 (author **久保田敏子**) gives **1792 (寛政4)** as its first appearance
  in print, in 『増補よしの山』. A **本調子 手事物**; ニッポニカ (author **平山けい子**) calls it
  「手事物の代表曲」.
- **雪 Yuki** — by **峰崎勾当**, and classified a **端歌, not a 手事物** (ニッポニカ 地歌 entry,
  author 井野辺潔). **[U]** its date — no source gave one. **Watch out:** 『雪』 is *also* a 端唄
  title, and there is a separate jiuta 手事物 『四季の雪』. Three different things.
- **八千代獅子 Yachiyo-jishi** — a composite, not a simple composition. Originally a **shakuhachi**
  piece; **政島検校** transferred it to **胡弓**; **藤永検校** arranged it for shamisen (ニッポニカ
  dissents with **藤島検校**); lyrics attributed to **園原勾当**. One of the 獅子物. ブリタニカ: the
  original predates 貞享 (1684–88); 世界大百科事典 (久保田敏子) cites its first appearance in print
  in the 寛延4 (1751) 『糸のしらべ』; treated as a **手事物 from 1789 (寛政元)** onward, with three
  段 of tegoto at its core. Tuning **本調子**; a **三下り 替手** by **国山勾当** exists; the koto part
  varies by region — 平調子 in Kansai, 雲井調子 in 山田流.
- **六段 Rokudan — do NOT list this as a jiuta piece.** It is a **箏曲 段物**, instrumental,
  attributed to **八橋検校 (1614–85)** — and 世界大百科事典 (平野健次) marks even that attribution as
  「伝えられる」, with rival theories including a 北島検校 arrangement. A **shamisen version** exists,
  「六段すががき」, attributed to **深草検校**. The jiuta connection is a derivative, not the
  canonical piece.

#### 常磐津節 tokiwazu-bushi

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neck** | **[V] 中棹** (JAC; Japanese Wikipedia specifies 紅木 with an ivory bachi; ニッポニカ 三味線). One player takes the high **上調子** part. |
| **Class** | **[V] 語り物** — but used as **dance accompaniment**: that duality is the point. |
| **Founder** | **[V] 初代常磐津文字太夫, 1709–1781**, founded **1747 (延享4)** in Edo. Two sources agree on 1781 with no question mark. **[U]** exact death date. |
| **Tuning** | **[U] — not documented in any source read.** |

**[V] Derivation.** From **豊後節**. He was a disciple of 宮古路豊後掾, performing as **宮古路文字
太夫**; after the shogunate banned Bungo-bushi he stayed in Edo and 「豊後節に工夫を加えて」 — *added
his own refinements to Bungo-bushi* — developing it into tokiwazu-bushi (JAC). ニッポニカ and
ブリタニカ class it within 広義の豊後節; it is one of the **豊後三流**.
**[U]** Japanese Wikipedia tells a name story — that on renaming himself 「関東文字太夫」 in 1747 he
was prohibited by the 北町奉行 and on the way home took 「常盤津」 from his teacher's real name
石津左司馬, the 皿 element in 盤 later being changed to 石 as inauspicious ("plates break"). That
fetch returned a visibly garbled sentence, so treat the fine detail as unverified; the 1747/宮古路
core is corroborated elsewhere.

**[V] Role.** JAC: it accompanies kabuki dance, is tempo-focused, suits dancing, and by the late
18th century had established itself as kabuki dance music. Japanese Wikipedia: developed as
劇付随音楽 for dance plays, and in Edo kabuki frequently carried the 所作事浄瑠璃 at the climaxes —
the 大詰め of the first play and the 大切り of the second.

**[V] Vocal style.** JAC: natural in tone, relatively fast, with clearly audible pitch and 節回し;
the distinctive device is **「オトシ」**, a delivery that lowers the pitch in three steps. Japanese
Wikipedia: a move away from Bungo-bushi's 扇情的 (inflammatory) manner toward a balance of narration
and song and a refined style, incorporating elements of gidayū-bushi.

**Canonical pieces [V]:** 「関の扉」(= the fuller title 「積恋雪関扉」), 「将門」, 「戻駕」 (kotobank).
Japanese Wikipedia names 「老松」 (1747) as a first-generation work and 「蜘蛛糸梓弦」 (明和, 1765) as
marking the shift from Bungo-bushi to tokiwazu style. Entry authors: **安田文吉** (世界大百科事典),
**林喜代弘・守谷幸則** (ニッポニカ).

#### 清元節 kiyomoto-bushi

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neck** | **[V] 中棹** (JAC, kotobank, ニッポニカ; Japanese Wikipedia 清元節 itself does not state it). |
| **Class** | **[V] 語り物**, developed as kabuki **dance** accompaniment, later also standalone jōruri. |
| **Founder** | **[V] 初代清元延寿太夫, 1777–1825**, founded **1814 (文化11)** in Edo. Three sources agree. |
| **Tuning** | **[U] — not documented in any source read.** |

**[V] Derivation.** Split from **富本節**: he trained under 初代富本斎宮太夫, took the name
二世富本斎宮太夫, performed as 豊後路清海太夫, then became 延寿太夫, breaking away after a dispute
with the head of the Tomimoto school. It is the **latest-formed** of the Bungo-bushi-derived jōruri
styles, and one of the **豊後三流**.

**[V] The high tessitura — confirmed, and the mechanism is named.** JAC: 「高い声を巧みに使う独特の
発声」 — *a distinctive vocal production that skilfully uses high notes* — and 「高音を駆使した粋で
軽妙な曲調」 — *stylish, light melodies exploiting high tones.* Japanese Wikipedia: 「特にその高音を
多用する語りは江戸浄瑠璃の精髄を示すものとして広く愛された」 — *its narration, making heavy use of
high tones in particular, was widely loved as showing the essence of Edo jōruri* — contrasting it
with the more heroic quality of older narrative chanting. **ブリタニカ is the most technical:
「甲高い裏声を使い」 — a high falsetto**, with nasal resonance and a rolling vocal ornament. So
"famously high tessitura" is well supported, and the mechanism is **裏false/falsetto**, not merely
range.

**[V]** kotobank notes the shamisen accompaniment is soft, clear-toned and restrained; Japanese
Wikipedia notes its lyrical charm was strengthened by **nagauta** influence.

**Canonical pieces [V]:** ブリタニカ names 『保名』, 『神田祭』, 『隅田川』, 『三千歳』; Japanese
Wikipedia's list adds 明烏 and 助六. Entry authors: **林喜代弘・守谷幸則** (ニッポニカ),
**竹内道敬** (世界大百科事典).

#### 新内節 shinnai-bushi — several standard claims need correcting

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neck** | **[V] 中棹** (JAC; 山川 「中棹2挺」; ブリタニカ 「太目の中棹」 — a thick-ish chūzao; ニッポニカ 三味線). |
| **Class** | **[V] 語り物** (by inheritance from 浄瑠璃). |
| **Role** | **[V] Narrative**, in the parlour and on the street — **not** in the theatre, and that is a documented *change*. |
| **Ensemble** | **[V] Typically two shamisen**: a 太夫 who sings and plays, plus one **上調子** player on a higher-tuned decorative part over the 本手 basic melody. |
| **Tuning** | **[U] — not documented.** (Though 「二上り新内」 appears as a term in マイペディア's 二上り entry, suggesting an association worth chasing.) |

**[V] The founder is NOT "Tsuruga Shinnai" — correct this.** The lineage is two-step:
- **鶴賀若狭掾 Tsuruga Wakasa-no-jō (1717–1786)**, real name **高井庄兵衛**, founded the
  **鶴賀派 / 鶴賀節**. 世界大百科事典 (author **竹内道敬**): 「生没年：1717-86（享保2-天明6）」;
  ブリタニカ gives the death date 22 March 1786. A disciple of 宮古路加賀太夫 (later 富士松薩摩掾);
  first used the name 朝日若狭掾, taking 鶴賀若狭掾 around **1758**. ニッポニカ and ブリタニカ date
  the founding of 鶴賀節 to **1751 (宝暦元)**.
- **The name 新内節 comes from the second-generation 鶴賀新内**, whose popularity caused the style to
  take his name. JAC: 2代鶴賀新内 (?–1810); ブリタニカ and マイペディア give **1747–1810**. When the
  name took hold is disputed: ニッポニカ says the 文化 era (1804–18), 世界大百科事典 says it spread
  from about **1777 (安永6)**.
- Their relationship is itself contested: ブリタニカ calls the second Shinnai 「門下の2世鶴賀新内」
  (a *disciple*); 精選版日本国語大辞典 calls him 「同門の鶴賀新内を客分として提携」 (a *fellow
  disciple taken on as a guest partner*). Both agree they worked together to establish shinnai as
  **座敷浄瑠璃**, parlour jōruri.
- **[U]** There is **no standalone kotobank entry for 鶴賀新内**, and no "first-generation Tsuruga
  Shinnai" as *the* founder. Do not confuse any of this with 二代目鶴賀若狭掾 (1905–1969) or the
  3世若狭掾 (b. 1938).

**[V] Derivation.** From **富士松節**, itself from **豊後節**. In **1745 (延享2)** 宮古路加賀太夫
(1686–1757) became 富士松薩摩 and founded 富士松節; 富士松魯中 revived the Fujimatsu line in the
天保/安政 period.

**[V] 新内流し shinnai-nagashi — fully confirmed, including the two players.** ニッポニカ: street
performance in which **two players — one singing, one on shamisen — walk slowly while playing**,
chiefly in the Yoshiwara and other pleasure districts. 山川日本史小辞典 classes it as **門付**
(door-to-door). **The business method began in the 文化 era (1804–18)** (世界大百科事典; JAC
agrees). JAC's fuller description: performers walked the 遊里 **in pairs**, in kimono with a towel
on the head; when called into a 座敷, one sang while playing shamisen and the other played the
上調子. Japanese Wikipedia: performers went through the streets as 流し (門付け) centred on
**Yoshiwara**, 「一枚一挺で流す」 — one voice, one shamisen; and 三味線 article: 「立って演奏する
「新内流し」が有名」 — *the standing performance style is famous.* **Cross-reference §2.3 of `history.md`:** the
Tenpō 13 (1842) edict banning street singing of 宮園・豊後・新内 under house eaves, and burning the
performers' instruments, is this practice being suppressed.

**[V] The 爪 / plectrum claim — partially false as usually stated.** The common claim that shinnai
is played with a 爪 fingerpick instead of a bachi traces to **one sentence** in Japanese Wikipedia's
三味線 article — 「新内三味線：中棹。爪を用い、立って演奏する「新内流し」が有名。」 — and
**Japanese Wikipedia's own 新内節 article says nothing at all about 爪 or 撥**, while the 三味線
article carries an 「出典が不十分」 banner. The authoritative sources attach the pick **specifically
to the 上調子 part**: 世界大百科事典 says the 上調子 is played with **爪ようじ, 琴爪, or 小撥**;
山川日本史小辞典 says a **小撥 kobachi** (now ivory) of the 爪楊枝 type; JAC says the 上調子 player
uses a **小撥**, a small plectrum, rather than a standard bachi, and **specifies no plectrum for the
singing/main player at all**; ニッポニカ (藤田隆則) summarises shinnai as "a small bachi for the
上調子, **or in some accounts played with the fingers**." **Recommended wording:** *the 上調子 part
is played with a small pick (小撥) or a koto-type nail-pick rather than a full bachi; sources differ
on the main part, and Nipponica records a finger-plucked account.*

**[V] Pleasure-quarter and love-suicide association — confirmed, and the move there has a cause.**
JAC: the repertoire draws on 「駆け落ちや遊女と客の心中事件などを題材に」 — *themes such as
elopements and double suicides between courtesans and clients.* kotobank: mainly 心中 stories in the
端物 format, with 扇情的 (provocative) and 哀愁 (melancholy) melodies. ニッポニカ characterises the
appeal as **江戸情緒**, Edo sentiment. **[V] The cause of the move:** shinnai **was originally
performed in kabuki and shifted to 座敷浄瑠璃 after losing out to 常磐津節** (JAC). **[U]** the term
**道行 michiyuki** — Japanese Wikipedia 新内節 does not use it and I did not verify a
michiyuki-specific convention.

**[V] Vocal style.** JAC: glossy and lyrically rich, with a distinctive sorrowful melody; singers
mix **chest and falsetto** voice in a technically demanding way, cover a wide range, and emphasise
the ends of melodic phrases (**節尻**).

**[V] Repertoire categories.** **段物** are borrowed or adapted from 義太夫節; **端物** are the
pieces most characteristic of shinnai.

**Canonical pieces [V]:** 『蘭蝶 Ranchō』 and 『明烏 Akegarasu』 (full title 『明烏夢泡雪』) are both
confirmed as representative. kotobank's fuller list: 伊太八, 音羽丹七, 日高川, かさね, 城木屋,
千両幟, 梅川, 真夢, 佐倉宗五郎, 花井お梅, 高橋お伝, 夕霧, 膝栗毛.

#### 小唄 kouta and 端唄 hauta — the short-song parlour genres

##### 小唄 kouta

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neck** | **[V] 細棹, slightly oversized** — JAC 「やや大きめの細棹三味線」; ニッポニカ 三味線 (藤田隆則) lists 細棹 for 長唄/荻江節/小唄. **CONFLICT:** Japanese Wikipedia 小唄 says **中棹**, with thicker strings and a wooden 駒. Two authoritative sources against one under-sourced one; report 細棹 with the dissent noted. |
| **Class** | **[V] 歌い物.** |
| **Role** | **[V]** Accompaniment to a short song, in a small room. One shamisen + one singer is the norm, with 替手, 上調子 or 下調子 added in some pieces. Traditional 鳴り物 percussion is **not** used (modern performances sometimes add it). |
| **Tuning** | **[U] — not documented in any source read.** |

**[V] The bare-fingernail claim: verified in substance, wrong in one word.** Four independent
sources confirm that kouta is played **爪弾き tsumabiki, without a bachi**:
- Japanese Wikipedia 小唄: 「端唄は撥を使うのに対して小唄は爪弾きである。」 — *whereas hauta uses a
  bachi, kouta is tsumabiki.*
- Japanese Wikipedia 端唄 states the mirror sentence.
- JAC (`edc29/.../other/uta/`), kouta section: 「撥を使わない爪弾きをします。」 — and this is the only
  one of the three genres on that page (端唄 / うた沢 / 小唄) for which a technique is specified.
- ニッポニカ (kotobank 小唄): in principle 爪弾き, described as 「親指で人差し指の腹を押し、指頭の
  片側の肉を盛り上げてまろやかな音を出す」 — *pressing the pad of the index finger with the thumb so
  the flesh on one side of the fingertip bulges out, producing a mellow tone.*
- ニッポニカ 三味線 (藤田隆則) independently lists 小唄 as generally bachi-less, 爪弾き.

**So correct the wording.** The sources are explicit that the stroke uses the **fingertip flesh**,
with the nail ideally **not** touching the string; Japanese Wikipedia adds that **ロカン** or a
**小撥** are used in some cases. Write *"plucked with the fingertip (爪弾き tsumabiki) — the thumb
pressing the index pad — rather than with a bachi,"* not *"with the bare fingernail."*
**[V] And the reason is given:** performances were held mainly in 四畳半 parlours, and **the bachi
was avoided there because it was too loud** (Japanese Wikipedia 小唄).

**[V] Derivation and dates.** From **端唄**, at the **end of the Edo period** (デジタル大辞泉: 「端唄
から変化した」). JAC: it began in the late Edo period as **清元風端唄** — hauta in the kiyomoto
manner — created by 清元 performers. ニッポニカ describes the line as one that gave hauta
「品位をもたせ」, *lent it dignity*, and dates the **present form to the late Meiji period**.
Japanese Wikipedia: the abbreviated name became established in Meiji/Taishō; the term generally
means **江戸小唄**.

**[V] 清元お葉 — the founder figure, with dates and a dated founding piece.** **清元お葉
(1840–1901)**, per ニッポニカ (kotobank 小唄; entry authors **林喜代弘・守谷幸則**), who in
**1855 (安政2)** premiered **『散るはうき』**, a hit that 「小唄の礎を築いた」 — *laid the foundation
of kouta.* JAC independently names 「お葉 (1840–1901)」 among the 清元 performers who created
清元風端唄. **[U] The form 蔭山お葉 could not be verified** — ニッポニカ does not use it. **Use
清元お葉.**

**[V] First school / 家元 — with a discrepancy to flag.** **堀小多満** was the first to style
himself iemoto and founded **堀派会**, the first kouta school, with Tokyo authorities' approval —
but ブリタニカ and 山川 date this to **1917** while Japanese Wikipedia 小唄 dates it to
**1913 (大正2)**. Later schools 田村派, 蓼派, 吉村派, 春日派 branched from it.

**[V] Geisha context.** ニッポニカ: in the Meiji period kouta was favoured among 「花街で興じる
貴紳富豪」 — *the nobles and wealthy who amused themselves in the pleasure districts.* JAC: it was
transmitted mainly in the **花柳界** from mid-Meiji, spreading into ordinary households in Shōwa —
note that **JAC does not use the word 芸者**. マイペディア: it moved from お座敷芸 to concert format
in Taishō.

**[V] REFUTED — the "characteristic lack of instrumental preludes."** ニッポニカ and ブリタニカ both
state that kouta **has** a **前弾き maebiki** (prelude) and a **後弾き** (postlude). Drop the claim,
or invert it. The likeliest origin of the English error is a garbling of "no hayashi" — it is the
**鳴り物 percussion** that is absent.

**[V] Scale and canon.** Japanese Wikipedia: **over 2,000 pieces composed, c. 200 still performed.**
Canonical: 『散るはうき』 (1855), 『逢うて別れて』, 『夜桜や』. Incidental and telling: in **1930 the
Tokyo Metropolitan Police banned erotic kouta as harmful to children.**

##### 端唄 hauta

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neck** | **[V] 細棹** — JAC 「細棹三味線を用います。」 **[U]** from any second source; ニッポニカ's neck list omits hauta. |
| **Plectrum** | **[V] a 撥** (both Japanese Wikipedia articles, quoted above). |
| **Class** | **[V] 歌い物.** |
| **Role** | Accompaniment to a short song of about **one to three minutes**. |

**[V] Date of rise — precise, and causally tied to censorship.** ニッポニカ and 世界大百科事典
(both entries by **倉田喜弘**): the peak was **the 1850s to the early Meiji period (嘉永〜明治初期)**;
it was popular **among ordinary people rather than in theatres or geisha houses**; it faded by
mid-Meiji as Western-influenced music spread from the 1880s. **The trigger is datable: in
1842 (天保13), during the Tenpō Reforms, an edict banned 「浄瑠璃、はうた、稽古いたすまじきこと」** —
*jōruri and hauta are not to be practised.* After the ban eased, new songs appeared. Japanese
Wikipedia and JAC both tie the genre's popularity to the lifting of the Tenpō shamisen ban; Japanese
Wikipedia dates the ban's immediate effect to a vogue for **unaccompanied 都々逸 and 大津絵節**,
later set to shamisen, after which 「端唄」 became the general term. (Cross-reference §2.3 of `history.md`: this is
the same 1842 edict whose text I quote there. The two strands independently confirm each other.)
**Revival: 1968 (昭和43), 根岸登喜子 led the 「端唄の会」 concerts.**

**[V] Terminological trap — flag this prominently.** **端歌** (with 歌) is a separate **Kamigata
jiuta category**, not Edo **端唄**. Worse: Japanese Wikipedia 端唄 records that from mid-Edo 端唄 was
used as a **synonym for 小唄**, and that **until the 1920s kouta was also called hauta**, the terms
separating only later — which is exactly why different sources label the same song うた沢, 小唄 or
端唄. Its earliest documented use is in vol. 3 of 『松の葉』, **1703**.

**[V] Relation to kouta and utazawa.** デジタル大辞泉 calls 端唄 the **母体** (matrix) of both
うた沢 and 小唄; ブリタニカ adds that hauta's tempo sits **between** the two. JAC: hauta is the source
of both. JAC also calls it **江戸端唄** to distinguish it from the Kamigata 端歌.

**Canonical pieces [V]:** 春雨, 梅にも春, 夕ぐれ, 秋の夜, 紀伊の国, 京の四季, 槍さび, 雪, 黒髪,
わがもの (kotobank); 山川 picks 「春雨」「夕ぐれ」「秋の夜」「わがもの」 as representative.

##### うた沢 / 歌沢 utazawa — the genre the standard list forgets

**[V]** Worth including: the Japanese reference works always list it alongside 端唄 and 小唄 in the
歌い物 group. JAC: a vocal-focused form, 「格調高く繊細な」 — *dignified and delicate* — slow in
tempo, founded in the late Edo period as a hauta offshoot by **笹本彦太郎 (later 歌沢笹丸)** and
others, **modelled on 一中節 to give hauta more dignity**; split into the **寅派** and **芝派**,
collectively うた沢; uses the **中棹**; flourished in the 花柳界 in Taishō, then declined in Shōwa
as kouta rose. 山川 dates the emergence to **1853 (嘉永6)**, out of a hobbyists' group.

#### 民謡 min'yō — regional folk song

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neck** | **[V] 中棹 by default, but genuinely variable.** Japanese Wikipedia 三味線: 「民謡の三味線は、素朴な音色のする中棹が多く使われるが、太棹または細棹を使う地域や曲目もたくさんみられる。」 — *mostly a chūzao with a plain tone, but many regions and pieces use futozao or hosozao.* ニッポニカ's neck list **omits min'yō entirely**. Do not present min'yō as having one neck class. |
| **Role** | **[V] Optional.** JAC: accompaniment 「よく使われます」 — shamisen, 太鼓, 尺八, 笛 — but songs are also sung **unaccompanied or with handclaps alone**; Japanese Wikipedia: 「和楽器を加えるかどうかは厳密な規則はなく任意である」 — *whether to add Japanese instruments is optional, with no strict rule.* **This is the key taxonomic point: min'yō is the one "genre" in the map where the shamisen is not constitutive.** |

**[V] Two rhythmic types, and the term is useful.** JAC: **八木節様式** (metrical, sung to a beat)
vs **追分様式** (free of metre, usually sung solo, using the **コブシ** ornament technique).

**[V] The word 民謡 is a Meiji translation, not a native genre name.** kotobank 民謡 — ニッポニカ
(author **田井竜一**), 世界大百科事典 (Japanese section **三隅治雄**, general section **徳丸吉彦**):
**Volkslied** was coined by **Herder in 1773**; the Japanese word entered use **in the 1890s**, when
**森鷗外** and **上田敏** used it to translate *Volkslied* / "folk song"; in **1906** 志田義秀
described 民謡 as the folk counterpart to art poetry, after which the term spread. **Before this the
common words were 俚謡 and 俗謡.** The encyclopedias stress that the concept is historically bound to
European Romantic ideas of "the folk" — **民謡 is a modern analytic category imposed on older
material.** (Japanese Wikipedia hedges the 鷗外/上田 attribution with 「だという」.)

**[V] The actual historical link between the shamisen and min'yō is professional and commercial, not
village practice.** kotobank 民謡: the shamisen's arrival in the early modern period led to farming
and fishing songs being turned into **parlour songs (「座敷歌化」) in the pleasure quarters**; some
songs grew out of **瞽女 goze and 遊女** performers, which later produced works such as **八木節**.
This matters: the standard romantic account has the shamisen rising out of the villages, whereas the
Japanese reference literature has it coming *into* village song from the licensed quarters and the
itinerant professionals.

**[V] Meiji/Taishō standardisation and recording.** kotobank 民謡: the collection drive —
前田林外 ed. 『日本民謡全集』 (**1907**), 童謡研究会 ed. 『日本民謡大全』 (**1909**), the Ministry of
Education's 『俚謡集』 (**1914**); classification schemes by **柳田国男** (by setting: 田歌, 庭歌,
祝歌) and **町田佳聲** (natural vs created); **form was standardised** into a short five-line type
and a repeated 7-7 / 7-5 type called **口説**; records and radio actively promoted min'yō, some
records sold under the older label 俚謡; **professional singers emerged and their highly technical
singing became the mainstream style**; in **1979** the Agency for Cultural Affairs began a
nationwide emergency survey. **[V]** Japanese Wikipedia, citing **武田俊輔『民謡の再編成』**, argues
min'yō was largely a **20th-century construct from Taishō to early Shōwa**, assembled by lyricists,
composers, record companies, the Ministry of Education, folklorists and NHK, with regional melodies
「民謡」の名のもとに再編成・固定化し — *reorganised and fixed under the name "min'yō"* — and
industrialised for nationwide distribution, where traditional songs had previously varied in lyrics
and phrasing by person and occasion.

**[V] 新民謡 shin-min'yō.** Songs in min'yō style newly written **from the Taishō era (1912–26)
onward**, also called **創作民謡** and, for the regional promotional songs, **地方小唄**. Figures:
**北原白秋** (lyrics), **野口雨情** (lyrics), **中山晋平** (music) launched the movement; Japanese
Wikipedia adds 西条八十 and 藤井清水. Named examples: 「須坂小唄」, 「ちゃっきり節」 (**1927**),
「龍峡小唄」, 「八戸小唄」, 「三階節」.

#### 浪曲 rōkyoku / 浪花節 — the other futozao narrative genre

Included because it is the genre most often left out of English-language shamisen taxonomies and
because it is one of only two genres with a firmly documented default tuning.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neck and tuning** | **[V] 太棹, tuned 三下り**, played with a **small bachi**. Japanese Wikipedia 浪曲: 「浪曲の三味線は太棹を用い、調弦は三下りにして、小ぶりの撥で弾く」. JAC agrees. ニッポニカ (藤田隆則): 「浪曲 often stays in 三下り for all moods」 — i.e. it **does not follow the usual mood–tuning associations**. |
| **Class** | **[V] 語り物** (via the 浪花節 heading in デジタル大辞泉's list). |
| **Formation** | **[V]** Late-Edo **street** art, built on the 願人坊主' 「ちょぼくれ／ちょんがれ」 with デロレン祭文 and other **門付け** arts joining, influenced by 説経節 and, in Bakumatsu Osaka, by 講談. |

**[V] Names and dates.** First called 浮かれ節; popular in Osaka and Nagoya; reached Tokyo in early
Meiji where it took the name **浪花節**; spread nationwide in early Shōwa via radio and records; the
name **浪曲** spread after appearing in newspapers and became general in the Shōwa era (精選版's
earliest cited use of 浪曲 is **1947**). **[U]** a single clean date for the change. マイペディア
calls it the most popular form of mass entertainment c. **1900–1950**. Dated chronology from Japanese
Wikipedia: 1882–88 七色節 performers outnumbered 浪花節; 1891 reversal; c. 1892 established in the
寄席; **1900, 53 of Tokyo's 120 寄席 featured 浪花節**; **1903** first SP recording (浪花亭愛造);
**1907** 桃中軒雲右衛門's long run at the 本郷座, and a peak of **448 performers**.

**[V] Personnel and structure — and the term the gidayū "named duo" claim actually belongs to.**
The singer is the **浪曲師**, the shamisen accompanist the **曲師**, and **the regular partner is
called 相三味線** — this is where that term lives. There is **no score, only a script**, and
performance is improvisational. The delivery alternates **節** (sung — conveying the situation and
the characters' feelings) and **啖呵 tanka** (spoken — advancing the story through the characters'
lines), with the priority order 「一声、二節、三啖呵」. Regional styles: 関西節 (低調子,
melody-forward), 関東節 (高調子, tanka-forward), 中京節. A session runs about 30 minutes. One of the
「日本三大話芸」 with 落語 and 講談.

**Canonical [V]:** 二代目広沢虎造's 『清水次郎長伝』. **2代目京山幸枝若 was designated a 人間国宝
in December 2024 — the first in 浪曲語り.**

#### 津軽三味線 tsugaru-jamisen — placement only

Covered in depth by the `tsugaru-shamisen` skill. Its position in the map:

- **[V] 太棹**, played with a small bachi tipped in **鼈甲** tortoiseshell. Japanese Wikipedia 三味線:
  「津軽三味線：太棹。先端が鼈甲製の小ぶりの撥を用いる。津軽民謡の伴奏に使用。」 ニッポニカ
  (藤田隆則) groups it with 義太夫節 and 浪曲 under 太棹.
- **[V] Derived from 津軽民謡 accompaniment** — and the **solo-concert status is a 20th-century
  development with a date.** kotobank 津軽三味線, ニッポニカ entry (author **竹内勉**): originally for
  accompanying Tsugaru folk song, it became established 「独奏・合奏楽器として」 — *as a solo **and
  ensemble** instrument* — and the **曲弾き** showcase performances by **三橋美智也 and 木田林松栄
  around 1956** were the breakthrough. **The name 「津軽三味線」 itself became general only from about
  that time.** Figures named: 梅田豊月 (credited with adding 「はでな曲弾き」), 白川軍八郎,
  木田林松栄, 高橋竹山. Technique: a thick heavy bachi striking the 一の糸 forcefully for rhythm, the
  三の糸 decorated by a fine upward **スクイバチ** scoop.
- **Its structural place:** it is the **only genre in this taxonomy where the shamisen is primarily a
  solo concert instrument**. Every other genre here is vocal accompaniment or narrative. That framing
  is consistent with the sources — **but note that ニッポニカ says 「独奏・合奏」, solo *and*
  ensemble**, so "solo" should not be overstated.
- **[U] Two gaps for the Tsugaru file:** **仁太坊 Nitabō** is **not** mentioned on the kotobank page,
  and neither **門付け** nor **坊様 bosama** appear there. The term 「叩き」 is not used by kotobank
  either — it describes the striking action without that label. The bosama descent is real (Part 3)
  but it is **not** in the standard Japanese encyclopedia entry on the genre, which is worth knowing.

### 4.4 Genres the standard English list omits

**[V]** All are named by the Japanese reference works alongside the usual genres and belong in a
complete taxonomy:

- **一中節 ichūbushi** — founded by **都太夫一中 (1650–1724)** in Kyoto. The *parent* of Bungo-bushi,
  and therefore the grandparent of tokiwazu, tomimoto, kiyomoto, fujimatsu and shinnai.
- **河東節 katōbushi** — arose in **Edo in the 享保 era (1716–36)**; named for a place, not a founder.
- **富本節 tomimoto-bushi** — **1748**, the middle member of the 豊後三流 and the parent of kiyomoto.
- **宮薗節 / 薗八節 miyazono-bushi** — c. **1762 (宝暦12)**, from 宮古路薗八 in Kamigata. (Note: the
  1842 edict in §2.3 of `history.md` names 宮園 among the three street-sung genres being suppressed.)
- **荻江節 ogie-bushi** — **細棹, 歌い物**; listed in both 世界大百科事典's utaimono list and
  ニッポニカ's 細棹 list.
- **富士松節 fujimatsu-bushi** — **1745**, the parent of shinnai.
- **うた沢 / 歌沢 utazawa** — **1853**, between hauta and kouta (§4.1).
- **豊後節 bungo-bushi** — the banned ancestor of five of the above (§2.3 of `history.md`).
- **三味線組歌 shamisen kumiuta** — ブリタニカ: 「最古の芸術的な三味線音楽」, *the oldest artistic
  shamisen music* (§1.5 of `history.md`).

### 4.5 Claims in the standard English account to fix

Collected for convenience; each is sourced above.

1. **小唄 "has no instrumental preludes"** — **refuted.** ニッポニカ and ブリタニカ both document a
   前弾き and a 後弾き. Probably a garbling of "no 鳴り物 percussion."
2. **小唄 "played with the bare fingernail"** — the technique (爪弾き) is right, the description is
   wrong: it is the **fingertip flesh**, nail ideally not touching the string.
3. **残月's composer** — **峰崎勾当**, not 松浦検校.
4. **六段 as a canonical jiuta piece** — it is a **箏曲 段物** attributed to 八橋検校; the shamisen
   version is 六段すががき, attributed to 深草検校.
5. **新内 "uses a 爪 instead of a bachi"** — the pick belongs to the **上調子** part; the claim lives
   only in an under-sourced Wikipedia sentence.
6. **"founded by 鶴賀新内"** — the school's founder was **鶴賀若狭掾 (1717–1786)**, who founded
   鶴賀節 in **1751**; the *name* 新内節 comes from the **second-generation 鶴賀新内 (1747–1810)**.
7. **蔭山お葉** — unverifiable; the sources say **清元お葉 (1840–1901)**.
8. **三曲 = "shamisen + koto + shakuhachi (formerly kokyū)"** — right as the *ensemble*
   (三曲合奏), but **三曲** as a term names **four genres** (地歌・箏曲・尺八楽・胡弓楽), and can
   also mean three formal pieces.
9. **柳川三味線 as "the oldest surviving form"** — true of the **instrument**; the Yanagawa **line**
   retains only about six kumiuta where the Nogawa line transmits all 32.
10. **The gidayū tayū/shamisen "named duo"** — not verifiable as formal joint billing. The term
    **相三味線** belongs to **rōkyoku**.
11. **"The two-way split of shamisen music into utaimono and katarimono"** — the split is real and
    Japanese, but it is a **full-breadth vocal-music taxonomy**, not a shamisen-specific one, and
    the Japan Arts Council's own primary split is by **venue** (theatre vs parlour) instead.
