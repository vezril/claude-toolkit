# Medication-Induced Movement Disorders and Other Adverse Effects

**These are not mental disorders.** They are in the manual because they are essential to
differential diagnosis and to management — a tremor, a restlessness or a rigidity has to be
identified as iatrogenic rather than psychiatric, and the codes exist so it can be recorded.

Mostly caused by antipsychotics and other dopamine-blocking agents, but also lithium,
antidepressants, antiemetics (metoclopramide, prochlorperazine) and others.

## Acute effects

- **Medication-induced acute dystonia** — sustained abnormal posturing or muscle spasm within
  days of starting or raising a dose. Torticollis, oculogyric crisis, laryngeal dystonia.
  Frightening, sometimes airway-threatening, and rapidly reversible with anticholinergics.
- **Medication-induced acute akathisia** — subjective restlessness with an inability to stay
  still, often reported as an urge to move the legs or pace. **Associated with suicide risk and
  with treatment non-adherence**, and very frequently mistaken for agitation, anxiety, or
  worsening of the underlying illness — which leads to a dose increase and makes it worse. The
  single most clinically important entry in this chapter.
- **Medication-induced parkinsonism** and **other medication-induced parkinsonism** — tremor,
  rigidity, bradykinesia, masked facies, within weeks of starting or raising a dose.
- **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome** — **a medical emergency.** Hyperthermia, severe rigidity,
  autonomic instability, altered consciousness, raised creatine kinase. Mortality is
  significant. Stop the offending agent and treat acutely; this is not a formulation question.
- **Medication-induced postural tremor** — classically lithium, also valproate and some
  antidepressants.

## Tardive effects

- **Tardive dyskinesia** — involuntary choreiform or athetoid movements, typically orofacial,
  developing after **months to years** of exposure. May be irreversible, and may emerge or
  worsen on dose reduction, which complicates both diagnosis and management.
- **Tardive dystonia** and **tardive akathisia** — later-onset, more persistent forms of the
  acute syndromes.

**Acute versus tardive is the chapter's main axis**: time to onset, relation to dose changes,
and reversibility all differ, and so does the management.

## Other adverse effects

- **Antidepressant discontinuation syndrome** — within days of stopping or sharply reducing an
  antidepressant after ≥1 month: dizziness, sensory disturbances ("brain zaps"), insomnia,
  flu-like symptoms, anxiety. Commonest with short half-life agents such as paroxetine and
  venlafaxine. **This is not relapse and it is not dependence** — distinguishing it from
  returning depression is the clinical task, and the timing usually settles it.
- **Other adverse effect of medication** — the catch-all for anything else being recorded.

## Differentials, which are the reason the chapter exists

- **Akathisia vs agitation vs anxiety vs worsening psychosis** — get this wrong and the
  treatment makes the patient worse. Ask about the subjective urge to move and the temporal
  relation to the dose.
- **Medication-induced parkinsonism vs Parkinson's disease** — symmetry, temporal relation to
  the drug, and reversibility on withdrawal point to the medication. Dementia with Lewy bodies
  carries **severe neuroleptic sensitivity**, so an unusually severe reaction is itself a clue
  ([[dsm-neurocognitive]]).
- **Tardive dyskinesia vs stereotypies, tics, or the spontaneous dyskinesias** seen in
  never-medicated psychosis ([[dsm-neurodevelopmental]]).
- **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome vs serotonin syndrome vs malignant catatonia** — overlapping
  and all urgent; rigidity and hyporeflexia favour NMS, clonus and hyperreflexia favour
  serotonin syndrome ([[dsm-schizophrenia-spectrum]] for catatonia).
- **Discontinuation syndrome vs depressive relapse** — onset within days and sensory symptoms
  favour discontinuation ([[dsm-depressive]]).

## Rails

Not a diagnostic instrument. **Never assign a diagnosis to the user or a third party** — explain
what a category means instead. Criteria require clinically significant distress or impairment
plus exclusion of substance/medication and other medical causes. If self-harm, suicidality,
abuse or acute crisis surfaces, drop the taxonomy and respond to the person: **988** (US/Canada
call or text), or local emergency services. Criteria text is copyrighted and not reproduced —
named only. Full rails in [[dsm-5]].
